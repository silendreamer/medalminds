#### Bacteria vs. Archaea: Two Domains of Prokaryotes

Both **Bacteria** and **Archaea** lack a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, placing them in the prokaryotes. They were long grouped together, but molecular analysis (rRNA sequences) revealed they are evolutionarily distinct domains. Key differences include membrane lipid chemistry (ester-linked in bacteria; ether-linked, branched isoprenoids in archaea) and the absence of **peptidoglycan** in most archaeal cell walls.

#### Prokaryotic Cell Structure

A prokaryotic cell is bounded by a **plasma membrane** and, in most bacteria, a **cell wall** composed of **peptidoglycan** — a mesh of NAM/NAG sugars cross-linked by short peptides. The **Gram stain** exploits cell-wall thickness: **Gram-positive** bacteria (thick peptidoglycan layer) retain the crystal-violet stain and appear purple; **Gram-negative** bacteria (thin peptidoglycan plus an outer membrane) are decolorized and counterstained pink. Other structures include the **nucleoid** (circular chromosome), **ribosomes** (70S), **pili** (attachment), and **flagella** (locomotion). A **capsule** of polysaccharide can surround the wall for protection and immune evasion.

#### Archaea and Extremophiles

Archaea are famous as **extremophiles** — organisms thriving in extreme conditions. **Thermophiles** (e.g., *Thermus aquaticus*) live above 60 °C; **hyperthermophiles** above 80 °C. **Halophiles** tolerate saturated salt; **acidophiles** prefer pH < 3; **methanogens** (obligate anaerobes) produce **methane** as a metabolic byproduct and inhabit swamps, animal guts, and deep-sea sediments. These adaptations are partly due to unique lipid membranes and heat-stable enzymes.

#### Metabolic Diversity in Prokaryotes

Prokaryotes display unrivaled metabolic diversity. **Photoautotrophs** (e.g., cyanobacteria) perform **oxygenic photosynthesis**, contributing to Earth's atmospheric oxygen. **Chemoautotrophs** oxidize inorganic compounds (sulfur, ammonia, iron) for energy — critical in deep-sea hydrothermal vent ecosystems. **Heterotrophs** include both aerobic decomposers and strict **anaerobes** using alternate electron acceptors (nitrate, sulfate). **Nitrogen-fixing** bacteria (*Rhizobium*, *Azotobacter*) convert atmospheric N₂ into bioavailable ammonia, anchoring the nitrogen cycle.
