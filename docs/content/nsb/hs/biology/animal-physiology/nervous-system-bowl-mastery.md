#### Bowl Toss-up Patterns
"For 10 points, what cells produce the myelin sheath around axons in the peripheral nervous system?" → **Schwann cells**

"For 10 points, what term describes the rapid depolarization of a neuron's membrane potential from about -70 mV to +30 mV?" → **action potential**

"For 10 points, what neurotransmitter is released at the neuromuscular junction?" → **acetylcholine**

"For 10 points, what division of the autonomic nervous system is associated with 'fight or flight' responses and uses norepinephrine as its postganglionic neurotransmitter?" → **sympathetic nervous system**

"For 10 points, what ion rushes into a neuron through voltage-gated channels during the depolarization phase of an action potential?" → **sodium ions** (Na+)

#### Quick Distinctions
- Schwann cells = PNS myelin; oligodendrocytes = CNS myelin
- Sympathetic = thoracolumbar; parasympathetic = craniosacral
- Absolute refractory = no new AP possible; relative = strong stimulus can trigger
- EPSP = depolarization; IPSP = hyperpolarization
- ACh degraded by acetylcholinesterase; serotonin removed by reuptake

#### Review Questions
1. Why does the action potential propagate in only one direction along an axon?
2. Name two diseases caused by disruption of neurotransmitter function and identify which neurotransmitter is involved in each.
3. What is saltatory conduction and why is it faster than continuous conduction?
