#### Hypothalamus-Pituitary Axis
The hypothalamus produces **releasing hormones** (GnRH, TRH, CRH, GHRH) and **inhibiting hormones** that travel via the portal blood system to the **anterior pituitary** (adenohypophysis), which releases **tropic hormones** (FSH, LH, TSH, ACTH, GH, prolactin) that target other endocrine glands. **Posterior pituitary** (neurohypophysis) stores and releases ADH and oxytocin (made in hypothalamus). Negative feedback: target gland hormones inhibit hypothalamus and anterior pituitary. Example: CRH → ACTH → cortisol → cortisol inhibits CRH and ACTH release.

#### Key Glands and Hormones
- **Thyroid**: T3 (triiodothyronine) and T4 (thyroxine) — metabolic rate, growth, development; calcitonin — lowers blood Ca²+
- **Parathyroid**: PTH (parathyroid hormone) — raises blood Ca²+ (activates osteoclasts, increases Ca²+ reabsorption in kidney, activates vitamin D)
- **Adrenal cortex**: cortisol (stress, anti-inflammatory, gluconeogenesis), aldosterone (Na+/water balance)
- **Adrenal medulla**: epinephrine and norepinephrine (fight-or-flight; released quickly)
- **Pancreas**: insulin (lowers blood glucose), glucagon (raises blood glucose), somatostatin (inhibits both)
- **Pineal gland**: melatonin (circadian rhythm, sleep-wake cycle)
- **Ovaries**: estrogen (follicular development, female secondary sex characteristics, uterine proliferation), progesterone (maintains pregnancy, luteal phase)
- **Testes**: testosterone (male secondary sex characteristics, spermatogenesis)
