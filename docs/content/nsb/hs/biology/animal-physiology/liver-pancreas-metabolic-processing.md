#### Liver Functions
The liver receives nutrient-rich blood from the small intestine via the **portal vein** and processes it before release into systemic circulation. Key functions: (1) **Nutrient processing**: converts fructose/galactose to glucose; stores glucose as glycogen; converts excess amino acids to glucose (gluconeogenesis) or urea (urea cycle); packages lipids into VLDL lipoproteins. (2) **Protein synthesis**: albumin (plasma osmotic pressure), clotting factors (fibrinogen, prothrombin), transport proteins. (3) **Detoxification**: cytochrome P450 enzymes metabolize drugs, alcohol, and toxins; conjugates bilirubin (from hemoglobin breakdown) for excretion in bile. (4) **Bile production**: bile salts emulsify dietary fats; bile stored in gallbladder; released into duodenum when fat enters.

#### Pancreatic Functions
**Exocrine pancreas** (~99% of cells): acinar cells produce digestive enzymes released into duodenum via pancreatic duct. Enzymes secreted as zymogens (inactive precursors): trypsinogen → activated to trypsin by enterokinase in duodenum → trypsin activates chymotrypsinogen and other zymogens. Bicarbonate (HCO3−) neutralizes acidic chyme entering duodenum. **Endocrine pancreas** (islets of Langerhans, ~1%): alpha cells (glucagon), beta cells (insulin), delta cells (somatostatin).

#### Review Questions
1. Why is blood from the small intestine processed through the liver before entering systemic circulation? What would happen if liver function were severely impaired (consider nutrient processing, detoxification, and protein synthesis)?
2. Pancreatitis is inflammation of the pancreas in which digestive enzymes are activated within the pancreas rather than in the duodenum. Why is this so dangerous?
3. Alcohol is processed by the liver using NAD+ as a cofactor. Predict the metabolic consequences of heavy alcohol consumption on the liver's ability to carry out gluconeogenesis and fatty acid oxidation.
