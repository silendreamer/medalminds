#### Neuron Anatomy
**Dendrites**: receive signals from other neurons or sensory receptors; branch extensively. **Cell body (soma)**: contains nucleus; integrates inputs. **Axon**: long process that carries action potential away from cell body; may be myelinated. **Axon terminals (synaptic boutons)**: release neurotransmitters into synapse. **Myelin sheath**: made by Schwann cells (PNS) or oligodendrocytes (CNS); insulates axon; speeds conduction. **Nodes of Ranvier**: gaps in myelin where action potentials regenerate (saltatory conduction — "jumping" between nodes; much faster than unmyelinated conduction).

#### Resting Membrane Potential
~-70 mV (inside negative relative to outside). Maintained by: (1) Na+/K+ ATPase pump — actively moves 3 Na+ out, 2 K+ in per cycle (net outward positive charge movement); (2) K+ leak channels — K+ leaks out down its concentration gradient (more permeable at rest than Na+). At rest: high Na+ outside, high K+ inside. The membrane is polarized.

#### The Action Potential
**Threshold**: stimulus sufficient to depolarize membrane to ~-55 mV triggers action potential. **Depolarization**: voltage-gated Na+ channels open rapidly → Na+ rushes in → membrane potential rises to ~+30 mV. **Repolarization**: Na+ channels inactivate; voltage-gated K+ channels open slowly → K+ rushes out → membrane potential falls. **Hyperpolarization (undershoot)**: K+ channels slow to close → membrane briefly more negative than rest. **Refractory period**: absolute refractory (no new action potential possible — Na+ channels inactivated); relative refractory (strong stimulus can trigger action potential — K+ channels still open). All-or-nothing: either full action potential or none.

#### Saltatory Conduction
Myelinated axons conduct faster because action potentials jump between nodes of Ranvier (unmyelinated gaps). Conduction velocity: unmyelinated ~0.5–2 m/s; myelinated up to ~120 m/s. Multiple sclerosis: autoimmune destruction of myelin → slowed or blocked conduction.

#### Review Questions
1. Draw and label the phases of an action potential. For each phase, state which ion channels open or close and in which direction ions move.
2. Why is the action potential described as "all-or-nothing"? What is the consequence of not reaching threshold?
3. Why does multiple sclerosis slow nerve conduction? How does myelination normally increase conduction velocity?
