#### Integration: From Meal to Waste
A starch + protein + fat meal: mouth (amylase + mechanical) → esophagus (peristalsis) → stomach (HCl + pepsin; 2–4 hours churning) → duodenum (pancreatic enzymes + bile emulsification; HCO3− neutralization) → jejunum/ileum (brush border enzymes; absorption of monosaccharides + amino acids into capillaries; fatty acids + glycerol → chylomicrons → lacteals) → portal vein → liver processing → systemic circulation → cell uptake → metabolism. Waste: large intestine (water reabsorption; bacteria ferment indigestible fiber → gas; feces formation) → rectum → anus.

Nitrogenous waste: amino acid catabolism → ammonia (toxic) → urea cycle in liver → urea in blood → kidneys filter and excrete in urine. Uric acid: from nucleotide catabolism; gout = uric acid crystal deposition in joints.

#### Review Questions
1. Trace a triglyceride molecule from ingestion to its arrival in the bloodstream, naming all the chemical transformations and structures it passes through.
2. Compare urea and uric acid as nitrogenous waste products: where is each produced, from what precursors, and why do birds and reptiles excrete uric acid rather than urea?
3. A patient has their ileum (final segment of small intestine) surgically removed due to Crohn's disease. What nutrient deficiencies would you expect, and why? (Hint: which vitamins and nutrients are specifically absorbed in the ileum?)
