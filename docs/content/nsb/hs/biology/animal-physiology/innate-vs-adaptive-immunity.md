#### Innate Immunity: First and Second Lines
**First line** (physical barriers): skin (keratin), mucus, cilia, stomach acid, lysozyme (saliva, tears). **Second line** (innate immune cells and molecules): active when pathogens penetrate barriers. Key components: **phagocytes** (neutrophils — first responders, short-lived; macrophages — long-lived tissue residents; dendritic cells — bridges to adaptive immunity); **natural killer (NK) cells** — kill cells displaying abnormal surface signals (missing MHC I → virally infected or cancer cells); **complement system** — cascade of plasma proteins that lyse bacteria, coat pathogens for phagocytosis (opsonization), and attract immune cells (chemotaxis); **interferons** — secreted by virally infected cells to signal neighboring cells to increase antiviral defenses.

Pattern recognition: innate immune cells recognize **pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs)** via **pattern recognition receptors (PRRs)** such as **Toll-like receptors (TLRs)**. PAMPs include LPS (bacterial outer membrane), flagellin, double-stranded RNA (viruses). PAMPs are conserved across microbial groups (humans don't have them) → discriminates self from non-self without needing prior exposure.

#### Adaptive Immunity Overview
Specific, slow (days to weeks for primary response), generates immunological memory. Two branches: **humoral immunity** (B cells → antibodies → targets extracellular pathogens); **cell-mediated immunity** (T cells → target intracellular pathogens and cancer cells). Requires antigen presentation: dendritic cells phagocytose pathogens → present peptide antigens on MHC molecules → activate T cells in lymph nodes. **Clonal selection**: only lymphocytes with receptors matching the antigen are activated and clonally expanded.
