#### Peptide Hormones
Water-soluble; cannot cross the lipid bilayer; bind to membrane receptors. Binding activates **signal transduction cascades** via G-proteins (most) or receptor tyrosine kinases (insulin). G-protein-coupled receptor (GPCR) pathway: hormone → GPCR → G-protein activates adenylyl cyclase → ATP → **cAMP** (second messenger) → protein kinase A → phosphorylation cascade → cellular response. Response is fast (seconds to minutes), amplified (one hormone molecule → millions of cAMP molecules → millions of activated enzymes). Examples: epinephrine, glucagon, ADH, FSH, LH, TSH, insulin (receptor tyrosine kinase pathway).

#### Steroid Hormones
Lipid-soluble; synthesized from cholesterol; diffuse directly through plasma membrane; bind intracellular receptors. Hormone-receptor complex enters nucleus → binds hormone response elements (HRE) in DNA → alters gene transcription → change in protein synthesis. Response is slow (hours to days) but long-lasting. Examples: cortisol, aldosterone, estrogen, progesterone, testosterone, calcitriol (vitamin D). Important for long-term regulation: puberty, pregnancy, stress response, anti-inflammation.

#### Comparison
| Feature | Peptide | Steroid |
|---|---|---|
| Solubility | Water-soluble | Lipid-soluble |
| Receptor location | Plasma membrane | Intracellular (cytoplasm/nucleus) |
| Second messenger | Yes (cAMP, etc.) | No |
| Speed | Fast | Slow |
| Duration | Short | Long |
| Precursor | Amino acids | Cholesterol |
