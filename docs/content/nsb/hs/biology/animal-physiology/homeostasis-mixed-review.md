#### Cross-Concept Connections
All homeostatic mechanisms share the same architecture: sensor → control center → effector → corrective response → feedback to sensor. The hypothalamus is the primary control center for both temperature regulation and osmolarity regulation. ADH connects osmolarity and blood volume. Aldosterone connects blood pressure and Na+/water balance. Insulin and glucagon create an antagonistic pair for blood glucose. Exercise simultaneously challenges temperature homeostasis (increases heat production), osmolarity (sweating removes water and electrolytes), blood glucose (muscles consume glucose), and blood pressure (heart rate increases). The integrative physiological response to exercise illustrates how multiple homeostatic systems operate simultaneously and interact.

#### Review Questions
1. During intense exercise, body temperature rises, blood glucose falls, and blood osmolarity increases due to sweating. Describe the homeostatic responses to each of these three changes.
2. Compare and contrast the roles of the hypothalamus in thermoregulation and osmoregulation.
3. A patient has a tumor producing excess aldosterone (primary hyperaldosteronism). Predict the effects on blood Na+, K+, and blood pressure.
