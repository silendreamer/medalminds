#### Ovarian Cycle (~28 days)
**Follicular phase (days 1–14)**: FSH (anterior pituitary) stimulates follicle development; granulosa cells secrete estrogen; rising estrogen has positive feedback on pituitary → **LH surge** (day 13–14) → ovulation (secondary oocyte released from dominant follicle). **Luteal phase (days 14–28)**: LH transforms ruptured follicle into **corpus luteum**; corpus luteum secretes progesterone (dominant) and estrogen; progesterone → negative feedback → suppresses FSH and LH. If no fertilization → corpus luteum degenerates → progesterone and estrogen drop → menstruation (shedding of uterine lining).

#### Uterine Cycle
**Menstruation (days 1–5)**: progesterone withdrawal → uterine lining shed. **Proliferative phase (days 6–14)**: estrogen → endometrium thickens and grows. **Secretory phase (days 14–28)**: progesterone (from corpus luteum) → endometrium becomes highly vascularized and secretory (ready to receive blastocyst). If implantation occurs: trophoblast secretes **hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin)** → maintains corpus luteum → progesterone continues → no menstruation → pregnancy.

#### Hormonal Contraception
Combined oral contraceptives (estrogen + progestin): maintain high levels of both → negative feedback → suppress FSH and LH → no follicle development → no LH surge → no ovulation. Progesterone-only (minipill): thickens cervical mucus + thins endometrium; less reliable ovulation suppression. Emergency contraception (Plan B — levonorgestrel): high-dose progestin → delays or inhibits ovulation; must be taken within 72 hours.

#### Review Questions
1. Explain why hCG is used in home pregnancy tests and what its detection indicates about the state of the corpus luteum.
2. Trace the hormonal events from FSH secretion on day 1 to ovulation on day 14, explaining the positive feedback loop that creates the LH surge.
3. A woman takes a progesterone antagonist (blocks progesterone receptors) during the luteal phase. Predict the effect on the endometrium and on the possibility of pregnancy.
