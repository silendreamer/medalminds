#### Integration Questions
The nervous system's function from ion gradients to behavior: resting potential → threshold → action potential → propagation (faster with myelin) → synapse (neurotransmitter release → receptor binding → EPSP/IPSP) → summation at axon hillock → action potential or not → muscle contraction or gland secretion → behavior.

Multiple sclerosis destroys myelin → slows conduction → weakness, vision problems, cognitive effects. Myasthenia gravis: autoimmune attack on ACh receptors at neuromuscular junction → muscle weakness. Parkinson's disease: dopaminergic neurons in substantia nigra die → tremor, rigidity. Alzheimer's disease: loss of cholinergic neurons → memory loss.

#### Review Questions
1. A patient has a severed spinal cord at the thoracic level. Which reflexes remain intact, and why? What voluntary and autonomic functions are lost?
2. Explain how a single IPSP can prevent an action potential even if multiple EPSPs are simultaneously arriving on other dendrites.
3. Compare the mechanism of action of curare (blocks nicotinic ACh receptors) with that of organophosphates (inhibit acetylcholinesterase). Both cause disruption at the neuromuscular junction — why do they produce different symptoms (flaccid paralysis vs. spastic paralysis)?
