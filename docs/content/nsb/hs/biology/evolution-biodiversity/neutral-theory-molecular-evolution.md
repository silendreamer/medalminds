#### Neutral Theory of Molecular Evolution
Motoo Kimura (1968) proposed that most amino acid substitutions observed in protein evolution are neutral — they have no significant effect on fitness and are fixed by genetic drift rather than natural selection. Rationale: many mutations are synonymous (third codon position changes that do not alter the amino acid) and are therefore invisible to selection. Neutral mutations accumulate at a rate equal to the mutation rate (μ), independent of population size. Slightly deleterious mutations are eliminated; slightly beneficial ones are fixed slowly; neutral ones accumulate at a constant rate — forming the basis of the molecular clock.

#### Synonymous vs. Non-synonymous Substitutions
**Synonymous (silent) substitutions**: change the codon but not the amino acid (e.g., UCU → UCC, both code for Serine). **Non-synonymous (missense) substitutions**: change the amino acid. The ratio of non-synonymous to synonymous substitution rates (dN/dS ratio) indicates selection: dN/dS < 1 = purifying selection (most functional proteins); dN/dS = 1 = neutral evolution; dN/dS > 1 = positive selection (genes evolving rapidly to evade immune system or adapt to new functions).

#### Review Questions
1. What is the neutral theory of molecular evolution, and who proposed it?
2. What does a dN/dS ratio greater than 1 indicate about the selective pressure on a gene?
3. Why do synonymous substitutions accumulate faster than non-synonymous substitutions in functional protein-coding genes?
