#### Orthologs and Paralogs
**Orthologs**: genes in different species that descend from a single ancestral gene through speciation. They typically retain similar functions (e.g., human BRCA1 and mouse Brca1 — both DNA repair genes). **Paralogs**: genes within the same organism that arose through gene duplication within a lineage (e.g., human α-globin and β-globin — both bind oxygen but in different hemoglobin subunits). Gene families (olfactory receptor genes ~400 in humans; ~1,000 in mice) arise by repeated duplication and divergence.

#### Conserved Sequences and Genome Size
Highly conserved sequences (similar across distantly related species) are likely under strong purifying selection — any change is deleterious. Ultra-conserved elements (UCEs) — sequences >200 bp identical between human, rat, and mouse — are thought to be critical regulatory regions. Genome size (C-value) does not correlate with organismal complexity — onions have much larger genomes than humans (C-value paradox). Much of large genomes is repetitive DNA (transposable elements, satellite DNA). Human genome: ~3.2 billion base pairs, ~20,000 protein-coding genes (~1.5% of genome). "Junk DNA" is largely functional regulatory and structural sequence.
