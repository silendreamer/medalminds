#### The Core Principle
Natural selection requires four conditions: (1) variation exists among individuals in a population, (2) some variation is heritable (passed to offspring), (3) more offspring are produced than can survive (struggle for existence), and (4) individuals with favorable traits survive and reproduce at higher rates (differential reproduction). Over generations, favorable alleles increase in frequency. This is not goal-directed — organisms do not "try" to evolve. Selection acts on existing variation; it does not create new traits.

#### Fitness and Adaptation
Biological fitness is not strength or speed — it is reproductive success relative to others in the population. An adaptation is any heritable trait that increases fitness in a given environment. The same trait can be adaptive in one environment and neutral or harmful in another. Camouflage coloring in peppered moths (Biston betularia) is the classic example: dark variants became more common during industrial pollution when soot darkened tree bark, then decreased as air quality improved.

#### Types of Natural Selection
Three modes: **directional** selection shifts the mean phenotype toward one extreme (antibiotic resistance); **stabilizing** selection favors intermediate phenotypes (human birth weight); **disruptive** selection favors both extremes and can lead to two distinct phenotype clusters (beak size in African seedcrackers). Sexual selection — mate choice and competition — is a special form of natural selection that can drive traits like peacock tails that reduce survival but increase reproductive success.

#### Evidence from the Fossil Record
Fossils provide a chronological record of life. Transitional fossils show intermediate forms between ancestral and derived groups: Tiktaalik (fish-tetrapod transition), Archaeopteryx (dinosaur-bird), and whale ancestors (Pakicetus through Ambulocetus) document major transitions. Stratigraphic superposition places older layers below younger layers, so the sequence of fossil forms matches evolutionary predictions. Gaps in the fossil record are expected — fossilization is rare — but do not undermine evolution.

#### Review Questions
1. A population of beetles lives on green grass. Brown beetles are more visible to predators than green ones. Over 20 generations, what happens to allele frequencies for color, and what type of selection is this?
2. What four conditions are necessary for natural selection to occur?
3. Why is biological fitness defined in terms of reproductive success rather than physical strength?
