#### Traditional vs. Molecular Identification
Traditional taxonomy relies on morphological keys — dichotomous keys that use visible physical traits to identify organisms. These work well for well-studied groups (birds, mammals, flowering plants) but fail for bacteria, fungi, nematodes, and "cryptic species" (organisms that look identical but are genetically distinct). DNA barcoding uses a short, standardized gene region (CO1 gene in animals, rbcL/matK in plants) to identify species from a tissue sample. The Barcode of Life Data System (BOLD) houses millions of reference sequences. A specimen's barcode is matched against the database — if no match exists, it may be a new species.

#### Cryptic Species and Biodiversity Estimates
Cryptic species (sibling species) are morphologically identical but reproductively isolated and genetically distinct. Anopheles gambiae mosquitoes (major malaria vectors in Africa) turned out to be a complex of 8 cryptic species with different behaviors and vector competence — critical for malaria control. There may be 8–10 million species on Earth; fewer than 2 million are formally described. Most undescribed species are insects, nematodes, fungi, and microbes in tropical forests and deep oceans.
