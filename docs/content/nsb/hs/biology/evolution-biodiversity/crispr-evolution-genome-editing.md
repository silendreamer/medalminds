#### CRISPR: Bacterial Adaptive Immunity
CRISPR (Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats) is an adaptive immune system in bacteria and archaea. When bacteria survive a viral infection, they incorporate short viral DNA sequences into their CRISPR locus — a "memory" of past infections. Cas proteins use these sequences as guides to recognize and cut viral DNA upon re-infection. CRISPR arrays were first noticed in E. coli in 1987; their immune function was demonstrated by Barrangou et al. in 2007; their mechanism was characterized by Doudna, Charpentier, and others (2012 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, 2020).

#### CRISPR-Cas9 as a Genome Editing Tool
The Cas9 protein is guided to a specific genomic location by a guide RNA (gRNA) complementary to the target sequence. Cas9 cuts both strands of DNA (double-strand break). The cell repairs the break by **NHEJ** (error-prone, creates indels — useful for gene knockouts) or **HDR** (precise repair using a template — useful for corrections). The target must be adjacent to a **PAM sequence** (5'-NGG-3' for Streptococcus pyogenes Cas9) for cutting to occur. Applications: correcting sickle-cell disease mutations, creating disease-resistant crops, basic research knockouts, and (controversially) human germline editing.
