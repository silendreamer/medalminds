#### Bowl Toss-up Patterns
"For 10 points, what term describes a shared derived character used to unite taxa into a clade?" → **synapomorphy**

"For 10 points, in a phylogenetic analysis, what is the name for a taxon used as a reference point that is known to have diverged before the group of interest?" → **outgroup**

"For 10 points, what principle states that the preferred phylogenetic tree is the one requiring the fewest evolutionary changes?" → **parsimony** (Occam's razor, maximum parsimony)

"For 10 points, what type of similarity, caused by convergent evolution rather than common ancestry, can mislead phylogenetic analysis?" → **homoplasy**

"For 10 points, what method uses the rate of neutral mutation accumulation to estimate the timing of evolutionary divergence events?" → **molecular clock**

#### Quick-Fire Distinctions
- Node = common ancestor; branch = lineage; tip = taxon
- Clade = monophyletic = ancestor + ALL descendants
- Paraphyletic excludes some descendants; polyphyletic has multiple separate ancestors
- Synapomorphy unites a clade; plesiomorphy is ancestral, not informative for cladistics

#### Review Questions
1. "For 10 points, what do we call the branching points on a phylogenetic tree that represent common ancestors?" → What is the answer?
2. Birds evolved from dinosaurs. Is "Dinosauria" (as traditionally defined, excluding birds) monophyletic or paraphyletic?
3. Define homoplasy and explain why it complicates phylogenetic analysis.
