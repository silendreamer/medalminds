### Introduction to Neurons

Neurons are specialized cells that transmit information throughout the body via electrical and chemical signals. They are fundamental components of the nervous system, responsible for processing and communicating information. Understanding the structure and function of neurons is essential for grasping how the nervous system operates and how it influences behavior and bodily functions.

### Structure of Neurons

Neurons consist of three main parts: the cell body (soma), dendrites, and axon. The cell body contains the nucleus and organelles, serving as the metabolic center of the neuron. Dendrites are tree-like extensions that receive signals from other neurons, while the axon is a long, slender projection that transmits electrical impulses away from the cell body to other neurons or muscles.

### Dendrites: The Receiving End

Dendrites play a crucial role in neuron communication by receiving synaptic inputs from other neurons. They are covered in specialized receptors that bind neurotransmitters, leading to changes in the neuron's membrane potential. This process is vital for integrating signals from multiple sources, allowing the neuron to process information and determine whether to fire an action potential.

### The Axon: Signal Transmission

The axon is responsible for conducting electrical impulses known as action potentials away from the cell body. It is often insulated by a myelin sheath, which increases the speed of signal transmission through a process called saltatory conduction. The axon terminates at the axon terminals, where neurotransmitters are released into the synaptic cleft to communicate with adjacent neurons or target cells.

### Myelin Sheath and Nodes of Ranvier

The myelin sheath is a fatty layer that surrounds the axon, formed by glial cells, specifically oligodendrocytes in the central nervous system and Schwann cells in the peripheral nervous system. This insulation allows for faster transmission of electrical signals by preventing ion leakage and facilitating saltatory conduction at the Nodes of Ranvier—gaps in the myelin sheath where action potentials are regenerated. This efficient signaling is crucial for rapid communication within the nervous system.

### Synaptic Transmission

When an action potential reaches the axon terminals, it triggers the release of neurotransmitters into the synaptic cleft. These chemical messengers bind to receptors on the postsynaptic neuron, leading to either excitatory or inhibitory responses. This synaptic transmission is essential for neuronal communication, influencing everything from reflexes to complex behaviors.
