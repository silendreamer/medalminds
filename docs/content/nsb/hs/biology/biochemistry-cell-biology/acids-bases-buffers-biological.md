#### The pH Scale
pH ranges from 0 (most acidic) to 14 (most basic), with 7 as neutral. Each whole-number change represents a 10-fold concentration difference. Blood pH is maintained at 7.35–7.45 — a narrow range. Below 7.35 is acidosis; above 7.45 is alkalosis. Both are life-threatening because enzyme function, protein conformation, and membrane ion transport all depend on pH. The stomach operates at pH 1.5–3.5 (highly acidic, optimal for pepsin); the small intestine at pH 7–8 (optimal for pancreatic enzymes).

#### Buffer Systems
A buffer resists pH changes by neutralizing added acid or base. A buffer consists of a weak acid and its conjugate base in equilibrium. When H⁺ is added, the conjugate base reacts: A⁻ + H⁺ → HA (absorbs acid). When OH⁻ is added, the weak acid reacts: HA + OH⁻ → A⁻ + H₂O (absorbs base). Buffers are most effective at pH values near their pKa (the pH at which [HA] = [A⁻]). Buffer capacity is greatest within ±1 pH unit of pKa.

#### The Bicarbonate Buffer System
The bicarbonate buffer (H₂CO₃/HCO₃⁻) is the primary blood pH regulator. CO₂ produced by cellular respiration dissolves in blood plasma: CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. Elevated CO₂ increases H⁺ (lowering pH); decreased CO₂ decreases H⁺ (raising pH). The respiratory system tightly controls blood CO₂ — hyperventilating blows off CO₂, raising pH (respiratory alkalosis); hypoventilating retains CO₂, lowering pH (respiratory acidosis). The kidneys regulate HCO₃⁻ reabsorption over longer timescales, providing a secondary compensation mechanism.

#### Phosphate and Protein Buffers
Inside cells, the phosphate buffer system (H₂PO₄⁻/HPO₄²⁻, pKa 6.86) is important because intracellular pH is ~7.2, within the buffer's effective range. Proteins themselves act as buffers through the ionizable R-groups of histidine (pKa ~6), lysine, aspartate, and glutamate. Hemoglobin buffers blood pH by accepting H⁺ released when O₂ is delivered to tissues (the Bohr effect). This coupling of O₂ delivery and pH buffering elegantly integrates respiratory gas exchange with acid-base homeostasis.
