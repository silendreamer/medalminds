#### Carbohydrates
**Carbohydrates** are composed of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen in a roughly 1:2:1 ratio. Their monomers are **monosaccharides** (e.g., glucose, fructose, galactose), which join via **glycosidic bonds** to form **disaccharides** (e.g., sucrose, lactose) and **polysaccharides**. Key polysaccharides include **starch** and **glycogen** (energy storage in plants and animals, respectively) and **cellulose** (structural support in plant cell walls). Carbohydrates are the cell's primary and most rapidly mobilized energy source.

#### Lipids
**Lipids** are nonpolar, hydrophobic molecules that include **triglycerides**, **phospholipids**, and **steroids**. Triglycerides consist of a **glycerol** backbone esterified to three **fatty acid** chains; they serve as long-term energy storage (yielding more than twice the ATP per gram as carbohydrates). **Phospholipids** form the **bilayer** of all cell membranes — their hydrophilic heads face outward and hydrophobic tails face inward. **Steroids** (e.g., cholesterol, testosterone) share a four-ring carbon skeleton and serve structural and signaling roles.

#### Proteins
**Proteins** are polymers of **amino acids** linked by **peptide bonds**. Their four levels of structure are:
- **Primary** — the linear sequence of amino acids
- **Secondary** — local folding into **alpha helices** or **beta-pleated sheets** stabilized by hydrogen bonds
- **Tertiary** — overall 3-D shape driven by R-group interactions (hydrophobic, ionic, disulfide bridges)
- **Quaternary** — association of two or more polypeptide subunits

Proteins serve as enzymes, structural components (collagen, keratin), hormones (insulin), antibodies, and transport molecules (hemoglobin).

#### Nucleic Acids
**Nucleic acids** (**DNA** and **RNA**) are polymers of **nucleotides**, each consisting of a five-carbon **pentose sugar**, a **phosphate group**, and a nitrogenous **base**. DNA uses deoxyribose and the bases adenine, guanine, cytosine, and thymine; RNA uses ribose and substitutes **uracil** for thymine. **DNA** stores genetic information in a double helix; **RNA** (mRNA, tRNA, rRNA) carries and translates that information during protein synthesis. Nucleotides are also critical energy carriers — **ATP** is an adenine nucleotide.
