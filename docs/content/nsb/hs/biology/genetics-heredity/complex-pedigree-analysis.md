#### Interpreting Mixed Autosomal and X-Linked Traits
Some pedigrees involve both autosomal and X-linked loci. An individual's phenotype depends on both. For example, if a man is heterozygous for an autosomal trait (Aa) and hemizygous for an X-linked trait (X^b Y), his phenotype reflects both loci. Mating such an individual with a woman (Aa X^B X^b) requires separate analysis of each locus, then combining results.

#### Carrier Detection in Rare Recessive Diseases
A woman's brother has an autosomal recessive disease (genotype aa). Both parents are carriers (Aa). The woman is unaffected. What is her probability of being a carrier? Among the unaffected siblings, she could be AA (1/3) or Aa (2/3). If she marries an unaffected man from the general population (where the disease frequency is low), the probability he is a carrier is roughly equal to the disease allele frequency in the population (let's say 1/200). The probability their child is affected is (2/3) × (1/200) × (1/4) ≈ 1/1200 — much lower than if both parents were known carriers.

#### Genetic Counseling Scenario: Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD)
DMD is X-linked recessive. A woman's brother has DMD. Her father is unaffected; her mother must be a carrier (X^D X^d). The woman is unaffected; she is either X^D X^D or X^D X^d. Given her mother is a carrier, the woman has a 1/2 chance of being a carrier herself. If she has a son, the probability he has DMD is (1/2 chance she's a carrier) × (1/2 chance she passes X^d to son) = 1/4. Knowing her brother's status and her parents' genotypes allowed geneticists to calculate her risk.

#### Worked Pedigree: Hemophilia A (X-Linked Recessive)
A woman's father has hemophilia A (X^h Y). The woman is unaffected and married to an unaffected man (X^H Y). What is the probability their first son has hemophilia? The woman's father is X^h Y, so the woman inherited X^h from him and X^H from her unaffected mother. The woman is X^H X^h (carrier). Her unaffected husband is X^H Y. Their son has a 1/2 chance of inheriting X^h from mother (hemophilia) and a 1/2 chance of inheriting X^H (unaffected). Overall, P(son has hemophilia) = 1/2.

#### Consanguinity (Related Parents)
When parents are related (consanguineous marriage), the probability their child inherits two recessive alleles increases. If parents are first cousins and both are carriers of an autosomal recessive disease, the risk to a child is higher than if they were unrelated (P = 1/4) because they are more likely to carry the same rare alleles by descent. Consanguinity is common in some populations but uncommon in others, reflecting cultural and historical factors.

#### Review Questions

3. Why does consanguinity increase the risk of autosomal recessive diseases in offspring?
