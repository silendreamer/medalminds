#### Hemizygous Males and X-Linked Recessive Traits
Males have one X chromosome (XY); females have two (XX). A male with one recessive allele (X^a Y) expresses the recessive phenotype because there's no allele on the Y to mask it (hemizygous condition). A female needs two recessive alleles (X^a X^a) to express a recessive phenotype. This is why X-linked recessive traits like color blindness and hemophilia are more common in males than females.

#### X-Linked Recessive Cross: Carrier Female × Affected Male
A carrier female (X^A X^a) × affected male (X^a Y) produce: daughters X^A X^a (carrier) and X^a X^a (affected); sons X^A Y (unaffected) and X^a Y (affected). Daughters have a 50% chance of being affected; sons have a 50% chance of being affected. Notably, the carrier mother has an equal probability of passing the defective allele to sons and daughters, but the Y chromosome in sons guarantees they express the trait.

#### X-Linked Recessive Cross: Unaffected Female × Affected Male
An unaffected female (X^A X^A) × affected male (X^a Y) produce all carrier daughters (X^A X^a) and all unaffected sons (X^A Y). All daughters inherit the X^a from their father and the X^A from their mother. All sons inherit the X^A from their mother and the Y from their father. In the F2 (carrier females crossed with unaffected males), 1/2 of sons are affected.

#### X-Linked Dominant Inheritance
X-linked dominant alleles are expressed in heterozygous females (X^A X^a). An affected male (X^A Y) × unaffected female (X^a X^a) produce affected daughters (X^A X^a) but unaffected sons (X^a Y). All daughters inherit the X^A from father; all sons inherit the X^a from mother and the Y from father. X-linked dominant traits are typically rarer and often more severe in hemizygous males (sometimes lethal).

#### X-Inactivation in Females
Females have two X chromosomes but use only one per cell — the other is inactivated into a Barr body. This X-inactivation is random and occurs early in development. A heterozygous female (X^A X^a) is a mosaic: some cells express X^A, others express X^a. This can result in intermediate phenotypes or patchy phenotypes. A carrier of hemophilia might have slightly reduced clotting factors; a carrier of color blindness might have color vision deficiency in part of her visual field.
