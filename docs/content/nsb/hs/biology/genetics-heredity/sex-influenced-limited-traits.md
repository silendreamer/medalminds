#### Sex-Influenced Traits: Baldness
Human male baldness is often autosomal but sex-influenced. Let B = allele for baldness, b = allele for non-baldness. The allele B is dominant in males but recessive in females. A heterozygous male (Bb) is bald; a heterozygous female (Bb) is non-bald. BB males and females are bald; bb males and females are non-bald. The dominance relationship flips depending on sex — a reversal caused by sex hormones (androgens in males promote baldness; estrogens in females protect).

#### Phenotypic Ratios in Sex-Influenced Traits
A cross between two heterozygotes (Bb × Bb) produces 1 BB : 2 Bb : 1 bb. In males, BB and Bb are bald; bb is non-bald (3 bald : 1 non-bald). In females, BB is bald; Bb and bb are non-bald (1 bald : 3 non-bald). The phenotypic ratio differs by sex despite the same genotypic ratios.

#### Sex-Limited Traits: Egg Laying in Chickens
Sex-limited traits are expressed in only one sex, even though the alleles are autosomal. Egg-laying ability in chickens is sex-limited to females. A rooster doesn't lay eggs regardless of his genotype. A hen's phenotype depends on her alleles: the allele for high egg production is dominant. The alleles are present in both sexes but only expressed in females due to anatomy and physiology (ovaries, hormonal cycles).

#### Distinction: Sex-Linked vs Sex-Influenced vs Sex-Limited
Sex-linked: gene is on the X or Y chromosome (e.g., color blindness). Sex-influenced: gene is autosomal but dominance differs by sex (e.g., baldness). Sex-limited: gene is autosomal but expression occurs in only one sex (e.g., egg production). A pedigree can reveal which category a trait falls into: sex-linked traits often skip generations in females but appear in all sons of affected females; sex-influenced and sex-limited traits appear in both sexes but with different frequencies or patterns.

#### Phenotypic Prediction from Genotype and Sex
To predict an offspring's phenotype, determine the genotype first, then apply the sex-specific dominance rules. If parents are both Bb and the offspring is male with genotype Bb, the male is bald. If the same offspring were female with genotype Bb, she is non-bald. The same genotype produces opposite phenotypes depending on sex.

#### Review Questions
1. In a sex-influenced trait like baldness, why is a heterozygous male bald but a heterozygous female non-bald?
2. Two heterozygous parents (Bb × Bb) for a sex-influenced trait have a son and a daughter. What is the probability the son is bald and the daughter is non-bald?
3. Why is egg-laying ability in chickens considered sex-limited rather than sex-linked?
