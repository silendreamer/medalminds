#### Three-Locus Cross with Partial Linkage
A trihybrid cross (AaBbCc) is performed. Suppose A and B are linked (10 m.u. apart) but C is unlinked. The gamete types from the AaBb parent are: AB and ab (parental, 45% each), Ab and aB (recombinant, 5% each). The C locus segregates independently (1/2 C, 1/2 c in all gametes). To find the frequency of A_B_cc offspring, multiply: P(A_B_) × P(cc) = (parental AB frequency from AaBb × AaBb cross) × 1/4.

#### Worked Example: Linked Genes
Genes A and B are 15 m.u. apart. A trihybrid (ABc/abc) × homozygous recessive (abc/abc) produces offspring. Parental gametes from the trihybrid (AB/abc) are AB and abc (42.5% each). Recombinant gametes are Ab and aB (7.5% each). The homozygous recessive contributes only abc. So offspring are: ABabc (42.5%), abcabc (42.5%), Ababc (7.5%), aBcabc (7.5%). Phenotypically, the ratio is 42.5 A_bc : 42.5 aabc : 7.5 A_BC : 7.5 aaBC (a modified 1:1 ratio due to linkage).

#### Recombination Frequency Unknown
Two genes show a 1:1 recombinant:parental ratio when crossed with a homozygous recessive. This indicates ~50% recombination, implying the genes are either on different chromosomes or very far apart on the same chromosome (>50 m.u.). If genes show a 3:1 parental:recombinant ratio, they are linked with ~25% recombination frequency.

#### Sex-Linked Probability
An X-linked trait is controlled by alleles X^A (normal) and X^a (disease). A carrier woman (X^A X^a) and normal man (X^A Y) have children. Sons: 1/2 X^A Y (normal) and 1/2 X^a Y (affected). Daughters: 1/2 X^A X^A (normal) and 1/2 X^A X^a (carrier). If the couple plans three children, the probability of at least one affected son is 1 − (1/2)^3 = 7/8.

#### Predicting Genetic Diseases in Offspring
A couple plans pregnancy. The man has no family history of cystic fibrosis (CF); the woman's brother has CF (autosomal recessive). The woman is definitely a carrier (Aa). The man is likely AA (since CF is rare, ~1/2500 births) but could be a carrier. The probability of an affected child depends on the man's actual genotype. If he is AA, no children are affected. If he is Aa (low probability), 1/2 of children are affected. Genetic counseling uses population frequencies to estimate these probabilities.

#### Review Questions
1. Two genes (D and E) show 8% recombination. A testcross of DdEe produces 200 offspring. How many should be double recombinants?
2. A woman is heterozygous for an autosomal recessive disease; her partner is unaffected but from a family with the disease. What is the probability their first child is affected?
3. In an X-linked recessive trait, a carrier woman and affected man have a daughter. What is the probability their daughter is affected?
