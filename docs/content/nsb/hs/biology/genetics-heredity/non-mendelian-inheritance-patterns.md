#### Incomplete Dominance
In some crosses, heterozygotes show an intermediate phenotype. Red snapdragons (RR) × white (WW) produce pink offspring (RW). The heterozygote is not red (dominant) but intermediate. This is incomplete dominance: the heterozygote phenotype is different from both homozygotes. The F1 × F1 cross (RW × RW) produces 1 red : 2 pink : 1 white in the F2, not the typical 3:1 Mendelian ratio.

#### Codominance
In codominance, both alleles are fully expressed in heterozygotes. Human ABO blood types: An individual with genotype IAIB has both A and B antigens (blood type AB). Neither allele is dominant; both are expressed equally. Compare this to incomplete dominance: a type AB person has a distinct phenotype (AB) that is not intermediate between A and B, but rather expresses both.

#### Epistasis: Gene Interactions
Epistasis occurs when one gene's alleles mask or modify the expression of alleles at another locus. Classic example: Labrador coat color. E_B_ = black, E_bb = chocolate, eeB_ = yellow, eebb = yellow. The ee genotype masks the B locus effect; all ee individuals are yellow regardless of genotype at the B locus. An F1 cross (EeBb × EeBb) produces 9 black : 3 chocolate : 4 yellow (not 9:3:3:1).

#### Polygenic Traits
Height, skin color, and intelligence are controlled by many genes (polygenes), each with small additive effects. A 3-locus polygenic trait (AaBbCc) crossed with itself produces offspring with a range of phenotypes, from aabbcc (minimum) to AABBCC (maximum). The distribution is often roughly normal (bell-curve). Environmental factors also influence the final phenotype.

#### Maternal Inheritance
Mitochondria and chloroplasts are inherited maternally (from the mother's egg cytoplasm, not from the nucleus). A mitochondrial mutation passed from mother to child will appear in all cells. An affected mother passes the mutation to all her children; an affected father does not pass it to any children. Mitochondrial genetic diseases show a distinctive pedigree pattern unlike autosomal or X-linked traits.

#### Review Questions
1. Distinguish between incomplete dominance and codominance, using an example for each.
2. In a Labrador coat color cross (EeBb × EeBb), what is the expected phenotypic ratio, and why?
3. If a mitochondrial disease is passed from mother to both her sons and daughters, can her sons pass it to their children?
