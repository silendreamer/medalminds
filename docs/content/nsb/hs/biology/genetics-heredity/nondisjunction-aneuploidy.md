#### What Is Nondisjunction?
Nondisjunction is the failure of chromosomes or chromatids to separate during cell division. It can occur in meiosis I (homologous pairs don't separate) or meiosis II (sister chromatids don't separate). The result: one gamete gets two copies of a chromosome; the other gets zero. Upon fertilization, the zygote is aneuploid — not 2n.

#### Trisomy and Monosomy
Trisomy = three copies of one chromosome (2n + 1). Down syndrome (trisomy 21) is the most common autosomal trisomy compatible with life; individuals have three copies of chromosome 21. Monosomy = one copy (2n - 1). Autosomal monosomies are usually lethal in utero. Monosomy X (Turner syndrome) is an exception, sometimes viable in females with mosaic patterns.

#### Sex-Linked Aneuploidy
Aneuploidy of sex chromosomes is often viable because the Y carries few genes and X-inactivation compensates for multiple X copies. XXY (Klinefelter), XXX, and XYY are examples. These syndromes can have variable phenotypes depending on the number of Y chromosomes and X-inactivation patterns.

#### Down Syndrome: Life Expectancy and Genetics
Trisomy 21 individuals have three copies of chromosome 21, giving them 47 chromosomes total. Common traits include intellectual disability, distinct facial features, and heart defects. However, life expectancy has increased dramatically (now ~60 years) with medical support. It's important to recognize that trisomy 21 individuals have high capacity for learning and contribution to society.
