#### Core Principles Review
The scientific method is iterative: observation → question → hypothesis → experiment → analysis → conclusion → new question. A hypothesis must be testable and falsifiable. Controlled experiments isolate one variable at a time. The control group establishes a baseline. Sample size and replication increase reliability. Statistical significance (p < 0.05) distinguishes real differences from chance. Correlation ≠ causation. Peer review and reproducibility validate findings. These principles apply across all biology subdisciplines.

#### Connecting Design to Organism Biology
Biological experiments must account for organism-specific variability. Genetic heterogeneity in human populations requires large sample sizes. Developmental stage affects enzyme activity in embryos. Circadian rhythms mean time of day matters in behavioral and hormonal studies. Environmental acclimatization means a plant grown in shade differs physiologically from one grown in sun even if genetically identical. A good experimental biologist anticipates these sources of variation and controls or accounts for them.

#### Interpreting Published Results
When reading a research paper, evaluate: Was the sample size adequate? Was there a proper control? Were confounding variables addressed? Were the statistical tests appropriate for the data type? Did the conclusions follow from the data, or were they overstated? In Science Bowl, some questions present a study design or graph and ask students to identify the flaw, interpret a result, or determine the appropriate conclusion. These are the hardest question types and reward systematic reasoning over memorized facts.

#### 15 Must-Know Facts
1. Independent variable = what the experimenter changes. 2. Dependent variable = what is measured. 3. Control group = no treatment (baseline). 4. p < 0.05 = statistically significant. 5. Correlation ≠ causation. 6. Larger sample size = smaller standard error. 7. Double-blind = neither participant nor researcher knows group assignment. 8. Scientific theory = well-supported explanation, not a guess. 9. Falsifiability is required for scientific hypotheses. 10. Replication = repeating an experiment independently. 11. SEM = SD/√n. 12. TEM = internal ultrastructure; SEM = surface. 13. GFP = used to track proteins in living cells. 14. Confounding variable = uncontrolled factor that affects the DV. 15. Peer review = expert scrutiny before publication.

#### Review Questions
1. A researcher notices that plants near a highway are shorter than those in a forest. She hypothesizes this is due to air pollution. Design the key features of a controlled experiment to test this hypothesis.
2. Explain why a study with n=5 per group and p=0.04 might still be viewed skeptically by peer reviewers.
3. What is the difference between internal and external validity? Give a biological example of each.
