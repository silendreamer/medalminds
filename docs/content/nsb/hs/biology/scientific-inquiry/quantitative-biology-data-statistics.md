#### Descriptive Statistics
Descriptive statistics summarize a data set. The mean (average) describes central tendency. Standard deviation (SD) measures spread — how tightly data cluster around the mean. A small SD means values are close together (consistent results); a large SD means high variability. In biology, reporting mean ± SD is standard: "Plants treated with fertilizer grew 15.2 ± 2.1 cm." Median is preferred when data are skewed by outliers. For example, median household income is a better central tendency measure than mean when a few billionaires inflate the average.

#### Statistical Testing and p-Values
Inferential statistics determine whether differences between groups are real or due to random chance. The t-test compares means of two groups. ANOVA compares means across three or more groups. Chi-square tests compare observed vs. expected frequencies (e.g., in genetics problems). The p-value is the probability of observing the data if the null hypothesis (no difference) is true. p < 0.05 is the conventional threshold for statistical significance. A result with p = 0.03 means there is only a 3% chance the observed difference is due to random variation.

#### Graphing Data
The type of graph depends on the data type. Bar graphs compare means of discrete categories. Line graphs show trends over continuous time or conditions. Scatter plots show correlations between two continuous variables. Histograms show frequency distributions. A well-constructed graph includes labeled axes with units, a title, error bars representing standard deviation or standard error, and a legend if multiple data sets are shown. Error bars that do not overlap between two groups often indicate a statistically significant difference.

#### Calculating and Interpreting Error Bars
Standard error of the mean (SEM) = SD / √n, where n is sample size. SEM reflects how precisely the sample mean estimates the true population mean. Larger n → smaller SEM → more precise estimate. In AP Biology and Science Bowl, you may be asked to interpret graphs: overlapping error bars suggest groups are not significantly different; non-overlapping bars suggest they may be. However, error bar type matters — SEM bars are narrower than SD bars for the same data.

#### Experimental Design in Practice
A well-designed biological study specifies: the biological question, the hypothesis, the IV and DV with units, controlled variables, sample size justification, the statistical test planned, and criteria for accepting or rejecting the hypothesis. Science Bowl competition questions frequently test whether students can identify flaws in experimental design — missing controls, inadequate sample sizes, or confounded variables — making this a high-yield skill.

#### Review Questions
1. A student collects the following root lengths (cm) from five plants: 4, 6, 5, 7, 3. Calculate the mean and describe what a large standard deviation would indicate about this data set.
2. A study comparing two plant fertilizers finds p = 0.07. Should the researcher conclude there is a significant difference? Explain.
3. A scatter plot shows a positive correlation between sunlight hours and plant height. What additional evidence would strengthen the claim that sunlight causes increased growth?
