#### Cells Receive Many Signals Simultaneously
At any moment, a cell receives dozens of signals through multiple receptor types. The cell's response is not simply additive — signals interact through crosstalk, competition, and convergence. Two signals that each produce a weak response can together produce a much stronger effect (**synergy**), while competing signals can cancel each other (**antagonism**).

#### Threshold Responses and Switch-Like Behavior
Many cellular decisions are switch-like: below a threshold signal strength, nothing happens; above it, the full response is triggered. This "all-or-nothing" behavior is often achieved by positive feedback loops. For example, MAP kinase cascades include feedback phosphorylation steps that amplify weak signals — once activated past a threshold, the full kinase cascade fires even if the original signal fades.

#### Combinatorial Control of Gene Expression
Signal integration is especially important in development. A muscle cell expresses different genes than a neuron, even though both contain the same genome. Different signals activate different combinations of transcription factors, and it is the specific combination — not any one factor — that specifies cell identity. This combinatorial logic allows a genome of ~20,000 genes to produce over 200 distinct cell types.
