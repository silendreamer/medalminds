#### Normal Growth Controls Lost in Cancer
Normal cells exhibit two key growth controls: **contact inhibition** (cells stop dividing when they touch each other) and **anchorage dependence** (cells require attachment to a substrate to divide). Cancer cells lose both — they pile up in culture (loss of contact inhibition) and can grow in suspension (loss of anchorage dependence). These properties reflect changes in cell signaling: contact inhibition involves Hippo pathway activation (YAP inhibition) at cell-cell contacts.

#### Hallmarks of Cancer (Hanahan & Weinberg)
The six classic hallmarks of cancer are:
1. Self-sufficiency in growth signals (oncogene activation)
2. Insensitivity to anti-growth signals (tumor suppressor loss)
3. Evasion of apoptosis (Bcl-2 overexpression, p53 loss)
4. Limitless replicative potential (telomerase reactivation)
5. Sustained angiogenesis (VEGF production)
6. Tissue invasion and metastasis (E-cadherin loss)

Later additions include: evading immune destruction, reprogramming metabolism (Warburg effect), and genome instability.

#### Review Questions
1. Define contact inhibition and explain which signaling pathway mediates it.
2. How does telomerase reactivation contribute to "limitless replicative potential" in cancer cells?
3. Rank the six classic hallmarks by which you think is most fundamental, and justify your answer.
