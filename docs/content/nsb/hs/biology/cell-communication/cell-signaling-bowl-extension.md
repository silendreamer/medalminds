#### Bowl Toss-Up Patterns
Science Bowl questions on cell signaling often hinge on a few specific clue words:
- "Seven transmembrane domains" → **GPCR**
- "Dimerizes upon ligand binding" → **Receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK)**
- "Converts ATP to cyclic AMP" → **adenylyl cyclase**
- "Self-inactivating GTPase" → **G-protein (Gα subunit)**
- "Mutated in 30% of cancers, locks in active state" → **RAS**
- "Second messenger that activates PKA" → **cAMP**
- "Ligand is acetylcholine, opens Na⁺/K⁺ channel" → **nicotinic acetylcholine receptor**

#### Sample Toss-Ups
*"For 10 points — this enzyme is activated by Gα and catalyzes conversion of ATP to cyclic AMP."*
**Answer: adenylyl cyclase**

*"For 10 points — name the small GTP-binding protein mutated in many cancers that, when active, triggers the MAP kinase cascade."*
**Answer: RAS**

*"For 10 points — this class of receptors contains seven membrane-spanning helices and couples to heterotrimeric G-proteins."*
**Answer: G-protein coupled receptors (GPCRs)**

#### Common Traps
- Don't confuse **Gα** (the GTPase subunit) with **GTPase-activating proteins (GAPs)** — they're different molecules
- RTK phosphorylates **tyrosine**; PKA phosphorylates **serine/threonine** — the substrate residue is a common detail question
- cAMP is degraded by **phosphodiesterase** (not phosphatase)

#### Review Questions
1. What structural feature instantly identifies a GPCR?
2. Which second messenger activates PKA, and what enzyme makes it?
3. Why is RAS a proto-oncogene rather than a tumor suppressor?
