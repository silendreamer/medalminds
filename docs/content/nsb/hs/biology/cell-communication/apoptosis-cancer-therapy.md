#### How Cancer Cells Evade Apoptosis
Cancer cells evolve multiple strategies to resist apoptosis:
- **Bcl-2 overexpression**: The t(14;18) translocation in follicular lymphoma places BCL2 under the immunoglobulin promoter, driving Bcl-2 overexpression. Bcl-2 sequesters Bax and Bak, preventing cytochrome c release.
- **p53 loss**: Without p53 to activate Puma and Noxa, DNA-damaged cells fail to undergo apoptosis.
- **XIAP overexpression**: Directly inhibits caspase-3 and -9.
- **FasL/TRAIL resistance**: Downregulation of death receptors or overexpression of FLIP (which blocks caspase-8 activation).

#### Venetoclax: A BH3 Mimetic
**Venetoclax (ABT-199)** is a BH3 mimetic — a small molecule that mimics BH3-only proteins by binding Bcl-2 and displacing Bax/Bak, restoring their ability to permeabilize the mitochondria. Venetoclax is approved for chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) and AML, producing responses in patients previously resistant to chemotherapy. It is one of the first drugs designed from understanding apoptosis biology — a triumph of bench-to-bedside research.

#### Chemotherapy and Apoptosis
Most traditional chemotherapy drugs (cisplatin, doxorubicin, paclitaxel) work by inducing DNA damage or spindle disruption, ultimately activating p53 to trigger apoptosis. This is why tumors with p53 mutations are often chemotherapy-resistant — they can't apoptose in response to DNA damage. Understanding the apoptosis pathway explains both why chemotherapy works and why it fails.
