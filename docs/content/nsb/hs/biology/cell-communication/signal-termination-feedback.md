#### Why Termination Is as Important as Activation
A signal that cannot be turned off is as dangerous as no signal at all — cancer is often the consequence of constitutively active signaling. Cells terminate signals through multiple mechanisms: (1) ligand removal/degradation, (2) receptor inactivation or internalization, (3) phosphatase activity reverting phosphorylation, (4) GTPase activity inactivating G-proteins, and (5) inhibitory feedback proteins.

#### Receptor Downregulation
Sustained activation of a receptor often triggers its own removal from the cell surface — **downregulation**. Phosphorylation of the receptor's cytoplasmic tail recruits β-arrestin, which blocks further G-protein coupling (desensitization) and promotes receptor internalization via clathrin-coated vesicles. Internalized receptors are either recycled to the surface or degraded. This is why prolonged drug use leads to tolerance — opioid receptor downregulation reduces the number of available receptors.

#### SOCS Proteins: Feedback Inhibitors of JAK-STAT
JAK-STAT activation transcriptionally induces **SOCS (Suppressors of Cytokine Signaling)** proteins, which then bind JAKs and block their kinase activity. This is a classic **negative feedback loop** — the signal produces the very proteins that shut it off. Pathogens like Mycobacterium tuberculosis exploit this by inducing SOCS to dampen immune JAK-STAT responses.
