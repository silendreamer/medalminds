#### What Makes a Report Credible?
A credible physics report states a clear hypothesis, identifies all variables, describes the procedure in enough detail to reproduce it, reports raw data and statistical analysis, and draws conclusions that follow directly from the data. It also acknowledges limitations.

#### Spotting Flaws: A Sample Report
"I tested whether mass affects falling time by dropping a feather and a bowling ball from 2 m. The bowling ball hit first. Conclusion: heavier objects fall faster." What's wrong? Air resistance is a confounding variable — the feather's large surface area causes drag, not its low mass. The experiment didn't control for drag.

#### Evaluating Conclusions
Does the conclusion go beyond the data? "Heavier objects always fall faster" goes beyond a two-object comparison. "In the presence of air resistance, objects with lower surface area-to-mass ratios fall faster" is a better, bounded conclusion.

#### Peer Review and Replication
Science becomes reliable through peer review (experts critique the methods and logic before publication) and independent replication (other labs reproduce the result). A single experiment, however well-designed, is not enough for a scientific consensus.

#### Review Questions
1. What is a confounding variable? Give an example from the feather-bowling ball experiment.
2. Why is replication important even if the original experiment was well-designed?
3. A paper claims "Exercise improves physics test scores" based on one trial of 10 students. Identify two weaknesses in this study.
