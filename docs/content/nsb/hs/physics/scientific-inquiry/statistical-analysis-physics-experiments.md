#### Mean and Standard Deviation
Mean x̄ = Σxᵢ/n. Standard deviation σ = √[Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²/(n−1)]. Standard deviation measures spread — how much individual measurements vary around the mean. A small σ → precise measurements. A large σ → scattered measurements.

#### Standard Error of the Mean
Standard error = σ/√n. This estimates how close the sample mean is to the true population mean. It shrinks as you take more measurements. Reporting your result as x̄ ± SE (e.g., 9.81 ± 0.03 m/s²) means you're 68% confident the true value is in that range.

#### Normal Distribution
Many measurement errors follow a normal (Gaussian) distribution: bell-shaped, symmetric. About 68% of measurements fall within 1σ of the mean; 95% within 2σ; 99.7% within 3σ. This is the basis for confidence intervals in physics reporting.

#### Propagating Uncertainty Through Calculations
Addition/subtraction: add absolute uncertainties. Multiplication/division: add relative (percentage) uncertainties. Example: density = mass/volume. Δmass/mass = 0.1/50.0 = 0.2%; Δvolume/volume = 0.5/20.0 = 2.5%. Δdensity/density = 2.7%. If density = 2.50 g/cm³, absolute uncertainty = 2.5 × 2.7% ≈ 0.07 g/cm³.

#### Review Questions
1. Five measurements: 9.80, 9.78, 9.81, 9.82, 9.79 m/s². Calculate the mean and estimate standard error as σ/√n.
2. What happens to standard error as you quadruple the number of measurements?
3. If the uncertainty in force is 2% and in distance is 3%, what is the uncertainty in work (W = Fd)?
