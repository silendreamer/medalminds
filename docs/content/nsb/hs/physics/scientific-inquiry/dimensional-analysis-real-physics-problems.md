#### Problem Set: Unit Conversions
Convert these: (a) 55 miles/hour → m/s. (b) 1.0 atm → Pa (1 atm = 101,325 Pa). (c) 1 eV → joules (1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J). (d) 100 W for 1 hour → joules.
Solutions: (a) 55 × 1609/3600 = 24.6 m/s. (b) 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa. (c) 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. (d) 100 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁵ J.

#### Verifying Equations by Dimensions
Verify the period of a simple pendulum: T = 2π√(L/g). Units: √(m / (m/s²)) = √(s²) = s ✓. Now check T = 2π√(m/k) for a spring-mass system. Units: √(kg / (N/m)) = √(kg / (kg/s²)) = √(s²) = s ✓. Both give seconds — dimensionally consistent.

#### Order-of-Magnitude Estimates
How much energy does a car engine produce per year if it runs at 100 kW for 4 hours a day? 100 kW × 4 hr × 365 days = 146,000 kWh ≈ 1.5 × 10⁵ kWh = 5.3 × 10¹¹ J. This is called a Fermi estimate — physicists use these constantly to sanity-check calculations.

#### Review Questions
1. Convert 1 light-year to meters. (Speed of light ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s; 1 year ≈ 3.15 × 10⁷ s)
2. Verify that the equation for gravitational potential energy PE = mgh has units of joules.
3. Estimate the number of heartbeats in a human lifetime (assume 70 years, 70 bpm).
