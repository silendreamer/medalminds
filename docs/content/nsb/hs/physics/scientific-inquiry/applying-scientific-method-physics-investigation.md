#### The Investigation: Does Launch Angle Affect Range?
You want to know: at what angle does a projectile travel the farthest horizontally? Start with theory — kinematics predicts maximum range at 45°. Your hypothesis: "A projectile launched at 45° will have the greatest horizontal range compared to other angles, given constant initial speed."

#### Setting Up the Experiment
Independent variable: launch angle (15°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 75°). Dependent variable: horizontal range (measured in meters). Controlled variables: initial speed (set by a spring launcher), projectile mass (same ball), launch height, and surface angle. Run 5 trials at each angle; record and average.

#### Collecting and Graphing Data
Plot angle (x-axis) vs. mean range (y-axis). If theory is correct, you'll see a parabolic shape peaking at 45°. Notice that 30° and 60° give the same range — complementary angles are symmetric. Draw error bars representing ± one standard deviation.

#### Evaluating Error and Conclusion
Sources of error: variability in launch speed, air currents, measurement of landing position. Conclusion: "The data supports the hypothesis. The 45° launch produced the greatest range (4.82 ± 0.08 m). The complementary angle symmetry was observed (30° ≈ 60°), consistent with kinematic theory."

#### Review Questions
1. In the projectile experiment, what would happen to your conclusion if the launch speed varied between trials?
2. Why do complementary angles (e.g., 30° and 60°) produce the same range?
3. A student plots their data and finds the peak at 40°, not 45°. Propose two possible explanations.
