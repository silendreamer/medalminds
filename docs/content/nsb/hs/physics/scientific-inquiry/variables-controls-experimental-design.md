#### Types of Variables
Every experiment has three kinds of variables: independent (what you change), dependent (what you measure), and controlled (what you keep constant). In an experiment testing how angle affects projectile range, the launch angle is independent, horizontal distance is dependent, and initial speed, projectile mass, and air conditions are controlled.

#### The Role of the Control Group
A control group receives no treatment — it represents the baseline. In a physics experiment testing whether a magnetic field affects current, the control setup runs the circuit without the magnet. The control lets you compare treated vs. untreated conditions with confidence.

#### Replication and Repeatability
A single trial proves nothing. Physicists repeat experiments multiple times and report the mean and standard deviation. If three trials give 9.78, 9.81, and 9.80 m/s² for gravitational acceleration, the mean is more reliable than any single measurement. Repeatability (same experimenter) and reproducibility (different labs) both matter.

#### Identifying Errors
Systematic errors shift all measurements in the same direction (e.g., a miscalibrated balance always reads 2 g too high). Random errors scatter measurements around the true value (e.g., reaction time variability). Systematic errors are more dangerous because averaging doesn't eliminate them.

#### Review Questions
1. Name the three types of variables in a controlled experiment.
2. What is the difference between systematic error and random error?
3. A student measures the speed of sound three times and gets 340, 338, and 342 m/s. What is the mean? What type of error do these variations represent?
