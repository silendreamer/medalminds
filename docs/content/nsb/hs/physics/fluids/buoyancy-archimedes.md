#### Archimedes' Principle

**Archimedes' principle** states that any object fully or partially submerged in a fluid experiences an upward **buoyant force** equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces. The equation is **F_b = rho_fluid · g · V_displaced**, where rho_fluid is the density of the surrounding fluid, g is gravitational acceleration, and V_displaced is the volume of fluid displaced by the object. This upward force arises because fluid pressure on the bottom face of a submerged object exceeds fluid pressure on the top face.

#### Floating and Sinking Conditions

Whether an object floats or sinks depends on comparing its average density to the fluid density:

- If **rho_object < rho_fluid**, the buoyant force at full submersion exceeds the object's weight; the object rises and floats partially submerged.
- If **rho_object = rho_fluid**, the object is in **neutral buoyancy** and remains at rest at any depth.
- If **rho_object > rho_fluid**, gravity exceeds the buoyant force and the object sinks to the bottom.

For a floating object, only a fraction V_sub/V_total = rho_object/rho_fluid of its volume is submerged, since at equilibrium F_b = W_object requires rho_fluid · g · V_sub = rho_object · g · V_total.

#### Apparent Weight

A submerged object feels lighter than it does in air. Its **apparent weight** is W_apparent = W_actual - F_b = (rho_object - rho_fluid)·g·V. This relation lets experimenters determine an object's density by weighing it in air and then in a fluid of known density. The fractional reduction in weight equals the ratio rho_fluid/rho_object, providing a classic method for measuring the **specific gravity** of solids.

#### Applications and Examples

Ships are made of dense steel yet float because their hulls enclose large volumes of air, reducing average density below that of seawater. **Submarines** control buoyancy by flooding or emptying ballast tanks to adjust their average density. **Hydrometers** float higher in denser liquids because less volume must be displaced to support the instrument's weight. Atmospheric buoyancy similarly acts on balloons: a helium balloon floats because the weight of displaced air exceeds the combined weight of the helium and the balloon envelope.

#### Review Questions
1. A solid aluminum sphere (density 2700 kg/m³, volume 2 × 10⁻³ m³) is fully submerged in water (density 1000 kg/m³). What is the buoyant force acting on it?
2. A wooden block floats with 60% of its volume submerged in water. What is the density of the wood?
3. An object weighs 50 N in air and 32 N when fully submerged in water. What is the object's volume?
