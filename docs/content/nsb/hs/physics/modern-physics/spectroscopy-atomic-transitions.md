#### Selection Rules
Not all transitions are allowed. Electric dipole transitions must obey selection rules: Δl = ±1 and Δs = 0 (spin doesn't change). These rules arise from the interaction of the electric field (from the photon) with the electron's orbital and spin angular momenta. A transition between two s orbitals (Δl = 0) is forbidden and has extremely low probability. Selection rules explain why some spectral lines are absent or very dim.

#### Fine Structure
Higher-resolution spectroscopy reveals that spectral lines split into closely spaced components. This fine structure arises from spin-orbit coupling: the electron's spin magnetic moment interacts with the magnetic field produced by its orbital motion. This coupling causes small energy shifts depending on the relative orientations of spin and orbital angular momentum. Spectral lines split into multiplets reflecting these different couplings.

#### Hyperfine Structure
Even finer structure results from the nuclear spin's interaction with the electron's magnetic field (hyperfine coupling). This produces small energy splittings that can be resolved only with high-precision spectroscopy. The 21 cm line of hydrogen (used in radio astronomy) results from a hyperfine transition between parallel and antiparallel spins of the electron and nucleus.

#### Doppler Broadening & Natural Line Width
Thermal motion of atoms causes Doppler shifts, broadening spectral lines. Hotter gases have broader lines due to larger velocity distributions. Additionally, excited states have finite lifetimes (due to spontaneous emission), which according to the uncertainty principle limits the precision to which energy (and hence frequency) can be defined, causing an intrinsic broadening called natural line width.
