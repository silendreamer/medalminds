#### Measuring Radioactive Activity
Activity (A = λN) is measured in becquerels (Bq) or curies (Ci). For radiometric dating, the fraction of a radioisotope remaining after time t is N(t)/N₀ = e^(−λt) = (½)^(t/t₁/₂). Carbon-14 dating: ¹⁴C has t₁/₂ = 5730 years; living organisms maintain constant ¹⁴C through food chains, but once dead, ¹⁴C decays. Measuring the ¹⁴C activity in ancient artifacts determines their age (valid up to ~50,000 years).

#### Radiation Types & Shielding
- Alpha particles (⁴He): stopped by paper or skin, but dangerous if inhaled or ingested (internal exposure).
- Beta particles (electrons): stopped by aluminum foil or plastic; more penetrating than alpha.
- Gamma rays (high-energy photons): penetrate deeply, stopped by lead or thick concrete. Shielding thickness increases exponentially: each "half-value layer" reduces intensity by half. No shielding is 100% effective; reducing exposure relies on distance (intensity ∝ 1/r²) and time (less time = less dose).

#### Biological Damage
Ionizing radiation damages cells by creating ions and free radicals. At low doses, cells repair damage and survive; at high doses, cells die (useful for killing cancer, but harmful to normal tissue). Very high doses cause acute radiation syndrome (nausea, vomiting, hair loss, death). Long-term effects include increased cancer risk and genetic damage. Dose is measured in gray (Gy) or rem (roentgen equivalent man), accounting for radiation type and biological effect.

#### Natural Background Radiation
Everyone receives ~2–3 mSv/year from cosmic rays, radon, and natural radioisotopes (potassium-40, radium). Higher altitudes have more cosmic rays; radon-rich regions have higher internal exposure. Human tolerance for radiation is roughly 500 mSv acute dose (lethal) or 100 mSv annual dose (safe limit). Medical X-rays and CT scans deliver ~0.01–10 mSv per procedure, well below dangerous levels for occasional procedures.
