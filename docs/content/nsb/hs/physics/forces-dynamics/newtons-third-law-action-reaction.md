#### The Precise Statement
"For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction." More precisely: if object A exerts force F on object B, then object B exerts force −F on object A. The two forces are equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and act on *different objects*. This is crucial — the two forces in a Third Law pair can never cancel each other because they act on different objects.

#### Why Third Law Pairs Don't Cancel
Students often ask: "If the floor pushes up on me with the same force I push down, why do I accelerate?" Answer: they act on *different* bodies. Your weight pulls Earth up toward you (yes — you exert a gravitational pull on Earth), and Earth's gravity pulls you down. The pair acts on you and Earth, not on you alone. When analyzing YOUR motion, you only include forces ON you.

#### Identifying Third Law Pairs
A Third Law pair: same type of force, same magnitude, opposite direction, one force on each of two objects. Example: You push a wall right (30 N). Wall pushes you left (30 N). That's the pair. The wall's normal force on you and the floor's friction on you are NOT a Third Law pair — they're different types and act on the same object (you).

#### Propulsion Applications
Rocket engines work by Newton's Third Law. Hot exhaust gases are expelled backward; the rocket is pushed forward. A swimmer pushes water backward with their hand; water pushes the swimmer forward. Walking: your foot pushes backward on the ground; the ground pushes your foot forward. In every propulsion scenario, ask: what are you pushing against, and what pushes you?

#### Review Questions
1. A 60 kg skater pushes off a wall with 180 N of force. What force does the wall exert on the skater, and in which direction?
2. Earth pulls a falling apple downward with force F. What is the reaction force, and on what object does it act?
3. A horse pulls a cart forward with 500 N. The cart pulls the horse backward with 500 N. Why does the system accelerate forward?
