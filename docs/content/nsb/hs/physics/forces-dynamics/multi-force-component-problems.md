#### Why Components Work
Forces are vectors. The x-components and y-components are independent — a horizontal force doesn't affect vertical acceleration and vice versa (absent coupling through the geometry). Decompose every force: Fx = F·cosθ, Fy = F·sinθ. Then ΣFx = max and ΣFy = may are two independent equations.

#### Worked Example: Two Ropes at Angles
A 20 kg sign hangs from two ropes. Rope 1 makes 30° with horizontal, Rope 2 makes 60° with horizontal. Sign is in equilibrium (a = 0).
- x: T₁cos30° − T₂cos60° = 0 → T₁(0.866) = T₂(0.5)
- y: T₁sin30° + T₂sin60° − 196 = 0 → 0.5T₁ + 0.866T₂ = 196
- From x equation: T₁ = 0.577T₂. Substituting: 0.5(0.577T₂) + 0.866T₂ = 196 → 1.155T₂ = 196 → T₂ = 169.7 N; T₁ = 97.97 N.

#### Setting Up the Strategy
(1) Draw free body diagram. (2) Choose coordinate axes (align one axis with acceleration if possible). (3) Resolve all forces. (4) Write ΣF = ma in each direction. (5) Solve the system of equations. (6) Check signs and units.

#### Review Questions
1. A traffic light (mass 15 kg) hangs from two cables, each at 45° from horizontal. Find the tension in each cable.
2. Two horizontal forces act on a 5 kg object: 30 N at 0° and 40 N at 90°. Find the magnitude and direction of the net force and the resulting acceleration.
3. A 10 kg box is pulled at 37° above horizontal with 80 N. The floor exerts a normal force and 20 N of friction. Find the acceleration of the box.
