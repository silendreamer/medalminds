#### Formation of Standing Waves
A **standing wave** forms when two identical waves travel in opposite directions and superpose. Instead of propagating energy, the pattern is stationary, with fixed locations called **nodes** (zero displacement) and **antinodes** (maximum displacement). Standing waves require the medium to have boundaries — such as the fixed ends of a string or the closed/open ends of a pipe — that reflect the wave back on itself. The distance between adjacent nodes (or adjacent antinodes) is always λ/2.

#### Harmonics on a Stretched String
For a string of length L fixed at both ends, only wavelengths that fit an integer number of half-wavelengths are allowed: L = nλ/2, giving λ_n = 2L/n. The **harmonic frequencies** are:

f_n = nv / (2L),  n = 1, 2, 3, …

where v is the wave speed on the string (v = √(T/μ), T = tension, μ = linear mass density). The **fundamental** (n = 1) is f₁ = v/2L; harmonics at n = 2, 3, … are called **overtones**. A fixed end is always a **node**; a free end is always an **antinode**.

#### Harmonics in Air Columns (Pipes)
Pipe geometry determines which harmonics are present:

- **Open pipe** (both ends open): antinodes at both ends. All harmonics allowed: f_n = nv/2L, n = 1, 2, 3, … (same form as a string).
- **Closed pipe** (one end closed, one open): node at closed end, antinode at open end. Only **odd harmonics**: f_n = nv/4L, n = 1, 3, 5, …

The fundamental of a closed pipe (f₁ = v/4L) is one octave lower than an open pipe of the same length.

#### Resonance
**Resonance** occurs when a driving frequency matches one of the system's **natural frequencies** (harmonics). At resonance, amplitude grows dramatically because energy is added in phase with existing oscillation. In competition problems, resonance in a tube is confirmed by the loudest sound when air-column length satisfies L = nλ/4 (closed) or L = nλ/2 (open). The **quality factor Q** characterizes how sharply peaked the resonance is; high-Q systems ring for many cycles before damping.

#### Review Questions
1. A guitar string of length 0.65 m has a wave speed of 390 m/s. Find the frequencies of the first three harmonics.
2. A closed organ pipe resonates at its fundamental frequency of 110 Hz. If the speed of sound is 340 m/s, what is the length of the pipe?
3. Why does a closed pipe only produce odd harmonics? Draw the first two resonant mode patterns to justify your answer.
