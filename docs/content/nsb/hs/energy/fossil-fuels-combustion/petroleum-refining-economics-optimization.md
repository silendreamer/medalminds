#### Refinery Capacity and Global Supply
Global refining capacity is ~100 million barrels per day (b/d); operating capacity is ~92 b/d. Refineries are large capital assets (cost ~$1–2B for a 200,000 b/d crude distillation capacity). Profitability depends on:

1. **Crude oil cost:** Brent crude ~$70–100/barrel (2024); refiners profit from refining margin (product value − crude cost).
2. **Product demand:** Gasoline (30–40% of refinery output by volume, ~50% by value) has highest margin (~$15–30/barrel); diesel (~25% output, ~20% margin) and fuel oil (~15% output, ~5% margin) are less profitable.
3. **Utility costs:** Distillation, cracking, treating consume energy; at high natural gas prices (>$5/MMBtu), refining margins compress.

Typical refining margin is $5–15/barrel, meaning a 200,000 b/d refinery earns $1–3B annually in profit (before capital recovery, taxes).

#### Sulfur Removal and Economic Trade-offs
Sulfur regulation (IMO 2020 limited marine fuel sulfur to 0.5% from 3.5%) increased refinery desulfurization costs. Refineries can:

1. **Hydrotreating:** Deep desulfurization to <50 ppm (cost ~$5–10/barrel added capacity).
2. **Upgrading crude: Purchasing low-sulfur crudes (e.g., WTI @ $2–5/barrel premium).
3. **Blending:** Mixing low-sulfur and high-sulfur products to meet limits (lowest cost).

Desulfurization produces hydrogen sulfide (H₂S), which is recovered and converted to elemental sulfur via the Claus process. Sulfur is sold at ~$100–200/ton, partially offsetting desulfurization costs.

#### Cracking and Conversion Complexity
A simple refinery (distillation only) produces ~30% gasoline from crude; complex refineries (with fluid catalytic cracking, FCC) produce ~40–50% gasoline. FCC thermally breaks heavy gas oil into lighter fractions:

C₁₆–C₃₀ → C₄–C₁₂ (gasoline) + LPG (propane, butane) + coke

Coke (solid carbon residue, ~5% by weight) is burned for furnace fuel or sold as delayed coker feedstock. FCC requires ~$200–400M capital and increases complexity but boosts refinery margin by ~$3–5/barrel.

#### Review Questions
1. If Brent crude costs $90/barrel and a refinery's blended product value is $98/barrel, and operating costs are $8/barrel, calculate the refining margin and annual profit for a 200,000 b/d facility.
2. Explain the economic trade-offs between hydrodesulfurization (deep treatment) and crude blending to meet IMO 2020 sulfur limits (0.5% S in marine fuel).
3. How does fluid catalytic cracking increase gasoline yield and refining margin, and what byproducts are produced?
