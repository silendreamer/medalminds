#### CO₂ Atmospheric Chemistry and Radiative Forcing
Carbon dioxide is a stable, colorless, odorless gas that persists in the atmosphere for ~300 years (residence time). Fossil fuel combustion emits ~37 gigatons of CO₂ per year (2023); land-use change contributes ~4 Gt/year. Pre-industrial atmospheric CO₂ was ~280 ppm; current concentration is ~420 ppm (2024), increasing ~2.5 ppm/year. CO₂ absorbs infrared radiation at wavelengths 13–17 μm (the main atmospheric window through which Earth radiates heat). This absorption increases radiative forcing—the imbalance in radiation energy—by ~2 W/m² per CO₂ doubling (380→760 ppm), driving net warming.

#### Global Carbon Budget and Emissions Pathways
Limiting warming to 1.5°C (Paris Agreement target) requires reducing CO₂ emissions to net zero by 2050 and achieving net-negative emissions (carbon removal) thereafter. The remaining "carbon budget" for 1.5°C is ~400 Gt CO₂ at current emission rates, equivalent to ~10 years. For 2°C warming, the budget is ~1,150 Gt CO₂, equivalent to ~30 years. Fossil fuel combustion accounts for ~75% of CO₂ emissions; the remainder from deforestation, agriculture, and industrial processes.

#### Sectoral Emissions and Mitigation
**Power generation:** ~25% of global CO₂. Mitigation: renewable energy (solar, wind, hydro), nuclear, carbon capture and storage (CCS).
**Transportation:** ~24% of global CO₂. Mitigation: electric vehicles, biofuels, efficiency improvements.
**Industry:** ~21% of global CO₂. Mitigation: energy efficiency, electrification, hydrogen, CCS.
**Buildings:** ~16% of global CO₂ (mostly from heating/cooling). Mitigation: insulation, heat pumps, renewable electricity.
**Agriculture/forestry:** ~14% of global CO₂. Mitigation: regenerative agriculture, reforestation.

#### Carbon Intensity of Fuels
Carbon intensity (grams CO₂ per megajoule of energy) varies by fuel:
- Coal: ~90–100 g CO₂/MJ
- Natural gas: ~50 g CO₂/MJ
- Crude oil: ~70–80 g CO₂/MJ
- Biofuels: ~10–50 g CO₂/MJ (depending on production emissions)
- Electricity (coal-heavy grid): ~200–300 g CO₂/MJ
- Electricity (renewable-heavy grid): ~10–30 g CO₂/MJ

Switching from coal to natural gas for electricity reduces emissions ~50%; switching to renewables reduces emissions by >90%.

#### Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS)
Post-combustion CCS captures CO₂ from flue gas (6–15% CO₂ in air-fired furnaces) using chemical solvents (amines) or physical sorbents (zeolites). Pure CO₂ (>99%) is compressed to liquid (80 bar) for transport and injection into geological formations (saline aquifers, depleted oil/gas fields) or chemical utilization (building materials, chemicals, fuels). Current CCS deployment is ~45 million tonnes CO₂/year globally; cost is $50–100/ton CO₂ captured (significant for large-scale deployment).

#### Review Questions
1. Explain the greenhouse effect mechanism for CO₂, and why CO₂ persistence in the atmosphere (~300 years) matters for climate policy.
2. Calculate the remaining carbon budget for 2°C warming at current emission rates (37 Gt CO₂/year).
3. Compare the carbon intensity of coal (~95 g CO₂/MJ) vs. natural gas (~50 g CO₂/MJ). If a power plant switches fuel from coal to gas, what percentage reduction in CO₂ emissions is achieved?
