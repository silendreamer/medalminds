#### High-Yield Facts
- Most stable nucleus: iron-56 (highest binding energy per nucleon: 8.79 MeV/nucleon)
- 1 atomic mass unit (u) = 931.5 MeV/c²
- Magic numbers: 2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, 126
- Doubly magic: He-4, O-16, Ca-40, Ca-48, Pb-208
- Mass defect: difference between calculated and measured nuclear mass
- Binding energy = mass defect × 931.5 MeV/u
- Tin has the most stable isotopes of any element (10)
- Nuclear shell model: Goeppert Mayer and Jensen (1949, Nobel Prize)

#### Bowl Toss-Up Stems
- "For 10 points, this element with atomic number 26 has the highest binding energy per nucleon…" → **Iron**
- "For 10 points, the energy equivalent of the mass difference between a nucleus and its constituent nucleons is called…" → **Binding energy**
- "For 10 points, these special numbers of nucleons confer extra nuclear stability, analogous to noble gas configurations…" → **Magic numbers**

#### Review Questions
1. Which nucleus has the highest binding energy per nucleon, and what is its approximate value?
2. Why do elements heavier than iron require energy input to form via fusion?
3. What do magic numbers represent in the nuclear shell model?
