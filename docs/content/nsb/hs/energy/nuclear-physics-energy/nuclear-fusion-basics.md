#### Why Fusion Releases Energy
Fusion of light nuclei (below iron on the binding energy curve) releases energy because the products have higher binding energy per nucleon. The D-T reaction: ²H + ³H → ⁴He (3.5 MeV) + n (14.1 MeV), releasing 17.6 MeV total. Per kilogram of fuel, D-T fusion releases ~4× more energy than U-235 fission and ~340 million times more than coal combustion.

#### The Coulomb Barrier
Two positively charged nuclei repel each other electrostatically. To fuse, they must approach within ~1 fm (nuclear force range). At room temperature, thermal kinetic energies (~0.025 eV) are vastly insufficient to overcome the ~MeV Coulomb barrier. Fusion requires temperatures of ~100 million K (~10 keV) — where quantum tunneling through the barrier becomes significant. At these temperatures, electrons strip from atoms and matter exists as plasma.

#### The Sun's Fusion: Proton-Proton Chain
The sun fuses hydrogen to helium via the proton-proton chain: net result 4¹H → ⁴He + 2e⁺ + 2νₑ + 26.7 MeV. The sun's core temperature is ~15 million K — lower than needed for Earth-based fusion because the sun has 10²⁵ times more fuel and gravitational confinement. Even at 15 million K, each proton takes ~10 billion years on average to fuse — fusion in the sun is very slow.

#### Why D-T, Not Hydrogen?
The D-T reaction has the highest cross section (probability) at achievable temperatures (~100 million K), making it the most practical terrestrial fusion fuel. Deuterium is abundant in seawater (33 mg/L); tritium (t½ = 12.3 years) must be bred from lithium-6: ⁶Li + n → ³H + ⁴He. The world's lithium supply could fuel D-T fusion for millions of years.
