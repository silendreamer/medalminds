#### The Mass Defect
When protons and neutrons combine to form a nucleus, the total mass decreases. This missing mass is the mass defect (Δm). For helium-4: 2 protons (2 × 1.00728 u) + 2 neutrons (2 × 1.00867 u) = 4.03190 u, but the measured He-4 mass is 4.00150 u. Mass defect: Δm = 4.03190 − 4.00150 = 0.03040 u.

#### Binding Energy via E = mc²
Einstein's mass-energy equivalence: E = mc². The binding energy equals the mass defect converted to energy: Eb = Δm × 931.5 MeV/u. For He-4: Eb = 0.03040 × 931.5 = 28.3 MeV. This is the energy required to completely separate He-4 into 2 protons and 2 neutrons. It's also the energy released when those nucleons fuse.

#### Binding Energy Per Nucleon
Dividing binding energy by the number of nucleons gives binding energy per nucleon (BE/A) — a measure of nuclear stability. He-4: 28.3/4 = 7.07 MeV/nucleon. Iron-56: 8.79 MeV/nucleon — the highest of any nucleus, making it the most stable. BE/A peaks around A = 56 then slowly decreases. This graph explains why fission releases energy (splitting heavy nuclei) and fusion releases energy (combining light nuclei): both move nuclei toward iron's peak stability.

#### The Iron Peak
Iron-56 and nickel-62 sit at the peak of the binding energy per nucleon curve. Stars generate energy by fusing light elements up to iron; when an iron core forms in a massive star, fusion becomes endothermic — the star can no longer support itself against gravity, triggering a supernova. The elements heavier than iron are forged in supernovae and neutron star mergers.

#### Review Questions
1. Uranium-235 has a measured mass of 235.0439 u. The sum of its constituent protons and neutrons is 236.9590 u. Calculate the mass defect and binding energy in MeV.
2. Why does the binding energy per nucleon peak at iron-56?
3. Explain why both fission and fusion can release energy using the binding energy per nucleon curve.
