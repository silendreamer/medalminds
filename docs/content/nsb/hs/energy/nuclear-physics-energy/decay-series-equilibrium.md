#### What Is a Decay Series?
A single radioactive decay rarely produces a stable nucleus directly. Instead, heavy nuclei like uranium-238 undergo a sequence of alpha and beta decays — a decay series or decay chain — passing through many intermediate radioactive daughters before reaching stable lead-206. The uranium-238 series has 14 steps; the thorium-232 series has 10.

#### The Uranium-238 Series
U-238 (t½ = 4.47 Gy) → Th-234 → Pa-234 → U-234 → Th-230 → Ra-226 → Rn-222 → … → Pb-206 (stable). Each step releases energy. Radon-222 (t½ = 3.8 days) is a gas that can accumulate in buildings — it's the second leading cause of lung cancer in the US after smoking.

#### Secular Equilibrium
When a long-lived parent (very slow decay) produces a short-lived daughter, the daughter's activity eventually equals the parent's — this is secular equilibrium. At equilibrium, the activity of parent = activity of each daughter in the chain. This is why old uranium ores contain measurable amounts of all intermediate daughters despite their short half-lives.

#### Natural Background Radiation
Most natural background radiation comes from decay series: uranium and thorium in rocks produce radon gas; potassium-40 in soil and food; cosmic rays produce carbon-14 in the atmosphere. Average US background dose is ~3 mSv/year; radon contributes ~2 mSv. Understanding decay series is essential for assessing radiation exposure.
