#### Top Bowl Facts: Changes and Reactions
1. Physical change: composition unchanged; substance recoverable
2. Chemical change: new substances formed; bonds broken and formed
3. Law of conservation of mass: mass reactants = mass products
4. Synthesis: A + B → AB (two things make one)
5. Decomposition: AB → A + B (one thing makes multiple)
6. Single replacement: driven by the activity series (more reactive displaces less reactive)
7. Double replacement: ion exchange; often produces precipitate, gas, or water
8. Complete combustion: products are CO₂ + H₂O
9. Incomplete combustion: products include CO and/or soot (C)
10. Precipitation reaction: double replacement producing an insoluble product
11. Electrolysis: uses electricity to decompose compounds (H₂O → H₂ + O₂)
12. Photosynthesis and combustion are chemical changes

#### Common Bowl Traps
- Dissolving ≠ chemical change (even if temperature changes)
- Physical signs (bubbles, color change) can occur in physical changes — not 100% proof of chemical change
- Sublimation of dry ice = PHYSICAL change (CO₂ doesn't become a different substance)

#### Practice Toss-Up Stems
1. "For 10 points — what type of reaction takes one compound and produces multiple products?" → **decomposition**
2. "For 10 points — in a single replacement reaction, what determines whether the reaction will occur?" → **the activity series** (more active metal displaces less active)
3. "For 10 points — what law states that the total mass of reactants equals the total mass of products in a chemical reaction?" → **Law of Conservation of Mass** (accept: Lavoisier's law)

#### Review Questions
1. Is photosynthesis (6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂) a physical or chemical change? What type of reaction is it?
2. Magnesium burns in nitrogen gas to form Mg₃N₂. Is this synthesis or single replacement?
3. A student mixes two clear ionic solutions and a white solid forms. What type of reaction is this? What is the white solid called?
