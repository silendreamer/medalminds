#### Reaction Type Classification (Quick-Fire)
1. 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ → **Decomposition**
2. 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl → **Synthesis**
3. CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O → **Combustion**
4. 2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂ → **Single replacement**
5. AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃ → **Double replacement** (precipitation)
6. N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ → **Synthesis**

#### Conservation of Mass Problems
1. 4 g H₂ + 32 g O₂ → ? g H₂O → **36 g** (mass conserved)
2. CaCO₃ (100 g) → CaO (56 g) + CO₂ → CO₂ = **44 g**
3. 112 g Fe reacts with Cl₂ to form 325 g FeCl₃. How many grams of Cl₂ were consumed? → 325 − 112 = **213 g Cl₂**

#### Evidence Identification
Which piece of evidence suggests a chemical change (not just physical)?
(A) The temperature of the solution rises when NaOH dissolves in water → possibly physical
(B) A yellow precipitate forms when Pb²⁺ and I⁻ solutions are mixed → **chemical** (new substance)
(C) Gas bubbles form when vinegar meets baking soda → **chemical** (CO₂ produced)
(D) Iron turns reddish-brown over days in moist air → **chemical** (iron oxide formed)

#### Review Questions
1. What are two signs of chemical change that CANNOT be caused by a physical change?
2. A 50.0 g sample of calcium carbonate is heated until fully decomposed. The solid residue weighs 28.0 g. What gas was released and how many grams of it?
3. Classify and predict: Will silver react with hydrochloric acid? (Use the activity series: Ag is below H₂.)
