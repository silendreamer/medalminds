#### Top Bowl Facts: Properties
1. Intensive properties: density, color, melting point, boiling point, specific heat, conductivity, hardness, Rf value
2. Extensive properties: mass, volume, length, heat energy, moles
3. Physical properties observed without changing composition
4. Chemical properties describe reactivity — must observe a change to measure them
5. Malleability: can be hammered into sheets (metals) — physical
6. Ductility: can be drawn into wires (metals) — physical
7. Luster: shininess of metals — physical

#### Top Bowl Facts: Classification
8. Element: one type of atom; cannot be decomposed by chemical means
9. Compound: fixed ratio of two+ elements; CAN be decomposed chemically
10. Homogeneous mixture = solution (uniform, same composition throughout)
11. Heterogeneous mixture = visibly different phases
12. Alloy = homogeneous mixture of metals (bronze = Cu+Sn, brass = Cu+Zn, steel = Fe+C)

#### Top Bowl Facts: Separation
13. Distillation exploits differences in boiling point
14. Chromatography exploits differences in polarity/affinity for stationary phase
15. Filtration separates solids from liquids by particle size

#### Practice Toss-Up Stems
1. "For 10 points — what type of property does not depend on the amount of substance present?" → **intensive property**
2. "For 10 points — what separation technique exploits differences in boiling point?" → **distillation**
3. "For 10 points — is the ability of iron to rust a physical or chemical property?" → **chemical property**

#### Review Questions
1. Is "dissolving" a physical or chemical change? Justify at the molecular level.
2. A question describes a property that changes when you take a larger sample. Is this intensive or extensive?
3. Bronze is Cu + Sn. What type of mixture is it? What separation technique would you NOT use to separate them (and why)?
