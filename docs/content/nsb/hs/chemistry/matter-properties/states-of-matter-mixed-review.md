#### Heating Curve Annotation
On a standard heating curve for water: label (a) melting point (0°C), (b) boiling point (100°C), (c) the region where q = mcΔT for liquid water, (d) the region where q = mHv, (e) the highest-slope region (gas phase — lowest heat capacity), (f) the plateau with the greatest length (vaporization — largest latent heat).

#### Phase Diagram Classification
Given a phase diagram, identify: (A) Which region is liquid? (higher T and moderate P) (B) Where does sublimation occur? (solid → gas at low P, crossing the sublimation curve) (C) What happens at the triple point if pressure is decreased at constant temperature? (sublimation — goes from solid to gas) (D) Does this diagram belong to water or CO₂ if the solid-liquid boundary has a negative slope? (water)

#### KMT Prediction Problems
1. Gas X has molar mass 2 g/mol (H₂). Gas Y has molar mass 44 g/mol (CO₂). At 25°C, which moves faster on average? → H₂ (KE = ½mv²; same KE at same T, so lighter particles move faster)
2. Tripling the Kelvin temperature of a gas: what happens to the average KE? → triples (KE ∝ T in Kelvin)

#### Review Questions
1. A substance's vapor pressure is measured as a function of temperature. At 60°C it is 200 mmHg; at 100°C it is 760 mmHg. What is the normal boiling point of this substance?
2. Compare the density, particle spacing, and compressibility of each state of matter for a pure substance.
3. On a heating curve, two different substances A and B have identical heating slopes in the liquid phase. What can you conclude about their specific heat capacities as liquids?
