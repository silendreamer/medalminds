#### Classification Hierarchy
All matter divides into: **Pure substances** (fixed, uniform composition throughout) and **Mixtures** (two or more substances physically combined). Pure substances further divide into: **Elements** (cannot be broken down by chemical means; one type of atom; e.g., gold, oxygen) and **Compounds** (two or more elements chemically combined in fixed ratios; can be broken down by chemical means; e.g., water, table salt). Mixtures divide into: **Homogeneous** (uniform composition throughout; also called solutions; e.g., saltwater, air, brass) and **Heterogeneous** (non-uniform composition; visibly different phases; e.g., salad, sand and water, concrete).

#### Separation Techniques and the Property Each Exploits
| Technique | Property Exploited | Example |
|-----------|---------------------|---------|
| Filtration | Particle size | Remove sand from water |
| Distillation | Boiling point | Separate ethanol from water |
| Evaporation | Volatility | Recover salt from saltwater |
| Chromatography | Polarity/solubility | Separate ink dyes |
| Magnetic separation | Magnetic susceptibility | Separate iron from sulfur |
| Centrifugation | Density | Separate blood components |
| Crystallization | Solubility vs. temperature | Purify salts |

#### Chromatography in Detail
In paper chromatography, components travel different distances based on their affinity for the stationary phase (paper) vs. mobile phase (solvent). The Rf (retention factor) = distance spot traveled / distance solvent front traveled. More polar components travel less far on polar stationary phase (they "stick" more). Rf values are characteristic of each compound under specific conditions — used for identification.

#### Why Mixtures Can Be Separated by Physical Means
Because mixtures are held together by physical forces (not chemical bonds), you can exploit differences in physical properties to separate components without breaking any intramolecular bonds. Separation of compounds requires chemical reactions (you can't separate water into hydrogen and oxygen by filtration or distillation).
