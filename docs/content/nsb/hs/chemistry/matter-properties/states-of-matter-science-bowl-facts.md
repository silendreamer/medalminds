#### Top Bowl Facts
1. Water's triple point: 273.16 K, 611.7 Pa (0.01°C)
2. Water's critical point: 374°C, 218 atm
3. Latent heat of fusion of water: 334 J/g = 6.01 kJ/mol
4. Latent heat of vaporization of water: 2260 J/g = 40.7 kJ/mol
5. Specific heat of liquid water: 4.18 J/g·°C (highest of common substances)
6. Ice density ≈ 0.917 g/cm³; liquid water ≈ 1.000 g/cm³ (ice floats!)
7. Normal boiling point: temperature where VP = 1 atm
8. Sublimation: solid → gas directly (dry ice, iodine, moth balls)
9. Deposition: gas → solid directly (frost on windows)
10. Supercritical CO₂: used to decaffeinate coffee, Tc = 31.1°C, Pc = 73.8 atm
11. KMT: average KE = (3/2)kT where k = Boltzmann's constant
12. Plasma = 4th state of matter (ionized gas at extreme temperature)

#### Competition Trap
"Boiling" vs. "evaporation": evaporation occurs at the surface at any temperature below boiling point; boiling occurs throughout the liquid when VP equals external pressure. Both are liquid → gas transitions but via different mechanisms.

#### Practice Toss-Up Stems
1. "For 10 points — at what temperature and pressure do all three phases of water coexist?" → **273.16 K and 611.7 Pa** (accept: 0.01°C and 0.006 atm)
2. "For 10 points — what term describes the direct transition from solid to gas?" → **sublimation**
3. "For 10 points — why does ice float on liquid water?" → **Ice is less dense because the hexagonal hydrogen bond network spaces molecules farther apart**

#### Review Questions
1. A substance's critical temperature is 20°C. Can you liquefy it by applying pressure at 25°C? Why or why not?
2. List three substances that sublime at atmospheric pressure at room temperature.
3. Explain why steam burns cause more severe tissue damage than liquid water burns at the same temperature.
