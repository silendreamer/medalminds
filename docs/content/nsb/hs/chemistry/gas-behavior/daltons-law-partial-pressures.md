#### The Law
**P_total = P₁ + P₂ + P₃ + …**
Each gas in a mixture behaves independently (consistent with KMT — no intermolecular forces between different gas molecules). The partial pressure of gas *i* is the pressure it would exert alone:
**P_i = x_i × P_total** where x_i = mole fraction = n_i/n_total.

#### Calculating Partial Pressures
**Example:** A container holds 2.00 mol N₂ and 0.500 mol O₂ at 5.00 atm total.
- x_N₂ = 2.00/2.50 = 0.800; P_N₂ = 0.800 × 5.00 = **4.00 atm**
- x_O₂ = 0.500/2.50 = 0.200; P_O₂ = 0.200 × 5.00 = **1.00 atm**

#### Collecting Gas Over Water
Gas collected by water displacement is always contaminated with water vapor. Total pressure = P_gas + P_H₂O. At 25°C, vapor pressure of water = 23.8 mmHg. If total P = 760 mmHg: P_gas = 760 − 23.8 = **736.2 mmHg**.

#### Breathing Gas Mixtures
Air: ~78% N₂, ~21% O₂, ~1% Ar, 0.04% CO₂. At 1 atm (760 mmHg):
- P_O₂ = 0.21 × 760 = **160 mmHg** (this drives O₂ into blood)
- P_CO₂ = 0.0004 × 760 = **0.3 mmHg** (drives CO₂ out of blood)

At 8848 m (Everest), P_total ≈ 253 mmHg, P_O₂ ≈ 53 mmHg — severely hypoxic without supplemental oxygen.

#### Review Questions
1. A mixture of 1.00 mol He, 2.00 mol Ne, and 1.00 mol Ar is in a 5.00 L container at 25°C. Find P_total and P_He.
2. O₂ is collected over water at 20°C (vapor pressure of water = 17.5 mmHg). Total pressure = 740 mmHg. Find P_O₂ and volume of dry O₂.
3. Why does the partial pressure of oxygen in air at high altitude decrease, even though the fraction of oxygen (21%) stays the same?
