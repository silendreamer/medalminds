#### The Law
**V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂** (at constant n and P), where T is in **Kelvin** (always!). Volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature: V ∝ T. Converting to Kelvin: K = °C + 273.15 (use 273 for most calculations).

#### Why Kelvin Matters
Using Celsius leads to wrong answers. At −273°C, volume would theoretically reach zero (absolute zero). Celsius temperature is offset; Kelvin starts at true zero of molecular motion. A gas at 0°C is NOT at zero molecular motion — it's at 273 K.

#### Standard Calculations
**Example 1:** A gas occupies 5.00 L at 27°C. Volume at 127°C, constant pressure?
T₁ = 300 K, T₂ = 400 K
V₂ = V₁ × T₂/T₁ = 5.00 × (400/300) = **6.67 L**

**Example 2:** A balloon is 10.0 L at 25°C. It's placed in liquid nitrogen (−196°C). New volume?
T₁ = 298 K, T₂ = 77 K
V₂ = 10.0 × (77/298) = **2.59 L**

#### Physical Interpretation (KMT)
Higher temperature → faster molecules → hit walls harder → walls must expand to maintain constant pressure. Lower temperature → slower molecules → weaker wall force → volume decreases.

#### Extrapolation to Absolute Zero
If you plot V vs. T(K) for any ideal gas, you get a straight line that extrapolates to V = 0 at T = 0 K (−273.15°C). All gases would converge at absolute zero — this is how Kelvin was defined. Real gases liquefy before reaching 0 K.
