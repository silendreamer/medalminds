#### Effusion vs. Diffusion
**Effusion** is the escape of gas through a tiny pinhole into vacuum. **Diffusion** is the spreading of gas through another gas. Both are slower for heavier gas molecules because heavier molecules move more slowly at the same temperature.

#### Graham's Law
At constant temperature and pressure:
**rate₁/rate₂ = √(M₂/M₁)**

Where M is molar mass in g/mol and rate can be measured in time, distance, or relative amounts.

**Example:** How much faster does H₂ (M = 2 g/mol) effuse than O₂ (M = 32 g/mol)?
rate_H₂/rate_O₂ = √(32/2) = √16 = **4 times faster**

#### Identifying Unknown Gases
If an unknown gas effuses 1.5 times slower than N₂ (M = 28):
rate_N₂/rate_x = 1.5 = √(M_x/28)
1.5² = M_x/28 → M_x = 2.25 × 28 = **63 g/mol** (likely CuO, MW = 63.5 — or SO₂, MW = 64)

#### Practical Applications
- **Uranium enrichment:** U-235 and U-238 are separated as UF₆ gas. Graham's Law gives slightly different effusion rates: M(²³⁵UF₆) = 349, M(²³⁸UF₆) = 352. Thousands of effusion stages concentrate U-235.
- **Helium vs. air balloons:** Helium (M=4) effuses much faster than N₂ (M=28) through balloon rubber — why helium balloons deflate quickly.

#### Review Questions
1. Calculate the ratio of effusion rates of CO₂ (M=44) to H₂O (M=18).
2. A gas effuses at half the rate of O₂. What is its molar mass?
3. Explain why the effusion process is slower than the average speed of molecules might suggest.
