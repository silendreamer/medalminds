#### The Equation
**P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂** (at constant n)

This equation contains all three two-variable laws as special cases:
- Hold T constant → P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ (Boyle's)
- Hold P constant → V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ (Charles's)
- Hold V constant → P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂ (Gay-Lussac's)

#### Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP)
**STP = 0°C (273 K) and 1 atm.** At STP, 1 mole of ideal gas occupies **22.4 L** (the molar volume). This is a useful conversion factor: 1 mol gas ↔ 22.4 L at STP.

**Note:** An older definition used 0°C and 1 atm; IUPAC's current STP is 0°C and 100 kPa (giving 22.7 L/mol). Most textbooks still use the 22.4 L/mol version.

#### Worked Example
A gas occupies 4.00 L at 25°C and 1.50 atm. What volume does it occupy at 0°C and 3.00 atm?
- P₁ = 1.50, V₁ = 4.00, T₁ = 298 K
- P₂ = 3.00, T₂ = 273 K
- V₂ = (1.50 × 4.00 × 273) / (298 × 3.00) = 1638/894 = **1.83 L**

Check: pressure doubled → volume halved (1.83 vs. 2.0 is close); temperature decreased → volume decreased further → both effects reduce volume. ✓

#### Review Questions
1. A gas at 2.0 atm, 10.0 L, 300 K is changed to 1.0 atm and 400 K. Find the new volume.
2. Convert 5.00 L of gas at 100°C and 0.500 atm to STP conditions. What volume?
3. A diver's 12 L tank at 200 atm and 20°C is used to 1 atm at 37°C. How many liters of gas were released (at final conditions)?
