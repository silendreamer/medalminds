#### The Breathing Mechanism
Your lungs do not expand on their own — they are pulled open by the diaphragm. When the diaphragm contracts and flattens, chest volume increases. By Boyle's Law, increased volume → decreased pressure. The lung pressure drops below atmospheric pressure, and air flows in (inhalation). When the diaphragm relaxes, chest volume decreases → pressure increases above atmospheric → air flows out (exhalation).

#### Numbers
At rest, lung volume changes by about 0.5 L per breath. Inhalation pressure drops approximately 1–3 mmHg below atmospheric (760 mmHg). This small pressure gradient is sufficient because the airway resistance is low.

#### Scuba Diving and Boyle's Law
At 10 m depth, water pressure = 2 atm total. Air breathed at depth is compressed to match. If a diver holds their breath while ascending, lung volume doubles as pressure halves (Boyle's Law) — potentially causing a **pulmonary embolism** (lung rupture). This is why divers are taught to breathe continuously and never hold their breath while ascending.

#### Pressure Conversions Used in Physiology
Blood pressure is measured in mmHg; atmospheric is 760 mmHg; partial pressures of O₂ (~160 mmHg) and CO₂ (~0.3 mmHg) drive gas exchange via Fick's Law. Boyle's Law underlies all ventilation; partial pressures govern diffusion across membranes.
