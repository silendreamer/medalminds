#### The Law
**P₁/T₁ = P₂/T₂** (at constant n and V). Pressure is directly proportional to absolute temperature when volume is fixed. This applies to sealed rigid containers.

**Example:** A tire at 20°C reads 32 psi. After a long drive at 60°C, what is the pressure?
T₁ = 293 K, T₂ = 333 K
P₂ = 32 × (333/293) = **36.4 psi**

This is why tire pressure recommendations specify cold inflation — warm tires read higher.

#### Safety Applications
Sealed containers (aerosol cans, propane tanks) carry warnings not to heat above certain temperatures. By Gay-Lussac's Law, the pressure inside increases proportionally with temperature. A propane tank at 20°C (293 K) with 200 psi reaches 273 psi at 80°C (353 K) — still safe. At 500°C (773 K), pressure would be ~530 psi — potentially catastrophic.

#### Autoclave Sterilization
Medical autoclaves use steam at 121°C and 15 psi above atmospheric (29.7 psia) to sterilize equipment. The elevated pressure keeps water liquid above 100°C and allows higher temperature — more effective sterilization.
