#### Gas Density from Ideal Gas Law
Rearranging PV = nRT with n = m/M (mass/molar mass):
PV = (m/M)RT → **d = PM/(RT)** where d = m/V = density in g/L.

**Example:** Density of O₂ at STP?
d = (1.00 atm)(32.00 g/mol) / [(0.08206)(273)] = 32.00/22.4 = **1.43 g/L**

Notice: denser (heavier) gases have higher density at same conditions. Helium (M=4): d = 4/22.4 = 0.178 g/L — much less dense than air (M_air ≈ 29: d ≈ 1.29 g/L).

#### Identifying an Unknown Gas
A gas has density 1.96 g/L at STP. What is it?
M = dRT/P = 1.96 × 22.4 = **43.9 g/mol** ≈ 44 g/mol → **CO₂** or **propane (C₃H₈)** — need additional info to distinguish.

#### Real Lab: Dumas Method
In the Dumas method, a small amount of volatile liquid is vaporized in a flask of known volume at known T and P. The condensed gas is weighed. Using PV = nRT: n = PV/(RT), then M = mass/n.

**Example:** 0.150 g of unknown liquid vaporizes in a 250 mL flask at 1.00 atm and 100°C.
n = (1.00)(0.250)/[(0.08206)(373)] = 0.250/30.61 = 0.00817 mol
M = 0.150/0.00817 = **18.4 g/mol** → close to water (18.02) ✓

#### Stoichiometry with Ideal Gas Law
Gases produced in reactions can be quantified. 2H₂O₂(l) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g). If 0.100 mol H₂O₂ decomposes, moles O₂ = 0.0500 mol. Volume at 25°C, 1 atm: V = nRT/P = (0.0500)(0.08206)(298)/1.00 = **1.22 L**.
