#### Synthesis (Combination)
Two or more substances combine to form one product. 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl. Usually: A + B → AB. Often exothermic (release heat). Examples: rusting (Fe + O₂ → Fe₂O₃), formation of ammonia (N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃).

#### Decomposition
One compound breaks into two or more substances. 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂. Usually: AB → A + B. Many are endothermic (require heat). Examples: thermal decomposition of limestone (CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂), electrolysis of water (2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ with electricity).

#### Combustion
A substance reacts with oxygen, usually releasing heat and light. CₓHᵧ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O. Nearly always exothermic. Examples: burning wood, candles, fossil fuels. Characterized by rapid oxidation and visible flames or heat.

#### Single Displacement (Replacement)
One element displaces another from a compound. Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu. Pattern: A + BC → AC + B. Must use the activity series to predict if the reaction occurs—more active elements displace less active ones.

2. Classify: AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃
