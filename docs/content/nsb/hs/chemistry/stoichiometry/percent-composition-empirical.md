#### Calculating Percent Composition
For glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆), molar mass = 180.2 g/mol. % C = (6 × 12.01 / 180.2) × 100% = 40.0%. % H = (12 × 1.008 / 180.2) × 100% = 6.7%. % O = (6 × 16.00 / 180.2) × 100% = 53.3%. Note: 40.0 + 6.7 + 53.3 = 100%.

#### Finding Empirical Formula from Percent Composition
If a compound is 40.0% C, 6.7% H, and 53.3% O, assume 100 g sample. Convert to moles: C: 40.0 g / 12.01 g/mol = 3.33 mol. H: 6.7 g / 1.008 g/mol = 6.65 mol. O: 53.3 g / 16.00 g/mol = 3.33 mol. Divide by smallest: C: 1, H: 2, O: 1. Empirical formula is CH₂O.

#### Molecular Formula from Molar Mass
The empirical formula mass of CH₂O is 30.0 g/mol. If the true molar mass is 180 g/mol, the molecular formula is (180/30) = 6 times the empirical: C₆H₁₂O₆.

#### Worked Example
A compound contains 85.6% C and 14.4% H by mass. Its molar mass is 28.0 g/mol. Find the molecular formula. Assume 100 g: C: 85.6/12.01 = 7.13 mol, H: 14.4/1.008 = 14.3 mol. Ratio: C:H = 7.13:14.3 = 1:2. Empirical formula CH₂, empirical mass 14.0. Molecular formula is (28.0/14.0) × CH₂ = C₂H₄ (ethylene).
