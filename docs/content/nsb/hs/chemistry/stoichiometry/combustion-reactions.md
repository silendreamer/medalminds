#### General Combustion Equation
Hydrocarbon CₓHᵧ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O. Ethane example: C₂H₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O. Balance carbon: 2CO₂. Balance hydrogen: 3H₂O. Balance oxygen: need 4 + 3 = 7, so 3.5O₂ or multiply by 2: 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O.

#### Real-World Application: Fuel Economy
Gasoline is approximately C₈H₁₈. Complete combustion: 2C₈H₁₈ + 25O₂ → 16CO₂ + 18H₂O. For every 1 mole of octane (114 g), 16 moles (704 g) of CO₂ are produced—why cars contribute to climate change.

#### Incomplete Combustion
When oxygen is limited, products include CO and C (soot) in addition to CO₂: 2C₂H₆ + 5O₂ → 4CO + 6H₂O. Incomplete combustion produces less energy and more pollution.

#### Thermochemistry Connection
Combustion releases energy (exothermic, ΔH < 0). The equation 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O ΔH = -572 kJ tells you that 572 kJ is released per 2 moles of H₂ burned. This is why combustion powers vehicles and electricity generation.

#### Review Questions
1. Balance the combustion of propane (C₃H₈).
2. How many moles of O₂ are needed to completely burn 1 mole of benzene (C₆H₆)?
3. When 2 g of H₂ burns in O₂, releasing -285.8 kJ/mol, how much energy is released?
