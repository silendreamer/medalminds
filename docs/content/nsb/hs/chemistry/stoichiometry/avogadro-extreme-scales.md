#### Bowl-Style Toss-Up #1
"A single water molecule has a mass of approximately 3 × 10⁻²⁶ kg. How many water molecules are in 18 grams of water?" (Answer: 6 × 10²³ = 1 mole.)

#### Bowl-Style Toss-Up #2
"The number 6.022 × 10²³ is known by what name? It's the number of particles in one _____ of any substance." (Answer: Avogadro's number / mole.)

#### Proportional Reasoning Trap
Students sometimes confuse molar mass with Avogadro's number. Remember: molar mass is grams per mole (varies with the substance). Avogadro's number is particles per mole (constant for all substances).

#### Speed Strategy
In bowl competitions, recognize "6.022 × 10²³" or "mole" instantly. Most Avogadro's-related questions are conversions—set up dimensional analysis and cancel units. Avoid rounding until the final answer.

#### Review Questions
1. For 10 points: How many atoms are in 1.0 mol of copper metal?
2. For 10 points: 3.0 g of carbon contains approximately how many carbon atoms?
3. Bonus (20 points): The average mass of one atom of element X is 5.0 × 10⁻²³ g. What is its molar mass?
