### Introduction to NMR Spectroscopy  
Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) spectroscopy is a powerful analytical technique used to determine the structure of organic compounds. It exploits the magnetic properties of certain nuclei, primarily hydrogen-1 (¹H) and carbon-13 (¹³C), when placed in a magnetic field. The resulting spectra provide information about the number of hydrogen atoms in different environments, their connectivity, and the overall molecular framework.

### Peak Integration  
In NMR spectra, the area under each peak correlates with the number of protons contributing to that signal. This concept, known as peak integration, allows chemists to deduce the relative number of protons in various environments within a molecule. For instance, if a peak corresponding to a certain chemical shift integrates to twice the area of another peak, it indicates that there are twice as many protons in that environment.

### Splitting Patterns  
The splitting of NMR signals, also known as multiplicity, occurs due to J-coupling, which is the interaction between neighboring non-equivalent nuclei. The number of peaks observed in a signal is determined by the n+1 rule, where n is the number of neighboring protons. For example, a proton with one neighboring proton will appear as a doublet, while one with two neighboring protons will appear as a triplet. Understanding these patterns is crucial for elucidating the connectivity of atoms in a molecule.

### Functional Group Identification  
Different functional groups exhibit characteristic chemical shifts in NMR spectra, allowing for their identification. For example, protons on a carbon adjacent to an electronegative atom, such as oxygen or nitrogen, will resonate downfield (higher ppm values) due to deshielding effects. Recognizing these shifts can help chemists quickly identify functional groups, such as alcohols, aldehydes, and aromatic rings, within a compound.

### J-Coupling  
J-coupling, or scalar coupling, is the interaction between spins of nearby nuclei that leads to splitting of NMR signals. The magnitude of J-coupling is measured in Hertz (Hz) and can provide insight into the spatial arrangement of atoms. For example, a larger J-value often indicates that the coupled protons are closer together in space, which can help in determining the stereochemistry of a molecule.

### Analyzing NMR Spectra  
To analyze an NMR spectrum, one should start by identifying the number of signals, their chemical shifts, and their integration values. Next, the splitting patterns should be examined to deduce the number of neighboring protons. Finally, by correlating the chemical shifts and splitting patterns with known values for functional groups, a comprehensive understanding of the molecular structure can be achieved.

### Review Questions

2. According to the n+1 rule, how many peaks would a proton with two neighboring protons produce?  
3. How does the presence of electronegative atoms affect the chemical shift of protons in NMR spectroscopy?
