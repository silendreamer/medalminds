#### Autoionization of Water
Pure water undergoes **autoionization** (self-ionization): 2 H2O ⇌ H3O+ + OH-. This equilibrium is endothermic and lies far to the left. At 25 °C the equilibrium constant for this process is the **ion product of water**, Kw = [H3O+][OH-] = 1.0 × 10-14. Because water is the solvent its activity is 1, so it does not appear in the Kw expression. In pure water [H3O+] = [OH-] = 1.0 × 10-7 M.

#### The pH and pOH Scales
**pH** is defined as pH = -log[H3O+] and **pOH** as pOH = -log[OH-]. Taking the negative log of Kw gives the fundamental relationship: **pH + pOH = pKw = 14.00** at 25 °C. This equation allows calculation of pOH from pH and vice versa. A pH below 7 is **acidic** ([H3O+] > [OH-]), pH = 7 is **neutral**, and pH above 7 is **basic** at 25 °C. Note that Kw (and therefore pKw) change with temperature — at 37 °C Kw ≈ 2.4 × 10-14, so neutral pH ≈ 6.8.

#### Calculating [H3O+] and [OH-]
Given pH, recover concentration via [H3O+] = 10-pH. For a strong acid like HCl at 0.010 M: [H3O+] ≈ 0.010 M, pH = -log(0.010) = 2.00. Then [OH-] = Kw / [H3O+] = (1.0 × 10-14) / (0.010) = 1.0 × 10-12 M, and pOH = 12.00. Always verify pH + pOH = 14 as a quick check.

#### Temperature Dependence of Kw
Because autoionization is endothermic, **Kw increases with temperature**. At 100 °C, Kw ≈ 10-12, so neutral pH ≈ 6.0 at that temperature — a solution with pH 6 at 100 °C is still neutral, not acidic. Competition problems sometimes test this subtlety to distinguish between "acidic" (excess H3O+) and "low pH."

#### Review Questions
1. At 25 °C, a solution has [OH-] = 2.5 × 10-3 M. Calculate [H3O+], pH, and pOH, and state whether the solution is acidic, basic, or neutral.
2. Explain why a solution with pH = 6.5 at 37 °C is considered neutral rather than acidic, using Kw at body temperature.
3. Derive the relationship pH + pOH = 14 from the expression Kw = [H3O+][OH-] = 1.0 × 10-14 at 25 °C.
