#### Definition and Components
A solution consists of a solute (the substance being dissolved) and a solvent (the substance doing the dissolving). Saltwater is a solution: salt is the solute, water is the solvent. Most common solutions use water as the solvent (aqueous solutions). The solute dissolves at the molecular or ionic level—individual particles of solute are evenly distributed throughout the solvent, creating a homogeneous mixture (uniform appearance and properties everywhere).

#### Dissolution Process
When an ionic compound like NaCl dissolves in water, the polar water molecules surround and separate the ions. Hydration occurs—water molecules orient around each ion with partial charges facing the opposite charge on the ion. This breaks the crystal lattice and allows ions to move freely throughout the solution.

#### Saturated vs. Unsaturated Solutions
An unsaturated solution has less solute than could dissolve at that temperature. Adding more solute will cause it to dissolve. A saturated solution contains the maximum amount of solute at a given temperature—if you add more solute, it won't dissolve; it settles as a precipitate or solid.

#### Factors Affecting Solubility
- **Temperature**: Most salts dissolve more as temperature increases (endothermic dissolution). Gases dissolve less at higher temperatures.
- **Pressure**: Affects gas solubility significantly (Henry's Law); affects liquids and solids minimally.
- **Polarity**: "Like dissolves like"—polar solvents dissolve polar and ionic solutes; nonpolar solvents dissolve nonpolar solutes.
