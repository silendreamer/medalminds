#### Titration Setup
A known concentration (titrant, in a burette) is slowly added to an unknown concentration solution (analyte, in a flask) until the reaction is complete (equivalence point), indicated by a color change (indicator). At equivalence point: moles of titrant = moles of analyte (from stoichiometry).

#### Acid-Base Titration Example
Titrate 20.0 mL of unknown HCl with 0.100 M NaOH. The indicator (phenolphthalein) turns pink at equivalence point when 18.5 mL of NaOH is added. Moles of NaOH = 0.0185 L × 0.100 M = 0.00185 mol. From HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O (1:1 ratio), moles of HCl = 0.00185 mol. Molarity of HCl = 0.00185 mol / 0.0200 L = 0.0925 M.

#### Polyprotic Acid Titration
If titrating a diprotic acid (like H₂SO₄), the stoichiometry is different. H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O. If 25 mL of 0.50 M H₂SO₄ requires 50 mL of NaOH: (0.50 M)(25 mL) × (2 mol NaOH / 1 mol H₂SO₄) = 25 mmol NaOH needed. Molarity of NaOH = 25 mmol / 50 mL = 0.50 M. Check: (0.50 M)(50 mL) = 25 mmol ✓.

#### Standardization
Many reagents (like HCl) aren't exactly the molarity printed on the bottle due to evaporation, impurities, etc. To standardize: titrate a primary standard (a pure, known compound) of the reagent's counterpart. Example: titrate NaOH with pure oxalic acid (H₂C₂O₄) to determine NaOH's exact molarity.

#### Review Questions
1. In an acid-base titration, 25.0 mL of unknown HNO₃ requires 30.0 mL of 0.125 M KOH. What is the molarity of HNO₃?
2. A 50 mL sample of vinegar (acetic acid) requires 40 mL of 0.50 M NaOH to neutralize. What is the molarity of acetic acid?
3. For a diprotic acid (H₂A), 20 mL of unknown solution requires 35 mL of 0.20 M NaOH. Find the molarity of H₂A.
