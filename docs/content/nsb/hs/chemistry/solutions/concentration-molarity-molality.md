#### Molarity
**Molarity** (symbol **M**) measures the amount of solute per unit volume of solution: **M = mol solute / L solution**. It is the most common concentration unit in chemistry and is used directly in stoichiometry of reactions in solution. Because volume changes with temperature, molarity is temperature-dependent. A 1 M solution of NaCl contains 1 mole of NaCl dissolved in enough water to make exactly 1 liter of solution — not 1 liter of water plus NaCl.

#### Molality
**Molality** (symbol **m**) measures the amount of solute per unit mass of solvent: **m = mol solute / kg solvent**. Unlike molarity, molality is temperature-independent because mass does not change with temperature. Molality is the required concentration unit for colligative property calculations (boiling-point elevation, freezing-point depression) because those phenomena depend on the ratio of solute particles to solvent molecules, not on solution volume. Note the distinction: molality uses kilograms of **solvent**, not of solution.

#### Converting Between Molarity and Molality
To convert molarity to molality (or vice versa), you need the solution density. Given density d (g/mL) and molarity M: mass of 1 L solution = 1000d grams; mass of solute = M × molar mass (g); mass of solvent = (1000d − M × MM) grams = that many / 1000 kg; therefore **m = M / (d − M × MM/1000)**. For dilute aqueous solutions (d ≈ 1.00 g/mL, solute mass negligible), M ≈ m numerically, but they are conceptually distinct.

#### Dilution
When a concentrated **stock solution** is diluted with solvent, the moles of solute remain constant while the volume increases. This yields the **dilution equation**: **M1V1 = M2V2**, where M1 and V1 are the initial molarity and volume, and M2 and V2 are the final molarity and volume. Units for V must be consistent (both in mL or both in L). For example, to prepare 500 mL of 0.10 M HCl from 6.0 M stock: V1 = (0.10 × 500) / 6.0 = 8.3 mL of stock, diluted to 500 mL total.

#### Worked Strategy for Concentration Problems
1. Identify the given quantity (mass, moles, or volume of solute) and what type of concentration is asked for.
2. Convert mass to moles using molar mass when needed.
3. Apply **M = mol / L** (solution volume) or **m = mol / kg** (solvent mass).
4. For dilutions, apply M1V1 = M2V2 after confirming units match.
5. Check reasonableness: diluting always decreases molarity; switching from M to m requires solution density.

#### Review Questions
1. How many moles of glucose (MM = 180 g/mol) are in 250 mL of a 0.40 M solution? What mass of glucose is this?
2. A solution is prepared by dissolving 58.5 g of NaCl (MM = 58.5 g/mol) in 500 g of water. Calculate the molality of the solution.
3. What volume of 12 M HCl stock solution is needed to prepare 2.0 L of 0.30 M HCl?
