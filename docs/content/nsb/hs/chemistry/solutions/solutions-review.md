#### Multi-Step Concentration Problem
Prepare a dilute solution and calculate its concentration. Step 1: You have 5.0 M NaCl. Step 2: Dilute 50 mL to 250 mL with water. Step 3: Final molarity = (5.0 M)(50 mL) / (250 mL) = 1.0 M. Step 4: This 1.0 M solution can be used in titrations or other applications.

#### Titration Problem
Unknown HCl is titrated with 0.10 M NaOH. 30 mL of HCl requires 25 mL NaOH. Molarity of HCl = (0.10 M × 25 mL) / 30 mL = 0.083 M.

#### Mixed Units Problem
A 0.50 M NaCl solution is diluted by adding 200 mL of water to 100 mL of original solution. New concentration: (0.50 M)(100 mL) / (300 mL) = 0.167 M. Also express as mass percent if density ≈ 1.00 g/mL: mass of NaCl = 0.167 mol × 58.5 g/mol = 9.8 g (in 300 mL). Mass percent = (9.8 g / 300 g) × 100% = 3.3%.

#### Review Questions
1. 2.0 M H₂SO₄ (100 mL) is diluted to 500 mL. What is the new concentration?
2. In a titration, 15 mL of unknown Ba(OH)₂ requires 30 mL of 0.20 M HCl. Find the molarity of Ba(OH)₂.
3. Calculate the mass percent of 0.75 M KBr in a solution with density 1.05 g/mL.
