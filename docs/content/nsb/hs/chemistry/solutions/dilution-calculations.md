#### Dilution Equation
When you add solvent to a solution, the number of moles of solute stays the same, but volume increases. moles = M × V (constant). So M₁V₁ = M₂V₂. If you dilute a 2.0 M solution from 100 mL to 500 mL: (2.0 M)(100 mL) = (M₂)(500 mL), so M₂ = 0.40 M.

#### Worked Example: Diluting Concentrated Acid
You need 500 mL of 0.10 M HCl. You have 12 M HCl (concentrated). Use the dilution equation: (12 M)(V₁) = (0.10 M)(500 mL), so V₁ = 4.2 mL. Dilute 4.2 mL of concentrated HCl to 500 mL with water (slowly, with care—mixing concentrated acid is exothermic!).

#### Serial Dilutions
Preparing very dilute solutions often requires serial dilutions. Example: prepare 10⁻⁶ M solution from 1 M stock. First dilution: 1:100 → 0.01 M. Second dilution: take 0.01 M and dilute 1:100 → 10⁻⁴ M. Third dilution: 1:100 → 10⁻⁶ M. Each step is a factor of 100 dilution.

#### Mixing Solutions of Different Concentrations
If you mix V₁ liters of M₁ solution with V₂ liters of M₂ solution, the final concentration is: M_final = (M₁V₁ + M₂V₂) / (V₁ + V₂). Example: Mix 200 mL of 0.50 M NaCl with 300 mL of 0.20 M NaCl. M_final = (0.50 × 0.2 + 0.20 × 0.3) / 0.5 = (0.10 + 0.06) / 0.5 = 0.32 M.
