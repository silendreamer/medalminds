#### Must-Memorize Conversion Values
1. 1 mol = 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number)
2. 1 mol ideal gas at STP = 22.4 L (0°C, 1 atm)
3. 1 cal = 4.184 J; 1 kcal = 4184 J
4. 1 atm = 760 mmHg = 101,325 Pa
5. 1 L = 1000 mL; 1 mL = 1 cm³
6. Molar mass of H₂O = 18.02 g/mol; NaCl = 58.44 g/mol; CO₂ = 44.01 g/mol; H₂ = 2.016 g/mol
7. K = °C + 273.15

#### Quick Calculation Patterns
- Grams → moles: divide by molar mass
- Moles → grams: multiply by molar mass
- Moles → molecules: multiply by 6.022 × 10²³
- Moles of gas → liters at STP: multiply by 22.4

#### Practice Toss-Up Stems
1. "For 10 points — how many grams are in 2.00 moles of CO₂?" → **88.0 g** (2.00 × 44.01)
2. "For 10 points — what volume does 0.500 mol of an ideal gas occupy at STP?" → **11.2 L** (0.500 × 22.4)
3. "For 10 points — how many molecules are in 0.100 mol of glucose?" → **6.02 × 10²² molecules**

#### Review Questions
1. How many atoms of oxygen are in 2.00 mol of CO₂?
2. What mass of NaCl contains the same number of formula units as 18.0 g of H₂O?
3. A 2.24-L sample of gas at STP — how many moles is this?
