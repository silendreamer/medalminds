#### Preparing a Molar Solution
A lab procedure calls for 250 mL of 0.500 M NaCl. How many grams of NaCl do you weigh out?

Step 1: Find moles needed: 0.500 mol/L × 0.250 L = 0.125 mol NaCl
Step 2: Convert moles to grams: 0.125 mol × 58.44 g/mol = **7.31 g NaCl**

Procedure: weigh 7.31 g, dissolve in some distilled water, transfer to a 250-mL volumetric flask, add water to the calibration mark.

#### Dilution Calculation
A student has 2.0 M HCl stock solution. How many mL of stock must be diluted to 500 mL to make 0.10 M HCl?

Using C₁V₁ = C₂V₂: (2.0 M)(V₁) = (0.10 M)(500 mL) → V₁ = 25 mL

Always add acid to water, not water to acid (exothermic — dilution in concentrated acid can splatter).

#### Density as a Conversion Factor
A flask contains 150.0 mL of ethanol (density = 0.789 g/mL). What is the mass? 150.0 mL × 0.789 g/mL = **118.4 g**. Density links volume and mass — use it as a conversion factor like any other.

#### Converting Between Energy Units
A reaction releases 847 J. Convert to calories (1 cal = 4.184 J):
847 J × (1 cal / 4.184 J) = **202.4 cal = 0.2024 kcal**

#### Review Questions
1. You need 100 mL of 0.250 M CaCl₂. What mass of CaCl₂ (molar mass = 111.1 g/mol) do you dissolve?
2. How many mL of 6.0 M H₂SO₄ do you need to prepare 1.0 L of 0.30 M H₂SO₄?
3. A reaction produces 12,500 J of heat. Convert to (a) calories, (b) kilocalories, (c) kilojoules.
