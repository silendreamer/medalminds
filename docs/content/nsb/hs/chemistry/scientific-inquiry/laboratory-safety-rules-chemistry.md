#### Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)
Chemistry lab safety begins with PPE. **Safety goggles** (not glasses) protect against chemical splash — a corrosive drop to the eye can cause permanent damage. **Lab coat or apron** protects skin and clothing from spills. **Closed-toe shoes** protect feet from dropped glassware or chemical spills. **Gloves** protect hands — but not all gloves protect against all chemicals (nitrile vs. latex vs. neoprene have different chemical resistances). Long hair must be tied back near open flames.

#### Hazard Communication: GHS and SDS
The Globally Harmonized System (GHS) standardizes chemical hazard communication worldwide. Every chemical container must have: product name, hazard pictograms (skull/crossbones = toxic; flame = flammable; exclamation = irritant; corrosive = burns), signal word (DANGER or WARNING), and hazard/precautionary statements. Safety Data Sheets (SDS, formerly MSDS) give complete hazard, handling, storage, and first-aid information for each chemical.

#### Handling Hazardous Chemicals
Acids: always add acid to water (not water to acid) — mixing is exothermic; adding water to concentrated acid can cause violent spattering. Never pipette by mouth. Use a fume hood for volatile organics, concentrated acids, and toxic vapors. Never work alone in a chemistry lab. Never taste or smell chemicals directly.

#### Emergency Procedures
Know the location and operation of: eyewash station (flush eyes for 15 minutes for chemical exposure), safety shower (for large spills on skin/clothing), fire extinguisher (CO₂ extinguisher for chemical fires — not water), fire blanket (for clothing fires — stop, drop, roll or wrap), and emergency exits. Chemical spills: contain, neutralize (acid spill → sodium bicarbonate; base spill → dilute acid), and clean up with appropriate materials.

#### Science Bowl Safety Facts
Science Bowl may ask: "What is added to water when mixing concentrated acid?" (acid), or "What system labels chemical hazards with pictograms?" (GHS), or "For how long should you flush eyes after chemical exposure?" (15 minutes).

#### Review Questions
1. Why must you add acid to water rather than water to acid when preparing dilute solutions?
2. A student spills 5 mL of HCl (1 M) on the lab bench. What are the steps to respond safely?
3. What is the purpose of a Safety Data Sheet (SDS)?
