#### Top Bowl-Ready Safety Facts
1. Add acid to water — never water to acid
2. Eye wash station: flush for 15 minutes after chemical eye contact
3. Safety goggles (not glasses) required in chemistry labs
4. GHS = Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals
5. SDS (Safety Data Sheet) replaced MSDS (Material Safety Data Sheet)
6. NFPA diamond: Blue = health, Red = flammability, Yellow = reactivity, White = special hazards
7. Fume hood: used for volatile, toxic, or flammable chemicals
8. CO₂ extinguisher for chemical lab fires; water extinguisher is inappropriate
9. Carcinogen = cancer-causing; teratogen = causes birth defects; mutagen = causes DNA mutations
10. Flammable: flash point < 60°C; combustible: flash point 60–93°C

#### Pictogram Identification
- Skull and crossbones → acutely toxic
- Flame → flammable or pyrophoric
- Corrosion → causes skin burns / metal corrosion
- Exclamation mark → irritant, sensitizer, or harmful
- Health hazard (silhouette) → carcinogen, reproductive toxin, etc.
- Oxidizer → fire hazard (circle with flame)

#### Practice Toss-Up Stems
1. "For 10 points — when mixing concentrated sulfuric acid with water, what must be added to what, and why?" → **Acid added to water; because the exothermic reaction can cause spattering if water is added to acid**
2. "For 10 points — what is the minimum time you should flush your eyes after a chemical splash?" → **15 minutes**
3. "For 10 points — what system standardizes chemical hazard communication globally using pictograms?" → **GHS (Globally Harmonized System)**

#### Review Questions
1. A chemical's SDS indicates a flash point of −40°C. What does this mean for storage?
2. What is the difference between a carcinogen and a teratogen?
3. A student wants to smell an unknown chemical to identify it. What is the correct technique (if any)?
