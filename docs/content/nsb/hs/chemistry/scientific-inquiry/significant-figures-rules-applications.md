#### What Significant Figures Represent
Significant figures (sig figs) communicate measurement precision. Every reported digit must be meaningful — either measured directly or estimated with confidence. Reporting "12.345678 g" from a balance that only reads to 0.01 g is meaningless false precision. The rules exist to ensure your answer reflects what you actually measured.

#### Counting Significant Figures: The Rules
1. All nonzero digits are significant: 5.23 g → 3 sig figs
2. Zeros between nonzeros are significant: 5.03 g → 3 sig figs; 1001 g → 4 sig figs
3. Leading zeros (before first nonzero) are NOT significant: 0.0023 → 2 sig figs
4. Trailing zeros AFTER a decimal point ARE significant: 5.30 → 3 sig figs; 100. → 3 sig figs
5. Trailing zeros WITHOUT a decimal point are ambiguous: 1200 could be 2, 3, or 4 sig figs → use scientific notation (1.2 × 10³ = 2 sig figs; 1.200 × 10³ = 4 sig figs)

#### Multiplication and Division
Round the answer to the same number of significant figures as the measurement with the FEWEST sig figs. Example: 4.52 cm × 3.1 cm = 14.012 cm² → round to 2 sig figs → **14 cm²** (because 3.1 has only 2 sig figs).

#### Addition and Subtraction
Round the answer to the same number of DECIMAL PLACES as the measurement with the fewest decimal places. Example: 12.11 g + 18.0 g + 1.013 g = 31.123 g → round to 1 decimal place → **31.1 g** (because 18.0 has only 1 decimal place).

#### Exact Numbers
Exact numbers (counted quantities, defined conversions) have infinite sig figs and don't limit your answer. The number 12 in "12 eggs" is exact. The conversion 1 inch = 2.54 cm is exact. Only measured values with uncertainty limit sig figs in a calculation.

2. Calculate and round to proper sig figs: 6.021 × 0.52 = ?
