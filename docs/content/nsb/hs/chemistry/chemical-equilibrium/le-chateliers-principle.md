#### Le Chatelier's Principle Defined
**Le Chatelier's principle** states that if a system at **chemical equilibrium** is subjected to an external **stress**, the system will shift in the direction that partially counteracts that stress and re-establishes equilibrium. Stresses include changes in concentration, pressure (volume), and temperature.

#### Concentration Shifts
Adding a **reactant** or removing a **product** drives the equilibrium to the right (toward products), increasing the forward reaction rate until a new equilibrium is reached. Conversely, adding a product or removing a reactant shifts equilibrium to the left. The **equilibrium constant K** does not change with concentration; only the position of equilibrium shifts.

#### Pressure and Volume Effects
For gaseous reactions, decreasing the volume increases pressure, shifting equilibrium toward the side with fewer moles of gas. For example, in N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g), decreasing volume favors NH3 production (2 moles vs. 4 moles). Adding an **inert gas** at constant volume does not shift equilibrium because the partial pressures of reactants and products are unchanged.

#### Temperature Effects
Temperature changes alter the value of **K** itself. For an **endothermic reaction** (ΔH > 0), heat can be treated as a reactant; raising temperature shifts equilibrium toward products and increases K. For an **exothermic reaction** (ΔH < 0), heat is a product; raising temperature shifts equilibrium toward reactants and decreases K. Cooling an exothermic system increases K and drives product formation.

#### Catalysts and Equilibrium
A **catalyst** lowers the activation energy for both the forward and reverse reactions equally. It speeds up the rate at which equilibrium is reached but does **not** change the equilibrium position or the value of K. Catalysts are critical in industrial processes (e.g., the Haber process for NH3 synthesis) where rapid attainment of equilibrium is economically important.

#### Review Questions
1. For the reaction 2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g) (ΔH = −198 kJ), predict the direction of shift when the temperature is increased and explain why K changes.
2. A sealed container holds CO(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ CH4(g) + H2O(g). If the volume is halved, which direction does equilibrium shift, and why?
3. Explain why adding a catalyst to an equilibrium system does not change the value of K but does affect the time required to reach equilibrium.
