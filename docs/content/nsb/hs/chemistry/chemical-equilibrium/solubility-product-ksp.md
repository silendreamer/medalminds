#### The Solubility Product Constant Ksp
The **solubility product constant (Ksp)** is the equilibrium constant for the dissolution of a sparingly soluble ionic compound. For a generic salt MₐXb(s) ⇌ a M^(b+)(aq) + b X^(a−)(aq), the expression is Ksp = [M^(b+)]^a [X^(a−)]^b. Pure solids are excluded from the expression. A smaller Ksp indicates lower solubility; comparing Ksp values is only straightforward for salts that dissolve with the same number of ions.

#### Calculating Molar Solubility
**Molar solubility (s)** is the number of moles of solute that dissolve per liter of solution. To find s, set up an ICE table with the dissolution equilibrium:

- For AgCl(s) ⇌ Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq), Ksp = [Ag⁺][Cl⁻] = s · s = s². If Ksp = 1.8 × 10^−10, then s = √(1.8 × 10^−10) ≈ 1.34 × 10^−5 M.
- For Ag2CrO4(s) ⇌ 2Ag⁺(aq) + CrO4^2−(aq), Ksp = (2s)^2(s) = 4s³. Solve for s = (Ksp/4)^(1/3).

Always account for stoichiometric coefficients when writing equilibrium concentrations in terms of s.

#### Predicting Precipitation: Ion Product Q vs. Ksp
The **ion product Q** (also called the reaction quotient) uses the same expression as Ksp but with current ion concentrations. If Q > Ksp, the solution is **supersaturated** and a precipitate will form. If Q < Ksp, the solution is **unsaturated** and more solid can dissolve. If Q = Ksp, the solution is at equilibrium (saturated). This comparison is essential for predicting whether mixing two solutions will produce a precipitate.

#### The Common-Ion Effect
The **common-ion effect** is a direct application of Le Chatelier's principle: adding a soluble salt that supplies an ion already present in a sparingly soluble salt's equilibrium suppresses dissolution and decreases molar solubility. For example, dissolving AgCl in a solution containing 0.10 M NaCl means [Cl⁻] starts at 0.10 M rather than 0, so s = Ksp / 0.10 = 1.8 × 10^−9 M — roughly 10,000 times less soluble than in pure water. The common-ion effect is exploited in gravimetric analysis to achieve more complete precipitation.

#### Review Questions
1. Calculate the molar solubility of Ca3(PO4)2 (Ksp = 2.07 × 10^−33) in pure water, expressing your answer in terms of s and the Ksp expression before solving.
2. A student mixes 50.0 mL of 2.0 × 10^−4 M BaCl2 with 50.0 mL of 2.0 × 10^−4 M Na2SO4. Given Ksp(BaSO4) = 1.1 × 10^−10, calculate Q and predict whether a precipitate forms.
3. Explain quantitatively why the molar solubility of PbI2 (Ksp = 9.8 × 10^−9) decreases when dissolved in 0.10 M KI solution compared to pure water.
