#### Chemical Equilibrium and the Equilibrium Constant

**Chemical equilibrium** is the dynamic state in which the forward and reverse reaction rates are equal, so the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant over time. For a general reaction aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, the **equilibrium constant expression** is:

Kc = [C]^c [D]^d / ([A]^a [B]^b)

where concentrations are in mol/L and each is raised to its stoichiometric coefficient. Pure solids and pure liquids are **omitted** from the expression because their "concentrations" are constant and absorbed into Kc.

#### Kc vs. Kp

When reactants and products are gases, the equilibrium constant can also be expressed in terms of **partial pressures**:

Kp = (P_C)^c (P_D)^d / ((P_A)^a (P_B)^b)

Kc and Kp are related by: Kp = Kc (RT)^(Δn_gas), where R = 0.08206 L·atm/(mol·K), T is temperature in Kelvin, and **Δn_gas** = (moles of gaseous products) - (moles of gaseous reactants). When Δn_gas = 0, Kp = Kc.

#### Interpreting the Magnitude of Keq

The value of Keq quantitatively describes the **equilibrium position**:

- **Keq >> 1** (e.g., >10^3): equilibrium lies far to the right; products predominate.
- **Keq << 1** (e.g., <10^-3): equilibrium lies far to the left; reactants predominate.
- **Keq ≈ 1**: significant amounts of both reactants and products are present at equilibrium.

Keq is **temperature-dependent** but is independent of pressure, volume, or the initial concentrations used to reach equilibrium. Changing these conditions shifts concentrations (Le Chatelier) but does not change Keq itself (unless T changes).

#### The Reaction Quotient Q

The **reaction quotient Q** has the same algebraic form as Keq but uses current (non-equilibrium) concentrations. Comparing Q to Keq predicts the direction of reaction:

- Q < Keq → reaction proceeds forward (more products form).
- Q > Keq → reaction proceeds in reverse (more reactants form).
- Q = Keq → system is at equilibrium.

#### Review Questions

1. Write the equilibrium-constant expression Kc for the reaction: N2(g) + 3 H2(g) ⇌ 2 NH3(g), and state whether products or reactants predominate if Kc = 6.0 × 10^5 at 500 K.
2. For the reaction CO(g) + 3 H2(g) ⇌ CH4(g) + H2O(g), Δn_gas = -2. If Kc = 3.9 at 900 K, calculate Kp using Kp = Kc(RT)^(Δn_gas).
3. A reaction vessel initially contains [N2] = 0.50 M, [H2] = 1.50 M, and [NH3] = 0.20 M. Given Kc = 6.0 × 10^5, calculate Q and determine which direction the reaction will proceed.
