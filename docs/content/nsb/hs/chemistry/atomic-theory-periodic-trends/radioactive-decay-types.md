#### Alpha Decay (α)
Alpha particles = ⁴₂He (helium-4 nucleus: 2 protons, 2 neutrons). Alpha decay: A decreases by 4, Z decreases by 2.
Example: ²³⁸₉₂U → ⁴₂He + ²³⁴₉₀Th
Alpha particles have high mass and charge, travel only a few centimeters in air, and are stopped by paper or skin (external exposure low risk; internal ingestion very dangerous).

#### Beta Decay (β⁻)
Beta particles = high-energy electrons (⁰₋₁e). In beta decay, a neutron converts to a proton + electron (+ antineutrino): n → p + e⁻ + ν̄. A stays same, Z increases by 1.
Example: ¹⁴₆C → ¹⁴₇N + ⁰₋₁e
Beta particles penetrate farther than alpha — stopped by a few mm of aluminum.

#### Gamma Radiation (γ)
Gamma rays = high-energy electromagnetic radiation (no particle, no mass, no charge). Often accompany alpha or beta decay as nucleus releases excess energy. A and Z unchanged. Gamma rays are the most penetrating — require several cm of lead or meters of concrete to block.

#### Nuclear Equation Balancing
In any nuclear equation: sum of mass numbers on each side must be equal, and sum of atomic numbers on each side must be equal. Check: ²³⁸₉₂U → ⁴₂He + ²³⁴₉₀Th: mass 238 = 4 + 234 ✓; atomic number 92 = 2 + 90 ✓.

#### Review Questions
1. Write the nuclear equation for alpha decay of ²²⁶₈₈Ra.
2. Rank alpha, beta, and gamma radiation from least to most penetrating.
3. In beta decay, what subatomic particle is converted to what other particles?
