#### First Ionization Energy (IE₁)
IE₁ is the energy required to remove the outermost electron from a gaseous neutral atom: X(g) → X⁺(g) + e⁻. Units: kJ/mol or eV. IE₁ generally increases across a period (stronger nuclear pull on valence electrons) and decreases down a group (valence electrons farther, more shielded).

#### Exceptions in IE₁
Two notable exceptions in period 2: (1) Be (1s²2s²) → B (1s²2s²2p¹): IE₁ of B is lower because 2p electrons are higher in energy and easier to remove than 2s. (2) N (2p³) → O (2p⁴): IE₁ of O is lower because in oxygen, one 2p orbital is doubly occupied — electron-electron repulsion makes that electron easier to remove.

#### Successive Ionization Energies
IE₂ > IE₁ (removing a second electron from an already positive ion is harder). When a large jump occurs between IEₙ and IEₙ₊₁, the (n+1)th electron is from an inner shell — this reveals the element's group. Na: IE₁ = 496 kJ/mol, IE₂ = 4,562 kJ/mol → big jump after 1 → 1 valence electron → Group 1.

#### Electronegativity
Electronegativity is the ability of a bonded atom to attract electrons toward itself (Pauling scale). F is the most electronegative element (4.0). Trend: increases across period, decreases down group. Noble gases typically not assigned values. Used to predict bond polarity: |ΔEN| > 1.7 → ionic; 0.4–1.7 → polar covalent; < 0.4 → nonpolar covalent.

#### Review Questions
1. Why is the first ionization energy of oxygen lower than that of nitrogen, despite oxygen having a higher atomic number?
2. An element has ionization energies: IE₁ = 578, IE₂ = 1,817, IE₃ = 2,745, IE₄ = 11,578 kJ/mol. Which group does it belong to?
3. Arrange F, Cl, Br, I in order of decreasing electronegativity.
