#### Toss-Up Stems
"For 10 points, this type of intermolecular force, present in all substances, results from temporary induced dipoles and increases in strength with increasing molecular mass and polarizability." → **London dispersion forces**

"For 10 points, water's anomalously high boiling point, surface tension, and viscosity relative to its small molecular size are attributed to this specific type of intermolecular force." → **hydrogen bonding**

"For 10 points, the requirement for hydrogen bonding is that hydrogen must be directly bonded to one of three highly electronegative elements — name all three." → **fluorine, oxygen, and nitrogen (F, O, N)**

#### Key Numbers
- Water BP: 100°C (vs. predicted ~−80°C from group 16 trend)
- HF BP: 19.5°C (vs. HCl: −85°C despite HF being lighter)
- NH₃ BP: −33°C (vs. PH₃: −87°C despite PH₃ being heavier)
- H-bond strength: 5–30 kJ/mol; typical covalent bond: 100–400 kJ/mol

#### Review Questions
1. What three elements must hydrogen be bonded to in order for hydrogen bonding to occur?
2. A question describes "the force responsible for water's high surface tension and ability to form droplets." What IMF is being described?
3. Rank these by decreasing boiling point: CH₄, NH₃, HF, H₂O. Explain the order.
