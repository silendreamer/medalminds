#### IMF Identification Drill
For each substance, identify all IMFs present (from weakest to strongest applicable):
- I₂: LDF only (nonpolar, large)
- HCl: LDF + dipole-dipole
- CH₃OH (methanol): LDF + dipole-dipole + H-bonding
- N₂: LDF only
- DNA: LDF + dipole-dipole + H-bonding (between bases)
- NaCl in water: ion-dipole (special IMF between ions and polar solvent)

#### Property Ranking
Rank by increasing boiling point: N₂, H₂O, HCl, CH₃OH.
- N₂ (LDF only, very small): −196°C
- HCl (LDF + dipole-dipole): −85°C
- CH₃OH (LDF + H-bonding): 65°C
- H₂O (LDF + strong H-bonding, small molecule so dense H-bond network): 100°C

#### Review Questions
1. Explain why NH₃ has a higher boiling point than PH₃ despite being lighter.
2. Identify the IMFs in ethylene glycol (HOCH₂CH₂OH) and explain why it is used as antifreeze.
3. Place in order of increasing vapor pressure at 25°C: H₂O, diethyl ether, ethanol. Explain.
