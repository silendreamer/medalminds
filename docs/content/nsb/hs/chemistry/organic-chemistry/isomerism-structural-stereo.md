#### Structural (Constitutional) Isomers
**Structural isomers** share a molecular formula but differ in the connectivity of their atoms. For example, C4H10 has two structural isomers: **butane** (straight chain: CH3CH2CH2CH3) and **2-methylpropane** (isobutane: (CH3)3CH). Structural isomers can differ in chain branching, ring vs. chain form, or in the position of a functional group — for instance, 1-propanol (CH3CH2CH2OH) vs. 2-propanol (CH3CH(OH)CH3) are positional isomers. Different connectivity means different physical and chemical properties.

#### Stereoisomers: Same Connectivity, Different Arrangement in Space
**Stereoisomers** have identical connectivity but atoms arranged differently in three-dimensional space. They are divided into two categories: **geometric (cis-trans) isomers** and **optical isomers (enantiomers)**.

#### Cis-Trans (Geometric) Isomerism
Cis-trans isomerism arises when rotation about a bond is restricted — most commonly across a **C=C double bond** or in a ring. Each carbon of the double bond must bear two different substituents. In **cis** isomers, identical (or higher-priority) groups are on the **same side** of the double bond; in **trans** isomers they are on **opposite sides**. Example: cis-but-2-ene has both methyl groups on the same side; trans-but-2-ene has them on opposite sides. These isomers differ in boiling point, polarity, and biological activity. The systematic alternative uses **(Z)/(E)** designators (from German *zusammen/entgegen*) based on Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules.

#### Optical Isomerism and Chirality
A carbon bearing **four different substituents** is a **chiral center** (stereocenter). Such molecules are non-superimposable mirror images called **enantiomers**. A pair of enantiomers rotates plane-polarized light in equal but opposite directions — one is **(+) dextrorotatory**, the other **(-) levorotatory**. An equal mixture of enantiomers is a **racemic mixture** and is optically inactive. Molecules with two stereocenters can also have **diastereomers** — stereoisomers that are not mirror images — such as meso compounds, where internal symmetry makes the molecule achiral despite having stereocenters.

2. Explain why but-1-ene does NOT exhibit cis-trans isomerism but but-2-ene does.
3. Lactic acid (2-hydroxypropanoic acid) has one chiral center. Describe its two enantiomers, explain how a polarimeter distinguishes them, and define a racemic mixture.
