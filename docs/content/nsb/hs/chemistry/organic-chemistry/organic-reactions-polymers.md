#### Substitution Reactions
In a **substitution reaction**, one atom or group replaces another on a carbon skeleton. **Halogenation** of alkanes is a classic example: a hydrogen is replaced by a halogen via a free-radical chain mechanism (initiation, propagation, termination). **Nucleophilic substitution** (S_N1 and S_N2) at sp3 carbons involves a nucleophile displacing a leaving group; S_N2 proceeds through a single bimolecular step with inversion of configuration, while S_N1 proceeds through a carbocation intermediate.

#### Addition Reactions
**Addition reactions** occur across double or triple bonds, converting pi bonds to sigma bonds. Common examples include **hydrogenation** (H2/catalyst), **halogenation** (Br2 adds across a double bond, used as a bromine-water test), **hydrohalogenation** (follows **Markovnikov's rule**: H adds to the less-substituted carbon), and **hydration** (H2O adds in the presence of acid). Addition reactions are characteristic of **alkenes** and **alkynes**.

#### Monomers and Polymerization Overview
A **monomer** is a small, repeating molecular unit that links together to form a **polymer**. The process of joining monomers is called **polymerization**. The two major classes — **addition polymerization** and **condensation polymerization** — differ in mechanism and in whether a small molecule is released as a byproduct.

#### Addition Polymerization
In **addition polymerization** (also called chain-growth polymerization), alkene monomers join end-to-end by opening their C=C double bonds without losing any atoms. The repeat unit is identical to the monomer. Examples include **polyethylene** (from ethylene, CH2=CH2), **polypropylene** (from propylene), **polystyrene** (from styrene), and **PVC** (from vinyl chloride). The degree of polymerization and molecular-weight distribution depend on initiator concentration and temperature.

#### Condensation Polymerization
In **condensation polymerization** (step-growth), monomers with two functional groups react and expel a small molecule — usually **water** or **HCl** — with each bond formed. Monomers must carry two complementary reactive groups (e.g., diol + diacid, or diamine + diacid). **Nylon** (polyamide) forms from a diamine and a diacid; **polyester** (e.g., PET) forms from a diol and a diacid. The **amide bond** (–CO–NH–) and **ester bond** (–CO–O–) are the characteristic linkages in these two families.
