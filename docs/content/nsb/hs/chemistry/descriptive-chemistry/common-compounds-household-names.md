#### Acids and Bases in Common Use

Many household and industrial chemicals are known by **trivial (common) names** rather than systematic IUPAC names. **Muriatic acid** is hydrochloric acid (HCl), used in masonry cleaning. **Oil of vitriol** is concentrated sulfuric acid (H2SO4). **Caustic soda** is sodium hydroxide (NaOH), a drain cleaner. **Caustic potash** is potassium hydroxide (KOH). **Baking soda** is **sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3)**; **washing soda** is **sodium carbonate (Na2CO3)**; and **lye** refers broadly to NaOH or KOH solutions.

#### Salts and Oxides

**Table salt** is sodium chloride (NaCl). **Epsom salt** is hydrated magnesium sulfate (MgSO4·7H2O), used medicinally and in gardening. **Gypsum** is calcium sulfate dihydrate (CaSO4·2H2O), the basis of plaster of Paris (CaSO4·0.5H2O) when partially dehydrated. **Quicklime** is calcium oxide (CaO), produced by heating limestone (CaCO3); adding water gives **slaked lime**, Ca(OH)2. **Dry ice** is solid carbon dioxide (CO2). **Baking powder** contains NaHCO3 plus an acid salt such as cream of tartar (potassium hydrogen tartrate, KHC4H4O6).

#### Hydrogen Compounds and Organic Trivials

**Laughing gas** is dinitrogen monoxide (N2O), used as an anesthetic and propellant. **Grain alcohol** (ethanol, C2H5OH) and **wood alcohol** (methanol, CH3OH) are distinguished by toxicity — methanol is lethal in small doses. **Acetone** (propanone, (CH3)2CO) is a common solvent. **Acetic acid (CH3COOH)** is the active component of vinegar (~5%). **Formic acid (HCOOH)** gives ant stings their sting. **Urea (CO(NH2)2)** is the main nitrogen waste product excreted by mammals and is used in fertilizers.

#### Water-Related and Atmospheric Compounds

**Heavy water** is deuterium oxide (D2O), used as a neutron moderator in CANDU reactors. **Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2)** is a bleaching agent and antiseptic. **Dry air** is roughly 78% N2, 21% O2, and ~0.04% CO2 by volume. **Ozone (O3)** in the stratosphere absorbs UV radiation; at ground level it is a pollutant. **Freons** (chlorofluorocarbons, CFCs) such as CCl2F2 were used as refrigerants until their ozone-depleting properties led to the Montreal Protocol ban.

#### Review Questions
1. What is the chemical formula and systematic name for the compound commonly called Epsom salt?
2. Distinguish between quicklime and slaked lime: give the formula and the reaction that converts one to the other.
3. Why is methanol (wood alcohol) far more toxic to humans than ethanol (grain alcohol), and what are their respective chemical formulas?
