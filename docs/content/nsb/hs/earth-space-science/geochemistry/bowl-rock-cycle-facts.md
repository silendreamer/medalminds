#### Key Facts
- Granite: intrusive, felsic, coarse-grained (quartz + K-feldspar + mica)
- Basalt: extrusive, mafic, fine-grained (pyroxene + plagioclase) — oceanic crust composition
- Limestone: sedimentary, CaCO₃; fizzes with HCl; can contain fossils; forms karst topography
- Shale: finest-grained clastic sedimentary; fissile; most common sedimentary rock by volume
- Quartzite: metamorphosed sandstone (non-foliated); very hard
- Marble: metamorphosed limestone; crystalline calcite; non-foliated
- Schist: foliated metamorphic; visible mica; medium-grade
- Gneiss: foliated metamorphic; compositional banding; high-grade
- Bowen's Reaction Series: olivine crystallizes first (high T), quartz last (low T)
- Obsidian: volcanic glass (extrusive, fastest cooling, no crystals)

#### Toss-Up Patterns
- "…coarse-grained, felsic, forms in magma chambers…" → granite
- "…fine-grained, mafic, makes up oceanic crust…" → basalt
- "…fizzes with hydrochloric acid, often contains fossils…" → limestone
- "…foliated metamorphic rock with visible mica flakes…" → schist
- "…alternating light and dark bands, highest grade metamorphism…" → gneiss
- "…forms when limestone is metamorphosed…non-foliated…" → marble

#### Review Questions
1. For 10 points — what type of rock forms when magma cools slowly underground, producing large interlocking crystals?
2. A rock sample fizzes when HCl is applied and contains fossil shells. Name the rock.
3. What process transforms sedimentary rocks into metamorphic rocks?
