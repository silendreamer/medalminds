#### Carbon Reservoirs
The major carbon reservoirs (in gigatons of carbon, GtC): lithosphere (rocks, including fossil fuels): ~100,000,000 GtC; ocean (dissolved inorganic carbon, DIC): ~38,000 GtC; terrestrial vegetation and soils: ~2,000 GtC; atmosphere (as CO₂): ~860 GtC (pre-industrial ~590 GtC). The atmosphere is small but highly connected to other reservoirs. Residence time: carbon in the atmosphere has a mean residence time of ~4–5 years (though perturbed CO₂ takes centuries to millennia to fully re-equilibrate because of slow ocean and rock weathering).

#### The Fast Carbon Cycle (Biological)
Photosynthesis: CO₂ + H₂O + light → glucose + O₂ — removes ~123 GtC/year from the atmosphere. Respiration (all organisms): glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O — returns ~120 GtC/year. Net terrestrial uptake ~ 3 GtC/year currently. Ocean air-sea exchange: ~90 GtC/year absorbed, ~88 GtC/year outgassed — oceans absorb ~2.5 GtC/year net. Marine photosynthesis (phytoplankton) fixes carbon in surface waters; the biological pump exports some as sinking organic matter to the deep ocean (sequestration on timescale of centuries to millennia).

#### The Slow Carbon Cycle (Geological)
On timescales of millions of years: volcanic degassing adds CO₂; silicate weathering (Ca/Mg silicates + CO₂ → carbonates + silica) removes CO₂. The Urey reactions: CaSiO₃ + CO₂ → CaCO₃ + SiO₂. This geological thermostat — the Berner-Caldeira feedback — has regulated Earth's climate over geologic time: high CO₂ → warmer → more rain → more weathering → more CO₂ drawdown → cooling. Subduction of carbonate rocks and organic matter releases CO₂ back through volcanism. Over 300 million years, burial of dead organisms produced coal and oil — concentrating carbon underground. Human burning of fossil fuels releases ~10 GtC/year — geological instantaneous but geologically unprecedented in rate.
