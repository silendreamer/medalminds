#### Normal Conditions and the Walker Circulation
Under normal conditions, the western Pacific (near Indonesia) is warmer than the eastern Pacific, creating warm water over Southeast Asia and relatively cool water along South America. Trade winds blow from east to west over the Pacific, driven by a pressure gradient (high pressure in the east, low in the west). These trade winds pile warm water in the western Pacific, creating a warm pool (26–30°C). Cold, nutrient-rich water wells up along South America's coast in the eastern Pacific (cold tongue, 18–20°C). This east-west temperature gradient drives the Walker circulation—air rises over the warm western Pacific (low pressure), moves eastward at altitude, descends over the cool eastern Pacific (high pressure), and returns westward at the surface (trade winds). This circulation supplies cool, upwelled water and supports huge fisheries (Peruvian anchoveta). It also modulates rainfall—the ITCZ is pulled westward, creating wet conditions in Indonesia and dry conditions in the eastern Pacific.

#### El Niño Conditions
During El Niño, the normal state weakens and reverses. Trade winds diminish, reducing the eastward piling of warm water. Warm water spreads eastward into the central and eastern Pacific. Upwelling off Peru weakens, reducing the cold tongue. Sea surface temperature in the eastern Pacific rises by 2–5°C above normal. The atmospheric circulation also weakens—the Walker circulation stalls, pressure gradients flatten, and convection shifts eastward. An El Niño develops over ~6 months and typically persists 9–18 months. The Southern Oscillation Index (SOI), based on pressure difference between Darwin and Tahiti, is negative during El Niño (<-0.5 standard deviations). El Niño events are roughly every 2–7 years (quasi-periodic). The 2015–2016 El Niño was one of the strongest on record, causing global precipitation and temperature anomalies.

#### La Niña Conditions
La Niña is the opposite phase: trade winds strengthen, the warm pool intensifies, the cold tongue deepens, and the Walker circulation intensifies. Sea surface temperatures in the eastern Pacific are 1–3°C colder than normal. Convection shifts westward, creating wetter conditions in Indonesia and drier in the eastern Pacific (and parts of the Americas). SOI is positive (>+0.5). La Niña events typically last 1–3 years. The 2020–2021 La Niña followed the 2015–2016 El Niño, demonstrating the quasi-periodic switching. Back-to-back La Niñas are unusual but do occur (e.g., 2010–2012 La Niña followed the 2009–2010 El Niño).

#### Teleconnections and Global Impacts
ENSO impacts extend globally through "teleconnections"—atmospheric patterns that link tropical forcing to remote regions. During El Niño: the North Pacific westerlies split, deepening low-pressure systems over the northeastern Pacific, bringing mild, wet winter conditions to the Pacific Northwest and warm winters to Canada and the Arctic. The Atlantic is warmer due to weakened trade winds, affecting hurricane season (fewer hurricanes). Africa experiences reduced rainfall (drought risk in East Africa). The Indian monsoon is typically weaker. Australia experiences drier conditions (drought risk in Queensland). During La Niña, these patterns reverse. These predictable patterns allow seasonal forecasting—knowing El Niño is developing, seasonal forecasters can predict drier monsoons 3–6 months ahead, enabling agricultural adaptation.

#### Mechanisms and Debates
ENSO involves both oceanic and atmospheric feedbacks. The Bjerknes feedback: weakened trade winds reduce upwelling, warm the eastern Pacific, reduce the east-west pressure gradient, further weaken trade winds. The thermocline feedback: displaced thermocline responds to wind changes, allowing deepened thermocline (less upwelling) in the east and shoaled thermocline (more upwelling) in the west during El Niño. Debates persist about predictability limits—the "Lorenz limit" (~2 weeks for deterministic weather prediction) doesn't apply to ENSO because of slower oceanic memory. ENSO predictions 6–12 months ahead have useful skill, allowing climate adaptation.
