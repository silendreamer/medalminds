#### Wave Types Summary
| Wave | Motion | Speed | Medium | Damage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P | Compressional (parallel) | Fastest | Solid + liquid | Low (sharp jolt) |
| S | Shear (perpendicular) | ~60% of P | Solid only | Moderate |
| Love | Horizontal shear | Faster surface | Surface | High (sideways) |
| Rayleigh | Elliptical retrograde | Slower surface | Surface | Highest |

#### Earthquake Mechanics Summary
- Elastic rebound theory (Harry Reid, 1906): strain builds up, releases suddenly
- Normal fault = tension (divergent); Reverse/thrust = compression (convergent); Strike-slip = shear (transform)
- San Andreas = right-lateral strike-slip; Himalayas = thrust fault
- Wadati-Benioff zone = inclined plane of deep earthquakes at subduction zone

#### Measurement Summary
- Moment magnitude (Mw) now standard; logarithmic (31.6× energy per step)
- Largest recorded: 1960 Valdivia, Chile, Mw 9.5
- MMI scale = intensity (observed effects, varies with location); Mw = magnitude (one value)
- S-P method: D ≈ 8 × (t_S − t_P) km

#### Hazards Summary
- Liquefaction: saturated loose sediment acts like liquid; worst on reclaimed/deltaic land
- Tsunami: seafloor displacement; 800 km/h in deep water; ~40 m runup (2011 Japan)
- Aftershocks: Omori's Law (rate ∝ 1/time)
- Fire, landslides, disease = major secondary killers

#### Review Questions
1. Put the four seismic wave types in order from fastest to slowest, and identify which causes the most damage.
2. A student claims that two seismograph stations are sufficient to locate an earthquake epicenter. Why is this wrong, and how many are needed?
3. Compare the Modified Mercalli Intensity scale and the moment magnitude scale: what does each measure and why are both useful?
