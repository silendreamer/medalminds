#### Top 20 High-Yield Facts

1. P-waves = primary/compressional; S-waves = secondary/shear
2. S-waves cannot travel through liquids (no shear strength)
3. Love waves = horizontal shear (fastest surface wave); named for A.E.H. Love
4. Rayleigh waves = elliptical retrograde (most destructive); named for Lord Rayleigh
5. Arrival order: P → S → Love → Rayleigh
6. S-P time method: D ≈ 8 × Δt km (approximate)
7. Triangulation needs ≥3 stations to locate epicenter
8. Richter scale: invented by Charles Richter, 1935; logarithmic; saturates at Mw ~7
9. Moment magnitude (Mw): preferred today; 31.6× energy per magnitude step
10. Largest earthquake: 1960 Valdivia, Chile, Mw 9.5
11. Elastic rebound theory: Harry Reid, after 1906 San Francisco earthquake
12. San Andreas Fault: right-lateral strike-slip, transform boundary
13. Wadati-Benioff zone: inclined plane of deep earthquakes in subducting slab
14. Focus/hypocenter: point of rupture underground; epicenter: point on surface above it
15. Shallow focus: 0–70 km; intermediate: 70–300 km; deep: 300–700 km
16. Tsunami speed in deep ocean: ~800 km/h; triggered by Mw ≥ ~7.5
17. 2011 Tōhoku, Japan: Mw 9.0; ~40 m tsunami runup; triggered Fukushima nuclear accident
18. Liquefaction: saturated loose sediment loses shear strength during shaking
19. MMI scale (Modified Mercalli Intensity): I–XII; measures local shaking intensity
20. Omori's Law: aftershock rate decreases as 1/time after mainshock

#### Practice Toss-Up Stems
1. "For 10 points, name the type of seismic wave that moves in an elliptical retrograde pattern and causes the most structural damage." *(Rayleigh wave)*
2. "For 10 points, who proposed elastic rebound theory and in response to what earthquake?" *(Harry Reid; 1906 San Francisco earthquake)*
3. "For 10 points, at what speed do tsunamis travel in the deep ocean?" *(~800 km/h / ~500 mph)*
4. "For 10 points, name the inclined zone of deep earthquakes that traces a subducting plate into the mantle." *(Wadati-Benioff zone)*
5. "For 10 points, what is the largest earthquake ever recorded by a seismograph, and where did it occur?" *(1960 Valdivia, Chile; Mw 9.5)*

#### Review Questions
1. Why is the moment magnitude scale preferred over the Richter scale for large earthquakes?
2. Name the four seismic wave types, state who described each surface wave type, and identify which cannot travel through liquids.
3. What is the Wadati-Benioff zone and what does its geometry tell us about subduction?
