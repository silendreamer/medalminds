#### History Timeline
- 1912: Wegener proposes continental drift, names Pangaea
- 1930: Wegener dies on Greenland expedition; theory mostly rejected
- 1962: Hess proposes seafloor spreading
- 1963: Vine, Matthews, Morley explain magnetic anomaly stripes
- 1965: Wilson describes transform faults and hot spots
- 1967–1968: Plate tectonics theory formally established (McKenzie, Parker, Morgan)

#### Evidence Summary
1. Fit of continents (especially South America + Africa)
2. Matching fossils across oceans (*Glossopteris*, *Mesosaurus*)
3. Matching rock sequences and glacial deposits
4. Mid-ocean ridges and magnetic anomaly stripes
5. Age increase of ocean floor away from ridges
6. Hot spot chains (Hawaii-Emperor)
7. Seismicity patterns (trenches, ridges, transforms)
8. GPS measurements of current plate motion

#### Key Numbers
- Oldest ocean floor: ~200 Ma (western Pacific)
- Oldest continental crust: ~4.0 Ga (Acasta Gneiss, Canada)
- Mariana Trench depth: ~11 km (deepest ocean point)
- Pacific Plate speed: ~7–9 cm/yr
- Indian Plate speed historically: ~15 cm/yr
- Pangaea breakup: began ~180 Ma

#### Review Questions
1. List four types of evidence that supported Wegener's original continental drift hypothesis.
2. What is the difference between seafloor spreading and continental drift? How is one a mechanism for the other?
3. Why is slab pull considered the dominant driving force of plate tectonics, and what evidence supports this over mantle convection drag?
