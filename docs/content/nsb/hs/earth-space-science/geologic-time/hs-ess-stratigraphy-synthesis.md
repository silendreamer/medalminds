#### Complex Cross-Section Exercise
A cross-section shows (from bottom to top): horizontal granite → tilted limestone layers (A, B, C) cut by a fault → horizontal sandstone (D, E) lying unconformably above the tilted layers → a vertical basalt dike cutting through all layers → a second horizontal mudstone (F) at the very top. What is the sequence of events?

**Solution:** (1) Granite intrudes (oldest); (2) Limestone layers A, B, C deposited horizontally on the granite; (3) Tectonic deformation tilts the limestone; (4) Faulting occurs (cuts limestone but not sandstone); (5) Erosion of tilted limestone creates an angular unconformity surface; (6) Sandstone D, E deposited horizontally above the unconformity; (7) Basalt dike intrudes, cutting through everything (youngest igneous event); (8) Mudstone F deposited last. Note: F is not cut by the dike — this tells us F was deposited after the dike.

#### Review Questions
1. In the cross-section above, is the fault older or younger than the angular unconformity? How do you know?
2. What two pieces of evidence tell you that mudstone F is the youngest unit in the cross-section?
3. An angular unconformity represents how many distinct geological events minimum? List them.
