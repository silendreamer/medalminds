#### What Is a Proxy?
A **proxy** is a measurable quantity that varies with a climate variable we cannot measure directly. Because we can't sample ancient atmosphere directly (except from ice cores going back ~800,000 years), we use indirect chemical signals preserved in rocks and fossils. Proxies include: stable oxygen isotopes in foraminifera shells (record paleotemperature and ice volume); boron isotopes in carbonates (record past ocean pH, a proxy for CO₂); stomata density in fossil leaves (plants reduce stomatal density when CO₂ is high); and the carbon isotope ratio δ¹³C in organic carbon (records biological productivity and mass extinctions).

#### Ice Cores as Direct Archive
Antarctic ice cores (EPICA Dome C, 800,000 years; Vostok, 420,000 years) trap ancient air bubbles as snow compresses to ice. Analyzing these bubbles gives direct measurements of past atmospheric CO₂ and CH₄ concentrations. The data show a strong correlation between greenhouse gas concentrations and Antarctic temperature over eight glacial-interglacial cycles: CO₂ ranged from ~180 ppm (glacial maxima) to ~280 ppm (interglacials) — and now exceeds 420 ppm for the first time in at least 800,000 years.

#### The Carbon Isotope Excursion at the P-Tr Boundary
At the Permian-Triassic boundary, a large negative excursion in δ¹³C occurs globally in marine carbonate rocks. This indicates a massive injection of isotopically light carbon into the ocean-atmosphere system — consistent with Siberian Traps eruptions releasing CO₂ and thermogenic methane from coals and carbonates. The negative δ¹³C excursion is one of the key chemical signals used to identify and study the P-Tr mass extinction worldwide.

#### Review Questions
1. Why do paleoclimate scientists use oxygen isotopes from foraminifera shells to reconstruct past temperatures?
2. What do ice core records show about CO₂ concentrations during glacial maxima compared to interglacials?
3. How does a negative δ¹³C excursion in the rock record at a mass extinction boundary support a volcanic CO₂ release hypothesis?
