#### The Problem with Single-Mineral Dates
Simple parent/daughter ratio dating assumes you know the initial amount of the daughter isotope at the time of crystallization — but daughter isotopes may have been present in the rock even before it crystallized. For example, Sr-87 was already present in magma before Rb-87 began decaying into it. If you don't know the initial Sr-87, your age calculation will be wrong.

#### The Isochron Solution
The isochron method uses multiple co-genetic minerals from the same rock that initially had the same Sr-87/Sr-86 ratio but different Rb-87/Sr-86 ratios (because different minerals incorporate different amounts of Rb). Over time, minerals with more Rb-87 generate more radiogenic Sr-87. Plotting Sr-87/Sr-86 vs. Rb-87/Sr-86 for multiple minerals produces a straight line (isochron) whose slope gives the age and whose y-intercept gives the initial Sr-87/Sr-86 ratio. This simultaneously solves for both unknowns without assuming the initial daughter concentration.
