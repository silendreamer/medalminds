#### Steno's Laws (1669)
Nicolas Steno, a 17th-century Danish scientist, formulated principles that remain foundational to geology. **Law of Superposition:** in an undisturbed sequence of strata, the oldest layer is at the bottom and the youngest at the top. **Law of Original Horizontality:** sediments are deposited as horizontal layers; tilted or folded strata indicate post-depositional deformation. **Law of Lateral Continuity:** a layer extends horizontally in all directions until it thins and pinches out or reaches the edge of the depositional basin. If you see the same rock type on opposite sides of a canyon, they were once continuous. **Principle of Cross-Cutting Relationships:** any feature (fault, igneous intrusion, vein) that cuts through existing rock is younger than the rock it cuts.

#### Applying Cross-Cutting Relationships
If a granite batholith intrudes into limestone, the granite is younger than the limestone. If a fault offsets both the limestone and the granite but is in turn cut by a younger dike, the fault is younger than the granite and older than the dike. These relationships allow geologists to sequence events: intrusion → faulting → dike emplacement, reading relative age from structural relationships alone.

#### Inclusions Principle
**Inclusions** are fragments of one rock enclosed within another. The inclusion is always older than the rock surrounding it — you can't include something that doesn't exist yet. A granite that contains xenoliths (inclusions) of basalt means the basalt existed before the granite intruded and melted the surrounding rock.

#### Practical Application: Reading a Block Diagram
Geologic block diagrams show rock layers, faults, and intrusions in three dimensions. To read one: identify the undisturbed layers first (apply superposition); trace faults and determine which layers they offset (cross-cutting); identify intrusions and their contacts; note inclusions. Then write the sequence of events from oldest to youngest.
