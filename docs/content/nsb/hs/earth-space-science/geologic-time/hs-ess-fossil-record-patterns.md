#### Evolutionary Radiations After Extinctions
After each mass extinction, surviving lineages undergo **adaptive radiations** — rapid diversification into vacant ecological niches. After the K-Pg extinction, placental mammals diversified explosively from shrew-like ancestors into whales, bats, horses, primates, and elephants within ~10 million years. This pattern — extinction opening ecological opportunity — is called **incumbency release**. The more severe the extinction, the more radical the subsequent diversification can be.

#### Lazarus Taxa and Pull of the Recent
**Lazarus taxa** are groups that disappear from the fossil record for an interval and then reappear — apparently "rising from the dead." This often reflects sampling gaps (the organisms existed but lived in environments unlikely to fossilize) rather than true repeated origination. The coelacanth, known only from fossils until discovered alive in 1938, is the classic living example. The **Pull of the Recent** is a bias in the fossil record: the most recent sediments are best preserved and best sampled, making recent biodiversity appear higher than ancient biodiversity even when it isn't.
