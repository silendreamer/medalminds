#### The Structure of the Lithosphere
Earth's outer rigid layer — the **lithosphere** — consists of the crust plus the uppermost mantle. It is broken into ~15 major tectonic plates and several minor ones. Oceanic lithosphere is denser (basaltic composition, ~3.0 g/cm³) and thinner (5–10 km of crust). Continental lithosphere is less dense (granitic composition, ~2.7 g/cm³) and thicker (25–70 km of crust, up to 100 km in cratons). Plates move over the **asthenosphere**, a partially molten, ductile layer of the upper mantle that flows on geologic timescales.

#### Driving Mechanisms
The primary driver of plate motion is **slab pull**: as cold, dense oceanic lithosphere sinks at subduction zones, it drags the rest of the plate behind it like a tablecloth being pulled off a table. **Ridge push** (the weight of elevated mid-ocean ridges pushing material away) is secondary. Mantle convection — driven by heat from Earth's interior (radioactive decay + residual heat of accretion) — moves material that transfers heat to the base of plates, but is not the simple conveyor belt once imagined. GPS measurements show plates move 1–15 cm/yr (fingernail growth rate to a few inches/year).

#### Three Types of Boundaries
Plates interact at three types of boundaries: **divergent** (plates move apart), **convergent** (plates move toward each other), and **transform** (plates slide horizontally past each other). Each produces characteristic geological features and seismicity patterns. Most of Earth's seismic and volcanic activity occurs at plate boundaries.

#### The Wilson Cycle
The **Wilson cycle** describes the birth, growth, and closure of ocean basins over hundreds of millions of years: continental rifting → mid-ocean ridge formation → seafloor spreading → subduction initiation → ocean closure → continental collision. The Atlantic Ocean is currently in its opening phase; the Pacific is closing. This cycle recycles oceanic crust (maximum age ~200 Ma) but preserves ancient continental crust (up to 4.4 Ga).
