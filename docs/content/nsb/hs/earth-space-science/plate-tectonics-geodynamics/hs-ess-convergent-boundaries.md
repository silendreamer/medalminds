#### Oceanic-Continental Convergence
When dense oceanic lithosphere converges with less dense continental lithosphere, the oceanic plate **subducts** beneath the continent. The descending slab carries water-saturated sediments and hydrated minerals into the mantle. As the slab sinks and warms, water is driven off (dehydration), lowering the melting point of the overlying mantle wedge. This produces silica-rich (andesitic to rhyolitic) magmas that rise to form a **volcanic arc** on the overriding continent — the Andes, the Cascades, Japan's volcanoes, and the Aleutians all formed this way. A deep **oceanic trench** forms where the slab bends downward (Mariana Trench, 11 km deep, is the world's deepest).

#### Oceanic-Oceanic Convergence
When two oceanic plates converge, the denser/older plate subducts. This produces **island arcs** — chains of volcanic islands parallel to the trench. The Lesser Antilles arc (Caribbean), the Aleutian Islands, and the Mariana Islands are examples. The trench-arc system has the same deep seismicity as continent-ocean convergence.

#### Continental Collision
When oceanic lithosphere between two continents is fully subducted, the continents eventually collide. Neither can subduct (both are too buoyant), so the collision zone compresses and thickens the crust, building massive mountain ranges. The **Himalayas and Tibetan Plateau** formed when India (moving northward after splitting from Gondwana ~130 Ma) collided with Asia ~50 Ma ago — collision continues today, raising the Himalayas ~5 mm/yr. The **Appalachians** and **European Alps** also record ancient and more recent continental collisions.

#### Accretionary Wedge and Mélange
As sediments on the subducting slab are scraped off against the overriding plate, they pile up in a deformed **accretionary wedge** (or accretionary prism). The jumbled, chaotically mixed rock produced in subduction zones is called **mélange** — diagnostic of ancient subduction zones preserved in mountain belts.

#### Review Questions
1. Why does subduction of oceanic crust produce arc volcanism, and what makes the magmas silica-rich?
2. Why can't continental crust subduct when two continents collide?
3. How do the Himalayas and the Appalachians differ in terms of their current tectonic activity, and why?
