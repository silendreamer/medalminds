#### Nucleosynthesis Across Cosmic History
The Big Bang produced H, He, and trace Li. All heavier elements formed through stellar nucleosynthesis. Main-sequence fusion (proton-proton chain, CNO cycle) produces He. Evolved stars fuse He → C and O (triple-alpha process). Massive stars build up to iron via successive burning stages. The s-process (slow neutron capture) in AGB stars builds elements up to lead along the valley of stability (Ba, Sr, Pb). The r-process (rapid neutron capture) in neutron star mergers and some supernovae creates elements heavier than iron that lie off the stability valley — including Au (gold), Pt, U. The 2017 neutron star merger detected by LIGO/Virgo showed spectral signatures consistent with heavy r-process elements.

#### Supernova Enrichment of the Galaxy
Supernovae disperse heavy elements into the interstellar medium (ISM), mixing with primordial gas. Subsequent generations of stars form from this enriched material — astronomers describe stars by their metallicity [Fe/H], measured logarithmically relative to solar. Population I stars (like the Sun) are metal-rich; Population II stars (old globular cluster stars) are metal-poor. Population III stars (the first, hypothetical, from pristine H and He gas) have never been directly observed but are predicted to have been massive and short-lived.
