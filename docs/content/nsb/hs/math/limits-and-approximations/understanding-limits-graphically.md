### Understanding Limits Graphically

### Definition of Limits
In calculus, a limit describes the behavior of a function as its input approaches a certain value. Formally, we say that the limit of \( f(x) \) as \( x \) approaches \( c \) is \( L \) if, for every number \( \epsilon > 0 \), there exists a number \( \delta > 0 \) such that whenever \( 0 < |x - c| < \delta \), it follows that \( |f(x) - L| < \epsilon \). This definition highlights the concept of approaching a value rather than necessarily reaching it, which is crucial for understanding continuity and differentiability.

### One-Sided Limits
One-sided limits are used to analyze the behavior of a function as it approaches a specific point from one side only. The left-hand limit, denoted as \( \lim_{x \to c^-} f(x) \), considers values of \( x \) that approach \( c \) from the left, while the right-hand limit, \( \lim_{x \to c^+} f(x) \), considers values approaching from the right. If both one-sided limits exist and are equal, then the overall limit at that point exists; otherwise, the limit does not exist. This concept is particularly useful in identifying points of discontinuity.

### Continuity
A function is continuous at a point \( c \) if the limit of the function as \( x \) approaches \( c \) equals the function's value at \( c \). Formally, \( f \) is continuous at \( c \) if \( \lim_{x \to c} f(x) = f(c) \). A function can be continuous over an interval if it is continuous at every point within that interval. Understanding continuity is essential for analyzing the behavior of functions and determining where limits can be evaluated directly.

### Vertical Asymptotes
Vertical asymptotes occur in functions where the limit approaches infinity as \( x \) approaches a certain value \( c \). This typically happens when the function is undefined at \( c \) but approaches either \( +\infty \) or \( -\infty \) from one or both sides. For example, in the function \( f(x) = \frac{1}{x - 2} \), there is a vertical asymptote at \( x = 2 \) because the function approaches infinity as \( x \) approaches 2. Identifying vertical asymptotes is crucial for understanding the overall behavior of rational functions.

### Graphical Representation of Limits
Graphically, limits can be analyzed by examining the behavior of a function's graph near a specific point. If the graph approaches a particular \( y \)-value as \( x \) approaches \( c \), then the limit exists and is equal to that \( y \)-value. Discontinuities can be identified visually, such as holes in the graph or jumps, which indicate where limits may not exist. Understanding how to interpret graphs is a vital skill in calculus, as it provides immediate insights into the function's behavior.

### Applications of Limits
Limits have numerous applications in calculus, particularly in defining derivatives and integrals. The derivative of a function at a point is defined as the limit of the average rate of change of the function as the interval approaches zero. Similarly, integrals can be understood as the limit of Riemann sums as the partition of the interval becomes infinitely fine. Mastering limits is foundational for further studies in calculus and higher mathematics, making them a critical topic for students preparing for competitions like the Science Bowl.
