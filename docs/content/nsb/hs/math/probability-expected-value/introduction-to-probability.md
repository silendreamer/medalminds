### Introduction to Probability

Probability is a branch of mathematics that deals with the likelihood of events occurring. It quantifies uncertainty and provides a framework for making predictions based on known information. Understanding probability is essential in various fields, including science, finance, and everyday decision-making.

### Sample Space

The sample space is the set of all possible outcomes of a random experiment. For example, when flipping a coin, the sample space consists of two outcomes: heads (H) and tails (T). In a more complex scenario, such as rolling a six-sided die, the sample space includes the numbers 1 through 6. Defining the sample space is the first step in calculating probabilities, as it provides the foundation for identifying events of interest.

### Events

An event is a specific outcome or a set of outcomes from the sample space. Events can be simple, consisting of a single outcome, or compound, consisting of multiple outcomes. For instance, rolling an even number on a die (which includes the outcomes 2, 4, and 6) is a compound event. Understanding how to categorize events is crucial for calculating their probabilities accurately.

### Probability Formula

The probability of an event is calculated using the formula:  
\[ P(E) = \frac{\text{Number of favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total number of outcomes in the sample space}} \]  
Where \( P(E) \) represents the probability of event \( E \). For example, if we want to find the probability of rolling a 3 on a six-sided die, there is one favorable outcome (rolling a 3) and six possible outcomes in total. Thus, the probability is \( P(3) = \frac{1}{6} \).

### Types of Probability

There are two main types of probability: theoretical and experimental. Theoretical probability is based on the assumption that all outcomes are equally likely, as demonstrated in the previous examples. Experimental probability, on the other hand, is derived from actual experiments and observations. For instance, if you flip a coin 100 times and it lands on heads 55 times, the experimental probability of getting heads is \( P(H) = \frac{55}{100} = 0.55 \).

### Expected Value

The expected value (EV) is a key concept in probability that represents the average outcome of a random event over a large number of trials. It is calculated by multiplying each possible outcome by its probability and summing these products. For example, if a game offers a payout of $10 with a probability of 0.1 and a payout of $0 with a probability of 0.9, the expected value would be \( EV = (10 \times 0.1) + (0 \times 0.9) = 1 \). This means that, on average, a player can expect to win $1 per game played.
