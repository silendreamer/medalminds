### Introduction to Geometric Transformations

Geometric transformations are operations that alter the position, size, or orientation of shapes in a coordinate plane. The four primary types of transformations are translations, rotations, reflections, and dilations. Understanding these transformations is essential for solving complex geometric problems and is a fundamental concept in both mathematics and real-world applications, such as computer graphics and engineering.

### Translations

A translation moves every point of a shape a constant distance in a specified direction. This transformation can be described using vector notation, where a vector indicates the direction and distance of the movement. For example, translating a point \( (x, y) \) by a vector \( (a, b) \) results in a new point \( (x + a, y + b) \). Importantly, translations preserve the shape and size of the figure, maintaining congruence before and after the transformation.

### Rotations

Rotations involve turning a shape around a fixed point, known as the center of rotation, by a certain angle. The angle of rotation can be measured in degrees or radians, and the direction of rotation is typically counterclockwise unless specified otherwise. For instance, rotating a point \( (x, y) \) around the origin by an angle \( \theta \) results in the new coordinates \( (x', y') = (x \cos \theta - y \sin \theta, x \sin \theta + y \cos \theta) \). Like translations, rotations also preserve the shape and size of the figure.

### Reflections

A reflection flips a shape over a line, known as the line of reflection, creating a mirror image. The most common lines of reflection are the x-axis, y-axis, and the line \( y = x \). For example, reflecting a point \( (x, y) \) over the y-axis results in the point \( (-x, y) \). Reflections maintain congruence, ensuring that the original shape and its image are identical in size and shape, but their orientations differ.

### Dilations

Dilation is a transformation that alters the size of a shape while maintaining its proportions. This transformation is defined by a center of dilation and a scale factor. If the scale factor is greater than 1, the shape enlarges; if it is between 0 and 1, the shape shrinks. For example, a dilation of a point \( (x, y) \) about the origin with a scale factor \( k \) results in the new coordinates \( (kx, ky) \). Unlike the other transformations, dilations do not preserve congruence but maintain similarity.

### Properties of Transformations

Each transformation has distinct properties that can be used to solve problems effectively. Translations, rotations, and reflections are rigid motions, meaning they preserve distances and angles, resulting in congruent figures. In contrast, dilations change the size but keep the shape proportional. Understanding these properties allows students to predict the outcomes of transformations and apply them in various mathematical contexts, such as coordinate geometry and algebra.

### Review Questions

1. What is the result of translating the point \( (3, 4) \) by the vector \( (2, -1) \)?
2. How do the coordinates of a point \( (x, y) \) change when it is reflected over the x-axis?
3. If a shape is dilated by a scale factor of 3, what happens to the lengths of its sides?
