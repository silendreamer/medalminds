### Definition of Rational Expressions

A rational expression is defined as the quotient of two polynomials, where the denominator is not equal to zero. This means that a rational expression can be represented in the form \( \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} \), where \( P(x) \) and \( Q(x) \) are polynomials. For example, \( \frac{2x^2 + 3x - 5}{x - 1} \) is a rational expression, as both the numerator and denominator are polynomials. Understanding this definition is crucial for identifying and working with rational expressions in various mathematical contexts.

### Identifying Numerator and Denominator

In any rational expression, the numerator is the polynomial located above the division line, while the denominator is the polynomial located below it. For instance, in the expression \( \frac{3x^2 + 2}{x^2 - 4} \), \( 3x^2 + 2 \) is the numerator and \( x^2 - 4 \) is the denominator. Recognizing these components is essential for performing operations such as simplification, addition, and subtraction of rational expressions. Students should practice identifying the numerator and denominator in various examples to solidify their understanding.

### Simplifying Rational Expressions

To simplify a rational expression, one must factor both the numerator and the denominator whenever possible. This involves expressing each polynomial as a product of its factors. For example, to simplify \( \frac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 - 3x} \), we can factor the numerator as \( (x - 3)(x + 3) \) and the denominator as \( x(x - 3) \). After factoring, we can cancel out the common factor \( (x - 3) \), leading to the simplified expression \( \frac{x + 3}{x} \), provided \( x \neq 3 \).

### Restrictions on Rational Expressions

When working with rational expressions, it is important to identify any restrictions on the variable that prevent division by zero. The values that make the denominator equal to zero are excluded from the domain of the rational expression. For example, in the expression \( \frac{2x}{x - 4} \), the denominator \( x - 4 \) cannot equal zero, which means \( x \) cannot be 4. Understanding these restrictions helps prevent undefined expressions and ensures accurate mathematical operations.

### Operations with Rational Expressions

Rational expressions can be added, subtracted, multiplied, and divided, but each operation has specific rules. When adding or subtracting rational expressions, a common denominator must be found. For multiplication, you simply multiply the numerators together and the denominators together. For division, you multiply by the reciprocal of the second expression. Mastery of these operations is crucial for solving complex problems involving rational expressions.

### Real-World Applications of Rational Expressions

Rational expressions are not only theoretical; they have practical applications in various fields such as physics, engineering, and economics. For instance, they can be used to model rates, such as speed or density, where quantities are expressed as ratios. Understanding how to manipulate rational expressions allows students to apply mathematical concepts to real-world scenarios, enhancing their problem-solving skills and analytical thinking.

3. What restriction must be considered when simplifying the expression \( \frac{x^2 - 1}{x - 1} \)?
