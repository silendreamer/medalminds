### Introduction to Prime Factorization

Prime factorization is the process of expressing a composite number as a product of its prime factors. A prime number is defined as a natural number greater than 1 that has no positive divisors other than 1 and itself. Understanding prime factorization is crucial in various areas of mathematics, including number theory, cryptography, and simplifying fractions. This lesson will explore methods for finding the prime factorization of composite numbers, emphasizing the unique factorization theorem, which states that every integer greater than 1 can be represented uniquely as a product of prime numbers, disregarding the order of the factors.

### Methods of Prime Factorization

There are several methods to find the prime factorization of a composite number. The two most common methods are the factor tree method and the division method. The factor tree method involves breaking down a composite number into its factors, continuing the process until all factors are prime. The division method, on the other hand, involves dividing the number by the smallest prime number possible and continuing the process with the quotient until the result is a prime number.

### Factor Trees

A factor tree is a visual representation used to determine the prime factors of a composite number. To create a factor tree, start with the composite number at the top and divide it by any of its factors, writing the factors below. Repeat this process with the resulting composite factors until only prime numbers remain. For example, to factor 60, you could start with 60, divide it by 2 to get 30, then divide 30 by 2 to get 15, and finally divide 15 by 3 to get 5, resulting in the prime factorization of 2 × 2 × 3 × 5.

### Division Method

The division method is another effective way to find prime factorization. Begin by dividing the composite number by the smallest prime number, which is 2. If the number is even, continue dividing by 2 until you reach an odd number. Then, switch to the next smallest prime number and repeat the process. For instance, to find the prime factorization of 84, divide by 2 to get 42, then divide 42 by 2 to get 21, and finally divide 21 by 3 to get 7, leading to the prime factorization of 2 × 2 × 3 × 7.

### Unique Factorization Theorem

The unique factorization theorem, also known as the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, asserts that every integer greater than 1 can be expressed uniquely as a product of prime numbers, except for the order of the factors. This theorem is significant because it guarantees that the prime factorization of a number is consistent, regardless of the method used to find it. For example, the prime factorization of 30 is always 2 × 3 × 5, whether you use a factor tree or the division method. This uniqueness is foundational in various mathematical applications, including simplifying fractions and finding least common multiples.

### Applications of Prime Factorization

Prime factorization has several practical applications in mathematics and beyond. It is essential in simplifying fractions, finding the greatest common divisor (GCD), and determining the least common multiple (LCM) of numbers. In cryptography, prime factorization plays a critical role in securing data, as the difficulty of factoring large composite numbers into their prime factors underpins many encryption algorithms. Additionally, prime factorization is used in algorithms for computer science, such as those that involve number theory and data analysis.
