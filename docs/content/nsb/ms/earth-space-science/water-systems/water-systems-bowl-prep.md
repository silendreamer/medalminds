#### Key Facts
- Largest aquifer in U.S.: **Ogallala Aquifer** (High Plains Aquifer)
- Process of water vapor entering atmosphere from plants: **transpiration**
- World's largest tidal range: **Bay of Fundy**
- Average ocean salinity: **35 ppt**
- Sea level rise from complete Greenland Ice Sheet melt: **~7 meters**
- Largest fresh water reservoir: **glaciers and ice sheets** (~69% of fresh water)
- Eutrophication is caused by excess: **nitrogen and phosphorus** (from fertilizers)
- Dead zone in Gulf of Mexico fed by: **Mississippi River agricultural runoff**

#### Toss-Up Stems
- "This large aquifer beneath the Great Plains provides irrigation water to much of the central U.S...." → **Ogallala Aquifer**
- "Name the process by which plants release water vapor through their leaves..." → **transpiration**
- "Excessive nutrient enrichment of a water body, often from fertilizer runoff, is called..." → **eutrophication**
- "For 10 points — where is most of Earth's fresh water stored?" → **glaciers and ice sheets**
- "This type of tide, with the greatest tidal range, occurs during new and full moon phases..." → **spring tide**

#### Common Traps
- **Sea ice melting** does NOT raise sea level; **land ice** (glaciers, ice sheets) melting DOES.
- The Ogallala Aquifer is sometimes called the High Plains Aquifer — know both names.
- Eutrophication ends with algal blooms and then hypoxia (low oxygen) — the dead fish are from oxygen depletion, not toxins (usually).

#### Review Questions
1. What is the difference between a confined and unconfined aquifer?
2. Does melting Arctic sea ice raise sea level? Explain why or why not.
3. What agricultural inputs most commonly cause eutrophication?
