#### How Mantle Convection Works
The mantle isn't a static solid — it convects over millions of years. Hot rock near the core-mantle boundary heats up, becomes slightly less dense, and slowly rises. As it rises, it cools, becomes denser, and sinks. This giant conveyor belt — **mantle convection** — is the engine that drives plate tectonics. A complete convection cycle takes tens of millions of years, but it moves tectonic plates centimeters per year.

#### Mantle Plumes and Hot Spots
In some places, exceptionally hot columns of mantle rock — **mantle plumes** — rise from deep within the mantle. Where a plume reaches the crust, it creates a **hot spot**: a fixed point of volcanic activity. As a tectonic plate moves over a hot spot, it leaves a chain of volcanoes. The Hawaiian Islands formed this way — the Big Island of Hawaii sits over the active hot spot today, while older islands (Maui, Oahu, Kauai) are progressively older and more eroded to the northwest.

#### Magnetic Reversals: A Record in Rock
Earth's magnetic field has reversed many times in geologic history — the north and south magnetic poles swap. Evidence of past reversals is preserved in **oceanic crust**: as magma erupts at mid-ocean ridges and cools, iron minerals align with the current magnetic field and lock in its direction. The symmetric striped pattern of normal and reversed magnetism on both sides of mid-ocean ridges was key evidence for seafloor spreading — and confirmed plate tectonic theory.

#### Why Earth Has a Magnetic Field and Mars Doesn't
Mars once had a magnetic field, proven by ancient magnetized rocks on its surface. About 4 billion years ago, Mars's small iron core cooled and solidified completely, shutting down the geodynamo. Without a magnetic field, solar wind stripped away most of Mars's atmosphere, explaining why Mars is now cold, dry, and thin-aired. Earth remains magnetically protected because its larger core retains enough heat to keep the outer core liquid.

#### Using Earth's Interior in Everyday Life
The geodynamo that protects us is also used practically: compass needles align with Earth's magnetic field, letting humans navigate for thousands of years. GPS satellites account for Earth's magnetic field in their calculations. Geothermal heat from Earth's interior heats homes in Iceland, the Philippines, and New Zealand. Understanding Earth's layers helps engineers assess earthquake risks for building construction.
