#### The Three Rock Types
All rocks on Earth belong to one of three families. **Igneous rocks** form when magma or lava cools and solidifies. **Sedimentary rocks** form when sediment (fragments of other rocks, minerals, or organic material) is deposited, compacted, and cemented together. **Metamorphic rocks** form when existing rocks are transformed by intense heat, pressure, or chemical fluids — without melting completely. Understanding how each forms and how they convert into one another is the rock cycle.

#### Igneous Rocks: Fire-Born
"Igneous" comes from the Latin for fire. When magma cools **slowly underground**, large crystals form — producing **intrusive (plutonic) igneous rock** like **granite**. When lava cools **quickly at the surface**, crystals have no time to grow, producing **extrusive (volcanic) igneous rock** like **basalt** (fine-grained) or **obsidian** (glassy, cooled almost instantly). The size of crystals indicates cooling rate: large crystals = slow cooling; small/no crystals = fast cooling.

#### Sedimentary Rocks: Layer by Layer
Sedimentary rocks form through **DEWC**: Deposition, Erosion, Weathering, and Compaction/Cementation. Weathering breaks rock into fragments; erosion transports them; deposition settles them in layers (strata); compaction and cementation bind them into rock. **Clastic sedimentary rocks** (like **sandstone**, **shale**, **conglomerate**) form from rock fragments. **Chemical sedimentary rocks** (like **limestone** from calcium carbonate, **rock salt**) form from dissolved minerals. **Organic sedimentary rocks** (like **coal**) form from biological material. Sedimentary rocks often contain fossils.

#### Metamorphic Rocks: Changed by Heat and Pressure
Metamorphic rocks form when existing rocks are subjected to temperatures above ~200°C and high pressure without completely melting. Common transformations: **limestone → marble**, **shale → slate → phyllite → schist → gneiss** (increasing metamorphism), **sandstone → quartzite**. Metamorphic rocks often show **foliation** — parallel alignment of minerals due to directional pressure. They form deep in the crust where tectonic forces act.

#### The Rock Cycle: All Paths Lead Somewhere
No rock type is permanent. Igneous rocks can be weathered and eroded to form sedimentary rocks. Sedimentary or igneous rocks can be buried and metamorphosed. Any rock can be melted (subducted or brought to high enough temperatures) to form magma, which cools into igneous rock again. There is no starting point or ending point — it's a continuous cycle driven by Earth's internal heat and surface processes (weathering, erosion).
