#### What Is the Mohs Hardness Scale?

The **Mohs hardness scale** is a tool that measures how hard a mineral is. Created by geologist Friedrich Mohs in 1812, it ranks minerals from 1 to 10, with 1 being the softest and 10 being the hardest. The scale is ordered so that each mineral can scratch all the minerals below it. For example, quartz (7) can scratch feldspar (6), but feldspar cannot scratch quartz. This simple ranking helps geologists and mineral hunters identify unknown rocks and minerals they find.

#### Understanding the Scale

At the soft end of the scale, talc (1) is so soft you can scratch it with your fingernail. In the middle, minerals like quartz (7) are hard enough to scratch glass. At the top, diamond (10) is the hardest natural mineral—nothing can scratch it except another diamond. The scale isn't evenly spaced; the jump in hardness from talc to diamond is enormous, but the scale compresses these differences into ten convenient ranks. Between each number, there are countless other minerals, so scientists sometimes use numbers like 7.5 to be more precise.

#### How the Scratch Test Works

To use the Mohs scale, you perform a **scratch test**. You take a known mineral from the scale and try to scratch your unknown mineral with it. If the known mineral scratches your sample, your mineral is softer. If it doesn't scratch, your mineral is harder. You repeat this with different minerals until you figure out where your sample fits on the scale. A simple trick: your fingernail ranks about 2.5, a copper penny ranks about 3, and glass ranks about 5.5. These everyday items help you narrow down hardness without needing the whole collection of reference minerals.

#### Why Hardness Matters for Identification

**Hardness is one of the key properties** geologists use to identify minerals because it's quick to test and reliable. When you find a rock, knowing its hardness immediately tells you what it might be. For example, if you find a red mineral that's harder than feldspar but softer than quartz, it's likely **ruby** or **corundum**. Hardness also matters in real life: we choose hard minerals for tools, cutting surfaces, and gemstones, while softer minerals are used for other purposes.
