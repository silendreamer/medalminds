#### Meteoroids, Meteors, and Meteorites
- **Meteoroid**: a rocky or metallic object in space, ranging from dust to ~1 meter (larger = asteroid)
- **Meteor**: the streak of light when a meteoroid enters Earth's atmosphere and burns up — also called a "shooting star"; air friction heats the meteoroid to incandescence
- **Meteorite**: a meteoroid that survives the trip through the atmosphere and lands on Earth's surface; classified as stony, iron, or stony-iron

#### Meteor Showers
**Meteor showers** occur when Earth passes through debris trails left by comets. Every year, Earth crosses these debris streams predictably, creating annual showers: Perseids (August, from Comet Swift-Tuttle), Leonids (November, from Comet Tempel-Tuttle), Geminids (December, from asteroid 3200 Phaethon). During a shower, dozens to hundreds of meteors per hour appear to radiate from a single point in the sky (the **radiant**).

#### Chicxulub and Mass Extinction
About 66 million years ago, an asteroid ~10 km across struck the Yucatán Peninsula in what is now Mexico — the **Chicxulub impact**. The impact released energy equivalent to billions of nuclear bombs, triggering wildfires, mega-tsunamis, and a "nuclear winter" from ejected dust blocking sunlight. The result was a mass extinction event that killed about 75% of all species, including the non-avian dinosaurs. This event marks the boundary between the Cretaceous and Paleogene periods (K-Pg boundary).
