#### Key Facts
- Sun's energy source: **nuclear fusion** (hydrogen → helium)
- Solar core temperature: **~15 million °C**
- Photosphere temperature: **~5,500°C**
- Corona temperature: **~1–3 million °C**
- Solar constant: **~1,361 W/m²**
- Solar cycle duration: **~11 years**
- Solar wind speed: **~400–800 km/s**
- Aurora borealis = **Northern Lights**; aurora australis = **Southern Lights**
- Most common aurora color: **green** (oxygen at ~100 km altitude)

#### Toss-Up Stems
- "This layer of the Sun, with temperatures near 5,500°C, is the visible surface..." → **photosphere**
- "Name the stream of charged particles continuously emitted by the Sun..." → **solar wind**
- "The process by which hydrogen nuclei are joined to form helium in the Sun's core..." → **nuclear fusion**
- "For 10 points — what are the Northern Lights also called?" → **aurora borealis**
- "This outermost layer of the Sun, visible during total solar eclipses, reaches temperatures of over one million degrees Celsius..." → **corona**

#### Common Traps
- The corona is hotter than the photosphere (counterintuitive!) — a fact often tested
- Sunspots are COOLER than surrounding photosphere, not hotter
- Auroras result from solar wind particles, not just the solar wind's magnetic field alone

#### Review Questions
1. What nuclear process powers the Sun?
2. Why is the corona's temperature surprising compared to the photosphere?
3. What causes the colors seen in auroras?
