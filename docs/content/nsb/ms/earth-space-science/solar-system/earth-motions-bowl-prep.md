#### Key Facts
- Earth's axial tilt: **23.5°**
- Earth's rotation period: **24 hours**
- Earth's revolution period: **365.25 days**
- Perihelion (closest to Sun): **~January 3**
- Aphelion (farthest from Sun): **~July 4**
- Summer solstice (NH): **~June 21**
- Winter solstice (NH): **~December 21**
- Vernal equinox: **~March 20**
- Synodic month (lunar cycle): **29.5 days**
- Seasons are caused by: **axial tilt** (NOT distance from Sun)

#### Toss-Up Stems
- "For 10 points — what causes Earth's seasons?" → **Earth's axial tilt**
- "This is the point in Earth's orbit when it is closest to the Sun..." → **perihelion**
- "Name the two dates per year when day and night are approximately equal..." → **vernal and autumnal equinoxes**
- "This is the longest day of the year in the Northern Hemisphere..." → **summer solstice**
- "How many days does it take for the Moon to complete one cycle of phases?" → **29.5 days**

#### Common Traps
- Earth is actually CLOSEST to the Sun in January (perihelion), during Northern Hemisphere winter — seasons are NOT caused by distance
- Lunar phases are NOT caused by Earth's shadow (that's a lunar eclipse)
- The equinox is ~equal day/night, but not exactly 12 hours everywhere due to atmospheric refraction

#### Review Questions
1. What is perihelion, and does it occur during Northern Hemisphere summer or winter?
2. Correct this misconception: "Earth is farther from the Sun in winter, which makes it colder."
3. What is the difference between a lunar phase and a lunar eclipse?
