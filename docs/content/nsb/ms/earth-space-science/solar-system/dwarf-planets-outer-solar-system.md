#### What Is a Dwarf Planet?
In 2006, the International Astronomical Union (IAU) defined a **planet** as a body that (1) orbits the Sun, (2) has enough gravity to be roughly spherical, and (3) has **cleared its orbital neighborhood** of other debris. Pluto fails the third criterion — it shares its orbit in the Kuiper Belt with many other objects. So Pluto was reclassified as a **dwarf planet**. Currently recognized dwarf planets include Pluto, Eris, Haumea, Makemake, and Ceres (in the asteroid belt).

#### Pluto
Pluto was considered the ninth planet from 1930 to 2006. It orbits in the Kuiper Belt, is about 2/3 the size of Earth's Moon, and has five moons (largest: Charon, nearly half Pluto's size — sometimes called a double dwarf planet). NASA's New Horizons spacecraft flew by Pluto in 2015, revealing a heart-shaped plain of frozen nitrogen ice (Tombaugh Regio), mountains of water ice, and a thin atmosphere.

#### The Kuiper Belt and Oort Cloud
The **Kuiper Belt** (30–50 AU from the Sun) is a disk of icy bodies beyond Neptune — the source of short-period comets. It contains Pluto, Eris, and thousands of other objects. Beyond the Kuiper Belt (up to ~100,000 AU) lies the **Oort Cloud** — a spherical shell of icy bodies thought to be the source of long-period comets. The solar system's reach extends much farther than the eight planets suggest.
