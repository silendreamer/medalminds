#### Planetary Order

The eight planets, in order from the Sun, are **Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune**. A common memory trick is "My Very Educated Mother Just Served Us Nachos." The four inner planets (Mercury through Mars) are small, rocky **terrestrial planets**. The four outer planets (Jupiter through Neptune) are large **gas giants** (Jupiter, Saturn) and **ice giants** (Uranus, Neptune). The **asteroid belt** lies between Mars and Jupiter. Pluto is now classified as a **dwarf planet**.

#### Relative Size and Scale

Planets differ enormously in size. **Jupiter** is the largest — more than 1,000 Earths could fit inside it — and **Mercury** is the smallest. The **Sun** is by far the biggest object in the solar system, holding more than 99% of its total mass. Distances are also huge: astronomers measure them in **astronomical units (AU)**, where 1 AU is the average Earth–Sun distance (about 150 million km). Neptune orbits about 30 AU from the Sun, so the outer solar system is mostly empty space.

#### Phases of the Moon

The Moon does not make its own light — it **reflects sunlight**. As the Moon orbits Earth (about every 29.5 days), we see different amounts of its lit half, called **phases**. The cycle goes **new moon → waxing crescent → first quarter → waxing gibbous → full moon → waning gibbous → third quarter → waning crescent**. "**Waxing**" means the lit part is growing; "**waning**" means it is shrinking. A new moon happens when the Moon is between Earth and the Sun; a full moon happens when Earth is between the Sun and Moon.

#### Eclipses

An **eclipse** happens when the Sun, Earth, and Moon line up. A **solar eclipse** occurs at **new moon**, when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth and casts its shadow on Earth, briefly blocking the Sun. A **lunar eclipse** occurs at **full moon**, when Earth passes between the Sun and Moon and Earth's shadow falls on the Moon, often turning it reddish ("blood moon"). Eclipses don't happen every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted about 5°, so the three bodies only line up occasionally.
