#### Predicting Volcanic Eruptions
Unlike earthquakes, volcanic eruptions give warning signs that scientists can monitor. Key indicators:
- **Seismicity**: increased earthquakes as magma moves underground, fracturing rock
- **Ground deformation**: GPS and tiltmeters detect the ground swelling as magma accumulates
- **Gas emissions**: increased SO₂ and CO₂ released as magma rises (gases escape at lower pressure)
- **Thermal anomalies**: infrared satellite imagery detects heat changes
- **Volcanic tremor**: continuous low-frequency seismic signal indicating flowing magma

The 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo in the Philippines was successfully predicted; 80,000 people were evacuated before the second-largest eruption of the 20th century, saving an estimated 5,000 lives.

#### Supervolcanoes: Catastrophic Scale
A **supervolcano** is a volcano capable of a VEI (Volcanic Explosivity Index) 8 eruption — ejecting more than 1,000 km³ of material. The **Yellowstone supervolcano** sits beneath Yellowstone National Park; its caldera (the collapsed crater from a past supereruption) is 55 km × 72 km. Yellowstone last erupted ~640,000 years ago. A full supereruption would blanket much of North America in ash and trigger a **volcanic winter** that could last years.

#### Volcanic Winter and Climate Effects
Large eruptions inject SO₂ into the stratosphere, forming sulfuric acid aerosols that scatter sunlight. The **1815 eruption of Mt. Tambora** (VEI 7, the largest in recorded history) caused the **"Year Without a Summer"** in 1816: temperatures dropped 0.4–0.7°C globally, causing crop failures across North America and Europe. Historian estimated 90,000 deaths directly from the eruption and 200,000 more from famine. The **1783 Laki eruption** in Iceland killed 25% of Iceland's population and contributed to famine in Europe and Japan.

#### Benefits of Volcanoes
Despite their hazards, volcanoes provide crucial benefits:
- **Fertile soil**: weathered volcanic rock (especially basalt) creates extremely fertile agricultural land. The slopes of Indonesian volcanoes support some of the highest population densities on Earth.
- **Geothermal energy**: volcanic heat powers Iceland's electricity and heating.
- **Mineral deposits**: hydrothermal systems associated with volcanism deposit gold, silver, copper, and zinc.
- **Atmospheric evolution**: early volcanic activity provided most of Earth's early atmosphere (water vapor, CO₂, nitrogen) through **outgassing**.
- **New land**: volcanic eruptions create new land — the Hawaiian Islands grew entirely from volcanic activity.
