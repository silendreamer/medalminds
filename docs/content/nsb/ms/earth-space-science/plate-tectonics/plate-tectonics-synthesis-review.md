#### 20 Must-Know Plate Tectonics Facts

1. Wegener proposed continental drift in 1912; his supercontinent was **Pangaea**.
2. Evidence for drift: continental fit, fossil matches, rock correlations, ancient climates.
3. Mesosaurus fossils found in both **South America and Africa** — can't cross oceans.
4. Seafloor spreading proposed by **Harry Hess** (1962).
5. New oceanic crust forms at **mid-ocean ridges**.
6. Oldest oceanic crust is no older than **~200 million years**; it's recycled at subduction zones.
7. Magnetic striping in ocean crust proves seafloor spreading and records magnetic reversals.
8. The Mid-Atlantic Ridge runs the length of the Atlantic Ocean.
9. Earth's lithosphere is divided into ~15 major tectonic plates.
10. Plates move at **2–15 cm/year** (rate of fingernail growth).
11. **Divergent**: plates move apart → new crust, mid-ocean ridges, rift valleys.
12. **Convergent**: plates collide → trenches, volcanoes, mountains.
13. **Transform**: plates slide past → earthquakes, no volcanoes (e.g., San Andreas Fault).
14. Oceanic crust **subducts** under continental crust because it's denser.
15. Ocean-continent convergence → volcanic mountain chain on continent + trench.
16. Continent-continent convergence → non-volcanic mountain range (e.g., Himalayas).
17. The **Ring of Fire** contains 75% of Earth's volcanoes and 90% of earthquakes.
18. Hawaiian Islands formed as Pacific Plate moves over a **hot spot**.
19. The East African Rift is a continental divergent boundary.
20. The Himalayas formed when the **Indo-Australian Plate** collided with the Eurasian Plate.

#### Review Questions
1. Distinguish between the three types of plate boundaries. Which one creates new crust? Which destroys crust? Which does neither?
2. How does ocean-continent convergence differ from continent-continent convergence in the structures it produces?
3. Why is oceanic crust no older than ~200 million years, while continental crust can be billions of years old?
