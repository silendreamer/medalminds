#### Mountain Type Quick Reference
| Type | Formation process | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Fold | Compression, plate convergence | Himalayas, Appalachians, Alps |
| Fault-block | Crustal extension, normal faults | Sierra Nevada, Tetons |
| Volcanic | Magma eruption | Cascades, Andes volcanics |
| Dome | Magma intrusion + erosion | Black Hills, Adirondacks |

#### Key Terms to Know
- **Anticline**: upward fold in rock layers
- **Syncline**: downward fold in rock layers
- **Graben**: down-dropped fault block (valley)
- **Horst**: uplifted fault block (mountain)
- **Timberline**: upper limit of tree growth on mountains
- **Rain shadow**: dry area on leeward side of mountain
- **Isostasy**: crust floating on mantle; mountains have deep roots

#### Review Questions
1. The Basin and Range Province of the southwestern U.S. shows alternating ridges and valleys formed by crustal extension. What mountain type are the ridges, and what are the valleys?
2. Explain why the eastern slopes of the Rocky Mountains are much drier than the western slopes (assuming westerly winds).
3. As erosion removes material from a mountain peak over millions of years, what happens to the mountain's root, according to the principle of isostasy?
