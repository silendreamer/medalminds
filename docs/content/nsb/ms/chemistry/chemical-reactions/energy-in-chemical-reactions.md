#### Why Reactions Involve Energy
Chemical bonds store energy. When bonds break, energy is absorbed; when bonds form, energy is released. The energy difference between bonds broken (reactants) and bonds formed (products) determines whether a reaction releases or absorbs energy overall:
- If energy released from forming new bonds > energy absorbed breaking old bonds → **exothermic** (releases energy to surroundings)
- If energy absorbed breaking old bonds > energy released forming new bonds → **endothermic** (absorbs energy from surroundings)

#### Exothermic Reactions
**Exothermic**: energy is released to the surroundings (usually as heat and/or light). The products have lower energy than the reactants. Surroundings get warmer.
Examples:
- Combustion (burning): CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O + energy
- Rusting of iron (slow)
- Hand warmers (iron oxidation)
- Explosions
- Cellular respiration
- Condensation (gas → liquid)

#### Endothermic Reactions
**Endothermic**: energy is absorbed from the surroundings. The products have higher energy than the reactants. Surroundings get cooler.
Examples:
- Photosynthesis (absorbs light)
- Cooking an egg (absorbs heat)
- Dissolving ammonium nitrate in water (cold packs)
- Evaporation (liquid → gas)
- Electrolysis of water

#### Activation Energy
**Activation energy** is the minimum energy needed to start a reaction — even exothermic reactions need a "push" to get started. Example: wood doesn't spontaneously burst into flame — you need a spark to provide activation energy.

#### Review Questions
1. What is the difference between an exothermic and endothermic reaction?
2. Hand warmers release heat when you open them and expose them to air. Are they exothermic or endothermic?
3. What is activation energy, and why is it needed even for exothermic reactions?
