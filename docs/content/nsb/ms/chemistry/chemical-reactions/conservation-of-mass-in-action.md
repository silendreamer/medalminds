#### Mass Calculations Using Conservation of Mass
If you know the mass of all reactants and all but one product, you can calculate the unknown:
- 28 g of iron (Fe) + 8 g of sulfur (S) → ? g of iron sulfide (FeS)
- Total reactant mass = 28 + 8 = 36 g → 36 g FeS forms (conservation of mass)

Reverse: If 90 g of water is electrolyzed into hydrogen and oxygen, and 80 g of oxygen is collected, how much hydrogen was produced?
- 90 g total products; oxygen = 80 g → hydrogen = 90 − 80 = **10 g**

#### Seeming Violations and Their Explanations
- **Burning a candle**: Candle appears to lose mass → wax converts to CO₂ and H₂O vapors that escape into the air. Total mass is conserved in a closed system.
- **Rusting iron**: Mass of rusty iron is GREATER than pure iron → iron absorbed oxygen from air. Conservation holds; the iron gained mass from O₂.
- **Fizzing tablet in water**: Mass of tablet + water = mass of solution + CO₂ gas escaping. Open system; CO₂ leaves, so remaining solution appears lighter.

#### Industrial Application
Chemical plants use conservation of mass (and stoichiometry) to calculate exactly how much raw material they need to produce a certain amount of product — minimizing waste and cost.

#### Review Questions
1. A reaction produces 44 g of CO₂ and 18 g of H₂O. If one of the reactants was oxygen (32 g), what was the mass of the other reactant?
2. A student observes that a piece of iron gains mass after sitting outside for several weeks. Explain this using the Law of Conservation of Mass.
3. If 100 g of hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) decomposes completely, what is the total mass of water and oxygen gas produced?
