#### Defining a Chemical Reaction
A **chemical reaction** is a process in which the atoms of one or more substances (reactants) are rearranged to form new substances (products). The key is that **new substances with different properties are formed** — this distinguishes a chemical reaction from a physical change. In every chemical reaction, atoms are neither created nor destroyed; they are rearranged.

#### Reading a Chemical Equation
A **chemical equation** uses symbols and formulas to represent a reaction. The general format is:
**Reactants → Products**

For example: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
- Reactants (left of arrow): methane (CH₄) and oxygen (O₂)
- Products (right of arrow): carbon dioxide (CO₂) and water (H₂O)
- Coefficients (numbers in front): show how many molecules of each substance are involved (the "2" in front of O₂ means 2 molecules of O₂)
- Arrow means "reacts to form" or "yields"

#### Types of Chemical Reactions
Five main types covered in Science Bowl:
1. **Synthesis (combination)**: A + B → AB. Two reactants combine to form one product.
2. **Decomposition**: AB → A + B. One reactant breaks down into two or more products.
3. **Single replacement**: A + BC → AC + B. One element replaces another in a compound.
4. **Double replacement**: AB + CD → AD + CB. Ions switch partners.
5. **Combustion**: fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O (+energy). Burning in oxygen.
