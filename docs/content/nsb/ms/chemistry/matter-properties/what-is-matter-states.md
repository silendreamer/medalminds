#### Defining Matter and Its States
Matter is anything that has mass and takes up space. Scientists organize matter into three common states: solid, liquid, and gas. In a **solid**, particles are packed tightly together in fixed positions — they vibrate in place but don't move freely. This is why solids have a definite shape and volume. In a **liquid**, particles are close together but can slide past each other, giving liquids a definite volume but no fixed shape (they take the shape of their container). In a **gas**, particles move rapidly and far apart, so gases have neither a definite shape nor a definite volume — they expand to fill any container.

#### How Particles Move in Each State
Temperature determines how fast particles move. At low temperatures, particles move slowly and attractions between them hold them in place → solid. As temperature rises, particles move faster, breaking free to slide → liquid. At higher temperatures still, particles move fast enough to overcome all attractions → gas. The key word is **kinetic energy**: the energy of motion. Higher temperature = more kinetic energy = particles move faster = less structure.

#### Phase Changes and Their Names
When matter changes state, energy is either absorbed or released — but the substance stays the same substance. Phase change names:
- **Melting**: solid → liquid (absorbs energy)
- **Freezing**: liquid → solid (releases energy)
- **Evaporation/Boiling**: liquid → gas (absorbs energy)
- **Condensation**: gas → liquid (releases energy)
- **Sublimation**: solid → gas directly (e.g., dry ice, CO₂)
- **Deposition**: gas → solid directly (e.g., frost forming)

Temperature stays constant during a phase change — all the energy goes into changing state, not raising temperature.

#### Why This Matters for Science Bowl
Phase change questions appear frequently. Key trap: students think temperature rises continuously when heat is added — it actually plateaus during phase changes. Also memorize: dry ice sublimates (solid CO₂ → gas). Water's boiling point is 100°C (212°F) at sea level; melting point is 0°C (32°F).

#### The Fourth State: Plasma
Plasma is sometimes called the fourth state of matter. It forms when a gas is heated so intensely that electrons are stripped from atoms, creating a mix of ions and free electrons. Plasma is the most common state of matter in the universe — stars (including the Sun) are made of plasma. Lightning bolts and neon signs also contain plasma. In Science Bowl, if a question asks "what is the most abundant state of matter in the universe," the answer is plasma.

#### Review Questions

2. Name the phase change in which a gas becomes a solid directly, and give one real-world example.
3. Why does temperature remain constant while a substance is changing phase?
