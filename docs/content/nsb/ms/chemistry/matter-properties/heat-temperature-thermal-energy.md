#### Temperature vs. Heat

**Temperature** measures how fast the particles in a substance are moving. It's a number (like 25°C or 77°F) that tells you how "hot" or "cold" something is. **Heat**, on the other hand, is energy that *flows* from a hotter object to a cooler one. Heat is not "stored" in an object; rather, it travels from warmer to cooler materials. Think of temperature as a measurement and heat as energy in motion.

#### Thermal Energy

**Thermal energy** is the total energy of all the particles moving around inside a substance. A large cup of warm water has more thermal energy than a small cup of boiling water, even though the small cup is hotter, because the large cup has more particles moving. Thermal energy depends on both temperature *and* the amount of material.

#### Particle Motion and Heat Transfer

All particles are always moving and jiggling around. Higher temperature means faster, more energetic motion. When you add heat to something—say, by putting it on a stove—you're making those particles move faster, which raises the temperature. When heat leaves an object and goes into cooler surroundings, the particles slow down and the temperature drops.

#### How Heat Flows

Heat flows in three main ways: **conduction** (through direct contact, like a spoon in hot soup), **convection** (through flowing liquids or gases, creating currents), and **radiation** (through space, like heat from the sun). Understanding these helps explain why metal heats up faster than plastic, and why the top of a heated pot gets warm.

#### Review Questions

1. A large cup of water at 50°C and a small cup of water at 80°C—which contains more thermal energy and why?
2. What is the difference between temperature and heat?
3. Name the three ways heat can flow from one place to another.
