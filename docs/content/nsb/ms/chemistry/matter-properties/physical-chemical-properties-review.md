#### Classification Practice
Classify each as physical (P) or chemical (C) property:
- Melting point of iron = 1538°C → **P**
- Iron rusts in moist air → **C**
- Gold is yellow and shiny → **P**
- Gasoline burns in air → **C**
- Sugar dissolves in water → **P**
- Baking soda reacts with vinegar to produce CO₂ → **C**

Classify each as physical (P) or chemical (C) change:
- Tearing paper → **P**
- Burning paper → **C**
- Melting butter → **P**
- Milk souring → **C**
- Dissolving salt → **P**

#### Intensive vs. Extensive Properties
Physical properties split further:
- **Intensive**: don't depend on sample size — density, melting point, boiling point, color. These are best for identification.
- **Extensive**: depend on sample size — mass, volume, length. Measuring mass tells you how much you have, not what it is.

#### Key Chemical Change Signs Recap
Gas production · precipitate formation · unexpected color change · temperature change · light/flame · irreversibility · new odor.

#### Review Questions
1. Is volume an intensive or extensive property? Explain.
2. A substance changes color when mixed with an acid. Is this a physical or chemical change?
3. Why are intensive properties more useful than extensive properties for identifying an unknown substance?
