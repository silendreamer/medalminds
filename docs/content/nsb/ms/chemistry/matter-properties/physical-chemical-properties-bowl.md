#### Toss-Up Patterns

**Pattern 1:**
*"For 10 points, classify the following as a physical or chemical property: the ability of iron to rust in the presence of oxygen."*
Answer: **Chemical property**. Buzz on "ability to rust" or "react with oxygen" — these are chemical.

**Pattern 2:**
*"What term describes a property that does not change with the amount of a sample, such as density or melting point?"*
Answer: **Intensive property**. Buzz on "does not change with sample size."

**Pattern 3:**
*"When copper is heated in air, it turns black due to oxidation. Is this a physical or chemical change?"*
Answer: **Chemical change** — a new substance (copper oxide) is formed.

#### The Three Traps
1. **Dissolving ≠ chemical change** in most cases. Salt dissolving = physical; antacid dissolving with bubbles = chemical.
2. **Color change** alone doesn't confirm a chemical change unless it's unexpected. Mixing red and blue paint changes color but it's physical.
3. **Temperature change** in a reaction is NOT a phase change — it signals chemical reaction (exothermic or endothermic).

#### Review Questions
1. Is density an intensive or extensive property?
2. A student observes that magnesium burns in air with a bright white flame, leaving a white powder. Is this a physical or chemical change? What evidence supports this?
3. Give one example each of a physical property and a chemical property of water.
