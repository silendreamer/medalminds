#### Essential Lab Measuring Tools

Every science lab has special **measuring tools** to get exact amounts of substances. A **graduated cylinder** measures the volume of liquids in **milliliters (mL)** or **liters (L)**. A **balance** or **scale** measures mass (weight) in **grams (g)** or **kilograms (kg)**. A **thermometer** measures temperature in **degrees Celsius (°C)**. A **beaker** holds liquids but isn't precise enough for exact measurements. A **pipette** or **dropper** delivers tiny amounts of liquid, drop by drop. Each tool has a job: use the right tool for the right measurement, or your results will be wrong.

#### Metric Units Matter

Scientists around the world use the **metric system** (also called SI units) so everyone speaks the same language. Length is measured in **meters (m)**, volume in **liters (L)**, mass in **grams (g)**, and temperature in **Celsius (°C)**. For small amounts, we use **prefixes**: **milli-** means one-thousandth (milliliters, milligrams), **centi-** means one-hundredth (centimeters), and **kilo-** means one-thousand (kilometers). When you read measurements, always note the unit—saying "5" without specifying milliliters or grams is useless. Metric makes math easier: converting between units involves moving decimal points, not doing tricky fractions.

#### Proper Measurement Technique

To measure liquid accurately, use a **graduated cylinder** placed on a flat surface and read at **eye level**. The liquid surface curves—read at the bottom of the curve (called the **meniscus**). Always pour carefully and avoid spilling. For solids, place the item on a **balance** and make sure the balance reads zero before you start. Record measurements immediately. Never guess or round unless the lab instructions allow it. **Precision** (being exact and repeatable) matters just as much as accuracy (being close to the true value).

#### Lab Safety Rules

Lab safety is non-negotiable. Always wear **safety goggles** to protect your eyes, and tie back long hair. Wear a **lab coat** if available. Know where the **fire extinguisher**, **eyewash station**, and **first aid kit** are located. Never eat or drink in the lab, and wash your hands before you leave. If you break glass, tell an adult—don't touch broken pieces. Keep your workspace clean and organized so you don't knock things over. If a chemical spills on you, rinse immediately with water and tell an adult right away.

#### Handling Hot and Hazardous Materials

When heating liquids, use a **beaker** or **flask** and stir with a **glass rod**. Never point the opening at anyone—liquids can splash or boil over. Use **heat-resistant gloves** or **tongs** to handle hot containers. Let things cool before you touch them. For hazardous chemicals, read the label, use the smallest amount needed, and work in a **fume hood** if available. Never mix chemicals unless the lab says it's safe. Report any accident, spill, or injury to an adult immediately—there's no such thing as a "too small" problem.
