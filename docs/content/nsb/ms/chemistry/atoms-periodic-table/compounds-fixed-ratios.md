#### What Is a Compound?
A **compound** is a pure substance made of two or more different elements chemically bonded together in a fixed ratio. The properties of a compound are completely different from the properties of its constituent elements:
- Hydrogen (H₂) = flammable gas; Oxygen (O₂) = supports combustion; together → Water (H₂O) = liquid that extinguishes fire
- Sodium (Na) = silvery metal that explodes in water; Chlorine (Cl₂) = toxic yellow-green gas; together → Sodium chloride (NaCl) = table salt (harmless white crystals)

#### Law of Definite Proportions
**The Law of Definite Proportions** (Proust's Law): A chemical compound always contains the same elements in the same ratio by mass. For example, water is always 88.9% oxygen and 11.1% hydrogen by mass, regardless of where the sample comes from or how it was made. This distinguishes a compound from a mixture (which can have any ratio).

#### Chemical Formulas
A **chemical formula** shows which elements are in a compound and how many atoms of each. Subscripts indicate the number of atoms:
- H₂O: 2 hydrogen, 1 oxygen
- CO₂: 1 carbon, 2 oxygen
- NaCl: 1 sodium, 1 chlorine
- C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose): 6 carbon, 12 hydrogen, 6 oxygen
- H₂SO₄ (sulfuric acid): 2 hydrogen, 1 sulfur, 4 oxygen

#### Key Common Compounds
Water (H₂O), carbon dioxide (CO₂), ammonia (NH₃), salt (NaCl), glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆), methane (CH₄), sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄), hydrochloric acid (HCl).
