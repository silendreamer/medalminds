#### Ionic Bonds
An **ionic bond** forms when one atom transfers one or more electrons to another atom. This creates oppositely charged ions that attract each other electrostatically. Ionic bonds typically form between metals and nonmetals.
- Sodium (Na, metal) gives up 1 electron → Na⁺
- Chlorine (Cl, nonmetal) accepts 1 electron → Cl⁻
- Na⁺ and Cl⁻ attract → NaCl (sodium chloride, table salt)

Properties of ionic compounds: high melting points, crystalline solids at room temperature, conduct electricity when dissolved in water (electrolytes), soluble in water.

#### Covalent Bonds
A **covalent bond** forms when two atoms **share** electrons. Covalent bonds typically form between nonmetals. The shared electrons count toward both atoms' octets:
- H₂: two hydrogen atoms share 2 electrons (single bond)
- O₂: two oxygen atoms share 4 electrons (double bond)
- N₂: two nitrogen atoms share 6 electrons (triple bond)
- H₂O: oxygen shares one pair each with two hydrogen atoms

Properties of covalent compounds (molecules): often gases or liquids at room temperature, lower melting/boiling points than ionic compounds, don't conduct electricity.

#### How to Predict Bond Type
Use electronegativity difference:
- Large difference (>1.7): ionic bond (metal + nonmetal)
- Small/moderate difference (<1.7): covalent bond (nonmetal + nonmetal)
- Zero difference: nonpolar covalent (same element: H₂, N₂)

#### Review Questions
1. What is the difference between ionic and covalent bonding in terms of what happens to electrons?
2. Is the bond in NaCl ionic or covalent? What about the bond in H₂O?
3. Which properties distinguish ionic compounds from covalent compounds?
