#### Groups and Families on the Periodic Table

The **periodic table** is organized into vertical columns called **groups** or **families**. Elements in the same group share similar **chemical properties** because they have the same number of **valence electrons**—the electrons on the outer shell that control how elements react. Learning the main groups helps you predict how elements will behave without memorizing every single one.

#### Alkali Metals

The **alkali metals** form Group 1 (on the left side of the periodic table, below hydrogen). They include lithium, sodium, potassium, and rubidium. Alkali metals are soft, shiny, and very reactive—they react vigorously with water and oxygen. They are never found alone in nature because they bond so easily with other elements. Sodium and potassium are common in foods and body chemistry, but they're too reactive to handle safely in the laboratory.

#### Halogens

The **halogens** are in Group 17 (the right side, near the noble gases). They include fluorine, chlorine, bromine, and iodine. Halogens are also very reactive and always form bonds with other elements. Chlorine is used to disinfect swimming pools and drinking water. Iodine is essential for human health and is often added to table salt. Unlike alkali metals, halogens exist naturally as diatomic molecules (Cl₂, Br₂) rather than single atoms.

#### Noble Gases

The **noble gases** sit in Group 18 (the far right). They include helium, neon, argon, krypton, and xenon. Noble gases are **unreactive** because their outer electron shells are completely full. They rarely form chemical bonds with other elements. Helium floats balloons, neon glows in signs, and argon is used in light bulbs. Their stability makes them useful but unreactive.

#### Using the Periodic Table to Predict Properties

By knowing an element's group, you can predict many of its properties without looking up each one individually. Elements in the same group react similarly, have similar atomic radii, and form similar types of compounds. This is why the periodic table is so useful—it organizes chemistry in a logical way.

#### Review Questions

1. Why do elements in the same group have similar chemical properties?
2. What is the main difference between alkali metals and halogens in terms of reactivity and where they are found in nature?
3. Explain why noble gases are unreactive.
