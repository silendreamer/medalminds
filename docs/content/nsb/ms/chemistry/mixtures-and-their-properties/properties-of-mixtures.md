### Introduction to Mixtures

Mixtures are combinations of two or more substances that retain their individual properties. Unlike pure substances, which consist of only one type of particle, mixtures can be made up of solids, liquids, and gases. The components of a mixture can be separated by physical means, such as filtration or evaporation, because they do not undergo a chemical change when mixed.

### Types of Mixtures

Mixtures can be classified into two main types: homogeneous and heterogeneous. Homogeneous mixtures, also known as solutions, have a uniform composition throughout, meaning that the individual components are not distinguishable. Examples include saltwater and air. In contrast, heterogeneous mixtures have a non-uniform composition, where the different substances can be seen and separated, such as salad or a mixture of sand and iron filings.

### Physical Properties of Mixtures

The physical properties of mixtures can vary widely depending on the components involved. Common physical properties include color, texture, boiling point, and density. For example, a mixture of sand and sugar will have a different texture and density compared to a pure substance like water. These properties can be measured and used to identify the components of a mixture.

### Chemical Properties of Mixtures

While physical properties can help in identifying mixtures, chemical properties are also important. Chemical properties describe how a substance interacts with other substances, such as reactivity and flammability. In mixtures, the chemical properties of the individual components remain unchanged, allowing them to retain their original characteristics. For instance, when iron filings are mixed with sulfur, they do not react until they are heated, demonstrating that the mixture's chemical properties differ from those of the pure elements.

### Density and Solubility in Mixtures

Density is a critical physical property that can help distinguish between components in a mixture. It is defined as mass per unit volume and can vary significantly between different substances. Solubility, the ability of a substance to dissolve in a solvent, is another important property. For example, salt is soluble in water, while sand is not. Understanding these properties allows scientists to separate and analyze mixtures effectively.

### Measuring Properties of Mixtures

To explore the properties of mixtures, various methods can be employed. For density, students can measure the mass of a sample and divide it by its volume. For solubility, they can mix a known amount of solute with a solvent and observe whether it dissolves. These measurements provide valuable data that can help in identifying the components of a mixture and understanding their interactions.

### Review Questions

1. What are the two main types of mixtures, and how do they differ?
2. How can density be defined, and why is it an important property in mixtures?
3. What happens to the chemical properties of substances when they are mixed to form a mixture?
