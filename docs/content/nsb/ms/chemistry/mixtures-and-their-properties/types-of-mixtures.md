### Introduction to Mixtures

Mixtures are combinations of two or more substances that retain their individual properties. Unlike compounds, where elements are chemically bonded, the components of a mixture can be separated by physical means. Understanding mixtures is essential in chemistry as they are prevalent in everyday life, from the air we breathe to the food we eat.

### Homogeneous Mixtures

Homogeneous mixtures are uniform in composition and appearance throughout. This means that any sample taken from the mixture will have the same proportions of its components. Common examples include saltwater and air, where the individual components are not distinguishable. Homogeneous mixtures are often referred to as solutions, particularly when a solute is dissolved in a solvent.

### Heterogeneous Mixtures

In contrast, heterogeneous mixtures consist of visibly different substances or phases. The components in a heterogeneous mixture can be easily identified and separated. Examples include salad, where you can see the individual ingredients, and sand mixed with pebbles. The properties of heterogeneous mixtures can vary significantly from one part of the mixture to another.

### Solutions

Solutions are a specific type of homogeneous mixture formed when one substance (the solute) is dissolved in another (the solvent). The solute can be a solid, liquid, or gas, while the solvent is usually a liquid. An important characteristic of solutions is that they do not scatter light, which distinguishes them from colloids. Common examples of solutions include sugar dissolved in water and carbonated beverages.

### Colloids

Colloids are mixtures where tiny particles of one substance are evenly distributed throughout another substance. The particles in a colloid are larger than those in a solution but smaller than those in a heterogeneous mixture, typically ranging from 1 nanometer to 1 micrometer in size. Colloids can scatter light, a phenomenon known as the Tyndall effect, which can be observed in mixtures like milk or fog. Unlike solutions, colloids do not settle upon standing, maintaining their dispersed state.

### Comparing Mixtures

To summarize the differences between homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures, consider their composition and appearance. Homogeneous mixtures are consistent and uniform, while heterogeneous mixtures show distinct, separate components. Solutions are a subset of homogeneous mixtures, while colloids, though homogeneous, exhibit unique properties such as light scattering. Understanding these distinctions helps in identifying and classifying mixtures in various scientific contexts.

### Review Questions

1. What is the primary difference between homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures?
2. Give an example of a colloid and explain why it is classified as such.
3. What is a solution, and how does it differ from a colloid in terms of particle size and light scattering?
