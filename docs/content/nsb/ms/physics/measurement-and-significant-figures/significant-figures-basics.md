### Introduction to Significant Figures

Significant figures are essential in scientific measurements as they convey the precision of the data collected. A significant figure is any digit in a number that contributes to its accuracy. This includes all non-zero digits, any zeros between significant digits, and any trailing zeros in a decimal number. Understanding significant figures helps scientists communicate the reliability of their measurements clearly.

### Rules for Counting Significant Figures

To accurately count significant figures, several rules must be followed. First, all non-zero digits (1-9) are always significant. Second, any zeros between significant digits are significant. For example, in the number 205, there are three significant figures. However, leading zeros (zeros to the left of the first non-zero digit) are not significant; for instance, in 0.0045, there are only two significant figures: 4 and 5.

### Leading Zeros

Leading zeros are the zeros that appear before the first non-zero digit in a number. These zeros serve only as placeholders and do not affect the precision of the measurement. For example, in the number 0.0032, the leading zeros (0.00) are not counted as significant figures, leaving us with only two significant figures: 3 and 2. Recognizing leading zeros is crucial for correctly interpreting the significance of a measurement.

### Trailing Zeros

Trailing zeros are the zeros that come after the last non-zero digit in a number. Their significance depends on whether there is a decimal point present. For example, in the number 1500, there is only two significant figures unless specified with a decimal point (1500.0), which would then have five significant figures. This distinction is vital in scientific notation, where trailing zeros can indicate the precision of a measurement.

### Scientific Notation and Significant Figures

Scientific notation is a way to express very large or very small numbers while maintaining clarity about significant figures. In scientific notation, only the digits in the coefficient (the number before the multiplication sign) are counted as significant figures. For example, in the number 3.00 × 10^4, there are three significant figures because the coefficient 3.00 includes the trailing zeros, indicating precision. This method simplifies the representation of significant figures in complex calculations.

### Rounding and Significant Figures

When performing calculations, it is essential to round the final answer to the correct number of significant figures. The general rule is to round to the least number of significant figures present in any of the numbers used in the calculation. For instance, if you multiply 2.5 (two significant figures) by 3.42 (three significant figures), your answer should be rounded to two significant figures, resulting in 8.5. This practice ensures that the precision of the result reflects the precision of the measurements used.

### Review Questions

2. In the number 150.0, how many significant figures are present?
3. What is the significance of trailing zeros in a number without a decimal point?
