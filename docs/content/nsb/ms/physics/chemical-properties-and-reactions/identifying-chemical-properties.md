### Introduction to Chemical Properties

Chemical properties are characteristics of a substance that become evident during a chemical reaction. Unlike physical properties, which can be observed without changing the substance's chemical identity, chemical properties describe how a substance interacts with other substances. Understanding these properties is crucial for predicting how substances will behave in various chemical reactions, which is essential knowledge for Science Bowl competitions.

### Distinguishing Physical and Chemical Properties

Physical properties can be observed without altering the chemical structure of a substance. Examples include color, boiling point, melting point, and density. In contrast, chemical properties involve the potential of a substance to undergo a chemical change, such as flammability or reactivity with acids. Recognizing the difference between these types of properties helps students analyze substances more effectively during experiments and competitions.

### Reactivity with Acids

One important chemical property is a substance's reactivity with acids. Some substances, like metals, can react vigorously with acids, producing hydrogen gas and a salt. For example, when zinc reacts with hydrochloric acid, it releases hydrogen gas and forms zinc chloride. Understanding how different materials react with acids is vital for predicting outcomes in chemical experiments and for safety in the lab.

### Flammability as a Chemical Property

Flammability is another key chemical property that describes how easily a substance can ignite and burn in the presence of oxygen. Substances like gasoline and paper are highly flammable, while others, like water or sand, are not. Assessing flammability is crucial in various applications, from safety protocols in laboratories to understanding fire hazards in everyday life. Students should be aware of the flammability of common materials they encounter.

### Examples of Chemical Properties in Everyday Life

Chemical properties can be observed in everyday life, making them relatable and easier to understand. For instance, vinegar (acetic acid) reacts with baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) to produce carbon dioxide gas, demonstrating a chemical reaction. Similarly, rusting of iron is a chemical change that occurs when iron reacts with oxygen and moisture in the air, leading to the formation of iron oxide. Recognizing these examples can help students connect classroom learning with real-world applications.

### Importance of Chemical Properties in Science

Understanding chemical properties is essential for various scientific fields, including chemistry, biology, and environmental science. Knowledge of how substances react allows scientists to develop new materials, create medications, and address environmental issues. In competitions like Science Bowl, students are often tested on their understanding of these concepts, making it crucial to grasp the significance of chemical properties in scientific inquiry.

### Review Questions

1. What is the difference between a physical property and a chemical property?
2. What gas is produced when zinc reacts with hydrochloric acid?
3. Why is flammability considered a chemical property?
