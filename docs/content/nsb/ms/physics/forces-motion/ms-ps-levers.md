#### Three Classes of Levers
**First class:** Fulcrum between effort and load. Examples: teeter-totter, scissors, pliers, crowbar. Can have MA > 1, < 1, or = 1 depending on arm lengths. **Second class:** Load between fulcrum and effort. Examples: wheelbarrow, nutcracker, bottle opener. Effort arm always longer than resistance arm → MA always > 1. **Third class:** Effort between fulcrum and load. Examples: tweezers, fishing rod, baseball bat, forearm lifting. Effort arm always shorter → MA always < 1 (but gain speed/distance).

#### The Lever Equation
For a lever in equilibrium: F_effort × d_effort = F_resistance × d_resistance. A first-class lever has a fulcrum 1 m from a 100 N load and 4 m from where you push. Effort needed: F_e × 4 = 100 × 1 → F_e = 25 N. MA = 100/25 = 4.

#### Recognizing Lever Class in Bowl
Bowl often describes a lever and asks for its class or mechanical advantage. Tip: find the fulcrum (pivot), then locate the effort and load. If the fulcrum is in the middle → first class. If the load is in the middle → second class. If the effort is in the middle → third class. Memorize examples for each.
