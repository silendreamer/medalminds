#### F = ma: The Most Important Formula in Mechanics
Newton's Second Law: The net force on an object equals its mass times its acceleration. F = ma. The SI unit of force is the **newton (N)**: 1 N = 1 kg·m/s². If a 5 kg object accelerates at 3 m/s², the net force is F = 5 × 3 = 15 N. If a 10 N force acts on a 2 kg object, acceleration = F/m = 10/2 = 5 m/s². This equation is the engine of classical mechanics — almost every force problem uses it.

#### How Mass and Force Affect Acceleration
For a constant force, doubling the mass halves the acceleration: a = F/m. For a constant mass, doubling the force doubles the acceleration. These are direct and inverse proportionalities — a favorite Science Bowl question type. "If the net force on an object doubles and its mass triples, what happens to acceleration?" a = 2F/3m — it changes by a factor of 2/3.

#### Weight vs. Mass
Mass (kg) measures how much matter an object contains. Weight (N) is the force gravity exerts on that mass: W = mg. On Earth, g = 9.8 m/s², so a 10 kg object weighs ~98 N. On the Moon (g = 1.6 m/s²), the same object weighs only 16 N — but its mass is still 10 kg. Mass doesn't change with location; weight does. Science Bowl regularly tests this distinction.

#### Solving F = ma Problems
Given: A 3 kg cart experiences forces of 12 N east and 6 N west. Find acceleration. Net force = 12 − 6 = 6 N east. a = F/m = 6/3 = 2 m/s² east. Always find the net force first; then divide by mass. Direction matters — include it in your answer for velocity-related quantities.

#### Review Questions
1. A 4 kg object accelerates at 5 m/s². What net force acts on it?
2. A 20 N net force acts on a 10 kg object. What is its acceleration?
3. A 70 kg person weighs how many newtons on Earth (g = 10 m/s²)?
