#### Sonar: Mapping the Ocean
SONAR (Sound Navigation And Ranging) uses echo timing to measure depth. A pulse of sound is emitted; the time until the echo returns gives the distance: d = v × (t/2), where t is the round-trip time and v ≈ 1,480 m/s in seawater. If an echo returns in 3 seconds: d = 1480 × 1.5 = 2,220 m. Submarines, fishing fleets, and ocean mapping all rely on sonar. Bats and dolphins use biological sonar called echolocation.

#### Medical Ultrasound
Ultrasound imaging uses high-frequency sound (1–20 MHz) to create images of internal organs. The probe emits pulses; reflections from different tissue boundaries return at different times. Harder tissues reflect more strongly. No radiation is used — ultrasound is considered safe for prenatal imaging. Doppler ultrasound can detect blood flow velocity from the frequency shift of the returning pulses.

#### Musical Instruments and Resonance
Strings, pipes, and membranes all work through resonance. A guitar string plucked at a certain tension produces a specific fundamental frequency. Placing a finger on the string shortens the vibrating length, raising the frequency (higher pitch). Wind instruments use resonance in air columns: shorter tube = higher pitch. A flute is an open pipe; a clarinet is a closed pipe — each produces different patterns of harmonics.

3. How does shortening a guitar string change its pitch? Use the concept of resonance.
