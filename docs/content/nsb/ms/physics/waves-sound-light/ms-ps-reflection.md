#### The Law of Reflection
When light hits a surface and bounces back, the angle of the incoming ray (angle of incidence, θᵢ) equals the angle of the reflected ray (angle of reflection, θᵣ), both measured from the **normal** (a line perpendicular to the surface at the point of contact). Law: θᵢ = θᵣ. This applies to all reflecting surfaces. A ray hitting a mirror at 30° from the normal reflects at 30° from the normal — not 30° from the mirror surface.

#### Specular vs. Diffuse Reflection
**Specular reflection:** from smooth surfaces (mirrors, still water, polished metal) — all rays reflect in parallel, producing a clear image. **Diffuse reflection:** from rough surfaces (paper, walls, skin) — rays scatter in many directions because the surface has many tiny, randomly oriented facets. Most of what you see depends on diffuse reflection — objects scatter light toward your eyes from all directions, making them visible.

#### Plane Mirrors and Virtual Images
A flat (plane) mirror forms a **virtual image** — the image appears to be behind the mirror, at the same distance as the object is in front of it. The image is the same size as the object and laterally inverted (left and right are swapped). The image is not real — you cannot project it onto a screen. Curved mirrors (concave and convex) produce real or virtual images of different sizes, covered in more detail at HS level.
