#### High-Yield Facts
- **Carrying capacity** (K) = max sustainable population size
- **Exponential growth** = J-curve; occurs when resources are unlimited
- **Logistic growth** = S-curve; population levels off at K
- **Density-dependent** limiting factors: disease, competition, predation
- **Density-independent** limiting factors: natural disasters, extreme weather
- **Demographic transition model**: four stages from high birth/death rates to low birth/death rates
- **Minimum viable population** = smallest population that can survive long-term
- **Predator-prey cycle**: prey peak leads predator peak; classic example = hare and lynx
- **Inbreeding depression** = reduced fitness from mating between close relatives

#### Bowl-Style Toss-Up Stems
- "This type of population growth produces an S-shaped curve and occurs when a population approaches its environment's maximum supportable size…" → **logistic growth**
- "This term describes the maximum number of individuals an environment can sustain given available resources…" → **carrying capacity**
- "In this classic ecological relationship, populations of hares and Canadian lynx show regular oscillations about every 10 years…" → **predator-prey cycle** (Lotka-Volterra dynamics)
- "This type of limiting factor — such as disease or competition for food — becomes more intense as population density increases…" → **density-dependent limiting factor**

#### Common Traps
- J-curve = exponential growth (unlimited resources); S-curve = logistic growth (limited resources)
- Carrying capacity is NOT fixed — it changes with resource availability
- Predator peak follows prey peak (prey go up first; predators lag behind)

#### Review Questions
1. What shape is the graph of exponential population growth? What shape is logistic growth?
2. Give one example each of a density-dependent and a density-independent limiting factor.
3. In a predator-prey cycle, which population peaks first — predator or prey?
