#### Savannas
The African savanna receives 50–130 cm of rain per year, but with a pronounced dry season. The mix of wet and dry seasons supports both grasses and scattered trees (acacia, baobab). Savannas support the largest land animal migrations on Earth — over 1 million wildebeest, zebra, and gazelle follow seasonal rains across East Africa. Cheetahs, lions, leopards, elephants, giraffes, and hippos all depend on the savanna ecosystem.

#### Temperate Grasslands
North American prairies, South American pampas, African veldts, and Eurasian steppes are temperate grasslands. They receive 25–75 cm of rain per year — enough for grasses but not trees (trees can grow along river corridors). Deep, fertile soils (mollisols) developed under prairie grass are among Earth's most productive agricultural soils. The American Midwest (the "Corn Belt") was originally tallgrass prairie. Less than 1% of original North American tallgrass prairie remains.

#### Deserts
Deserts cover ~33% of Earth's land surface. Hot deserts (Sahara, Arabian, Sonoran) have daytime temperatures >50°C and near-freezing nights. Cold deserts (Gobi, Atacama, Antarctic interior) are cold year-round. Both receive <25 cm/year precipitation. Desert organisms conserve water through structural (succulent stems, waxy skin), behavioral (nocturnal activity), and physiological (concentrated urine, metabolic water) adaptations.

#### Temperate Deciduous Forests
Found in eastern North America, Europe, and East Asia. Four distinct seasons: warm summers, cold winters. Deciduous trees (oak, maple, hickory, beech) lose leaves in fall to conserve water and energy during winter. The leaf litter decomposes over winter and spring, enriching the soil. These forests were largely cleared for agriculture — much of the eastern U.S., Europe, and China were once covered by temperate deciduous forest.
