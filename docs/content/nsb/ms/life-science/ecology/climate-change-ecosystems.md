#### The Greenhouse Effect (Natural vs. Enhanced)
The *greenhouse effect* is a natural process: greenhouse gases (water vapor, CO₂, methane, nitrous oxide) in the atmosphere absorb infrared radiation emitted by Earth's surface and re-emit it, warming the planet. Without the natural greenhouse effect, Earth would be about −18°C (rather than the current ~15°C average). The *enhanced greenhouse effect* is the amplification of this warming by human-released greenhouse gases — primarily CO₂ from burning fossil fuels (now ~420 ppm, up from 280 ppm pre-industrial) and methane from agriculture and landfills.

#### Effects on Ecosystems
Warming temperatures cause: (1) *Coral bleaching* — coral reefs expel their symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae) when water gets too warm, turning white and often dying (the Great Barrier Reef has lost >50% of its coral since 1995); (2) *Species range shifts* — organisms move poleward or to higher elevations as their habitat warms (mountain species can run out of habitat); (3) *Phenological mismatches* — changes in the timing of biological events (migration, flowering, hatching) can cause species to become out of sync (birds arrive for breeding season before their insect prey hatch).

#### Ocean Acidification
The ocean absorbs ~25% of human CO₂ emissions. CO₂ dissolves in water to form carbonic acid (H₂CO₃), lowering ocean pH. Ocean pH has fallen from ~8.2 to ~8.1 since industrialization (a 26% increase in acidity because pH is logarithmic). This *ocean acidification* makes it harder for corals, oysters, clams, and other organisms to build calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) shells and skeletons — with cascading effects throughout marine food webs.
