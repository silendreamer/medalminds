#### Probability Basics
Probability is the likelihood of an event occurring, expressed as a fraction (1/4), decimal (0.25), or percentage (25%). In genetics, probability tells us what fraction of offspring from a cross will have a specific genotype or phenotype.

#### The Product Rule
When two independent events both need to happen, multiply their individual probabilities. Example: What is the probability that an offspring from Tt × Tt will be both homozygous recessive (tt) AND will be female? P(tt) = 1/4; P(female) = 1/2. P(tt AND female) = 1/4 × 1/2 = 1/8.

#### The Sum Rule
When either of two mutually exclusive events can give the desired outcome, add their probabilities. Example: What is the probability of being tall (TT or Tt) from Tt × Tt? P(TT) = 1/4; P(Tt) = 2/4. P(tall) = 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4.

#### Genetics Ratios to Know Cold
- Monohybrid Aa × Aa → 1AA:2Aa:1aa (genotype); 3 dominant:1 recessive (phenotype)
- Monohybrid Aa × aa → 1Aa:1aa (genotype); 1 dominant:1 recessive (phenotype)
- Monohybrid AA × aa → all Aa (genotype); all dominant (phenotype)
- Dihybrid AaBb × AaBb → 9:3:3:1 (phenotype)

#### Expected vs. Observed
Punnett square ratios are expected probabilities from random chance. Actual offspring may deviate due to small sample size. If you flip a coin 4 times, you don't always get exactly 2 heads. With thousands of offspring (as Mendel had), observed ratios closely approach expected ratios.

#### Review Questions
1. Using the product rule: in a cross AaBb × AaBb, what is the probability of an offspring that is homozygous recessive for BOTH genes (aabb)?
2. From a Aa × Aa cross, what is the probability of an offspring with the dominant phenotype?
3. Why do actual offspring ratios sometimes differ from Punnett square predictions?
