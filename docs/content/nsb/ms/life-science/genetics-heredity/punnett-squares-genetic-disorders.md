#### Autosomal Recessive Disorders
Cystic fibrosis (CF) is caused by two recessive alleles of the CFTR gene. In CF, a defective protein channel causes thick mucus to build up in lungs and digestive organs. Both parents must be carriers (Cc) for a child to be affected. Cross Cc × Cc → 1 CC : 2 Cc : 1 cc. Probability a child has CF = 1/4 (25%). Probability of being a carrier = 2/4 (50%).

#### Sickle Cell Disease
Sickle cell disease (SCD) is caused by a single amino acid change in hemoglobin (valine instead of glutamic acid). It's autosomal recessive. However, the heterozygous carrier (sickle cell trait) has some protection against malaria — an example of heterozygote advantage. SCD is most common in populations from malaria-endemic regions of Africa.

#### Autosomal Dominant Disorders
Huntington's disease is caused by a dominant allele — even one copy causes the disease. A parent with Huntington's (Hh) has a 50% chance of passing it to each child. The disease doesn't appear until ages 30–50, meaning many people don't know they carry it until after having children. Cross Hh × hh → 1/2 Hh (affected) : 1/2 hh (unaffected).

#### Why Recessive Conditions Are More Common
Recessive conditions can persist in populations because carriers (Cc) don't show the disease and can reproduce normally, continuing to pass on the recessive allele. Dominant lethal conditions (that kill before reproduction) are quickly eliminated from the gene pool.

#### Genetic Counseling
Genetic counselors help families understand their risk of passing on genetic disorders. Preimplantation genetic diagnosis (PGD) can test embryos created by IVF for specific genetic conditions before implantation.

#### Review Questions
1. Both parents are carriers for cystic fibrosis (Cc × Cc). What is the probability their child will have CF?
2. Why can two unaffected parents have a child with a recessive genetic disorder?
3. Huntington's disease is dominant (Hh is affected). A parent with Hh has children with an hh partner. What fraction of children will develop Huntington's?
