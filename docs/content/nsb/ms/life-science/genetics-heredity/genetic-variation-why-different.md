#### Why Variation Matters
Genetic variation is the raw material for evolution. Without variation, natural selection has nothing to act on — if all individuals are identical, no individual survives a new disease better than another. Variation allows some individuals to survive changing environments, disease outbreaks, or resource shortages that kill others.

#### Source 1: Mutation
Mutations are the ultimate source of all new genetic variation. Every new allele that has ever existed started as a mutation. Over time, mutations accumulate in populations, creating the diversity of alleles we see.

#### Source 2: Sexual Reproduction
Sexual reproduction shuffles existing alleles into new combinations every generation through two mechanisms: (a) Independent assortment — 23 pairs of chromosomes separate independently, creating 2²³ = ~8 million possible chromosome combinations per gamete. (b) Crossing over (recombination) — during meiosis, homologous chromosomes exchange segments, creating recombinant chromosomes with novel allele combinations.

#### Source 3: Random Fertilization
Even after independent assortment and crossing over, which sperm meets which egg is random. The number of possible genetically unique offspring from two human parents is astronomically large — more than the number of atoms in the universe.

#### Population-Level Effects
Genetic drift (random changes in allele frequencies in small populations) can cause certain alleles to disappear or become fixed even without natural selection. The founder effect (a small group starts a new population) and bottleneck effect (population crashes) can dramatically reduce genetic variation.
