#### Setting Up a Dihybrid Cross
A dihybrid cross tracks two independent genes at once. Example: pea plants with seed color (Y=yellow, y=green) and seed shape (R=round, r=wrinkled). Cross YyRr × YyRr.

Step 1: Determine gametes for YyRr parent: YR, Yr, yR, yr (four types, in equal proportion).
Step 2: Set up 4×4 Punnett square — 16 boxes total.
Step 3: Fill in all combinations.
Step 4: Count phenotypes.

#### The 9:3:3:1 Ratio
From a dihybrid cross of two double-heterozygotes: 9/16 yellow round (Y_R_), 3/16 yellow wrinkled (Y_rr), 3/16 green round (yyR_), 1/16 green wrinkled (yyrr). This ONLY works when the two genes assort independently (Law of Independent Assortment).

#### Shortcut: Using Probability Instead of a 4×4 Grid
For dihybrid problems, multiply monohybrid probabilities:
- P(Y_) from Yy×Yy = 3/4; P(R_) from Rr×Rr = 3/4
- P(Y_R_) = 3/4 × 3/4 = 9/16
- P(yyrr) = 1/4 × 1/4 = 1/16
This is faster than filling out the full 4×4 square in a timed bowl round.

#### When This Doesn't Work
The 9:3:3:1 ratio fails when genes are on the same chromosome (linked genes). Linked genes tend to be inherited together, breaking independent assortment. Recombination during meiosis (crossing over) can separate linked genes, but the ratio will still deviate from 9:3:3:1.

#### Bowl-Speed Strategy
In bowl, when you see a dihybrid problem: immediately check if they want a ratio (9:3:3:1 shortcut) or a specific genotype probability (multiply monohybrid fractions). Most bowl dihybrid questions just want the 9:3:3:1 ratio or a specific cell count from it.

#### Review Questions
1. In a dihybrid cross AaBb × AaBb, what fraction of offspring will be heterozygous for BOTH genes (AaBb)?
2. Why does the 9:3:3:1 ratio only apply when genes are on different chromosomes?
3. Using the probability shortcut: what is P(aaBb) from AaBb × AaBb?
