#### What DNA Is
DNA stands for deoxyribonucleic acid. It is the molecule that carries genetic instructions for the development, growth, reproduction, and functioning of all living organisms. DNA is found in the nucleus of eukaryotic cells and in the cytoplasm of prokaryotes. Every cell in your body (except red blood cells) contains about 6 feet of DNA, coiled and packed tightly into chromosomes. Humans have 46 chromosomes arranged in 23 pairs.

#### The Double Helix Structure
DNA looks like a twisted ladder — this shape is called a double helix, described by James Watson and Francis Crick in 1953 (building on X-ray data from Rosalind Franklin). The sides of the ladder are made of alternating sugar (deoxyribose) and phosphate molecules. The rungs are made of pairs of nitrogen bases. Each unit of one sugar + one phosphate + one base is called a nucleotide.

#### The Four Bases and Base Pairing
DNA has four nitrogen bases: Adenine (A), Thymine (T), Guanine (G), and Cytosine (C). They always pair the same way: A pairs with T, and G pairs with C. This is called complementary base pairing. If you know one strand of DNA, you can always figure out the other. For example, if one strand reads AATGCC, the complementary strand reads TTACGG.

#### Why the Structure Matters
The double helix is brilliant for storing information because (1) the base-pair sequence encodes instructions — like letters in an alphabet — and (2) the paired strands allow perfect copying: unzip the two strands, build a new partner strand on each, and you get two identical copies. This makes DNA replication extremely accurate.

#### Science Bowl Clue
When a question asks about DNA structure, look for keywords: "double helix," "nucleotides," "base pairs," "deoxyribose," or the names Watson, Crick, or Franklin. The base pairing rule — A-T and G-C — appears in nearly every DNA question set. Memorize it in both directions: A pairs with T; G pairs with C.
