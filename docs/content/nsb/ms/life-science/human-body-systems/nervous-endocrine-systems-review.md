#### Neuron Quick Review
- Dendrites: receive signals → Cell body: integrates → Axon: sends → Terminal: releases neurotransmitter
- Action potential: Na⁺ in (depolarization), K⁺ out (repolarization); all-or-nothing
- Myelin: speeds conduction (saltatory conduction)
- Synapse: neurotransmitter crosses synaptic cleft to next neuron

#### Brain Region Quick Review
- Cerebrum: thought, voluntary movement, senses (4 lobes)
- Cerebellum: coordination, balance, fine motor
- Brainstem: breathing, heart rate, automatic functions
- Hippocampus: memory; Amygdala: emotion; Hypothalamus: homeostasis link

#### Endocrine Gland Quick Table
| Gland | Key hormone(s) | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Hypothalamus | Releasing hormones | Controls pituitary |
| Pituitary | GH, ADH, TSH, FSH/LH | Master gland |
| Thyroid | Thyroxine | Metabolic rate |
| Pancreas | Insulin, Glucagon | Blood glucose |
| Adrenal | Epinephrine, Cortisol | Stress response |
| Gonads | Estrogen, Testosterone | Reproduction |

#### Key Feedback Loops
- Blood glucose high → insulin released → glucose enters cells → glucose normal
- Blood glucose low → glucagon released → liver releases glucose → glucose normal
- Dehydrated → ADH released → kidneys reabsorb water → blood diluted
- Body temp high → sweat, vasodilation → cooling → temp normal

#### Review Questions
1. What are the two parts of the peripheral nervous system and what does each control?
2. Which gland is called the "master gland" and why?
3. How do insulin and glucagon maintain blood glucose homeostasis?
