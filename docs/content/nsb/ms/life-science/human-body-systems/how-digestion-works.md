#### Mechanical vs. Chemical Digestion
Digestion is the process of breaking down food into molecules small enough to enter the bloodstream. Mechanical digestion is physical — chewing, churning — that breaks food into smaller pieces without changing its chemical nature. Chemical digestion uses enzymes to break chemical bonds in food molecules (carbohydrates → sugars, proteins → amino acids, fats → fatty acids + glycerol).

#### The Digestive Pathway
Mouth: teeth perform mechanical digestion; salivary amylase begins chemical digestion of starches. Esophagus: muscular tube that moves food by peristalsis (wave-like muscle contractions). Stomach: churning provides mechanical digestion; hydrochloric acid (pH ~2) kills bacteria; pepsin begins protein digestion. Small intestine: primary site of digestion and absorption; pancreatic enzymes digest carbohydrates, proteins, and fats; bile from the gallbladder emulsifies fats; nutrients absorbed into bloodstream through villi. Large intestine: water and electrolytes reabsorbed; remaining waste becomes feces. Rectum/anus: feces eliminated.

#### The Liver and Pancreas
Both are accessory digestive organs (not part of the GI tract directly). The liver produces bile (stored in gallbladder) to emulsify fats — breaking large fat droplets into tiny ones so lipase enzymes can reach them. The pancreas produces a suite of digestive enzymes (amylase, protease, lipase) and secretes sodium bicarbonate to neutralize stomach acid entering the small intestine.

#### Villi: The Absorption Specialists
The small intestine's inner surface is covered with finger-like projections called villi, and each villus is further covered with microvilli (brush border). This arrangement increases surface area enormously — from a smooth tube of ~6 m² to ~300 m² — maximizing nutrient absorption. Each villus contains blood capillaries (for amino acids and sugars) and lacteals (lymph vessels for fatty acids).

#### Science Bowl Clue
"Where does most digestion and absorption occur?" → small intestine. "What emulsifies fats?" → bile (produced by liver, stored in gallbladder). "What enzyme digests starch in the mouth?" → salivary amylase.
