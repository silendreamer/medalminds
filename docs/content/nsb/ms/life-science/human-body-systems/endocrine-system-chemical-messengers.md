#### What the Endocrine System Does
The endocrine system uses hormones — chemical signals released into the bloodstream — to regulate body functions. Unlike the nervous system (which sends fast, targeted electrical signals), the endocrine system sends slower, body-wide chemical messages with longer-lasting effects. Hormones travel through blood and act on specific target cells that have receptors for that hormone.

#### Major Glands and Their Hormones
- Hypothalamus: master control; links nervous and endocrine systems; controls pituitary
- Pituitary gland: "master gland"; produces growth hormone (GH), antidiuretic hormone (ADH), thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH), follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH), luteinizing hormone (LH), and others
- Thyroid: thyroxine controls metabolic rate and growth
- Pancreas: insulin (lowers blood glucose) and glucagon (raises blood glucose)
- Adrenal glands: epinephrine (adrenaline) — fight-or-flight response; cortisol — stress response
- Gonads (ovaries/testes): estrogen, progesterone, testosterone — reproductive development
- Parathyroid: parathyroid hormone (PTH) — regulates blood calcium

#### Negative Feedback
Most hormone regulation uses negative feedback loops: when hormone levels rise, signals are sent to reduce production; when levels fall, production increases. Example: low thyroid hormone → hypothalamus releases TRH → pituitary releases TSH → thyroid produces more thyroxine → high thyroxine inhibits hypothalamus and pituitary. This is analogous to a thermostat.

#### Nervous vs. Endocrine
Nervous: fast, electrical, short-lived, targeted (specific cells). Endocrine: slow, chemical, long-lasting, widespread (all cells with receptors). Many processes use both — stress triggers both the nervous system (adrenaline rush) and endocrine system (cortisol release).
