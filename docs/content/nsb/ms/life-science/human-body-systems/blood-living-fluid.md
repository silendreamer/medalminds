#### What Blood Is Made Of
Blood is a connective tissue composed of: (1) Plasma — the liquid matrix; a yellow, protein-rich fluid (~55% of blood volume). (2) Red blood cells (erythrocytes) — the most numerous cells; carry oxygen. (3) White blood cells (leukocytes) — much fewer; fight infection. (4) Platelets (thrombocytes) — cell fragments; clot blood to stop bleeding.

#### Red Blood Cells and Hemoglobin
Red blood cells are small, biconcave discs — this shape maximizes surface area for gas exchange. They contain hemoglobin, an iron-containing protein that binds O₂ in the lungs (where O₂ is abundant) and releases it in tissues (where O₂ is scarce). CO₂ is mostly transported dissolved in plasma or as bicarbonate ions. RBCs have no nucleus (in mammals), which maximizes space for hemoglobin. They live about 120 days.

#### White Blood Cells and Immunity
WBCs are the soldiers of the immune system. There are several types: neutrophils engulf and destroy bacteria; lymphocytes include B cells (make antibodies) and T cells (attack infected cells); monocytes become macrophages that engulf debris. WBCs are larger than RBCs and have nuclei.

#### Platelets and Clotting
When a blood vessel is damaged, platelets cluster at the injury site and release clotting factors. These factors trigger a cascade that converts fibrinogen (a plasma protein) to fibrin, forming a meshwork that traps RBCs and creates a clot. This stops blood loss. Disorders of clotting (like hemophilia) can cause uncontrolled bleeding.
