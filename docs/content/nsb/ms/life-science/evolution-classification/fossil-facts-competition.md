#### High-Yield Facts
- *Archaeopteryx* — transitional fossil between **therapod dinosaurs and birds**; found in **Bavaria, Germany**; discovered **1861**
- *Tiktaalik roseae* — transitional fossil between **fish and land vertebrates**; discovered **2004** in Canada
- Carbon-14 half-life = **5,730 years**; useful up to ~**50,000 years**
- Law of superposition: **older layers on the bottom**, younger on top
- **Index fossils** = widespread, short-lived, easily recognized → used for dating rock layers
- **Vestigial structures** = reduced structures inherited from ancestors (whale hips, human coccyx, snake pelvic bones)
- **Homologous structures** = same bone plan, different function (human arm, whale flipper, bat wing)

#### Bowl-Style Toss-Up Stems
- "This 375-million-year-old fish-tetrapod transitional fossil, discovered in Nunavut, Canada, had fin-bones arranged like a limb…" → **Tiktaalik**
- "This 150-million-year-old fossil, found in Bavaria, had feathers but also teeth and a bony tail…" → **Archaeopteryx**
- "This law of geology states that in undisturbed rock layers, the oldest are found at the bottom…" → **law of superposition**
- "This type of fossil is used to determine the relative age of rock layers because it is widespread and existed for a short time…" → **index fossil**

#### Common Traps
- Archaeopteryx bridges dinosaurs and birds — NOT fish and birds
- Tiktaalik bridges fish and land vertebrates — NOT fish and dinosaurs
- Carbon-14 is for recent organisms; uranium-lead is for ancient rocks

#### Review Questions
1. What is the half-life of carbon-14, and why can't it date million-year-old fossils?
2. Name two transitional fossils and the groups each bridges.
3. What is the difference between homologous and analogous structures?
