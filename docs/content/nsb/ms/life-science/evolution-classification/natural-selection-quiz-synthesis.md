#### Scenario 1: The Moth Problem
A forest floor is normally brown. A logging company clears trees and replaces them with light-colored birch. Moth populations in the forest currently have 70% dark moths and 30% light moths. Predict what will happen to the moth population over the next 50 years and explain your reasoning using the four steps of natural selection.

#### Scenario 2: The Antibiotic Farm
A chicken farm uses antibiotics routinely in chicken feed (a real and common practice). Explain, using natural selection, why this practice might be dangerous for human health over time.

#### Scenario 3: The Island Arrival
A pair of lizards is washed onto a new island on a floating log. The island has tall trees with insects in the bark and low shrubs with berries on the ground. After 10,000 years, researchers find two distinct lizard species on the island. One has long claws and a narrow snout; the other has a blunt snout and short legs. Explain how natural selection could have produced these two species from one ancestral pair.

#### Review Questions
1. Identify the selection pressure in Scenario 1 and predict the direction of change.
2. In Scenario 2, what trait is being selected for? What are the broader consequences?
3. What process (in addition to natural selection) was needed to produce two separate species in Scenario 3?
