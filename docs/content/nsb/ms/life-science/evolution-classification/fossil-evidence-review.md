#### Quick Recall
Match each term to its definition: (1) Stratigraphy, (2) Transitional fossil, (3) Vestigial structure, (4) Index fossil, (5) Superposition. Answers: (1) study of rock layers; (2) fossil showing intermediate features; (3) body part that lost its original function; (4) fossil used to date rock layers; (5) law stating older layers are on the bottom.

#### Synthesis Question
A paleontologist finds a fossil in a rock layer dated to 375 million years ago. The fossil has scales, gills, and fins — but the fins contain bones arranged like humerus-radius-ulna. (a) What is this fossil? (b) What groups does it bridge? (c) Why is it considered a transitional fossil?

#### Connecting Evidence
Explain how each of the following provides independent evidence for evolution: (1) the sequence of fossils in rock layers, (2) the existence of transitional fossils like Archaeopteryx, (3) vestigial structures like whale hip bones, (4) the geographic distribution of marsupials.

#### Review Questions
1. What two types of dating can be used on fossils, and what is the key difference between them?
2. Why is Archaeopteryx significant as a transitional fossil?
3. How do vestigial structures in living organisms provide evidence consistent with the fossil record?
