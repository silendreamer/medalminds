#### High-Yield Facts
- Linnaeus = **Carl Linnaeus** (Swedish, 1707–1778); developed binomial nomenclature and modern classification
- Classification hierarchy (broadest → narrowest): **Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species**
- Mnemonic: **D**ear **K**ing **P**hillip **C**ame **O**ver **F**or **G**ood **S**oup
- Three domains: **Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya**
- Archaea cell walls: **no peptidoglycan** (unlike bacteria)
- Five vertebrate classes: **fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals**
- Largest animal phylum: **Arthropoda**
- Scientific names: **genus capitalized, species lowercase**, always italicized
- Biological species concept: can **interbreed and produce fertile offspring**

#### Bowl-Style Toss-Up Stems
- "This Swedish botanist developed the system of binomial nomenclature…" → **Carl Linnaeus**
- "In this naming system, the first part of an organism's name indicates its genus…" → **binomial nomenclature**
- "This domain of prokaryotes is more closely related to eukaryotes than to bacteria, based on molecular evidence…" → **Archaea**
- "Organisms in this vertebrate class are ectothermic, lay amniotic eggs, and have dry scaly skin…" → **Reptiles**

#### Common Traps
- Domain Archaea ≠ Domain Bacteria (different cell wall chemistry, different ribosomal RNA)
- "Reptiles" in modern phylogenetics includes birds — but in traditional taxonomy, birds are separate
- Fungi are NOT plants — they have chitin cell walls (not cellulose) and are heterotrophs

#### Review Questions
1. What is the correct format for a binomial name? Give an example.
2. Why are Archaea placed in a separate domain from Bacteria?
3. What is the mnemonic for the eight levels of classification?
