#### The Cholodny-Went Experiment (Classic Bowl Question)
Went isolated auxin from decapitated coleoptile tips and showed it caused bending. This proved that a chemical (auxin) causes phototropism, not a mechanical signal. Key fact: auxin on one side causes bending toward the light source (toward high auxin on the shaded side). Know this experiment by name and result.

#### Phototropism vs. Gravitropism vs. Hydrotropism
Common bowl question: "A root bends downward toward moisture and against light — which tropism is this?" Answer: positive gravitropism (gravity dominates) and negative phototropism, but hydrotropism (toward water) might also be operating. The strongest stimulus usually wins, but multiple tropisms can interact.

#### Starch-Statolith Hypothesis Specifics
Key facts: amyloplasts are heavier than cytoplasm and sediment downward; statocytes are root-cap cells containing amyloplasts; sedimentation applies pressure to mechanoreceptors; this triggers signal transduction and auxin redistribution. The hypothesis predicts that preventing amyloplast settling should eliminate gravitropism (true in experiments).

#### Red/Far-Red Light Ratio in Photoperiodism
Shaded plants under trees receive more far-red light (red light is filtered out by chlorophyll above). Phytochrome PR/PFR ratio shifts, signaling "shade." Plants respond with shade-avoidance syndrome. This explains why forest understory plants are tall and thin — they've evolved to escape shade even at the cost of fragility.

#### Ethylene and Ripening, Abscission, Senescence
Ethylene is the "ripening hormone" — it promotes fruit ripening, flower senescence, and leaf abscission (dropping). High ethylene causes competitive stress responses. This is why rotting fruit "ripens" nearby fruit (ethylene diffuses and triggers ripening in neighbors). Key distinction: gibberellins promote growth; ethylene accelerates maturation and senescence.
