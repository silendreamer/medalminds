#### Drought Stress and ABA Signaling
Drought increases abscisic acid (ABA) levels. ABA closes stomata, reducing water loss. It also triggers root growth to seek water. High ABA levels cause wilting as a protective mechanism — a wilted plant loses less water. Prolonged drought kills most plants, but survivors quickly recover photosynthesis when water returns.

#### Heat Stress: HSP Production
High temperatures denature proteins and damage chloroplasts. Plants respond by producing heat-shock proteins (HSPs) that refold damaged proteins and provide cytoprotection. Short-term heat increases HSP production, improving heat tolerance. Repeated mild heat stress acclimate plants to survive hotter conditions.

#### Cold Stress and Dormancy Induction
Cold temperatures trigger dormancy in many plants. Short days + cold temperatures = deep dormancy. During dormancy, growth stops and cell sap concentrates, lowering the freezing point. Sugars accumulate as anti-freeze compounds. Hardy plants can survive temperatures far below their freezing point while dormant.

#### Mechanical Stress and Thigmomorphogenesis
Strong winds or physical contact cause plants to reduce height, increase stem thickness, and produce more roots — thigmomorphogenesis. This response involves ethylene signaling. A "windy" plant is shorter and more robust, better surviving strong wind. This response trades growth for strength and stability.

#### Disease and Pathogen-Triggered Responses
When pathogens attack, plants detect them via pattern recognition receptors and produce salicylic acid (SA). SA triggers systemic acquired resistance (SAR) — immune responses spread throughout the plant. Plants can "remember" past pathogens and respond faster to future challenges, similar to immune memory in animals.
