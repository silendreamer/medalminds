#### Seedling Stage (weeks 1-4)
The seedling establishes its root system and grows its first true leaves. Cotyledons provide initial nutrients but fade as the plant photosynthesizes. The seedling is vulnerable and requires consistent moisture, light, and protection from pests. Many seedlings die during this stage.

#### Vegetative Growth (weeks 4 - months)
The plant grows leaves and stems, expanding its above-ground structure and root system. Photosynthesis now provides all energy. In annuals, this stage may last weeks. In perennials, it may last years. The plant accumulates reserves in roots, bulbs, or wood.

#### Flowering Stage (weeks to months depending on species)
Environmental cues (day length, temperature, moisture) trigger flowering. The plant produces flowers to attract pollinators. Flower production is energetically expensive, so plants only flower when reserves are adequate. Annual plants flower once; perennials flower repeatedly.

#### Pollination and Fertilization (hours to weeks)
Pollinators visit flowers, transferring pollen. Pollen tubes grow, sperm reaches the egg, double fertilization occurs. Some plants self-pollinate, but cross-pollination produces more genetic diversity.

#### Seed Development and Dispersal (weeks to months)
Seeds develop inside fruits. Once mature, fruits and seeds are dispersed by wind, water, or animals. Seed dispersal spreads the species, reducing local competition. An annual plant completes its life cycle here. A perennial plant persists and flowers again.

#### In Perennials: The Cycle Repeats
Perennial plants live for years or decades. They produce seeds year after year. Some die after one seed production (monocarpic); others die as they age (senescent). Perennials can also reproduce asexually, spreading through runners, bulbs, or fragmentation.

2. What environmental cues trigger flowering?
3. How do annuals differ from perennials?
