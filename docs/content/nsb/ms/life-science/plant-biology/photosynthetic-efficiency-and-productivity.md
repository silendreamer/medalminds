#### Gross vs. Net Photosynthetic Productivity
Gross Primary Productivity (GPP) is total glucose made by photosynthesis. Net Primary Productivity (NPP) is GPP minus glucose lost to respiration — the energy available for growth and reproduction. In most plants, respiration uses 30-60% of gross photosynthesis.

#### Factors Limiting Photosynthesis
Light, CO₂, temperature, and water all limit photosynthesis. Whichever is scarcest becomes the limiting factor. In a forest, light is limiting. In a closed greenhouse, CO₂ may be limiting. In the desert, water is limiting. Identifying the limiting factor helps explain why plants grow slowly in certain conditions.

#### C3, C4, and CAM Plants
C3 plants (wheat, rice, beans) fix CO₂ directly into a 3-carbon compound. They are less efficient in hot, dry conditions because photorespiration wastes energy. C4 plants (corn, sugarcane) concentrate CO₂ before fixing it, reducing photorespiration — very efficient in heat. CAM plants (cacti) open stomata at night when it's cool, storing CO₂ to use during the day.

#### Why Efficiency Matters
Agricultural productivity depends on photosynthetic efficiency. Crop yields increase when light, water, CO₂, and temperature are optimized. Genetic engineering aims to increase C3 plant efficiency toward C4 levels, which could boost global food production.

#### Competition for Light in Ecosystems
In forests, tall trees shade understory plants, reducing their available light. This creates a strong selection pressure for shade tolerance (large, thin leaves) or rapid height growth to reach the canopy. Light competition shapes forest structure and biodiversity.
