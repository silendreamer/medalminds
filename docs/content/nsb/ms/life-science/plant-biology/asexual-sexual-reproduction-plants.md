#### Asexual Reproduction: No Sex Involved
Asexual reproduction produces offspring genetically identical to the parent (clones). Methods include fragmentation (a piece breaks off and grows), vegetative propagation (runners, bulbs, tubers), and budding. Potatoes, strawberries, and many ferns reproduce asexually.

#### Advantages of Asexual Reproduction
Asexual reproduction is fast — no need to find a mate or wait for pollination. It produces many copies of a successful genotype. Clones are guaranteed to have the parent's traits. This is why farmers propagate valuable crop varieties asexually.

#### Disadvantages of Asexual Reproduction
All clones are identical, so genetic diversity is zero. If a disease or pest evolves to attack the genotype, all clones are equally vulnerable. A monoculture of asexually reproduced plants is a disease waiting to happen — the Irish Potato Famine happened partly because potatoes were asexually reproduced clones.

#### Sexual Reproduction: Combining Genetic Material
Sexual reproduction involves a male gamete (pollen) and female gamete (ovule) fusing to form a zygote. The offspring has genetic material from both parents, making it genetically unique. This genetic recombination creates variation in the offspring.

#### Advantages of Sexual Reproduction
Genetic variation increases the chance that some offspring will survive new diseases, pests, or environmental conditions. Variation is the raw material for evolution. Sexual reproduction is slower but produces more robust, adaptable offspring.

#### Life Cycles: Haploid and Diploid Stages
In plants, both sexual and asexual reproduction can occur in a single life cycle. Flowering plants produce seeds (sexual) but can also propagate vegetatively (asexual). This flexibility is a major reason plants are so successful.
