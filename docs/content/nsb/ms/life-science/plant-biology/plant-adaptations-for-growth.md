#### Desert Plants: Water-Saving Adaptations
Cacti have thick, waxy cuticles to reduce water loss and few stomata that open only at night. Their leaves are modified into spines to minimize surface area. Roots spread wide and shallow to capture rare rainfall. Example: Saguaro cactus survives Arizona's heat with these features.

#### Aquatic Plants: Floating and Submerged Strategies
Water lilies have floating leaves with waxy tops and stomata on the upper surface only. Submerged plants like pondweed lack a thick cuticle since water surrounds them. Some aquatic plants have air-filled spaces (aerenchyma) for buoyancy. These adaptations let plants thrive in water where oxygen is scarce.

#### Shade Plants: Maximum Light Capture
Plants in forests have large, thin leaves to capture dim light. They grow tall to reach canopy light or spread leaves horizontally to intercept any available light. Example: Hostas have enormous leaves and live on forest floors beneath trees.

#### Climbing Plants: Structures for Support
Ivy uses adhesive rootlets to cling to walls. Peas use tendrils (modified stems) to twine around supports. Clematis uses leaf petioles as graspers. These adaptations allow weak-stemmed plants to reach light by climbing stronger structures.
