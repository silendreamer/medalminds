#### Auxin: Growth and Tropism
Auxin (mainly indoleacetic acid, IAA) promotes cell elongation in shoots and inhibits growth in roots. It's produced in shoot tips and transported downward. Unidirectional light causes auxin to accumulate on the shaded side, promoting cell elongation there and causing phototropism. Auxin is used commercially as a rooting hormone.

#### Gibberellin: Stem Elongation and Seed Germination
Gibberellins promote stem elongation and break dormancy in seeds. High gibberellin levels trigger germination, growth of new leaves, and flowering. Dwarf pea plants were used by Mendel; some dwarf mutants are deficient in gibberellin. Adding gibberellin makes dwarf plants grow tall — Mendel didn't know this!

#### Cytokinin: Cell Division and Senescence Delay
Cytokinins promote cell division (especially in roots) and delay leaf senescence (aging). A cytokinin-to-auxin ratio determines whether cells divide (high cytokinin) or elongate (high auxin). Cytokinins are produced mainly in roots and transported upward, coordinating root and shoot growth.

#### Ethylene: Fruit Ripening and Stress Response
Ethylene gas promotes fruit ripening, accelerates senescence, and triggers stress responses. Apples and bananas produce ethylene to ripen. Ethylene levels increase when plants are stressed (flooding, disease, drought). High ethylene can cause leaves to drop and flowers to wilt — a protective response to severe stress.

#### Abscisic Acid: Stress and Dormancy
Abscisic acid (ABA) accumulates during drought, promoting stomatal closure to reduce water loss. It also induces dormancy in seeds and buds, preventing germination during unfavorable seasons. ABA levels increase when plants wilt; this triggers root growth to find water. It's sometimes called the "stress hormone."
