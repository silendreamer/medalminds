#### Light Sensing: Phototropin Protein
Phototropin is a blue-light receptor protein in plant cells. When blue light hits phototropin (especially directional blue light from one side), it activates a signal transduction cascade inside the cell. This cascade amplifies the light signal, allowing the plant to sense the direction of the light source.

#### Auxin Redistribution: The Cholodny-Went Hypothesis
When a shoot tip receives directional light, the light-sensing cells asymmetrically pump auxin toward the shaded side. Auxin accumulates on the shaded side and is transported downward in the ensheathing tissues. High auxin concentration on the shaded side promotes cell elongation there, bending the shoot toward the light.

#### Differential Cell Elongation
Cells on the shaded side elongate more than cells on the lit side, causing the shoot to bend toward the light. This bending is a mechanical result of hormone-driven differences in cell elongation. Roots show the opposite — they bend away from light (negative phototropism) because high auxin inhibits root cell elongation.

#### Time Course of Phototropism
Phototropism takes hours to develop. Light sensing occurs quickly (minutes), but cell elongation takes hours. The process is reversible early on — if light direction changes, auxin redistributes and the bend changes direction. The response is elegant: a simple mechanism creates precise directional growth.

#### Evolutionary Advantage
Phototropism is adaptive: shoots reaching light maximize photosynthesis, improving energy capture. The mechanism is so fundamental that all plants show phototropism. It's one of the most studied tropisms, making it a frequent Science Bowl question — know the Cholodny-Went hypothesis by name.
