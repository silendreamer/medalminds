#### Photosynthesis Makes Glucose; Respiration Uses It
Photosynthesis stores energy in glucose. Respiration releases that energy to make ATP, which powers all plant activities. The simplified respiration equation is the reverse of photosynthesis: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + energy (ATP).

#### Glycolysis: Breaking Down Glucose
Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm and splits one glucose into two pyruvate molecules, releasing a small amount of ATP and electrons (carried by NADH). This is the first stage, common to both aerobic and anaerobic respiration.

#### The Citric Acid Cycle and Electron Transport
In mitochondria, pyruvate enters the citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle), producing more NADH and FADH₂. These electron carriers donate electrons to the electron transport chain, where energy powers ATP synthesis. This is where most ATP is made — one glucose yields about 30-32 ATP.

#### Why Plants Need Respiration
Plants use ATP for growth, cell division, active transport, protein synthesis, and stress response. Roots especially rely on respiration since they cannot photosynthesize in the dark soil. At night, all plant cells rely entirely on stored glucose and respiration to power activities.

#### Respiration Rate and Temperature
Respiration increases with temperature (roughly doubling for every 10°C increase, until proteins denature). In warm climates, plants respire faster, using more glucose. Cool nights favor plant growth by reducing respiration, so stored glucose can accumulate for growth rather than being burned for heat.
