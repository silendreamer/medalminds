#### Dermal Tissue: The Plant's Skin
The epidermis is a single layer of tightly packed cells covering leaves, stems, and roots. It protects the plant and reduces water loss through a waxy cuticle. Stomata, found mainly on leaf undersides, are tiny pores surrounded by guard cells that open and close to control gas exchange and water loss.

#### Ground Tissue: Storage and Support
Ground tissue (mesophyll in leaves, cortex in roots and stems) stores food, provides structural support, and conducts photosynthesis. In leaves, palisade mesophyll (tall cells just under the epidermis) absorbs light, while spongy mesophyll (loose cells with air spaces) facilitates gas exchange.

#### Vascular Tissue: Transport System
The two vascular tissues are xylem (transports water and minerals upward) and phloem (transports sugars downward). Xylem cells are dead at maturity, forming hollow tubes. Phloem cells are alive and have sieve-tube elements connected by perforated sieve plates for sugar transport.

#### How Tissues Work Together
The dermal tissue protects and controls what enters/exits. Ground tissue provides structure and processes materials. Vascular tissue distributes water and food. Together, these three tissue systems enable plants to live in varied environments.

#### Plant Tissue Organization in Different Organs
Leaves have epidermis on top and bottom, mesophyll in the middle, and veins (vascular tissue) running through. Roots have epidermis on the outside, cortex for storage, and a central vascular cylinder. Stems have a ring of vascular bundles surrounded by cortex.
