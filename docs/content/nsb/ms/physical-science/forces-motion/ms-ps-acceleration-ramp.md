#### Free Fall Acceleration
Near Earth's surface, gravity accelerates all objects downward at 9.8 m/s² (≈ 10 m/s²) regardless of mass — a feather and a hammer fall at the same rate in a vacuum (demonstrated on the Moon by astronaut Dave Scott in 1971). In free fall, after 1 second an object moves at ~10 m/s; after 2 seconds, ~20 m/s; after 3 seconds, ~30 m/s. The formula v = g × t gives velocity (with g ≈ 10 m/s²).

#### Objects on Ramps
On a frictionless inclined plane, gravity pulls the object straight down, but the ramp redirects the net force along the slope. A steeper ramp gives more acceleration; a gentler ramp gives less. At 90° (vertical), the object is in free fall at 9.8 m/s². At 0° (horizontal), no component of gravity acts along the ramp — zero acceleration. This is why Galileo used ramps to slow down free fall and measure acceleration accurately.

#### Calculating with g = 10 m/s²
A ball is dropped from rest. After 3 s: v = 10 × 3 = 30 m/s. Distance fallen: d = ½gt² = ½ × 10 × 9 = 45 m. Science Bowl often rounds g to 10 m/s² to keep arithmetic clean. Always use 10 unless the problem specifies 9.8.

#### Review Questions
1. A rock is dropped from a cliff. What is its speed after 4 seconds (use g = 10 m/s²)?
2. How far does the rock fall in those 4 seconds?
3. Why does a heavy bowling ball and a light tennis ball hit the ground at the same time when dropped in a vacuum?
