#### What Is Gravity?
Gravity is an attractive force between any two masses. Every object in the universe pulls on every other object. Near Earth's surface, gravity is approximately constant: g = 9.8 m/s² downward. The weight of an object equals W = mg. The more mass an object has, the greater its weight. Gravity is what keeps the Moon orbiting Earth, Earth orbiting the Sun, and your feet on the ground.

#### Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation
F_gravity = G × m₁ × m₂ / r², where G is the gravitational constant (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²), m₁ and m₂ are the two masses, and r is the distance between their centers. Key takeaways: (1) Force increases with mass — double either mass, double the force. (2) Force decreases with distance squared — double the distance, force drops to one-fourth. This inverse-square relationship is critical for Science Bowl.

#### Gravity Across the Solar System
Gravitational acceleration varies by planet. On the Moon: g ≈ 1.6 m/s² (about 1/6 of Earth's). On Mars: g ≈ 3.7 m/s². On Jupiter: g ≈ 24.8 m/s². An astronaut who weighs 700 N on Earth weighs only ~117 N on the Moon. Their mass doesn't change — just their weight. Science Bowl often asks you to calculate weight on other planets.

#### Review Questions
1. How does gravitational force change if the distance between two objects doubles?
2. A 60 kg astronaut is on the Moon (g = 1.6 m/s²). What is their weight?
3. Why doesn't the Moon fall into Earth, even though gravity pulls it toward Earth?
