#### What Is Acceleration?
Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity: a = Δv / t = (v_final − v_initial) / t. The SI unit is meters per second squared (m/s²). An object accelerates when it speeds up, slows down, or changes direction — because all three change velocity. A car going from 0 to 20 m/s in 4 seconds has acceleration = (20 − 0)/4 = 5 m/s². Gravity near Earth's surface accelerates falling objects at 9.8 m/s² downward (often rounded to 10 m/s² in bowl problems).

#### Positive vs. Negative Acceleration
When acceleration and velocity point in the same direction, the object speeds up. When they point in opposite directions, the object slows down — this is called deceleration (or negative acceleration). A braking car has negative acceleration: if v goes from 30 m/s to 0 m/s in 6 s, a = (0 − 30)/6 = −5 m/s². The magnitude is 5 m/s², and the negative sign means it's slowing down.

#### Acceleration and Direction Change
A car moving in a circle at constant speed is still accelerating — because direction changes constantly. This centripetal acceleration always points toward the center of the circle. On a roller coaster loop, you feel pressed into your seat at the bottom (centripetal acceleration points up) and lighter at the top (centripetal acceleration points down). Understanding that "constant speed ≠ zero acceleration" is a classic Science Bowl trap.

#### Graphing Motion
On a position-time graph, slope = velocity. On a velocity-time graph, slope = acceleration. A horizontal line on a v-t graph means zero acceleration (constant velocity). A straight diagonal line means constant acceleration. The area under a v-t graph gives displacement. Science Bowl sometimes shows a graph and asks you to identify what's happening — learn to read these at a glance.

#### Review Questions
1. A bicycle speeds up from 4 m/s to 10 m/s in 3 seconds. What is its acceleration?
2. Why is a car turning a corner at constant speed still accelerating?
3. On a velocity-time graph, what does the slope represent?
