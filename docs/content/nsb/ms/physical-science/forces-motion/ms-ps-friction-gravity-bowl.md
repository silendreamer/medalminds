#### Bowl-Ready Facts
- g on Earth: 9.8 m/s² (use 10); Moon: 1.6 m/s²; Mars: 3.7 m/s²; Jupiter: 24.8 m/s²
- Weight (N) = mass (kg) × g; mass never changes, weight does
- Gravity: inverse-square — double distance, 1/4 force
- Friction: doesn't depend on area; static > kinetic; depends on normal force and μ
- Normal force on flat surface = weight; on incline = weight × cosθ

#### Toss-Up Practice
- "For 10 points, what type of friction acts on a book sitting still on a tilted ramp?" — **Static friction**
- "For 10 points, if the distance between two masses doubles, the gravitational force between them becomes what fraction of the original?" — **One-fourth**
- "For 10 points, what is the weight of a 20 kg object on Earth, in newtons?" — **196 N (accept 200 N)**
- "For 10 points, this law states that every mass attracts every other mass with a force proportional to their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them." — **Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation**

#### Review Questions
1. A 5 kg object is on the Moon (g = 1.6 m/s²). What is its weight? Its mass?
2. Static friction on a box is at most 40 N. The kinetic friction is 25 N. If you push with 35 N, does the box move?
3. Name the constant G in Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation and give its approximate value.
