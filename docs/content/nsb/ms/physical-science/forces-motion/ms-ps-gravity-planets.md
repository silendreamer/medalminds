#### Gravity Comparison Problems
An astronaut has a mass of 80 kg. Find their weight on Earth, Moon, Mars, and Jupiter. Earth (g = 9.8 m/s²): W = 80 × 9.8 = 784 N. Moon (g = 1.6 m/s²): W = 80 × 1.6 = 128 N. Mars (g = 3.7 m/s²): W = 80 × 3.7 = 296 N. Jupiter (g = 24.8 m/s²): W = 80 × 24.8 = 1984 N. Mass stays 80 kg throughout — only the weight changes.

#### Using the Inverse-Square Law
If you move from Earth's surface (distance r from center) to twice that distance (2r above Earth's center), the gravitational force becomes: F ∝ 1/(2r)² = 1/4r² — one-fourth as strong. At 3r: 1/9 as strong. Gravity never reaches zero, but it decreases rapidly with distance. At the International Space Station (roughly 400 km altitude, r ≈ 1.06 × Earth radius), gravity is still about 89% as strong as at the surface — not zero! Astronauts are in free fall, which is why they feel weightless.

#### Why Astronauts Feel Weightless
Astronauts on the ISS are not outside Earth's gravity — they're in free fall around Earth. Both the station and astronauts fall together at the same rate, so there's no contact force between them (no "floor pushing up"). This is apparent weightlessness. True weightlessness only occurs infinitely far from all masses.

#### Review Questions
1. If a scale reads 600 N on Earth, what would the same person weigh on Mars (g = 3.7 m/s²)?
2. If gravity on a new planet is 4 times Earth's, how does the orbital period of a low satellite compare?
3. Why do astronauts on the ISS float, even though Earth's gravity still acts on them?
