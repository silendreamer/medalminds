#### Quick Concept Check
- Friction opposes relative motion — it always points opposite to the direction an object moves (or would move).
- Weight = mg; mass stays constant; weight changes with g.
- Gravity follows an inverse-square law: 4× distance → 1/16 force.
- Normal force on a flat surface = weight; on an incline, it's less (mg cosθ).
- Static friction > kinetic friction for the same surface pair.

#### Practice Problems
1. A 10 kg box is on a flat surface. Normal force = ? Weight = ? (Both = 98 N ≈ 100 N)
2. You move twice as far from Earth's center. Gravity force is now what fraction of original? (1/4)
3. Kinetic friction on a box is 20 N. The box weighs 100 N. Coefficient of kinetic friction ≈ ? (μ = F/N = 20/100 = 0.2)
4. An object weighs 300 N on Mars (g = 3.7 m/s²). What is its mass? (m = W/g = 300/3.7 ≈ 81 kg)

#### Review Questions
1. A box sits on a ramp. Which is greater: static friction or kinetic friction (for the same μ and normal force)?
2. How much does a 5 kg object weigh on Earth? On the Moon?
3. Two identical balls are dropped from the same height in a vacuum. One is lead, one is foam. Which hits first?
