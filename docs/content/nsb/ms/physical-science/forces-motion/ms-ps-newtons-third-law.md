#### The Law of Action-Reaction
Newton's Third Law: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. When object A exerts a force on object B, object B exerts an equally large force in the opposite direction on object A. The two forces are always: (1) equal in magnitude, (2) opposite in direction, (3) acting on different objects. A rocket pushes exhaust gases backward; the gases push the rocket forward. Your feet push backward on the ground; the ground pushes your feet forward (that's how walking works).

#### Why Don't Action-Reaction Pairs Cancel?
Students often ask: if forces are equal and opposite, why does anything move? The key: action-reaction forces act on **different** objects. When you push a book, the book pushes back on your hand — but the book and your hand are separate objects. Newton's Second Law (F = ma) applies to each object separately. The net force on the book is the force you applied; the book's reaction force acts on you, not on itself.

#### Examples to Know
- Gun and bullet: the bullet is pushed forward; the gun recoils backward.
- Swimmer pushes water back; water pushes swimmer forward.
- Earth pulls you down with gravity; you pull Earth up with equal force (but Earth barely moves because M is enormous).
- Balloon released: air rushes out one end; balloon moves the other way.

3. Why doesn't the Earth visibly accelerate toward you when you jump, even though you pull it with the same force it pulls you?
