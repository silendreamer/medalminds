#### Defining Mechanical Advantage
Mechanical advantage (MA) = output force ÷ input force = F_resistance / F_effort. If you apply 50 N to a lever and lift 200 N, MA = 200/50 = 4. The machine multiplied your force by 4. Mechanical advantage greater than 1 means the machine amplifies force (you use less force over a longer distance). MA less than 1 means you use more force but gain speed or distance. MA exactly 1 means the machine only changes direction (like a single fixed pulley).

#### Ideal Mechanical Advantage (IMA)
IMA is calculated from distances, not forces: IMA = distance of effort / distance of resistance = d_e / d_r. For an inclined plane: IMA = length of ramp / height. A 5 m ramp reaching a 1 m ledge: IMA = 5. For a lever: IMA = effort arm / resistance arm. A lever with a 3 m effort arm and 1 m resistance arm: IMA = 3.

#### Actual Mechanical Advantage (AMA)
AMA uses real forces and is always less than IMA due to friction: AMA = F_out / F_in. Efficiency = AMA/IMA × 100%. A pulley system with IMA = 4 but AMA = 3 has efficiency = 3/4 × 100% = 75%.

#### Review Questions
1. A lever requires 40 N of effort to lift a 160 N load. What is the mechanical advantage?
2. A ramp is 6 m long and 2 m high. What is its ideal mechanical advantage?
3. If the IMA of a machine is 5 and its efficiency is 80%, what is its AMA?
