#### How Charge Builds Up
**Charging by friction (triboelectric effect):** Rubbing two materials together transfers electrons from one to the other. Which gains or loses depends on the triboelectric series. Rubber (balloon) gains electrons from wool. Glass rubbed with silk loses electrons to silk. **Charging by conduction:** touching a charged object transfers charge directly. **Charging by induction:** a charged object brought near (not touching) a neutral object causes charge redistribution — no electrons transfer, but the neutral object develops areas of positive and negative charge (polarization).

#### Sparks and Discharge
Charge builds up until the electric force is strong enough to push electrons through air. Air is normally an insulator, but a large enough voltage ionizes it, allowing current to flow briefly — a spark. Common examples: shock from a doorknob after shuffling on carpet; lightning (discharge between cloud and ground or between clouds). A single lightning bolt discharges 5–30 coulombs over 30 microseconds.

#### Grounding
Grounding means connecting a charged object to the Earth (or a large conductor), which has an effectively infinite capacity to absorb or supply charge. This neutralizes the object. Lightning rods work by grounding — they provide a low-resistance path for lightning to reach the ground safely, protecting buildings. Electronic equipment is grounded to prevent static buildup from damaging sensitive circuits.
