#### Light as an Electromagnetic Wave
Light is a form of electromagnetic (EM) radiation — a transverse wave of oscillating electric and magnetic fields. Unlike sound, light requires no medium and travels through vacuum at c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s (approximately 300,000 km/s). Nothing in the universe can travel faster. Light travels slightly slower in transparent materials: in glass, about 2 × 10⁸ m/s; in water, about 2.25 × 10⁸ m/s. The ratio of light speed in vacuum to speed in a medium is the index of refraction (n = c/v).

#### Visible Light and the Color Spectrum
Visible light occupies a narrow range of the EM spectrum with wavelengths from about 380 nm (violet) to 750 nm (red). **ROY G BIV** in order of increasing wavelength (decreasing frequency and energy): Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet. Red has the longest wavelength; violet has the shortest. White light is a combination of all visible wavelengths. Prisms separate white light into its component colors by refraction (each wavelength bends by a different amount).

#### The Dual Nature of Light
Light behaves as both a wave and a particle (photon). For most MS-level purposes, treat light as a wave. The photon model is needed to explain the photoelectric effect and color of light emitted by hot objects. Each photon carries energy E = hf, where h is Planck's constant and f is frequency. Higher frequency light (violet/UV) carries more energy per photon than lower frequency light (red/IR).
