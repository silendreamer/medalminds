#### Core Facts to Know Cold
- Sound: longitudinal mechanical wave; needs a medium
- Speed in air: 343 m/s (20°C); faster in liquids and solids
- Pitch ↔ frequency; Loudness ↔ amplitude
- Doppler: approaching source → higher pitch; receding → lower pitch
- Decibels: +10 dB = 10× more intensity (logarithmic)
- Ultrasound > 20,000 Hz; Infrasound < 20 Hz

#### Mixed Practice
1. A siren is at 1,000 Hz. As it approaches, does the observed frequency increase or decrease? (Increase)
2. Two sounds: 80 dB and 90 dB. Which is 10× more intense? (90 dB)
3. Sound travels from air into steel. Does the frequency change? (No — frequency stays the same; speed and wavelength change)
4. Echolocation pulse returns after 0.04 s. v_sound = 340 m/s. Distance to object? (d = 340 × 0.02 = 6.8 m)

#### Review Questions
1. A guitar string and a flute both play the same note (440 Hz). If both sounds travel through air at 343 m/s, what is the wavelength?
2. Name one application of ultrasound in medicine and one in industry.
3. Why doesn't sound travel through outer space?
