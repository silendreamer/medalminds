#### Worked Example 1: Finding Wavelength
Radio station 101.1 FM broadcasts at 101.1 MHz (1.011 × 10⁸ Hz). Radio waves travel at 3 × 10⁸ m/s (speed of light). Find the wavelength: λ = v/f = (3 × 10⁸) / (1.011 × 10⁸) ≈ 2.97 m. FM radio waves have wavelengths of about 3 meters — much larger than visible light (~500 nm) but much smaller than AM waves (~300 m).

#### Worked Example 2: Period and Frequency
A tuning fork vibrates at 440 Hz (concert A). Find the period: T = 1/f = 1/440 ≈ 0.00227 s = 2.27 ms. Find the wavelength in air (v = 343 m/s): λ = v/f = 343/440 ≈ 0.78 m. This is about 78 cm — roughly the length of a guitar string vibrating at that pitch.

#### Worked Example 3: Ripple Tank
Water waves in a ripple tank have a frequency of 5 Hz and a wavelength of 0.04 m. Speed = 5 × 0.04 = 0.2 m/s. If frequency doubles to 10 Hz (same medium, same speed), new wavelength = 0.2/10 = 0.02 m — wavelength halved as frequency doubled.

#### Review Questions
1. A wave has speed 300 m/s and frequency 150 Hz. Find its wavelength and period.
2. A wave has wavelength 0.5 m and period 0.1 s. Find its frequency and wave speed.
3. If the frequency of a wave doubles while its speed stays constant, what happens to its wavelength?
