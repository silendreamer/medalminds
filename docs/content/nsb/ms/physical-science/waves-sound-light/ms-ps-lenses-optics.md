#### Convex Lenses: Converging
A **convex lens** (thicker in the middle) converges parallel rays of light to a point — the **focal point**. When an object is beyond the focal point, the lens forms a real, inverted image on the other side. Cameras, projectors, and the human eye use convex lenses. The closer the object is to the focal point (from outside), the farther away the image forms. Objects closer than the focal point produce a virtual, magnified image (like a magnifying glass).

#### Concave Lenses: Diverging
A **concave lens** (thinner in the middle) spreads rays apart — a diverging lens. It always forms a virtual, upright, reduced image on the same side as the object. Concave lenses are used to correct nearsightedness (myopia) — the eye focuses incoming rays too soon (before the retina), so a concave lens spreads them first.

#### The Human Eye
The cornea and eye lens (both convex) focus light onto the retina. The eye adjusts focus by changing lens thickness (accommodation). Nearsightedness: eyeball too long → focus falls in front of retina → correct with concave lens. Farsightedness: eyeball too short → focus would fall behind retina → correct with convex lens.
