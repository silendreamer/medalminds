#### Quick-Reference Summary
- Transverse: particles perpendicular to wave travel → light, ripples on water
- Longitudinal: particles parallel to wave travel → sound
- v = fλ; T = 1/f; these ALWAYS hold
- Amplitude ↑ → energy ↑ (proportional to A²)
- Refraction: speed changes → direction changes
- Reflection: angle in = angle out (from normal)
- Constructive interference: amplitudes add; Destructive: amplitudes cancel

#### Mixed Problems
1. f = 1000 Hz, λ = 0.34 m → v = 340 m/s ✓
2. T = 0.002 s → f = 500 Hz ✓
3. A sound wave doubles in amplitude → energy increases by factor of **4**
4. A wave refracts when crossing media → its **speed and wavelength** change, but **frequency** stays constant

#### Review Questions
1. Sound travels faster in steel than in air. What happens to the wavelength of a 500 Hz sound as it moves from air into steel?
2. Which type of wave can travel through a vacuum?
3. A wave reflects off a flat mirror. The angle of incidence is 35°. What is the angle of reflection?
