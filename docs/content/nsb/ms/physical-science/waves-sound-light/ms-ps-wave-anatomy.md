#### Key Wave Measurements
**Wavelength (λ):** The distance from one point on a wave to the next identical point — crest to crest or trough to trough. Measured in meters (m). **Frequency (f):** How many complete wave cycles pass a fixed point per second. Measured in hertz (Hz); 1 Hz = 1 cycle per second. **Amplitude (A):** The maximum displacement from the rest position — the height of a crest or depth of a trough. Amplitude is NOT related to frequency or wavelength.

#### The Wave Speed Formula
Wave speed (v) = frequency (f) × wavelength (λ): **v = fλ**. This is the single most important wave equation. If frequency increases and wave speed stays constant (same medium), wavelength decreases — they are inversely proportional. If a wave has frequency 500 Hz and wavelength 0.68 m, its speed = 500 × 0.68 = 340 m/s (sound in air). Rearrange to find any variable: f = v/λ; λ = v/f.

#### Period: The Inverse of Frequency
The period (T) is the time for one complete wave cycle: T = 1/f. If frequency = 5 Hz, period = 1/5 = 0.2 s. Frequency and period are always reciprocals. A wave with a 0.01 s period has a frequency of 100 Hz. Science Bowl regularly asks you to convert between period and frequency.

#### Energy and Amplitude
The energy carried by a wave is proportional to the square of its amplitude. Double the amplitude → four times the energy. A loud sound has larger amplitude than a quiet one; a bright light has larger amplitude than a dim one. Frequency doesn't determine energy for mechanical waves at the MS level (it does for photons, which is HS content).
