#### Bowl-Ready Facts
- Speed of sound in air ≈ 343 m/s; in water ≈ 1,480 m/s; in steel ≈ 5,100 m/s
- Doppler effect: source approaching → frequency ↑ (blueshift for light); receding → frequency ↓ (redshift)
- Ultrasound > 20,000 Hz; infrasound < 20 Hz; audible: 20 Hz – 20,000 Hz
- Decibel scale is logarithmic; +10 dB = ×10 intensity
- Resonance: driving at natural frequency causes large amplitude response
- Echolocation: bats and dolphins; sonar: submarines and ocean mapping

#### Practice Toss-Ups
- "For 10 points, what term describes the apparent change in frequency of a wave when the source moves relative to the observer?" — **Doppler effect**
- "For 10 points, what type of sound wave has a frequency greater than 20,000 Hz?" — **Ultrasound**
- "For 10 points, in which state of matter does sound travel fastest?" — **Solid**
- "For 10 points, if a star's light is redshifted, is it moving toward or away from Earth?" — **Away (receding)**

#### Review Questions
1. An ambulance siren has frequency 700 Hz at rest. As it approaches, is the observed frequency higher or lower than 700 Hz?
2. What is the approximate speed of sound in air at room temperature?
3. A bat emits ultrasound. What advantage does high frequency give it for echolocation?
