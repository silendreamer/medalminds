#### Toss-Up Patterns

**Pattern 1:**
*"For 10 points, what Greek mathematician discovered that the buoyant force on an object equals the weight of fluid it displaces, allegedly while stepping into a bath?"*
Answer: **Archimedes**. Buzz on "buoyant force equals weight of displaced fluid" or "stepping into a bath."

**Pattern 2:**
*"What is the density of liquid water at 4 degrees Celsius?"*
Answer: **1.00 g/cm³** (or 1 g/mL). Buzz immediately — this is pure recall.

**Pattern 3:**
*"An object has a density of 0.75 g/cm³. Will it float or sink in water, and what fraction of the object will be submerged?"*
Answer: It **floats**; 75% submerged (fractional submergence = object density ÷ fluid density = 0.75/1.00 = 75%).

**Pattern 4:**
*"What principle states that the buoyant force on an object equals the weight of the fluid the object displaces?"*
Answer: **Archimedes' principle**.

#### Quick-Fire Facts
- Ice density: 0.917 g/cm³ → floats in water, ~92% submerged (icebergs!)
- Mercury density: 13.6 g/cm³ → iron (7.87 g/cm³) floats in mercury
- Dead Sea: density ~1.24 g/cm³ → humans easily float
- Neutral buoyancy: density of object = density of fluid (submarine hovering)
- Density of gold: 19.3 g/cm³ (densest common metal)

#### Review Questions
1. What percentage of an iceberg is above the water's surface? (Ice density = 0.917 g/cm³, water = 1.00 g/cm³)
2. Iron floats in liquid mercury. What does this tell you about the density of iron compared to mercury?
3. Name the scientist associated with the discovery of the buoyancy principle, and describe the experiment (real or legendary) associated with the discovery.
