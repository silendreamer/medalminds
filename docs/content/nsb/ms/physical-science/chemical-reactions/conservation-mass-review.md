#### Key Fact Recap
- Law of Conservation of Mass: mass of reactants = mass of products (in a closed system)
- Atoms are rearranged, not created or destroyed
- Named for: Antoine Lavoisier (18th century France)
- This law is why we balance equations

#### Calculation Quick-Check
1. Reactants: 12 g carbon + 32 g oxygen → Products: ? g CO₂ → **44 g**
2. Reactants: 46 g Na + 71 g Cl₂ → Products: ? g NaCl → **117 g**
3. Reactant: 34 g H₂O₂ → Products: 18 g H₂O + ? g O₂ → **16 g**

#### Common Misconceptions
- "Burning destroys mass" → False: gases escape in open system but total mass conserved
- "Conservation of mass = conservation of molecules" → False: molecule count can change; atom count is conserved
- Nuclear reactions: E = mc² means mass converts to energy (mass-energy is conserved, not mass alone — above normal MS scope but good to know)

#### Review Questions
1. Who established the Law of Conservation of Mass and in what century?
2. A gas is produced when a solid reacts with an acid in a open flask. After the reaction, the flask is lighter. Does this violate conservation of mass? Explain.
3. 8 g of hydrogen gas reacts with 64 g of oxygen gas. What is the total mass of water produced?
