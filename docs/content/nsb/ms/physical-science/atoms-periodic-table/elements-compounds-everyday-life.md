#### Compounds in Food and the Body
Nearly everything in biology is chemistry — and chemistry is elements combined into compounds:
- **Water (H₂O)**: ~60% of your body by mass; essential for all biochemistry
- **Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆)**: sugar that powers your cells
- **Oxygen (O₂)**: required for cellular respiration (note: O₂ is an element, not a compound)
- **Carbon dioxide (CO₂)**: waste product of respiration; absorbed by plants in photosynthesis
- **Calcium phosphate (Ca₃(PO₄)₂)**: main mineral in bones and teeth
- **Hemoglobin**: large protein compound in red blood cells containing iron (Fe)

#### Household Chemicals
- Baking soda: sodium bicarbonate, NaHCO₃
- Vinegar: dilute acetic acid, CH₃COOH (also written C₂H₄O₂)
- Bleach: sodium hypochlorite, NaOCl
- Aspirin: acetylsalicylic acid, C₉H₈O₄
- Salt: sodium chloride, NaCl
- Lye/Drano: sodium hydroxide, NaOH

#### Pure Element Uses
- **Gold (Au)**: jewelry, electronics (non-reactive, excellent conductor)
- **Silicon (Si)**: computer chips (semiconductor)
- **Carbon (C)**: graphite in pencils, diamonds, carbon fiber
- **Aluminum (Al)**: cans, aircraft parts (lightweight metal)
- **Copper (Cu)**: electrical wiring (excellent conductor)

#### Review Questions
1. Name the compound that makes up the main mineral in human bones, and give its chemical formula.
2. Oxygen (O₂) and water (H₂O) are both involved in photosynthesis and respiration. Which is an element and which is a compound? How do you know?
3. What element is used in computer chips because of its semiconductor properties?
