#### The Three Subatomic Particles
Atoms are the smallest units of matter that still have the chemical identity of an element. But atoms themselves are made of smaller particles:
- **Proton**: positive charge (+1), found in the nucleus, mass ≈ 1 atomic mass unit (amu)
- **Neutron**: no charge (neutral), found in the nucleus, mass ≈ 1 amu
- **Electron**: negative charge (−1), found in the electron cloud (orbitals) outside the nucleus, mass ≈ 1/1836 amu (essentially negligible)

The number of protons defines the element — change the proton count and you have a different element. The number of protons is the **atomic number**.

#### The Nucleus and Electron Cloud
The **nucleus** is the tiny, dense core at the center of the atom — it contains protons and neutrons. Despite holding nearly all the atom's mass, the nucleus is incredibly small compared to the whole atom. If an atom were the size of a football stadium, the nucleus would be a marble at the center. Most of the atom is empty space occupied by the electron cloud.

**Electrons** occupy regions of space called orbitals outside the nucleus. They move so fast that they form a "cloud" of probability around the nucleus. Electrons are attracted to the positive nucleus by electromagnetic force.

#### Atomic Number vs. Mass Number
- **Atomic number** (Z) = number of protons in the nucleus. This identifies the element.
- **Mass number** (A) = number of protons + number of neutrons. Also called atomic mass (approximately).
- **Number of neutrons** = mass number − atomic number

Example: Carbon has atomic number 6 (6 protons). The most common carbon isotope has mass number 12, so it has 6 neutrons.

In a neutral atom, number of electrons = number of protons (equal positive and negative charges balance out).
