#### Rapid Classification
Classify each as ionic (I) or covalent (C):
- NaCl → **I** (metal + nonmetal)
- H₂O → **C** (nonmetal + nonmetal)
- MgO → **I**
- CO₂ → **C**
- KBr → **I**
- CH₄ → **C**
- CaCl₂ → **I**
- N₂ → **C** (nonpolar covalent)

#### Key Differences: Ionic vs. Covalent
| Property | Ionic | Covalent |
|---|---|---|
| Electron behavior | Transfer | Share |
| Typical components | Metal + nonmetal | Nonmetal + nonmetal |
| State at room temp | Solid | Gas, liquid, or solid |
| Melting point | High | Usually low |
| Conductivity in water | Yes (electrolyte) | Usually no |

#### Formula Prediction Practice
- K⁺ + Br⁻ → KBr
- Ca²⁺ + O²⁻ → CaO
- Al³⁺ + Cl⁻ → AlCl₃

#### Review Questions
1. What type of bond holds the atoms together in water (H₂O)?
2. Why do ionic compounds conduct electricity when dissolved in water?
3. A compound is a solid at room temperature and has a very high melting point. Is it more likely to be ionic or covalent?
