#### What Is Combustion?
Combustion is a rapid chemical reaction between a fuel and oxygen that releases heat and light. For fossil fuels, the fuel is always a hydrocarbon (compound containing carbon and hydrogen). Complete combustion of a hydrocarbon produces only carbon dioxide (CO₂) and water (H₂O): for methane, CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. This reaction is highly exothermic — it releases approximately 890 kJ per mole of methane. The combustion of fossil fuels is fundamentally the reverse of photosynthesis: plants captured solar energy to build carbon compounds; burning releases that stored energy.

#### Balancing Combustion Equations
Every carbon atom in the fuel becomes one CO₂ molecule; every two hydrogen atoms become one H₂O molecule. To balance: (1) Write C atoms first (coefficient on CO₂). (2) Write H atoms (coefficient on H₂O). (3) Balance O atoms last (coefficient on O₂). Examples:
- Methane: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
- Propane: C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O
- Octane (gasoline): 2C₈H₁₈ + 25O₂ → 16CO₂ + 18H₂O

#### Incomplete Combustion: When Oxygen Is Limited
Incomplete combustion occurs when there is insufficient oxygen relative to fuel. Products include carbon monoxide (CO), unburned hydrocarbons, and carbon (soot/particulates), in addition to CO₂ and H₂O. CO is particularly dangerous — it's odorless, colorless, and toxic. It binds to hemoglobin 200× more tightly than oxygen, preventing red blood cells from carrying oxygen. Soot (fine carbon particles) is a health hazard and a potent short-lived climate forcer (warms atmosphere by absorbing sunlight). Car engines with a too-rich fuel mixture (too much fuel, too little air) produce CO and unburned hydrocarbons.

#### Combustion Temperature and Emissions
The temperature of combustion affects which pollutants form. At very high temperatures (>1,800°C), nitrogen from the air (N₂) reacts with oxygen to form nitrogen oxides (NOₓ = NO + NO₂) — these contribute to smog and acid rain. This is why NOₓ emissions increase at higher combustion temperatures, even from burning clean fuels like natural gas. Catalytic converters in cars address this by converting CO, unburned hydrocarbons, and NOₓ to CO₂, H₂O, and N₂ over a platinum-palladium-rhodium catalyst.

#### Science Bowl Combustion Chemistry
Bowl questions on combustion test: (1) identifying combustion products (CO₂ and H₂O for complete combustion); (2) recognizing incomplete combustion produces CO and soot; (3) balancing combustion equations; (4) knowing that CO binds hemoglobin and is toxic. The word "exothermic" should immediately trigger association with combustion.

#### Review Questions
1. Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of propane (C₃H₈).
2. What products form during incomplete combustion that are not present during complete combustion?
3. Why is carbon monoxide (CO) dangerous to humans?
