#### Fossil Fuel Comparison Table

| Property | Coal | Oil | Natural Gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary composition | Carbon (C) | Hydrocarbons (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂) | Methane (CH₄) |
| Origin organism | Land plants | Marine microorganisms | Marine microorganisms |
| Formation era | Carboniferous | Devonian–Cretaceous | Same as oil |
| Primary use | Electricity | Transportation | Electricity, heating |
| CO₂ per kWh (electricity) | Highest | Medium | Lowest |
| SO₂ emissions | High (with S impurities) | Medium | Negligible |
| U.S. share of electricity (~2023) | 17% | <1% | 43% |

#### Key Vocabulary Drill
Define each in one sentence:
- **Kerogen**: solid organic matter in source rocks that converts to oil and gas under heat/pressure
- **Fracking**: hydraulic fracturing — injecting high-pressure fluid to crack shale and release gas
- **Cracking**: breaking large hydrocarbon molecules into smaller, more useful ones in a refinery
- **Scrubber**: device that removes SO₂ from power plant exhaust using limestone
- **BTU**: British Thermal Unit — energy needed to raise 1 pound of water by 1°F (≈1055 J)

#### 12 Must-Know Fossil Fuel Facts
1. All fossil fuels formed from ancient organisms — hence "fossil" fuels
2. Coal: primarily carbon; oil: hydrocarbons; gas: primarily methane (CH₄)
3. Burning releases stored solar energy captured millions of years ago
4. CO₂ from burning is the primary driver of anthropogenic climate change
5. U.S. is world's largest oil and gas producer (2023)
6. Natural gas replaced coal as #1 U.S. electricity source around 2016
7. Methane is ~80× more potent than CO₂ as a greenhouse gas (20-year)
8. Deepwater Horizon (2010): largest U.S. marine oil spill
9. Kingston Fossil Plant (2008): largest U.S. coal ash spill
10. DOE Office of Fossil Energy and Carbon Management oversees R&D
11. Carbon capture and storage (CCS) aims to prevent CO₂ from reaching atmosphere
12. Fossil fuels provide ~80% of global primary energy (2023)
