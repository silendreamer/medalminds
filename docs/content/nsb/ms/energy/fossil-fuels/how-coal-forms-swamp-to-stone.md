#### From Living Plants to Fossil Fuel
Coal forms from the compressed and chemically transformed remains of plants — primarily trees, ferns, and mosses — that lived in swampy forests hundreds of millions of years ago. These plants died and accumulated in oxygen-poor water, preventing full decomposition. Over millions of years, burial under sediment layers provided increasing heat and pressure, gradually transforming the organic matter first to peat, then to progressively higher grades of coal. Coal is classified as a fossil fuel because it is the fossilized carbon of ancient life.

#### The Carboniferous Period: When Coal Was Made
Most of Earth's coal deposits formed during the Carboniferous period, approximately 359–299 million years ago. During this time, vast tropical swamp forests covered much of what is now North America and Europe. The atmospheric oxygen level was much higher than today (~35% vs. ~21%), allowing massive plant growth. Importantly, microorganisms capable of decomposing lignin (the tough structural compound in wood) had not yet evolved, so dead plant matter accumulated rather than rotting — producing enormous peat deposits that eventually became coal.

#### The Coal Formation Sequence
The transformation from plants to coal proceeds in stages, each with higher carbon content and energy density:
- **Peat**: partly decomposed plant matter, ~50% carbon, low energy density, still wet — harvested in Ireland and Finland for fuel
- **Lignite** (brown coal): ~60–70% carbon, soft and crumbly, lowest-grade coal
- **Bituminous** coal: ~70–85% carbon, most abundant type, used in electricity generation and steel production
- **Anthracite**: ~86–97% carbon, hardest and most energy-dense coal, burns very cleanly

#### Where Coal Is Found
The United States holds the world's largest proven coal reserves (~25% of global supply), concentrated in the Powder River Basin (Wyoming/Montana), the Appalachian region (West Virginia, Kentucky), and the Illinois Basin. The Appalachian coalfields formed from Carboniferous forests; Wyoming's Powder River Basin coals are younger (Cretaceous period). China is the world's largest coal producer and consumer; Australia and Indonesia are major exporters.

#### Why Coal Matters for Science Bowl
Science Bowl Energy questions frequently test: (1) the formation sequence peat → lignite → bituminous → anthracite; (2) the Carboniferous period as the primary coal-forming era; (3) coal's chemical composition being primarily carbon; (4) coal classifications by carbon content. The word "bituminous" is a classic toss-up answer.

#### Review Questions
