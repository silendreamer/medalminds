#### Ecosystem Disruption
A dam fundamentally transforms a river from a flowing system to a reservoir, with profound ecological consequences. Coldwater fish that require flowing water for spawning — especially salmon — cannot reach their upstream spawning grounds when a dam blocks their path. Fish ladders (a series of stepped pools that fish can jump through) partially mitigate this but are imperfect solutions for large dams. The Snake River dams in the Pacific Northwest have contributed to the near-extinction of wild salmon runs despite federal salmon recovery programs costing billions of dollars. Below the dam, reduced sediment flow erodes beaches and river deltas; the Nile Delta has retreated significantly since the Aswan High Dam was completed in 1970.

#### Reservoir Emissions and Dam Removal
Reservoirs in tropical regions produce significant methane — a potent greenhouse gas — from decomposition of flooded vegetation and organic material under low-oxygen conditions. Some tropical dams have worse lifecycle greenhouse gas emissions per kilowatt-hour than natural gas plants. In temperate regions, reservoir emissions are much lower. Dam removal has become an increasingly common environmental restoration tool: as aging dams reach the end of their economic life, removing them can restore salmon runs and river ecosystems dramatically. The 2023 removal of four dams on the Klamath River in Oregon and California was the largest dam removal project in U.S. history.
