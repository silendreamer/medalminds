#### The Solar Learning Curve
Solar PV follows a "learning rate" of about 20% cost reduction for every doubling of installed capacity. When the first commercial solar panels shipped in the 1970s, they cost $76 per watt. By 2010, the price was ~$2/watt. By 2023, utility-scale solar costs had fallen to $0.20–$0.30 per watt — a 99.7% price reduction over 50 years, making solar the cheapest source of new electricity in history in most of the world. This rapid cost decline — faster than most energy analysts predicted — has made solar the fastest-growing energy source on Earth, with over 1 terawatt of total installed capacity as of 2023.

#### Emerging Solar Technologies
Perovskite solar cells use a class of crystals (calcium titanium oxide structure) that can be applied as thin films and are cheaper to manufacture than silicon. Lab efficiencies exceed 26%; tandem perovskite-silicon cells have reached 33%. Commercial durability (stability over 25 years) is still being improved. Bifacial panels capture sunlight from both front and back surfaces, using light reflected from the ground beneath them — improving output by 5–15%. Agrivoltaics combines solar panels with agricultural land, placing panels high enough for crops or sheep to coexist beneath them, sharing land between food and energy production.
