#### Most-Tested Efficiency Facts
- "Most efficient large-scale electricity source" → hydroelectric (~90%)
- "Least efficient common light source" → incandescent (~5%)
- "Most efficient motor type" → electric motor (~90%)
- "Betz limit" → theoretical max efficiency for a wind turbine = 59.3%
- "Carnot efficiency" → theoretical max for any heat engine = (1 − T_cold/T_hot) × 100%
- CAFE standards → set fuel economy requirements for cars/trucks
- Energy Star → DOE/EPA certification for efficient appliances
- Cogeneration / CHP → captures waste heat, up to 90% overall efficiency

#### 5 Practice Toss-Up Stems

1. "For 10 points — a motor receives 600 joules and does 480 joules of useful work. What is its efficiency?" → **80%**

2. "For 10 points — what is the approximate efficiency of a typical incandescent light bulb, expressed as a percentage?" → **5% (approximately)**

3. "For 10 points — this theoretical maximum efficiency for a wind turbine, equal to about 59.3 percent, is named for a German physicist." → **Betz limit**

4. "For 10 points — this type of power plant captures exhaust heat from electricity generation to heat buildings or run industrial processes, achieving overall efficiencies of up to 90 percent." → **Cogeneration (or combined heat and power)**

5. "For 10 points — a power plant inputs 1000 megawatts of thermal power and outputs 350 megawatts of electricity. What is its efficiency?" → **35%**

#### Review Questions
1. What does the Betz limit describe, and what is its value?
2. How does a cogeneration plant achieve higher overall efficiency than a conventional power plant?
3. A 70% efficient machine produces 700 J. What was the total energy input?
