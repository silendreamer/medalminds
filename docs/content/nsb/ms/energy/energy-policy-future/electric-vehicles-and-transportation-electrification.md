#### EV Adoption Trends
Global EV sales exceeded 10 million in 2023, ~14% of new car sales. Growth is exponential — doubling every 3-4 years. In some countries (Norway 88%, Germany 15%, China 35%), EVs are >10% of sales. Cost parity with ICE cars is expected within 5 years globally as battery costs continue falling. By 2050, ICE cars could be nearly obsolete.

#### Charging Infrastructure
Charging infrastructure is being deployed rapidly. Public charging networks are growing (Tesla Supercharger, Electrify America, ChargePoint, etc.). Home charging (Level 2, 240V) is common in developed countries. DC fast charging (400+ kW) enables 15-minute top-ups. Adequate charging infrastructure is essential for EV adoption — "range anxiety" is a common barrier. Investments in charging are accelerating, targeting 1 million public chargers by 2030.

#### Grid Integration and Demand Shifting
EVs add electricity demand to grids (~20% increase if all cars were electric). But most charging happens at night (cheap, off-peak hours), so peak load increases less than energy demand. Smart charging can shift EV charging to match wind/solar generation, improving grid utilization. Vehicle-to-grid (V2G) technology allows parked EVs to discharge into the grid during peak hours, providing storage and support. V2G is technically ready but requires standards and utility programs.

#### Battery Production and Recycling
EV batteries are produced in Asia (China 60%, South Korea 20%, others 20%). Supply chain is becoming a geopolitical issue. Battery recycling is essential — recovering lithium, cobalt, nickel reduces mining and extends supply. Recycling recovery rates are 90-95% for value metals. Second-life batteries (80% capacity) are used for stationary storage (grid, buildings). Recycling will eventually provide 30-40% of battery supply.

#### Emissions Benefits Across Grid Carbon Intensity
An EV's lifetime emissions depend on grid carbon intensity. In coal-heavy grids, EV emissions are only 30-40% lower than ICE cars. In clean grids (nuclear, renewables), EV emissions are <5% of ICE cars. As grids decarbonize, EV emissions drop retrospectively — a car that's 20 years old uses cleaner electricity today than it did when new. ICE cars' emissions are fixed (locked in at production).
