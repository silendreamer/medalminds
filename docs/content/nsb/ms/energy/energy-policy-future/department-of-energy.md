#### History and Purpose
The Department of Energy was created by President Jimmy Carter in 1977 in response to the 1973 oil crisis. DOE consolidated energy research from multiple agencies into one department. The mission: secure America's energy independence, advance scientific research, and maintain nuclear security. Today, DOE has ~13,000 federal employees and manages a ~$32 billion budget.

#### Energy Independence and Energy Security
Energy independence means relying less on foreign oil. The U.S. imports ~10% of petroleum today (down from 60% in 1973), partly due to domestic oil/gas production and energy efficiency improvements. Energy security also means protecting energy infrastructure from physical and cyber attacks and diversifying energy sources to avoid over-reliance on any one type.

#### Nuclear Weapons Stewardship
DOE maintains and safeguards the U.S. nuclear weapons arsenal. This is a major responsibility — ensuring weapons are safe, secure, and reliable without actually testing them (U.S. has had a Comprehensive Nuclear Test Ban since 1992). DOE's National Labs conduct classified research supporting this mission.

#### Scientific Innovation and Energy Research
DOE supports basic science and applied research in energy technologies. Research areas include renewable energy (solar, wind, geothermal), battery storage, nuclear power, fusion, and carbon capture. DOE National Labs conduct cutting-edge research that private companies might not fund due to high risk/cost. Many technologies developed by DOE are later commercialized (solar cells, lithium-ion batteries).

#### Cleanup and Environmental Remediation
Legacy nuclear weapons production left contaminated sites across the U.S. DOE spends billions cleaning up these sites — removing radioactive and hazardous materials, stabilizing facilities, and preventing environmental release. The process takes decades; some sites won't be clean for 20+ years.
