#### Business-as-Usual Scenario
If trends continue: fossil fuels remain dominant (70% of energy), energy consumption grows 1-2% annually, global warming reaches 2.8-3°C. Energy poverty persists in developing regions. Renewable energy grows (20-30% of electricity) but can't keep pace with rising demand. Coal is still used widely. This scenario is increasingly unlikely given falling renewable costs and growing climate pressure.

#### Paris-Compatible Scenario (2°C)
To achieve 2°C warming, fossil fuels drop to 10-20% of primary energy by 2050, renewables reach 60-80% of electricity. Energy consumption is flat (efficiency gains offset economic growth). Electrification is widespread (transport, heating, industry). Net zero CO₂ emissions are achieved through deep cuts + removals. This requires urgent, aggressive action starting now.

#### 1.5°C Scenario
More aggressive than 2°C: fossil fuels nearly eliminated by 2050, renewables >90% of electricity, net-negative CO₂ (large-scale removal). Global transformations required: rapid renewable deployment, complete transportation electrification, dramatic building efficiency, industrial transformation, afforestation/rewilding of 15% of land. This scenario is technically possible but requires maximum effort.

#### Post-Scarcity Energy Scenario
If advanced nuclear, fusion, and storage solve energy challenges: abundant, cheap, clean electricity powers everything (transportation, heating, industry, removing CO₂). Energy becomes nearly free (abundant sun and wind). This would transform society — desalination becomes cheap (solving water scarcity), electrofuels become affordable (aviation and shipping decarbonized), material abundance increases. This scenario requires technological breakthroughs and is uncertain.

#### Factors Determining Which Scenario Occurs
Policy (carbon prices, regulations, subsidies), investment (clean energy R&D), technology (breakthrough innovations), and public action (demand for change) will determine which scenario occurs. Current trajectory (1.9°C warming with current policies) is between business-as-usual and 2°C scenarios. Accelerating clean energy adoption and closing coal plants can push toward 1.5-2°C scenarios.

#### Review Questions
1. What percentage of energy comes from renewables in a 2°C scenario?
2. What is a post-scarcity energy scenario?
3. What factors determine which future occurs?
