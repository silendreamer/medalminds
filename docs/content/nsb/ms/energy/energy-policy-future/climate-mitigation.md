#### Decarbonization of Electricity
Electricity is ~25% of total emissions. Transitioning to renewables is essential. Most projections show electricity grids reaching 80-95% renewables by 2050. This requires: massive wind and solar deployment, grid modernization, storage systems, and backup generation (nuclear, natural gas with carbon capture). Wind and solar are now the fastest-growing electricity sources globally.

#### Transportation Electrification
Cars emit ~15% of global CO₂. Electrifying vehicles is essential. Electric vehicles have zero tailpipe emissions, though charging from grid electricity produces emissions (depends on grid carbon intensity). As grids decarbonize, EV emissions drop. Battery costs have fallen 90% in a decade; 50-60% of new cars sold in some regions are now electric. By 2050, ICE cars could be obsolete.

#### Building Efficiency and Electrification
Buildings emit ~30% of CO₂ (heat, cooling, hot water). Efficiency (insulation, air sealing) reduces demand. Electrification replaces gas furnaces with heat pumps, electric water heaters, and induction stoves. This seems backward (using electricity to heat?), but heat pumps are 2-3x more efficient than resistance heating. As grids decarbonize, building electrification eliminates fossil fuel use entirely.

#### Industrial Decarbonization
Industry (cement, steel, chemicals) emits ~30% of CO₂. Some industrial heat is hard to decarbonize — cement requires high-temperature heat for chemistry, not just warming. Solutions include: renewable electricity and heat, hydrogen for high-temperature processes, carbon capture and storage (CCS), and material efficiency (use less cement/steel). Industrial decarbonization is technically feasible but economically challenging.

#### Natural Climate Solutions
Forests, wetlands, and agricultural land absorb CO₂. Protecting and expanding forests sequesters carbon. Regenerative agriculture (cover crops, no-till) builds soil carbon. Wetland restoration sequesters carbon in peat. These solutions are cheaper than technology-based approaches but are limited in scale — they can mitigate 10-20% of emissions, not eliminate them.
