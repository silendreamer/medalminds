#### Pollution Disproportionality
Power plants, refineries, and other industrial facilities are disproportionately sited in low-income and minority communities. Residents of these frontline communities suffer higher pollution exposure, health impacts (asthma, cancer, heart disease), and reduced life expectancy. Example: "Cancer Alley" in Louisiana has petrochemical refineries and significantly higher cancer rates than U.S. average.

#### Climate Impacts on Vulnerable Populations
Climate change impacts (heat waves, flooding, droughts) hit hardest on the poorest and most vulnerable. Low-income communities often live in flood-prone areas (cheaper land), have older housing (less heat/cool efficient), have limited transportation access (vulnerable to heat waves), and have less adaptive capacity. Globally, Small Island Nations and Least Developed Countries face existential threats despite contributing <1% of emissions.

#### Just Transition for Workers
Energy transition (closing coal plants, phasing out natural gas) threatens jobs in coal regions and fossil fuel industries. Just transition policies provide retraining, income support, and economic diversification. Without just transition, communities become entrenched in opposition to climate policies. Some former coal miners have become wind/solar technicians; coal regions (Appalachia, Wyoming) are diversifying toward clean energy.

#### Indigenous Peoples and Land Rights
Indigenous territories often contain valuable resources (oil, minerals, forests). Historically, indigenous peoples are displaced for resource extraction. Today, many indigenous territories have strong climate protection (forests store carbon, biodiversity is high). Supporting indigenous land rights and leadership is recognized as essential for climate and conservation goals.

#### Intersectionality of Climate and Justice
Climate impacts, pollution, and poverty are interconnected. Women in developing nations suffer disproportionate climate impacts (water collection burden increases with drought). Children exposed to pollution suffer health and developmental impacts. LGBTQ and immigrant communities have less political voice in energy decisions. Addressing climate requires addressing these interconnected inequities.

2. Why do low-income communities suffer more climate impacts?
