#### UNFCCC and the United Nations Framework
The UN Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) is the primary international treaty on climate. Nearly all countries (195 parties) participate. Annual Conferences of Parties (COPs) negotiate climate action. Major agreements include: Rio Declaration (1992), Kyoto Protocol (1997, binding emissions limits for developed nations), Paris Agreement (2015, voluntary nationally determined contributions).

#### The Paris Agreement Goals
The Paris Agreement aims to limit warming to 1.5-2°C above pre-industrial levels. To achieve this, global emissions must drop 50% by 2030 and reach net zero by 2050. Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) are each country's climate plans. Current NDCs put the world on track for ~2.7°C warming — insufficient. Many countries are updating NDCs to tighten targets.

#### Net Zero Commitments
~130 countries have net zero targets (UK by 2050, EU by 2050, China by 2060, India by 2070). Net zero means total greenhouse gas emissions = zero, achieved by deep emissions cuts + removals (afforestation, CCUS). The timeline is critical — 1.5°C requires net zero by 2050; 2°C is compatible with 2060-2070. Some critics argue net zero targets are insufficient and too far in the future.

#### Just Transition and Loss and Damage
Wealthy nations' development was powered by fossil fuels. Developing nations are asked to skip fossil fuels and go directly to renewables. Climate justice calls for "just transition" — support for communities and workers affected by energy transition, and climate finance for developing nations to adapt and mitigate. Loss and Damage refers to impacts that can't be adapted to (island nations drowning, crop failures). Compensation is controversial but increasingly recognized as necessary.

#### Climate Finance
Developed nations pledged $100 billion annually for climate action in developing nations (mitigation, adaptation). Actual flows are lower (partially met). Developing nations argue they need $500 billion+ annually. Green bonds (bonds funding climate projects) are growing rapidly, mobilizing private finance. Concessional loans (lower interest rates for green projects) are a hybrid between charity and market rates.
