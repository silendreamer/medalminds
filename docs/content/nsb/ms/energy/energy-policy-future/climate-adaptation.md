#### Coastal Adaptation and Sea-Level Rise
Sea levels are rising ~3.4 mm/year, accelerating. Coastal cities face flooding — Miami, New Orleans, and Venice are already experiencing "sunny day flooding" (high tides cause flooding). Adaptation strategies: seawalls (expensive, $100+ million for a city), relocation of people/infrastructure (very expensive and difficult), nature-based solutions (wetland restoration, oyster reef restoration). Some islands and low-lying countries may face existential threats — entire nations could become uninhabitable.

#### Drought and Water Security
Droughts are intensifying in the Southwest, Mediterranean, and Australia. Reservoirs are declining — Lake Mead and Lake Powell (Colorado River) are at 25-30% capacity. Adaptation: water conservation, groundwater management, wastewater recycling, agricultural efficiency (drip irrigation instead of flood), regional water markets. Some regions may face hard choices — agriculture vs. urban water, agriculture vs. environmental flows.

#### Extreme Weather Resilience
Heat waves, floods, and hurricanes are intensifying. Adaptation: better forecasting and early warning systems, improved building codes (withstand storms), ecosystem-based solutions (mangroves reduce hurricane surge, forests reduce flooding). Emergency preparedness, evacuation routes, and recovery support are essential. Insurance costs are rising for high-risk areas, reflecting increased hazard frequency.

#### Agricultural Adaptation
Farming faces challenges: heat stress on crops, changing precipitation patterns, new pests/diseases moving into warming regions. Adaptation: breeding crops for heat/drought tolerance, shifting crop locations (Canadian prairies becoming suitable for corn), diversifying crops, improving irrigation efficiency. Some regions will benefit (longer growing seasons in high latitudes); others face decline (tropics getting too hot).

#### Climate Justice
Climate change impacts fall hardest on the poorest, least responsible — countries that contributed <1% of historical emissions (Small Island Nations, Bangladesh, Niger) face existential threats. Wealthy countries that benefited from carbon emissions have responsibility to help. Climate justice calls for fair burden-sharing in mitigation and adaptation, loss and damage compensation, and technology transfer to developing nations.
