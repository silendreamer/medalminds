#### Gravity Storage: Lifting Heavy Objects
Gravity storage works like pumped hydro but uses solid masses instead of water. Stacking heavy blocks (concrete, iron) then dropping them releases gravitational PE. Energy is stored mechanically, not chemically. Advantages: no chemical degradation (works forever), simple technology, uses abundant materials. Disadvantages: lower energy density than batteries, requires significant area for towers. Several companies (Energy Vault, Gravitricity) are developing gravity storage; first deployments happening now.

#### Thermal Storage at Massive Scale
Large thermal storage uses molten salt, heated rocks, or ice. Utility-scale thermal storage (10,000+ MWh) stores excess renewable heat/electricity as thermal energy. When demand peaks, the thermal reservoir supplies power. Round-trip efficiency is 40-70% (lower than batteries but costs are much lower — $20-50/kWh vs. $100-200 for batteries). Thermal storage is ideal for shifting daily renewable generation but not for seasonal storage (loses heat over time).

#### Hydrogen as Energy Vector
Hydrogen can be produced by electrolysis (splitting water using electricity). Renewable electricity produces "green hydrogen" with zero emissions. Hydrogen can be compressed for storage (high density by weight), transported through existing pipelines, and burned in turbines for electricity. Advantages: uses existing infrastructure, scalable to GW levels. Disadvantages: round-trip efficiency is 30-40% (electricity→hydrogen→electricity), expensive to produce and store (~$5-10 per kg green hydrogen today).

#### Hydrogen Uses Beyond Power Generation
Hydrogen is essential for steel and ammonia production (fertilizer). Today, hydrogen is produced from natural gas (dirty). Switching to green hydrogen would decarbonize these industries. Hydrogen can fuel aviation and shipping (batteries are too heavy for long-distance flight/sea). Hydrogen heating could replace natural gas furnaces. Hydrogen storage works at multi-month timescales — ideal for seasonal storage when renewables vary (lots of wind in winter, lots of sun in summer).

#### Long-Duration Storage Challenge
Weeks-to-months storage is the unsolved challenge. Renewables vary seasonally — winter has more wind, summer more solar. A grid with 80% renewables needs weeks of storage to buffer seasonal swings. Batteries (hours), pumped hydro (days), and thermal storage (days) are insufficient. Gravity, hydrogen, and compressed air can provide longer storage but need cost reductions to be economically viable. This is the frontier of storage research.
