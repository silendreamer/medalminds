#### Step-by-Step Toward Stability
A decay chain is a sequence of radioactive decays from an unstable parent isotope through several intermediate "daughter" isotopes until a stable end product is reached. Uranium-238 undergoes 8 alpha decays and 6 beta decays before becoming lead-206 — a process that takes billions of years for any given atom. One intermediate in this chain is radon-222, a gas that seeps into homes from uranium-bearing soil and rock. Because radon is a gas (unlike its solid parents), it can be inhaled and deposit alpha-emitting daughters directly in lung tissue. The EPA estimates radon is the second leading cause of lung cancer in the United States after cigarette smoking, causing about 21,000 deaths per year.

#### Why This Matters for Nuclear Waste
Understanding decay chains is essential for nuclear waste management. Spent nuclear fuel contains not just uranium but a complex mixture of fission products and transuranic elements — all radioactive with different half-lives ranging from seconds to millions of years. Safely storing nuclear waste requires containing it until the isotopes decay to stable, non-hazardous levels. Some fission products decay in 30 years; others remain dangerously radioactive for hundreds of thousands of years, which is why deep geological repositories are being considered worldwide.
