#### Three Mile Island (1979)
On March 28, 1979, a pressure relief valve stuck open in Unit 2 of the Three Mile Island plant in Pennsylvania, and operators misread their instruments, allowing the reactor core to overheat and partially melt. About half the core melted. Radioactive gases were released into the containment building. The containment structure worked as designed — very little radiation escaped, and epidemiological studies found no increase in cancer rates in the surrounding population. No one died from radiation. However, the accident shook public confidence in nuclear power, effectively halting new plant construction in the United States for decades, and triggered comprehensive safety reforms.

#### Chernobyl (1986)
The Chernobyl disaster in Soviet Ukraine was far more serious. During a poorly managed safety test on April 26, 1986, operators violated multiple safety protocols on a reactor with a dangerous design flaw (positive void coefficient). The reactor experienced a sudden power surge, leading to two explosions that blew off the reactor roof and exposed the burning graphite core directly to the atmosphere — the only event of its kind. Without a containment building, radioactive contamination spread across Europe. About 30 workers and first responders died acutely from radiation. Thousands of children developed thyroid cancer from radioactive iodine (nearly all survived after treatment). Approximately 350,000 people were permanently evacuated.

#### Fukushima (2011)
On March 11, 2011, Japan's largest recorded earthquake triggered a massive tsunami that overtopped the Fukushima Daiichi plant's seawall, flooding and destroying backup diesel generators. Without power for cooling, three reactor cores melted over the following days. Hydrogen explosions damaged reactor buildings. About 154,000 residents were evacuated; no one died directly from radiation, but the evacuation itself caused significant stress-related health effects. The accident drove Japan to shut down all its nuclear plants (most have since restarted or are restarting), reinforced the importance of passive safety systems, and led to global regulatory reviews.
