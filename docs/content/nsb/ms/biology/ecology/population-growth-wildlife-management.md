#### Minimum Viable Population
The *minimum viable population* (MVP) is the smallest population size at which a species can survive long-term. Too-small populations suffer from *inbreeding depression* (mating between close relatives reduces genetic diversity and increases harmful traits), *demographic stochasticity* (random variation in birth and death rates becomes significant), and vulnerability to catastrophic events. The Florida panther population fell below 30 individuals in the 1990s and began showing genetic defects — crossbreeding with Texas pumas was used to restore genetic diversity.

#### Overpopulation Without Predators
When predators are removed, prey populations can explode. White-tailed deer in the eastern U.S. number around 30 million — largely because wolves and mountain lions were eliminated. Deer overgrazing prevents forest regeneration, harms bird populations, increases tick-borne disease transmission, and causes millions of vehicle collisions annually. Hunting is used as a population management tool in the absence of natural predators.

#### Invasive Species and Population Explosions
Invasive species arrive in new environments without natural predators or diseases. Asian carp in the Mississippi River have no significant predators; they grow rapidly, outcompete native fish for zooplankton, and have spread through much of the river system. Zebra mussels in the Great Lakes filter enormous quantities of phytoplankton, disrupting the food web. Without natural population controls, invasive species can grow exponentially.

#### Review Questions

3. Why do invasive species often show rapid exponential growth in their new environment?
