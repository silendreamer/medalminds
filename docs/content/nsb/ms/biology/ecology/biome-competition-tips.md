#### High-Yield Facts
- **Taiga** = largest terrestrial biome; also called boreal forest; dominated by conifers
- **Tropical rainforest** = highest biodiversity; ~50% of all species; near equator
- **Tundra** = no trees; **permafrost**; <25 cm rain/year
- **Desert** = <25 cm rain/year; can be hot (Sahara) or cold (Gobi, Atacama)
- **Savanna** = tropical grassland with scattered trees; seasonal rainfall; Africa, South America, Australia
- **Chaparral** = hot dry summers, mild wet winters; fire-adapted plants
- **Estuary** = where river meets ocean; among most productive ecosystems
- **Rain shadow** = dry leeward side of mountain; reason for many deserts
- Permafrost = frozen soil that **stores large amounts of carbon**

#### Bowl-Style Toss-Up Stems
- "This biome, found near the Arctic Circle, is characterized by permafrost, a short growing season, and no trees…" → **tundra**
- "This largest terrestrial biome is dominated by coniferous trees and experiences long, cold winters…" → **taiga** (boreal forest)
- "This biome, found where rivers meet the ocean, is among Earth's most productive ecosystems…" → **estuary**
- "This effect, caused by moist air rising over a mountain range, creates dry conditions on the leeward side…" → **rain shadow effect**

#### Common Traps
- Deserts are defined by LOW PRECIPITATION, not high heat — the Atacama and Antarctic interior are cold deserts
- Taiga ≠ temperate deciduous forest (taiga has conifers and year-round cold; temperate forest has deciduous trees and four seasons)
- Estuaries are aquatic biomes, not terrestrial — don't confuse with wetlands (which can be inland)

#### Review Questions
1. What is the largest terrestrial biome, and what trees dominate it?
2. What makes an estuary productive, and where does it form?
3. How does the rain shadow effect create desert conditions?
