#### Conditions for Germination
Seeds need the right temperature, water, oxygen, and light. Most seeds germinate in a narrow temperature range (60-75°F for temperate crops). Water uptake (imbibition) triggers metabolic activity — enzymes activate, respiration increases, and growth resumes. Most seeds need oxygen for aerobic respiration; waterlogged soil may prevent germination.

#### Water Uptake and Activation
A dry seed is metabolically dormant, so it survives for years. When water is absorbed, seeds swell as water enters cells and spaces. Enzymes reactivate, hydrolysis breaks down stored nutrients, and cell division resumes. Within hours to days, the radicle (root) emerges through the seed coat.

#### Radicle and Shoot Emergence
The radicle usually emerges first, so the root establishes before the shoot. Roots grow downward (positive geotropism) toward water and minerals. Shoots grow upward (negative geotropism) toward light. Cotyledons provide nutrients and may photosynthesize once exposed to light.

#### The Seedling Stage
A seedling is a young plant with a root system, stem, and leaves. Seedlings are vulnerable to drying, pests, and shading. They must grow fast to reach light and establish enough root to find water. Seedling survival rate is low — most seeds never become seedlings, and most seedlings don't survive to adulthood.

#### Germination Timing and Dormancy
Seeds germinate at specific times because of photoperiod, cold exposure, or seed-coat breakdown. This prevents germination in fall (after seed production) when the seedling would die in winter. Dormancy ensures germination occurs when conditions favor survival. Example: winter wheat requires a cold period before it will germinate.
