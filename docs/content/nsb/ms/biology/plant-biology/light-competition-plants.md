#### Height as a Competitive Strategy
Tall plants shade shorter plants, capturing more light for their own photosynthesis. This favors evolution of height. In temperate forests, trees reach 80-130 feet. In tropical rainforests, emergent trees exceed 200 feet. But height requires energy and creates structural stress.

#### Leaf Size and Position Strategy
Large leaves capture more light but are mechanically weaker and lose more water. Small leaves are robust but capture less light. Some plants position leaves horizontally to intercept sunlight directly. Others angle them to reduce heat stress at midday.

#### Pigment Diversity and Light Capture
Chlorophyll absorbs blue and red light most. Carotenoids (orange) and xanthophyll (yellow) capture green light. Anthocyanins (red, purple) protect from excess light. Plants with diverse pigments capture more total light. This is why some leaves turn red in fall — anthocyanins protect leaves before they drop.

#### Chloroplast Arrangement in Leaves
In shade plants, chloroplasts are arranged toward the leaf surface to capture dim light. In sun plants, chloroplasts are arranged deeper in cells to avoid photoinhibition (light damage). This subtle adaptation reflects the plant's native environment.

#### Allelopathy: Chemical Competition for Light
Some plants release chemicals that inhibit neighbors' growth, reducing competition. Walnut trees release juglone, killing many plants nearby. This "chemical warfare" is less energetically costly than growing tall. It's a Science Bowl favorite — watch for juglone and allelopathy questions.
