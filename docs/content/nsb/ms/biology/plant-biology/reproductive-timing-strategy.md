#### Synchronous Flowering and Pollinator Attraction
Some plants flower at the same time, creating showy displays. Understory plants time flowering when trees leaf out, before shade becomes too dense. This synchronized timing takes advantage of pollinator availability and reduces competition for pollinator attention. Watch for questions about specific plant communities' flowering times.

#### Semelparous vs. Iteroparous Reproduction
Semelparous plants (like agave and bamboo) flower once then die, investing all reserves in one massive seed crop. Iteroparous plants flower multiple times over their lifetime, spreading reproductive effort. Each strategy has trade-offs — massive seed crops can overwhelm predators, but repeated flowering guarantees some offspring even if one year is poor.

#### Seed Number vs. Seed Size Trade-off
Plants allocate limited resources to seeds — they can make many small seeds or few large seeds. Small seeds disperse far but are vulnerable. Large seeds (nuts, acorns) face high predation but provide seedlings abundant reserves. Oaks produce many acorns in "mast years" to overwhelm squirrels and jays, ensuring some acorns escape predation.

#### Flowering Time and Climate
Early-flowering plants bloom before trees leaf out, capturing spring light. Late-flowering plants avoid frost but compete for pollinators and face shorter growing seasons. Climate change is shifting optimal flowering times — plants that don't adjust lose pollinator synchrony or flower into unseasonable frost.

#### Allelopathy in Reproductive Timing
Some plants chemically inhibit neighbors' growth, then flower when competition is reduced. Others time flowering to minimize competition by blooming when others don't. This temporal partitioning of flowering reduces direct competition for pollinators and allows different species to coexist.

#### Review Questions
1. What is the difference between semelparous and iteroparous reproduction?
2. What are the trade-offs between large and small seeds?
3. How does flowering time affect competitive success?
