#### The Chemical Equation
6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. This equation summarizes the entire process. Six molecules of carbon dioxide combine with six molecules of water using energy from light to produce one molecule of glucose (a sugar) and six molecules of oxygen.

#### Where Does Carbon Dioxide Come From?
Plants absorb CO₂ from the air through tiny pores called stomata on their leaves. During the day, stomata open to let CO₂ in. At night, they close to reduce water loss. In heavily populated areas or indoors, CO₂ levels around plants are similar to atmospheric CO₂ (~420 ppm).

#### How Plants Absorb Water
Plant roots absorb water from soil through root hair cells. Water moves up through the xylem from the roots to the leaves. Some water is used in photosynthesis, but most is lost through transpiration (evaporation from leaves). A single corn plant may lose 200+ liters of water during a growing season.

#### Why Light Is Essential
Only light energy can break apart water molecules and CO₂ to build glucose. Without light, photosynthesis cannot happen. Different wavelengths of light are absorbed differently — chlorophyll absorbs blue and red light most efficiently, which is why plants appear green (green light is reflected).

#### The Purpose of Photosynthesis
Photosynthesis serves two main purposes: (1) produce glucose for energy and growth, and (2) release oxygen as a waste product. All the oxygen in Earth's atmosphere comes from photosynthesis by plants, algae, and cyanobacteria over billions of years.
