#### Cardiovascular Disease
Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is the leading cause of death worldwide. It includes coronary artery disease (blockage of arteries supplying the heart), heart attack (myocardial infarction — heart muscle dies when its blood supply is cut off), and stroke (blockage or rupture of brain blood vessels). CVD often develops over decades before causing symptoms.

#### Atherosclerosis
Atherosclerosis is the buildup of plaques — fatty deposits, cholesterol, inflammatory cells — inside artery walls. Over time, plaques narrow the artery (reducing blood flow) and make artery walls less elastic. A rupture of a plaque can trigger a clot that completely blocks the artery, causing a heart attack or stroke.

#### Risk Factors
Modifiable risk factors include: high blood pressure, high LDL cholesterol, smoking, diabetes, obesity, physical inactivity, and unhealthy diet. Non-modifiable factors include: age, sex (males at higher risk earlier), family history, and genetics. Reducing modifiable factors substantially reduces CVD risk.

#### Fitness and the Heart
Regular aerobic exercise strengthens the heart muscle — trained athletes' hearts pump more blood per beat (higher stroke volume), so the heart doesn't need to beat as fast at rest. Exercise also lowers blood pressure, reduces LDL cholesterol, and increases HDL cholesterol (which carries cholesterol away from arteries). A resting heart rate of 40–50 beats per minute in trained athletes (vs. ~70 in sedentary adults) is evidence of cardiac efficiency.

#### Medical Interventions
Treatments include: statins (drugs that lower LDL cholesterol), beta-blockers (slow heart rate), angioplasty (balloon that opens a narrowed artery), and stent placement (wire mesh that keeps the artery open). Coronary bypass surgery routes blood around a blocked artery using a vessel from elsewhere in the body.
