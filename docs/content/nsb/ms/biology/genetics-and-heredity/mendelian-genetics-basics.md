### Introduction to Mendelian Genetics

Mendelian genetics is the study of how traits are passed from parents to offspring, based on the work of Gregor Mendel in the 19th century. Mendel conducted experiments with pea plants and discovered that traits are inherited in predictable patterns. His findings laid the groundwork for the field of genetics, allowing scientists to understand how characteristics are transmitted through generations.

### Key Terminology: Alleles and Genes

In genetics, a gene is a segment of DNA that encodes a specific trait, while an allele is a variant form of a gene. For example, a gene for flower color in pea plants may have a purple allele and a white allele. Each individual inherits two alleles for each gene, one from each parent, which can be either dominant or recessive.

### Dominant and Recessive Traits

Dominant traits are expressed in the phenotype (the observable characteristics) when at least one dominant allele is present. Conversely, recessive traits are only expressed when an individual has two recessive alleles. For instance, if purple flower color (P) is dominant over white flower color (p), a plant with either PP or Pp will have purple flowers, while only pp will produce white flowers.

### The Law of Segregation

Mendel's Law of Segregation states that during the formation of gametes (sperm and egg cells), the two alleles for a gene separate so that each gamete carries only one allele. This principle explains how traits can skip generations and reappear in offspring. For example, if two heterozygous parents (Pp) are crossed, the resulting offspring can exhibit a 3:1 ratio of dominant to recessive traits.

### Punnett Squares: Predicting Offspring Traits

A Punnett square is a tool used to predict the genetic outcomes of a cross between two organisms. By arranging the alleles of each parent along the top and side of a grid, students can visualize the possible combinations of alleles in the offspring. For example, crossing two heterozygous pea plants (Pp) using a Punnett square shows that there is a 25% chance for each genotype: PP, Pp, and pp.

### Applying Mendelian Genetics

Understanding Mendelian genetics is essential for predicting inheritance patterns in various organisms, including plants, animals, and humans. This knowledge can be applied in fields such as agriculture, medicine, and conservation biology. By studying these inheritance patterns, scientists can make informed decisions about breeding, genetic disorders, and biodiversity.
