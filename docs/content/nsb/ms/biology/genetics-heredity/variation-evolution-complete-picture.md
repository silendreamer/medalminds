#### The Chain: Mutation → Variation → Selection → Evolution
Every evolutionary change starts with a mutation. The mutation creates a new allele (variation). If the allele affects survival or reproduction, natural selection acts on it. Over many generations, allele frequencies in the population shift — this is evolution at the population level (microevolution).

#### Allele Frequency and Population Genetics
The frequency of an allele in a population can change due to: (1) Natural selection (advantageous alleles increase). (2) Genetic drift (random changes, especially in small populations). (3) Gene flow (alleles move between populations via migration). (4) Mutation (creates new alleles). (5) Non-random mating (certain genotypes mate more often).

#### Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium (Preview for HS)
A population is in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium when allele frequencies do NOT change between generations. This requires: no mutation, no selection, no migration, no genetic drift (infinitely large population), random mating. Real populations almost never meet all these conditions — so allele frequencies change, and evolution occurs.

#### Variation Fuels Adaptation
Populations with more genetic variation adapt more easily to environmental changes. This is why genetic diversity is important for conservation biology. A small, inbred population (low variation) may not have the right alleles to survive a new disease or climate change. Large, diverse populations are more resilient.

#### Competition Check: Common Bowl Patterns
Bowl questions on variation/evolution often ask: "What is the ultimate source of new genetic variation?" (mutation), "What process reduces genetic variation in small populations?" (genetic drift), or "What maintains a harmful allele at high frequency in a population?" (heterozygote advantage).

#### Review Questions
1. List the four mechanisms that can change allele frequencies in a population.
2. Why does genetic variation matter for conservation efforts to save endangered species?
3. A new disease kills 90% of a population. Why would a more genetically diverse population be more likely to survive?
