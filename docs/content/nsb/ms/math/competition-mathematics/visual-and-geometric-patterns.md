#### Triangular Numbers
Triangular numbers are 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28, .... Each is the sum of consecutive integers: T_n = 1 + 2 + 3 + ... + n = n(n+1)/2. Visually, they form triangles of dots. The 10th triangular number is T_10 = 10(11)/2 = 55.

#### Square and Pentagonal Numbers
Square numbers: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, ... (n²). Pentagonal numbers: 1, 5, 12, 22, 35, ... (formula: n(3n-1)/2). These appear in figurate number sequences, connecting algebra to geometry.

#### Tessellations and Symmetry
Regular polygons tessellate (tile a plane) under certain conditions. Triangles, squares, and hexagons tessellate alone. Combinations like triangles+squares also work. Symmetry group theory counts the distinct tilings — a deep mathematical topic relevant to crystal structures and art.

#### Fibonacci Spiral
The Fibonacci spiral is constructed by drawing quarter-circles in successive Fibonacci squares. The spiral appears in nature: nautilus shells, hurricanes, galaxy spirals. It's visually stunning and illustrates how mathematical sequences appear in the natural world.

#### Pascal's Triangle
The triangle starts 1, then 1 1, then 1 2 1, then 1 3 3 1, etc. Each entry is the sum of the two above it. Row n contains the binomial coefficients C(n, k). Diagonals are Fibonacci numbers, triangular numbers, etc. Pascal's triangle reveals deep combinatorial structure.

#### Review Questions
