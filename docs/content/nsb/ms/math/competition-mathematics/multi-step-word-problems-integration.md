#### Problem-Solving Strategy
(1) Read carefully, identify what's known and unknown. (2) Draw a diagram or make a table. (3) Set up equations. (4) Solve step-by-step. (5) Check reasonableness. (6) State the answer clearly.

#### Example: Mixture Problem
"A chemist mixes 200 mL of 30% acid with x mL of 70% acid to get 500 mL of 50% acid. Find x." Amount of pure acid from first: 200 × 0.3 = 60 mL. Amount from second: x × 0.7 mL. Total: 60 + 0.7x = 500 × 0.5 = 250. So 0.7x = 190, x ≈ 271.4 mL.

#### Example: Speed and Distance Problem
"Two cyclists start from opposite ends of a 100-mile path. One travels 20 mph, the other 15 mph. After how long do they meet, and how far has each traveled?" Combined speed: 35 mph. Time: 100/35 = 20/7 ≈ 2.86 hours. First cyclist: 20 × 20/7 = 400/7 ≈ 57 miles. Second: 15 × 20/7 = 300/7 ≈ 43 miles.

#### Example: Geometry and Algebra Combined
"A rectangle has perimeter 50 and area 150. Find its dimensions." Let L and W be dimensions. 2L + 2W = 50 → L + W = 25. LW = 150. From the first: W = 25 - L. Substitute: L(25 - L) = 150 → 25L - L² = 150 → L² - 25L + 150 = 0. Quadratic formula: L = (25 ± √(625-600))/2 = (25 ± 5)/2 = 15 or 10. Dimensions: 15 × 10.

#### Organization and Avoiding Errors
Write out every step clearly. State what each variable represents. Show unit conversions. Double-check arithmetic. These practices prevent the careless errors that are common in multi-step problems. In competitions, showing work is essential — partial credit exists even if final answer is wrong.

#### Review Questions
1. Solve a mixture or speed-distance problem from above.
2. What is the problem-solving strategy?
