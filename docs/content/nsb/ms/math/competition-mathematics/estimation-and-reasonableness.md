#### Rounding for Estimation
To estimate 47 × 213: round 47 to 50 and 213 to 200. Then 50 × 200 = 10,000. The actual answer (10,011) is close. This estimation checks reasonableness — if you calculated 987, you'd know it's wrong because the estimate is 10,000.

#### Order-of-Magnitude Thinking
How many piano tuners are in Chicago? Estimate: Chicago population ~3 million. Fraction with pianos: ~1/20 = 150,000. Pianos per tuner: ~500 per tuner per year. Tuners needed: 150,000 / 500 = 300 piano tuners. This "Fermi estimation" uses rough numbers to estimate what seems unknowable. The answer's probably between 100 and 1,000 (order of magnitude: 10²-³).

#### Significant Figures and Precision
Scientific measurements have uncertainty. 123.4 ± 0.1 has 4 significant figures. When multiplying/dividing, the result has as many sig figs as the least-precise input. 3.2 × 1.45 = 4.64, but with sig figs: 3.2 (2 sig figs) × 1.45 (3 sig figs) = 4.6 (2 sig figs). Over-reporting precision is misleading.

#### Checking Answers Against Reality
Does the answer make sense? If you calculated that the average American eats 10 lbs of food per day (way too high, ~2.5 lbs is typical), you'd catch the error. Reasonableness checks catch calculation mistakes. Example: if a car travels 60 mph for 2 hours, it goes 120 miles. If you calculated 3 miles, reasonableness check catches the error.
