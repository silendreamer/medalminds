#### Divisibility and Last Digit Questions
Frequent: "What is the last digit of 3^100?" Recognize: powers have cycles. 3's cycle is 3,9,7,1 with length 4. 100 mod 4 = 0, so answer is position 4 = 1. Or "Is 12,345 divisible by 9?" Digit sum = 15, not divisible by 9, so no.

#### Prime Factorization Questions
Frequent: "How many divisors does 72 have?" Factor: 72 = 2³ × 3². Divisor count: (3+1)(2+1) = 12. Or "Find GCF(60, 90)." Factors: 60 = 2² × 3 × 5, 90 = 2 × 3² × 5. GCF = 2 × 3 × 5 = 30.

#### Sequence and Series Questions
Frequent: "What is the sum 1 + 2 + 3 + ... + 100?" Formula: n(n+1)/2 = 100(101)/2 = 5,050. Or arithmetic series with non-unit differences: "What is 5 + 10 + 15 + ... + 500?" This is 5(1+2+3+...+100) = 5 × 5,050 = 25,250.

#### Proportion and Ratio Questions
Frequent: "If 3 candies cost $2, what do 18 candies cost?" Set up: 3/2 = 18/x. Answer: x = 12. Or "A recipe for 4 servings uses 2 cups flour. For 10 servings?" Proportion: 4/2 = 10/x, so x = 5 cups.

#### Geometry Area/Volume Questions
Frequent: "A circular pizza has radius 10 inches. What is its area?" A = πr² = 100π ≈ 314 in². Or "A sphere has radius 3. What is its volume?" V = 4πr³/3 = 4π(27)/3 = 36π ≈ 113 cm³.

#### Time Management Tip
Complex multi-step problems take time. If you get stuck, move on and return later. Single-step recognition (last digit, divisibility, formula application) should take <30 seconds. Don't spend 5 minutes on a 10-point problem; come back after easier questions.

#### Review Questions

2. How many divisors does 120 have?
3. What is 2 + 4 + 6 + ... + 50?
