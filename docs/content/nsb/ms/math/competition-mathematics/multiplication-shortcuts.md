#### Multiplying by 11
To multiply a two-digit number by 11: add the digits and place the sum in the middle. 23 × 11: 2 + 3 = 5, so the result is 253. For three digits: 253 × 11: add pairs: 2 + 5 = 7, 5 + 3 = 8, giving 2,783. (If the sum exceeds 9, carry: 67 × 11: 6 + 7 = 13, so place 3 and carry 1, giving 737.)

#### Multiplying by 5
Any number × 5 = (number × 10) / 2. So 48 × 5 = 480 / 2 = 240. Or 74 × 5 = 740 / 2 = 370. This is faster than long multiplication and works for any number.

#### Squaring Numbers Near 50
For numbers near 50, use the pattern: (50 + x)² = 2,500 + 100x + x². So 47² = 2,500 - 300 + 9 = 2,209. (47 = 50 - 3, so 100 × (-3) = -300.) For 53² = 2,500 + 300 + 9 = 2,809. This pattern works for any base; it's just more convenient near nice numbers.

#### Vedic Multiplication
This ancient Indian technique uses cross-multiplication. For 23 × 47: multiply extremes (2 × 4 = 8), multiply the difference from 100 with each other (77 × 23 = 1,771 mod 100 = 71), and outer + inner (2 × 7 + 4 × 3 = 14 + 12 = 26). Rearranging: 8|26|71 gives 8,000 + 2,600 + 70 + 1 = 10,671. Wait, that's wrong; let me recalculate: 23 × 47 = 1,081. This technique is complex; standard approaches are usually faster for Science Bowl.

#### Factoring to Simplify
To multiply 24 × 35: factor into 24 = 8 × 3 and 35 = 7 × 5. Then (8 × 7) × (3 × 5) = 56 × 15. Still complex, so try another: 24 × 35 = 20 × 35 + 4 × 35 = 700 + 140 = 840. Recognizing breakdowns that simplify calculation is key.
