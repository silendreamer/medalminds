#### Finding Intercepts
To find the y-intercept: set x = 0 and solve for y. To find the x-intercept: set y = 0 and solve for x. For 2x + 3y = 12: y-intercept (set x=0): 3y = 12 → y = 4 → (0, 4). x-intercept (set y=0): 2x = 12 → x = 6 → (6, 0). Plot these two points and connect them.

#### Graphing from Slope-Intercept Form
y = 2x/3 − 1: y-intercept = (0, −1). Slope = 2/3 (up 2, right 3). Second point: (3, 1). Third (optional check): (6, 3). Three collinear points confirm the line.

#### Testing Whether a Point Is on the Line
Is (3, 5) on the line y = 2x − 1? Substitute: 2(3) − 1 = 5. Yes ✓. Is (4, 6)? 2(4) − 1 = 7 ≠ 6. No. This substitution test is fast and reliable.

#### Parallel and Perpendicular Lines
- Parallel lines: same slope, different y-intercepts. y = 3x + 2 and y = 3x − 5 are parallel.
- Perpendicular lines: slopes are negative reciprocals. If m₁ = 2, then m₂ = −1/2. Product of perpendicular slopes = −1: m₁ × m₂ = −1.

#### Reading Graphs: Slope as Rate
If a graph shows distance (y) vs. time (x), the slope = speed. If y = cost and x = items, slope = cost per item. Science Bowl uses graphs to represent real-world relationships — always label axes and identify what slope means in context.

#### Review Questions

2. Is (−2, 7) on the line y = −2x + 3? Show your work.
