#### What Is an Angle?
An angle is formed when two rays share the same endpoint, called the **vertex**. We measure angles in **degrees** (°). A full rotation is 360°. Angles are named by their vertex or by three points (e.g., ∠ABC, where B is the vertex). You use a **protractor** to measure an angle's degree. Angles fall into four types: **acute** (less than 90°), **right** (exactly 90°, shown with a small square), **obtuse** (between 90° and 180°), and **straight** (exactly 180°, forming a line).

#### Angle Relationships
Pairs of angles have special names based on how they relate. **Complementary** angles sum to 90°. **Supplementary** angles sum to 180°. **Vertical** angles are across from each other when two lines cross — they are always equal. **Adjacent** angles share a vertex and a side but don't overlap. Knowing these relationships lets you solve for unknown angles without a protractor.

#### Angles in Parallel Lines
When a **transversal** crosses two parallel lines, it creates 8 angles. **Corresponding** angles (same position at each intersection) are equal. **Alternate interior** angles (between the lines, on opposite sides) are equal. **Co-interior** (same-side interior) angles are supplementary, summing to 180°. These rules are essential for geometry proofs and problems.

#### Lines and Their Relationships
**Parallel** lines never intersect and are always the same distance apart (symbol: ∥). **Perpendicular** lines intersect at exactly 90° (symbol: ⊥). **Skew** lines don't intersect but also aren't parallel (they exist in 3D space). A **transversal** is a line that crosses two or more other lines. Identifying line relationships is the first step in many geometry problems.

#### Sum of Interior Angles in Polygons
The interior angles of any triangle sum to **180°**. For any polygon with n sides, the interior angle sum is **(n − 2) × 180°**. So a quadrilateral sums to 360°, a pentagon to 540°, a hexagon to 720°. For a **regular polygon** (all sides and angles equal), each interior angle equals (n − 2) × 180° ÷ n. This formula is one of the most tested geometry facts in Science Bowl.

#### Review Questions
1. Two angles are supplementary. One angle measures 63°. What is the measure of the other angle?
2. What is the sum of interior angles of an octagon (8 sides)?
3. If two parallel lines are cut by a transversal and one alternate interior angle is 47°, what is the other alternate interior angle?
