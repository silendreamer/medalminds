#### Top 10 High-Yield Facts
1. Cross-product: a/b = c/d ↔ ad = bc
2. Unit rate = quantity ÷ 1 unit of the second measure
3. D = RT; T = D/R; R = D/T (the speed triangle)
4. Average speed = total distance ÷ total time (NEVER average the two speeds)
5. Direct proportion: y = kx; inverse: xy = k
6. Part-to-whole ratio = part/(sum of all parts)
7. Scale factor for similar figures: ratio of corresponding sides
8. Area scales as (side ratio)²; volume scales as (side ratio)³
9. Splitting T in ratio a:b: first part = a/(a+b) × T; second = b/(a+b) × T
10. "Workers × days = constant work" → if workers × days = W, double workers = half days

#### Common Traps
- Mixing part-to-part with part-to-whole (read the question twice)
- Averaging speeds instead of computing total distance ÷ total time
- Forgetting that inverse proportion is xy = k, not y = kx

#### Practice Toss-Up Stems
1. "For 10 points — if y is inversely proportional to x, and y = 6 when x = 8, what is y when x = 12?" → **4**
2. "For 10 points — a car travels 90 miles at 45 mph, then 90 miles at 30 mph. What is the average speed?" → **36 mph** (total: 180 mi / 5 hr)
3. "For 10 points — solve for x: 5/8 = x/40." → **25**
4. "For 10 points — split $480 in ratio 3:5. What is the smaller share?" → **$180**
5. "For 10 points — 3 printers print 90 pages in 5 minutes. At the same rate, how many pages can 5 printers print in 3 minutes?" → **90 pages** (3×5=15 printer-minutes; 5×3=15 — same!)

#### Review Questions
1. Define direct and inverse proportion. Give one Science Bowl example of each.
2. Two cities are 4 inches apart on a map with scale 1 in = 30 miles. How far apart are they really?
3. What is wrong with this statement: "I drove 30 mph for an hour and 60 mph for an hour, so my average speed was 45 mph."
