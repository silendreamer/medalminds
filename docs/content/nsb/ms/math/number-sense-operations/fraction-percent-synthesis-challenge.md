#### Problem Set A: Fraction Chains
Work through each step, showing intermediate results:
1. In a school of 600 students, 2/5 are in 6th grade, 1/3 of 6th graders are in band, and 1/4 of band members play trumpet. How many 6th graders play trumpet in band?
   Solution: 600 × 2/5 = 240 → 240 × 1/3 = 80 → 80 × 1/4 = **20 trumpet players**

2. A tank is 3/4 full with 90 liters. 1/3 of the water evaporates. How many liters remain?
   Solution: Full tank = 90 ÷ (3/4) = 120 L. After evaporation: 90 × 2/3 = **60 L remain**.

#### Problem Set B: Percent Chains
1. A store buys items for $50 and marks them up 40%. Then it offers a 10% discount. What is the selling price?
   Solution: $50 × 1.40 = $70 → $70 × 0.90 = **$63**

2. A population of 800 grows 25% in year 1, then decreases 20% in year 2. Final population?
   Solution: 800 × 1.25 = 1000 → 1000 × 0.80 = **800** (back to start! This is a classic trap.)

#### Identifying the Whole
The most common error: using the wrong denominator. "15 is 30% of what number?" Students often write 15 × 0.30 = 4.5. Wrong — that's 30% of 15, not 15 is 30% of something. Correct: 15 = 0.30 × W → W = 15/0.30 = **50**.

#### Strategy: Work Backwards
When given the end and asked for the start, reverse operations. A number after a 25% increase is 75. What was the original? Don't subtract 25%! Instead: 75 = original × 1.25 → original = 75/1.25 = **60**.

#### Competition Tip
Science Bowl math is timed. Estimate first, then solve exactly, then verify your exact answer is near your estimate. If not, you made an arithmetic error — recheck before buzzing.

#### Review Questions
1. A class has 24 students. 1/3 are absent. Of those present, 3/4 pass a quiz. How many students passed?
2. After two successive 10% increases, what single percent increase is equivalent?
3. 45 is 90% of what number?
