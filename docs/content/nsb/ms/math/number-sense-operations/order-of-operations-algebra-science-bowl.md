#### Evaluating Algebraic Expressions
"Evaluate 3x² − 2x + 5 when x = −2." Substitute carefully: 3(−2)² − 2(−2) + 5 = 3(4) − (−4) + 5 = 12 + 4 + 5 = **21**. Critical: evaluate (−2)² = 4 before multiplying by 3. If you write 3 × −2² = 3 × −4 = −12 first, you've violated PEMDAS.

#### Formula Evaluation
Science Bowl physics and chemistry questions often ask you to evaluate a formula: "Using F = ma, find the force when m = 3.5 kg and a = 4 m/s²." F = 3.5 × 4 = **14 N**. For compound formulas: "Evaluate d = ½at² for a = 10 m/s² and t = 3 s." d = ½ × 10 × 3² = ½ × 10 × 9 = **45 m**. Exponent (3²) before multiplication by ½ and 10.

#### Multi-Step Science Expressions
"Evaluate: 4(2 + 3)² ÷ 5 − 3." Step by step: (2+3)=5; 5²=25; 4×25=100; 100÷5=20; 20−3=**17**. Science Bowl multi-step math questions often pack five operations into one expression — careful evaluation is the only path to the right answer.

#### Expression vs. Equation
An expression (3x + 5) is evaluated for specific x values. An equation (3x + 5 = 14) is solved to find x. Order of operations applies to evaluating expressions; inverse operations apply to solving equations. Don't confuse the two procedures.

#### Science Bowl Reading Patterns
Bowl questions often say "For 10 points — what is [expression], to the nearest integer?" Read the expression carefully before computing. Clue phrases: "the quantity" = grouping (parentheses implied), "the square of the sum" = (sum)², "the sum of the squares" = a² + b².

#### Review Questions
1. Evaluate: 2a² − 3b + c for a = 3, b = −2, c = 1.
2. Evaluate: (5 + 3)² − 4 × 3 ÷ 6.
3. A formula gives P = 2(l + w). Find P when l = 8 and w = 5.
