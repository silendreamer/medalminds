#### The Fraction as Division
A fraction a/b literally means "a divided by b." The denominator tells you how many equal parts the whole is split into; the numerator counts how many of those parts you have. So 3/4 means a whole split into 4 equal parts, and you have 3 of them. This simple model — whole divided into equal parts — handles every fraction operation that comes later. Students who skip this and jump straight to rules end up guessing when problems look unfamiliar.

#### Equivalent Fractions
Two fractions are equivalent when they represent the same portion of a whole. Multiplying or dividing both numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number gives an equivalent fraction: 3/4 = 6/8 = 9/12. This isn't a trick — it's the same amount, just cut into more or fewer pieces. Finding the simplest form means dividing by the greatest common factor (GCF). Example: 12/18 → GCF is 6 → 12/18 = 2/3.

#### Placing Fractions on a Number Line
To place 3/5 on a number line between 0 and 1, divide that segment into 5 equal parts and count 3 from the left. This visualizes size immediately: 3/5 is less than 1/2? No — 1/2 = 2.5/5, and 3/5 > 2.5/5. A number line beats "butterfly" tricks for building real understanding. Benchmarks to memorize: 1/4 = 0.25, 1/3 ≈ 0.333, 1/2 = 0.5, 2/3 ≈ 0.667, 3/4 = 0.75.

#### Comparing Fractions Quickly
Cross-multiply to compare: for 3/7 vs. 4/9, compute 3×9=27 vs. 4×7=28. Since 27 < 28, we get 3/7 < 4/9. Or convert to a common denominator. Or use decimal approximations. For Science Bowl speed, cross-multiplication is fastest for two fractions. For three or more, convert to decimals.

#### The Fraction-Division Connection
Every fraction problem can be rewritten as a division: 3/4 ÷ 1 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75. This means division always produces a fraction. Why does 7 ÷ 2 = 3.5? Because 7/2 = 3 + 1/2 = 3.5. Understanding this connection makes mixed numbers, improper fractions, and long division all click together.

#### Review Questions
1. Simplify 36/48 to lowest terms.
2. Which is greater, 5/8 or 7/11? Show your method.
3. Convert 7/4 to a mixed number, then to a decimal.
