#### The Greenhouse Effect
Earth's atmosphere is transparent to incoming sunlight but absorbs outgoing infrared radiation. This is the natural greenhouse effect — without it, Earth would be ~60°F colder. Greenhouse gases (CO₂, methane, water vapor, nitrous oxide) absorb infrared radiation, trapping heat. More greenhouse gas = more heat trapped = warmer planet.

#### Radiative Forcing and Temperature Response
Radiative forcing measures the imbalance between incoming and outgoing radiation (watts per square meter). A 2°C warming corresponds to ~3.7 W/m² radiative forcing. CO₂ concentration has increased from 280 ppm (1850) to 420 ppm (2024), a 50% increase. This change alone implies 1-2°C warming (we've observed ~1.1°C so far). Climate sensitivity is the temperature increase per W/m² forcing — estimates are 3-4°C per doubling of CO₂.

#### Human Emissions and Attribution
Humans emit CO₂ from burning fossil fuels (80% of emissions), agriculture/deforestation (15%), and industrial processes (5%). Isotope ratios prove these emissions are from fossil fuels (old carbon), not from volcanoes or natural sources. Scientists attribute >99% of recent warming to human causes — natural factors (sun, volcanoes) can't explain the observed warming.

#### Climate Feedback Loops
Positive feedbacks amplify warming: less ice reflects less sunlight (ice-albedo feedback), warmer oceans release more CO₂ (solubility feedback), methane releases from thawing permafrost (methane feedback). These feedbacks mean that climate change accelerates — it's not a linear response. Negative feedbacks (more clouds, higher evaporation) are uncertain and likely weaker than positive feedbacks.

#### Observed Climate Changes
Global temperatures have risen 1.1°C since 1850. Ocean surface temperatures are rising faster than the historical average. Glaciers and ice sheets are melting — Greenland ice loss accelerated from 34 Gt/year (1990s) to 280 Gt/year (2010s). Sea levels are rising ~3.4 mm/year, double the 20th-century average. Extreme weather events (heat waves, flooding, hurricanes) are increasing in frequency and intensity.
