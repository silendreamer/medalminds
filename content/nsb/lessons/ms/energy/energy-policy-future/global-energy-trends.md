#### Historical Trends and Growth
Global primary energy consumption has grown 2-3% annually over the past 50 years. Total consumption is ~600 exajoules (EJ) annually (2024). This growth reflects population growth (1B to 8B) and rising living standards (more appliances, transportation, heating/cooling). Renewable energy is the fastest-growing category (10% annually) but still only ~15% of total.

#### Developed vs. Developing Nations
Developed nations consume ~200 EJ (33% of global, with 15% of population). Developing nations consume ~400 EJ (67% of global, with 85% of population). Per-capita consumption is vastly different: U.S. ~350 GJ per person per year; India ~20 GJ per person. As developing nations' economies grow, per-capita energy consumption rises (toward U.S. levels), driving global growth.

#### Electricity Access and Energy Poverty
~750 million people globally lack access to electricity (mostly Sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia). Even more lack clean cooking fuels (3+ billion use biomass/coal for cooking, causing health problems from indoor air pollution — 4 million deaths annually). Expanding electricity access is essential for development and health. Many developing nations are deploying solar mini-grids to extend access without building large grid infrastructure.

#### Peak Energy Demand Scenarios
Developed nations' energy consumption has plateaued or declined (due to efficiency and saturation). U.S. energy consumption has been flat for 15 years despite GDP growth. Developing nations' consumption is rising steeply. Global peak energy demand could occur 2040-2060 in optimistic scenarios (aggressive efficiency and renewable adoption) or 2080+ in business-as-usual scenarios. Peak demand determines how much renewable capacity is needed.

#### Energy Security and Trade
Energy resources are geographically concentrated. Oil reserves: Middle East 50%, Russia 6%, others 44%. Natural gas: Russia, Iran, Qatar, U.S. This concentration creates dependencies and geopolitical leverage. Diversifying energy sources (renewables, nuclear) and improving efficiency reduce import dependence. Germany's push for renewables was partly motivated by reducing Russian gas dependence (now confirmed geopolitical priority after Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine).
