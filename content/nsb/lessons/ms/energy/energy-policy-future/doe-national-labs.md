#### The 17 National Labs
Major labs by focus: Oak Ridge National Laboratory (Tennessee) — nuclear technology, materials science, high-performance computing. Argonne National Laboratory (Illinois) — battery technology, chemistry, physics. Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (California) — particle physics, energy sciences. National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL, Colorado) — solar, wind, geothermal, energy efficiency. Sandia National Laboratories (New Mexico/California) — nuclear weapons, grid security, energy storage.

#### Research Mission and Capabilities
National Labs have cutting-edge facilities that no private company can afford: supercomputers (exascale petaflop computers at Oak Ridge and NREL), particle accelerators, neutron sources for materials analysis, and specialized manufacturing facilities. Researchers from universities, private companies, and other government agencies collaborate at these labs. This enables science that would be impossible at individual institutions.

#### Supercomputing Power
The fastest supercomputers in the world are at DOE National Labs. Oak Ridge's Frontier supercomputer performs over 1 exaflop (10^18 floating-point operations per second). These machines simulate complex systems: climate models, materials physics, nuclear reactor safety, fusion plasma, drug discovery. Supercomputing is essential for energy research — simulating a wind farm's aerodynamics or a battery's electrochemistry requires massive computation.

#### User Program and Public Access
National Labs offer access to scientists through user programs. Universities and companies can apply to use lab facilities free or at reduced cost (as long as results are published). Hundreds of researchers use lab facilities each year. This democratizes access to world-class research infrastructure, accelerating innovation across the country.

#### Economic and Scientific Impact
The 17 National Labs employ ~60,000 people (including contractors) and generate ~$40 billion in annual economic activity. Scientists at DOE Labs have won numerous Nobel Prizes. Innovations developed at labs have spawned new industries (touchscreen technology, advanced batteries). The labs are major employers in their regions — Oak Ridge alone employs ~10,000 in Tennessee.
