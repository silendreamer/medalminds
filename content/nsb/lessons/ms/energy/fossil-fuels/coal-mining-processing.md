#### Two Major Mining Methods
Coal is extracted by two primary methods depending on depth. **Surface (strip) mining** removes soil and rock overlying shallow coal seams — using massive dragline excavators and bulldozers to expose the coal. It is cheaper and safer than underground mining but dramatically alters the landscape. The Surface Mining Control and Reclamation Act (1977) requires companies to restore mined land. **Underground (deep) mining** uses tunnels and shafts to reach coal buried too deep for surface access. Two main underground methods: room-and-pillar (leaving coal pillars to support the roof) and longwall mining (a large cutting machine shears a wall of coal).

#### Coal Processing: Cleaning and Preparation
Raw "run-of-mine" coal contains rock, sulfur, and other impurities. Coal preparation plants (also called prep plants or washeries) crush, screen, and wash coal to remove impurities. Density separation methods (water or dense media) exploit the fact that coal is less dense than rock — coal floats, rock sinks. Removing high-sulfur pyrite reduces sulfur dioxide emissions when coal is burned. Cleaned coal is then dried, sized, and loaded onto unit trains, barges, or conveyors for transport to power plants.

#### How Coal Generates Electricity
A typical coal power plant works through four steps: (1) Pulverized coal is burned in a boiler, releasing thermal energy. (2) Thermal energy heats water to produce high-pressure steam. (3) Steam drives a turbine, converting thermal energy to rotational kinetic energy. (4) The spinning turbine drives a generator, converting kinetic energy to electrical energy. Cooling towers release waste heat into the atmosphere. Fly ash (fine particles) is captured by electrostatic precipitators or fabric filters before exhaust exits the stack.

#### Coal's Industrial Uses Beyond Electricity
Coking coal (metallurgical coal) is heated without oxygen to produce coke, a nearly pure carbon product essential for steelmaking — coke provides the reducing agent and heat to smelt iron ore into iron. Coal tar, a byproduct of coke production, is refined into chemicals including benzene, toluene, and naphthalene used in plastics, dyes, and medicines. About 90% of U.S. coal is used for electricity generation; the remaining 10% goes to industrial uses, primarily steelmaking.
