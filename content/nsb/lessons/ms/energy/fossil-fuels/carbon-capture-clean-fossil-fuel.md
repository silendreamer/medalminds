#### Why Carbon Capture?
Carbon Capture and Storage (CCS) and Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Storage (CCUS) are technologies that capture CO₂ at the point of emission (power plants, factories) or from the air, then store it permanently or use it productively. The rationale: society needs some fossil fuels in the near-term transition, and some industrial processes (cement, steel) are very difficult to decarbonize directly. CCS allows continued use while drastically reducing CO₂ emissions. The DOE's Office of Fossil Energy and Carbon Management funds extensive CCS research.

#### Post-Combustion Capture
The most mature approach: capture CO₂ from flue gas (exhaust) after combustion. A chemical solvent (typically an amine solution) absorbs CO₂ from the flue gas; the solution is then heated to release concentrated CO₂, which is compressed for storage. The process requires significant energy — typically 15–30% of the power plant's output (the "energy penalty"). The world's largest operational CCS facility at a power plant is the Boundary Dam project in Saskatchewan, Canada. In the U.S., the Petra Nova project (Texas) demonstrated CCS at a coal plant (paused due to economics).

#### Pre-Combustion and Oxyfuel Capture
**Pre-combustion capture**: convert fuel to hydrogen and CO₂ before burning (gasification). The CO₂ is captured, and clean hydrogen is burned. **Oxyfuel combustion**: burn fuel in pure oxygen instead of air, producing a flue gas of mostly CO₂ and H₂O — CO₂ is easily separated by condensing the water. Both approaches have technical promise but are more expensive than post-combustion for retrofitting existing plants.

#### Direct Air Capture (DAC)
DAC removes CO₂ directly from the atmosphere (not just from smokestacks) — crucial for addressing historical emissions. Facilities use chemical solvents or solid sorbents to bind CO₂ from ambient air. Much more energy-intensive than point-source capture (because CO₂ is only ~0.04% of air vs. ~15% of flue gas). Current costs: ~$300–$600/ton CO₂. The DOE's "Carbon Negative Shot" targets DAC costs below $100/ton. Climeworks (Switzerland) and Heirloom Carbon (U.S.) operate commercial DAC facilities. The Inflation Reduction Act (2022) provides tax credits for DAC deployment.

#### Geological CO₂ Storage
Captured CO₂ is compressed to a supercritical fluid and injected into deep geological formations: depleted oil and gas reservoirs, saline aquifers (porous rock saturated with saltwater, thousands of meters underground). CO₂ injected at depth mineralizes over time — converting to solid carbonate minerals, which permanently sequesters it. The U.S. has vast geological storage capacity estimated at 500+ billion tonnes — far more than needed to store all projected U.S. CO₂ emissions this century.
