#### Scenario 1: Energy Content of Coal
One ton (2,000 lbs = 907 kg) of bituminous coal releases approximately 25 million BTU (British thermal units) when burned, equivalent to about 26,400 MJ or 7,333 kWh of thermal energy. A U.S. coal power plant with 35% efficiency generates: 7,333 kWh × 0.35 = 2,567 kWh of electrical energy per ton. This electricity could power a 100 W light bulb for 25,667 hours — about 2.9 years — or the equivalent of one U.S. home for about 2.5 months (average home uses ~900 kWh/month).

#### Scenario 2: Carbon Dioxide from Coal
Burning 1 ton of coal produces approximately 2.86 tons of CO₂ (carbon combines with oxygen from air: C + O₂ → CO₂; carbon makes up about 78% of bituminous coal). A 1,000 MW coal plant burning ~10,000 tons of coal per day would emit ~28,600 tons of CO₂ daily. Over a year, that's ~10.4 million tons of CO₂ from a single large plant. The entire U.S. coal fleet emitted about 900 million tons of CO₂ in 2022 (down from ~1.7 billion tons in 2007).

#### Scenario 3: Coal vs. Natural Gas Emissions
Natural gas (primarily methane, CH₄) produces roughly half the CO₂ of coal per unit of electricity generated, and virtually no sulfur dioxide or fly ash. This is one reason utilities switched from coal to gas — not only is gas cheaper, it also reduces regulatory compliance costs from SO₂ and particulate emission rules. Calculate: if a 500 MW plant switches from coal (35% efficient) to gas (55% efficient), it reduces CO₂ emissions by approximately 60% per MWh of electricity produced.

#### Scenario 4: Sulfur Dioxide and Acid Rain
High-sulfur coal (eastern U.S. bituminous) contains 1–3% sulfur. When burned: S + O₂ → SO₂. SO₂ in the atmosphere converts to sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) in the presence of water, causing acid rain. The Clean Air Act Amendments of 1990 created an SO₂ cap-and-trade system (Acid Rain Program), which dramatically reduced SO₂ emissions from power plants. Scrubbers (flue gas desulfurization systems) remove 95–99% of SO₂ using calcium carbonate (limestone): CaCO₃ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + CO₂.

#### Review Questions
1. If a coal plant generates 2,500 kWh of electricity per ton of coal at 35% efficiency, how much thermal energy (in kWh) does each ton contain?
2. Write the balanced chemical equation for the combustion of carbon in coal.
3. What chemical process turns sulfur dioxide from coal combustion into acid rain?
