#### Combustion Quick Reference
Complete combustion: hydrocarbon + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O (exothermic)
Incomplete combustion products: CO, C (soot), unburned hydrocarbons, + CO₂ + H₂O
High-temperature combustion also produces: NOₓ (nitrogen oxides)
Burning coal also produces: SO₂ (from sulfur impurities)

#### Pollutant → Source → Health Effect → Control
| Pollutant | Primary Fossil Fuel Source | Health/Environmental Effect | Control Technology |
|---|---|---|---|
| CO₂ | All combustion | Climate change | Carbon capture (developing) |
| SO₂ | Coal (sulfur content) | Acid rain, respiratory | Scrubbers (FGD) |
| NOₓ | All high-temp combustion | Smog, acid rain | Selective catalytic reduction |
| PM₂.₅ | Coal, diesel | Lung disease, heart disease | Electrostatic precipitators |
| CO | Incomplete combustion | Hemoglobin binding, death | Catalytic converters |
| Ground O₃ | Secondary (NOₓ + VOCs + sunlight) | Respiratory, crop damage | Reduce NOₓ and VOC emissions |
| Mercury | Coal | Neurotoxin, bioaccumulation | Activated carbon injection |

#### 10 Fast Facts
1. Complete combustion of methane: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
2. CO is toxic because it binds hemoglobin 200× more than O₂
3. Soot is fine carbon particles — a carcinogen and climate forcer
4. SO₂ + water → acid rain (sulfuric acid)
5. NOₓ forms at high combustion temperatures from atmospheric N₂
6. PM₂.₅ particles < 2.5 micrometers; can penetrate blood
7. Ground ozone = secondary pollutant (formed from NOₓ + VOCs + sunlight)
8. EPA's 6 criteria pollutants: PM, O₃, CO, SO₂, NO₂, Pb
9. Acid Rain Program (1990): cap-and-trade for SO₂
10. Catalytic converter: converts CO + hydrocarbons + NOₓ → CO₂ + H₂O + N₂
