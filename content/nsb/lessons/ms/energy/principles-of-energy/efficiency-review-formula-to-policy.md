#### Efficiency at a Glance
Formula: η (%) = (P_out / P_in) × 100 = (E_out / E_in) × 100. Rearrangements: E_out = η × E_in; E_waste = E_in − E_out; E_waste = (1 − η) × E_in.

Memorize these approximate efficiencies:
- LED bulb: 90% | Incandescent: 5%
- Electric motor: 90% | Gasoline engine: 20-25%
- Hydroelectric: 90% | Coal plant: 35%
- Natural gas combined cycle: 58% | Solar PV: 15-22%
- Diesel engine: 45% | Fuel cell: 60%
- Human body (mechanical work): 25%

#### Policy Connection: CAFE Standards
Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) standards, set by the DOE and National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), require automakers to meet average fuel economy targets for their fleets (currently ~49 mpg for 2026 model year cars). Higher mpg = higher efficiency = less fuel burned per mile. These standards have been a major driver of vehicle efficiency improvements since 1975.

#### 10 Quick Efficiency Problems
1. Input 500 J, output 350 J. η = ? → 70%
2. η = 40%, input 800 J. Output = ? → 320 J
3. η = 80%, output 400 J. Input = ? → 500 J
4. A 1000 W motor is 90% efficient. Useful power = ? → 900 W
5. η = 25%, input 2000 J. Waste heat = ? → 1500 J
6. Power plant: input 1000 MW, η = 35%. Electrical output = ? → 350 MW
7. Waste heat = 600 J, input = 1000 J. η = ? → 40%
8. LED: 9 W input, 8.1 W light. η = ? → 90%
9. Hydro plant: 95% efficient, generates 570 MW. Input power = ? → 600 MW
10. Electric car: 85% efficient motor, uses 40 kWh/100 mi. How much actually moves the car per 100 mi? → 34 kWh

#### Review Questions

2. A power plant burns fuel releasing 3000 MJ/hour and produces 1050 MJ of electricity. What is its efficiency?
3. Name one federal policy in the U.S. that requires minimum efficiency standards for vehicles.
