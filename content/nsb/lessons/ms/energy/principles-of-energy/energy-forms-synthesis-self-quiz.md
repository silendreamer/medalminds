#### Section 1: Identify the Form
Read each description and name the energy form(s) present.
1. A compressed spring ready to launch a ball. → *Elastic PE*
2. An apple hanging on a tree branch. → *Gravitational PE*
3. A bowl of sugar. → *Chemical PE*
4. A moving car. → *KE (and thermal in its engine)*
5. Sunlight traveling through space. → *Radiant (electromagnetic) energy*
6. A vibrating guitar string producing sound. → *Sound (mechanical wave) energy; originally KE/elastic PE*
7. Steam in a pipe. → *Thermal energy + KE of steam molecules*
8. Uranium fuel rod in a reactor. → *Nuclear PE*

#### Section 2: Formula Check
Solve each:
1. KE of a 10 kg object moving at 5 m/s? → KE = ½(10)(25) = **125 J**
2. GPE of a 3 kg book on a 2 m shelf? (g = 10) → GPE = 3×10×2 = **60 J**
3. Heat needed to warm 1 kg of water by 10°C? → Q = 1×4186×10 = **41,860 J**
4. Efficiency of a bulb using 60 J to produce 3 J of light? → 3/60×100 = **5%**

#### Section 3: Conversion Chain Writing
Write the complete energy conversion chain:
1. Coal power plant → home TV: chemical → thermal → kinetic → electrical → radiant + sound
2. Eating a sandwich and going for a run: chemical → chemical (ATP) → kinetic + thermal
3. Wind turbine charging a battery: kinetic → electrical → chemical (stored)

#### Common Exam Errors and Fixes
Error: Writing "KE = mv²" (missing the ½). Fix: Always write ½mv² — the ½ comes from calculus integration of F = ma over distance.
Error: Saying "the Sun produces energy." Fix: The Sun converts nuclear PE to radiant energy — it doesn't create energy.

#### Review Questions
1. What is the energy form stored in a fully charged battery?
2. In a pendulum, at which point is PE = 0 and KE maximum?
3. Write the formula for gravitational potential energy and identify all variables.
