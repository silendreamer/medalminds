#### Can't-Miss Facts
- Primary fission fuel: uranium-235 (enriched to 3–5% in reactor grade; weapons grade is >90%)
- Fission products: 2 lighter nuclei + 2–3 neutrons + ~200 MeV energy + gamma radiation
- Moderator slows neutrons; coolant removes heat (in light-water reactors, same substance: water)
- Control rods absorb neutrons (boron, cadmium, hafnium)
- Critical mass: minimum fuel needed for self-sustaining chain reaction
- U.S. nuclear: ~93 reactors, ~20% of U.S. electricity, ~93% capacity factor
- Three accidents: Three Mile Island (1979, PA), Chernobyl (1986, Ukraine), Fukushima (2011, Japan)
- Most common reactor type in U.S.: Pressurized Water Reactor (PWR)

#### Bowl Toss-Up Stems
*"For 10 points, what type of reactor accounts for the majority of U.S. nuclear power plants?"* → Pressurized water reactor (PWR)
*"For 10 points, what is the term for the minimum amount of fissile material needed to sustain a chain reaction?"* → Critical mass
*"For 10 points, what material is commonly used in control rods to absorb neutrons and slow a nuclear reaction?"* → Boron (also accept cadmium or hafnium)

#### Review Questions
1. What percentage of U.S. electricity comes from nuclear power?
2. What is the difference between enriched uranium used in reactors vs. weapons?
3. Name the three major civilian nuclear accidents in order of occurrence.
