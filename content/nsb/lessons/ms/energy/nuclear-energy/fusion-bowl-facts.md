#### Can't-Miss Facts
- Fusion fuel: deuterium (H-2) + tritium (H-3) → helium-4 + neutron + 17.6 MeV
- Deuterium source: seawater (1 in 6,400 hydrogen atoms)
- Tritium: bred from lithium-6 in the reactor
- Required temperature: ~100 million °C (hotter than the Sun's core)
- Plasma confinement: tokamak (magnetic) or inertial confinement (lasers)
- ITER: in Cadarache, France; 35-nation project; target Q = 10; not a power plant
- NIF: National Ignition Facility, Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (DOE); achieved ignition December 2022
- Fusion waste: activation products, mostly decay in ~100 years (unlike fission's millennia)
- Sun uses: proton-proton chain; ~600 million tons H → He per second

#### Bowl Toss-Up Stems
*"For 10 points, what international fusion project under construction in southern France aims to achieve a Q factor of 10?"* → ITER
*"For 10 points, what two hydrogen isotopes are used in the deuterium-tritium fusion reaction?"* → Deuterium and tritium
*"For 10 points, what DOE laboratory achieved fusion ignition in December 2022 using laser-driven inertial confinement?"* → National Ignition Facility (NIF) at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory

#### Review Questions
1. What does a Q factor of 10 mean for ITER?
2. Name the two approaches to plasma confinement in experimental fusion reactors.
3. Where is ITER being built, and how many nations are involved?
