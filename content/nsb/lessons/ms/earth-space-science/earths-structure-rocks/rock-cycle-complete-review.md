#### Rock Type Summary Table

| Property | Igneous | Sedimentary | Metamorphic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Forms from | Cooled magma/lava | Compacted sediment | Heat + pressure |
| Examples | Granite, basalt, obsidian | Sandstone, limestone, shale | Marble, slate, quartzite |
| Texture clue | Crystal size = cooling rate | Layers (strata) | Foliation (layered crystals) |
| Fossils? | No (heat destroys) | Yes (most fossils here) | Rarely (heat/pressure destroys) |
| Forms at | Surface or underground | Surface (low temp, low pressure) | Deep underground |

#### Rock Cycle Pathways to Know
- Igneous → Sedimentary: weathering, erosion, deposition, compaction, cementation
- Sedimentary → Metamorphic: burial, heat, pressure
- Metamorphic → Igneous: melting → magma → cooling
- Any rock → Magma → Igneous: melting
- Any rock → Sedimentary: weathering and deposition of fragments

#### Fastest vs. Slowest Rock Formation
Obsidian (volcanic glass) forms almost instantly. Granite batholiths cool over millions of years. Sedimentary rock formation varies: some limestone reefs grow meters per thousand years; coal takes millions of years. Metamorphic rock transformation takes tens of millions of years at depth. The rock cycle operates on geologic timescales — but it is always happening somewhere on Earth.
