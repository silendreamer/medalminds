#### The Five Criteria for a Mineral
Something is a mineral if it meets ALL five criteria: (1) **naturally occurring** — formed by natural geological processes, not made in a lab; (2) **inorganic** — not produced by living organisms (so coal, pearl, and amber are NOT minerals); (3) **solid** — liquid mercury is not a mineral; (4) **crystalline structure** — atoms arranged in a repeating geometric pattern; (5) **definite chemical composition** — either a pure element (like gold, Au) or a compound with a fixed ratio (like quartz, SiO₂).

#### Crystal Structure: The Hidden Architecture
Every mineral has a specific internal arrangement of atoms — its **crystal structure** — that determines its physical properties and external crystal shape. **Quartz** (SiO₂) always forms six-sided (hexagonal) prisms. **Halite** (NaCl, table salt) always forms cubic crystals. **Mica** forms thin, flat sheets. You can't always see crystals with the naked eye (if the mineral cooled quickly), but the internal structure is always there. Two minerals can have the same composition but different crystal structures — called **polymorphs** (e.g., diamond and graphite are both pure carbon, C).

#### Physical Properties Used to Identify Minerals
Geologists use several physical properties to identify unknown minerals: **hardness** (Mohs scale, 1=talc, 10=diamond), **color** (not always reliable — quartz can be purple, pink, white, or clear), **streak** (color of powder when scratched on ceramic plate — more reliable than surface color), **luster** (how light reflects: metallic vs. non-metallic), **cleavage** (breaks along flat planes) vs. **fracture** (breaks unevenly), **specific gravity** (density compared to water), and **special properties** (magnetism, fluorescence, acid reaction).

#### The Mohs Hardness Scale
Friedrich Mohs (1812) created a relative hardness scale from 1 to 10: 1=talc, 2=gypsum, 3=calcite, 4=fluorite, 5=apatite, 6=orthoclase feldspar, 7=quartz, 8=topaz, 9=corundum (ruby/sapphire), 10=diamond. A fingernail is about 2.5, a copper penny about 3, a steel file about 6.5. A mineral can scratch anything softer than itself. Diamond (10) can scratch everything; only diamond can scratch diamond.

#### Common Rock-Forming Minerals
The most abundant minerals in Earth's crust are **silicates** (containing silicon and oxygen): **quartz** (SiO₂), **feldspars** (most abundant mineral group in crust), **micas** (biotite, muscovite), **pyroxenes**, and **amphiboles**. Non-silicates include **carbonates** (calcite, dolomite), **oxides** (magnetite, hematite), **sulfides** (pyrite — "fool's gold"), and **halides** (halite). Knowing that 90% of the crust is silicate minerals is a key bowl fact.
