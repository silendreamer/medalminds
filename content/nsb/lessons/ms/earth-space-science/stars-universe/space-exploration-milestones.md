#### The Space Race
The **Space Race** between the U.S. and Soviet Union (late 1950s–1970s) drove rapid advancement:
- **Sputnik 1** (USSR, 1957): first artificial satellite; shocked the world
- **Yuri Gagarin** (USSR, 1961): first human in space
- **Apollo 11** (NASA, 1969): Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin became the first humans to walk on the Moon (July 20, 1969); Armstrong's famous words: "That's one small step for [a] man, one giant leap for mankind"
- **Apollo program** total: 6 Moon landings (Apollo 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 17); 12 humans walked on the Moon

#### Robotic Explorers
- **Voyager 1 and 2** (launched 1977): flew by Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus (2 only), Neptune (2 only); Voyager 1 crossed into interstellar space in 2012 — the most distant human-made object
- **Mars rovers**: Sojourner (1997), Spirit and Opportunity (2004), Curiosity (2012), Perseverance (2021) — searching for evidence of past water and life
- **Cassini-Huygens** (Saturn, 2004–2017): detailed study of Saturn's rings and moons; Huygens probe landed on Titan
- **New Horizons** (Pluto, 2015): first flyby of Pluto; now in the Kuiper Belt

#### Space Telescopes
- **Hubble Space Telescope** (1990–present): transformed astronomy with ultra-sharp images free of atmospheric distortion; determined universe age, discovered dark energy
- **James Webb Space Telescope (JWST)** (2021–present): 6.5-meter mirror; observes in infrared; studies atmospheres of exoplanets, early galaxies, star formation; first images released July 2022

#### The International Space Station
The **International Space Station (ISS)** has been continuously inhabited since November 2000 — over 25 years of continuous human presence in space. It orbits at ~400 km altitude, completes one orbit every ~90 minutes, and has hosted over 270 astronauts from 20 countries. It serves as a research laboratory for microgravity science, medicine, and materials.
