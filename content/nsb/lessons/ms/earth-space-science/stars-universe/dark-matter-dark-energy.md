#### Dark Matter
Astronomers noticed that galaxies rotate too fast at their outer edges — stars far from the galactic center orbit faster than they should if only visible matter provided the gravity. This implies unseen mass — **dark matter** — providing additional gravitational pull. Dark matter also explains the gravitational lensing of distant objects and the formation of large-scale structure in the universe. Dark matter comprises ~27% of the universe's total energy-mass content. Its nature is unknown — it doesn't interact with light, so it can't be seen, but its gravitational effects are clear.

#### Dark Energy
In 1998, two independent teams studying Type Ia supernova found that the universe's expansion is **accelerating** — distant galaxies are speeding away faster than expected. This requires a repulsive force counteracting gravity, dubbed **dark energy**. Dark energy comprises ~68% of the total energy-mass content of the universe. It may be Einstein's **cosmological constant** — an intrinsic energy of empty space — or something stranger. It's one of the greatest unsolved mysteries in science.

#### The Cosmic Composition
- **Ordinary (baryonic) matter** (atoms — everything we can see and touch): ~5%
- **Dark matter**: ~27%
- **Dark energy**: ~68%
This means everything we can directly observe and interact with — all stars, planets, gas, and dust — represents only about 5% of the universe's total content.
