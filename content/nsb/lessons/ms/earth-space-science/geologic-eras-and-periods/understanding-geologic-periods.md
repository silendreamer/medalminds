### Introduction to Geologic Eras and Periods

Geologic time is divided into several hierarchical categories, with the largest being eons, followed by eras, periods, epochs, and ages. Each of these divisions represents significant changes in Earth's geology, climate, and life forms. Understanding geologic periods is crucial for comprehending the history of life on Earth, including the evolution of organisms and the impact of mass extinctions.

### The Cambrian Period

The Cambrian period, which lasted from approximately 541 to 485 million years ago, is known for the "Cambrian Explosion," a rapid diversification of life. During this time, many major groups of animals first appeared in the fossil record, including trilobites, brachiopods, and early arthropods. The Cambrian seas were rich in nutrients, fostering the development of complex ecosystems and the first multicellular organisms.

### The Jurassic Period

Spanning from about 201 to 145 million years ago, the Jurassic period is famous for the dominance of dinosaurs on land. This era saw the emergence of iconic species such as the Brachiosaurus and the Allosaurus, as well as the first birds, like Archaeopteryx. Additionally, the Jurassic period was marked by significant geological activity, including the breakup of the supercontinent Pangaea, which led to the formation of new oceans and habitats.

### The Quaternary Period

The Quaternary period began around 2.58 million years ago and continues to the present day. It is characterized by repeated glacial and interglacial cycles, which have significantly influenced Earth's climate and ecosystems. During this period, modern humans (Homo sapiens) emerged, along with many other mammals, birds, and plants. The Quaternary is also notable for the extinction of several large mammals, such as woolly mammoths and saber-toothed cats, likely due to climate change and human activity.

### Mass Extinctions and Their Impact

Mass extinctions are significant events in Earth's history where a substantial percentage of species go extinct in a relatively short period. The most famous mass extinction occurred at the end of the Cretaceous period, around 66 million years ago, likely caused by a combination of volcanic activity and an asteroid impact. Other notable mass extinctions include the Permian-Triassic extinction, which eliminated about 90% of species. These events reshaped ecosystems and allowed for the evolution of new species in the aftermath.

### Key Events and Organisms of Each Period

Each geologic period is defined by key events and the organisms that thrived during that time. For example, the Cambrian period was marked by the first appearance of hard-shelled organisms, while the Jurassic period is known for the rise of dinosaurs. The Quaternary period is significant for the development of human civilizations and the impact of climate change on biodiversity. Understanding these events helps us appreciate the dynamic history of life on Earth.

### Review Questions

1. What significant event characterized the Cambrian period, leading to a rapid diversification of life?
2. Name one dinosaur that lived during the Jurassic period.
3. What major climatic changes define the Quaternary period, and how have they impacted human evolution?
