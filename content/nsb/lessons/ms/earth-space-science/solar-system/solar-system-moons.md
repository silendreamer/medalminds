#### Earth's Moon
Earth's Moon is unusually large relative to its host planet. It formed ~4.5 billion years ago from debris ejected when a Mars-sized body (Theia) collided with early Earth. The Moon is tidally locked — the same side always faces Earth. The lunar surface shows heavy cratering (the Moon has no plate tectonics or weathering to erase craters), with darker lowland areas called **maria** (ancient lava flows) and brighter highland regions.

#### The Galilean Moons of Jupiter
Discovered by Galileo in 1610, Jupiter's four largest moons are: **Io** (most volcanically active body in the solar system, due to tidal heating from Jupiter's gravity); **Europa** (smooth ice surface hiding a subsurface liquid water ocean — a prime candidate for extraterrestrial life); **Ganymede** (largest moon in the solar system, larger than Mercury); **Callisto** (heavily cratered, ancient surface).

#### Saturn's Moons
**Titan** is Saturn's largest moon — bigger than Mercury — with a dense nitrogen atmosphere and lakes of liquid methane and ethane. It's the only moon with a substantial atmosphere. **Enceladus** shoots water vapor and ice from its south pole through cracks in its icy surface, suggesting a subsurface ocean. The Cassini spacecraft flew through these plumes and detected organic molecules — making Enceladus another candidate for life.
