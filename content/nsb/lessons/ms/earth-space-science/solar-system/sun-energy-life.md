#### The Sun as the Foundation of Food Webs
Photosynthetic organisms — plants, algae, cyanobacteria — capture solar energy and convert it to chemical energy (glucose) via photosynthesis. This energy flows through food webs: primary producers are eaten by primary consumers (herbivores), which are eaten by secondary and tertiary consumers (carnivores). Energy is lost (as heat) at each step, which is why food chains are typically limited to 4–5 links and why you need far more plant biomass than animal biomass in an ecosystem.

#### Solar Energy Technology
Humans now harness solar energy directly through **photovoltaic (PV) cells**, which convert sunlight to electricity using the photoelectric effect (Einstein won his Nobel Prize for explaining this). Solar thermal systems use sunlight to heat water. Solar energy is renewable, produces no emissions during operation, and costs have dropped by 90% since 2010. The challenge is storage — the sun doesn't shine at night.

#### Ultraviolet Radiation: Benefits and Risks
UV radiation from the Sun has both beneficial and harmful effects. UVB radiation enables skin cells to produce **vitamin D**, essential for calcium absorption and bone health. However, excessive UV exposure damages DNA, causing sunburn and increasing skin cancer risk. The ozone layer in the stratosphere absorbs most harmful UV before it reaches Earth's surface — one reason protecting the ozone layer is so important.
