#### Oceans as Heat Reservoirs
Oceans cover 71% of Earth and absorb about 90% of the extra heat from the greenhouse effect. Because water has a high **heat capacity**, oceans warm and cool slowly compared to land. This moderates coastal climates — San Francisco stays cooler in summer and warmer in winter than Sacramento, which is inland. Ocean currents also carry warm water from the tropics toward the poles, distributing heat globally.

#### El Niño and La Niña
**El Niño** occurs when warm Pacific surface water shifts east toward South America. This disrupts normal weather patterns globally: California gets more rain, Australia and Indonesia face drought, the Atlantic hurricane season weakens. **La Niña** is the opposite — cooler-than-normal eastern Pacific water — intensifying hurricanes and bringing drought to the western U.S. These patterns occur every 2–7 years and last 9–12 months.

#### Monsoons
**Monsoons** are seasonal wind reversals driven by the temperature difference between land and ocean. In summer, land heats faster than the ocean, creating low pressure over land that draws in moist ocean air — bringing heavy rains. In winter, land cools faster, reversing the flow. The South Asian monsoon brings 80% of India's annual rainfall in just a few months, supporting over a billion people's agriculture.
