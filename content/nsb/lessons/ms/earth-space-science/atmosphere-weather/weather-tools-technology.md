#### Doppler Radar
**Doppler radar** sends out microwave pulses and detects the energy reflected back by precipitation. Crucially, it can measure the **motion** of precipitation — detecting whether rain is moving toward or away from the radar. This allows meteorologists to identify rotation inside thunderstorms (a tornado signature) before a tornado touches down, giving vital warning time. The U.S. operates over 160 NEXRAD Doppler radar stations.

#### Weather Satellites
NOAA (National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration) operates geostationary satellites that hover 35,786 km above one spot, providing continuous images of cloud patterns. Polar-orbiting satellites pass over both poles, gathering detailed atmospheric data globally. Satellite images reveal hurricane structure, wildfire smoke plumes, and large storm systems in real time.

#### Weather Balloons and Radiosondes
Twice daily, weather stations worldwide launch **weather balloons** carrying **radiosondes** — small instrument packages that transmit temperature, humidity, pressure, and wind data as they ascend. Balloons reach up to 40 km altitude before bursting. This vertical profile data is essential for computer weather models. The U.S. launches about 92 radiosonde balloons per day.

#### The National Weather Service
The **National Weather Service (NWS)** is NOAA's division responsible for weather forecasts, watches, warnings, and advisories. It feeds data to private weather services, emergency management agencies, and the public. The NWS issues **watches** (conditions favorable for severe weather) and **warnings** (severe weather is occurring or imminent) to help people take protective action.
