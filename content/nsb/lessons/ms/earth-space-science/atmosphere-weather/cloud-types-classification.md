#### High-Altitude Clouds

**High-altitude clouds** form above 20,000 feet and are made of ice crystals instead of water droplets because it's so cold up there. The most common high-altitude cloud is the **cirrus** cloud—thin, wispy, and feathery, like brushstrokes across the sky. Cirrus clouds often signal that a weather change is coming within the next 24 hours. Another high-altitude type is the **cirrocumulus**, which looks like a pattern of small, white bumps or ripples, sometimes called a "mackerel sky." These clouds generally mean fair weather, but they can indicate that a storm is approaching.

#### Mid-Altitude Clouds

**Mid-altitude clouds** form between 6,500 and 20,000 feet. The most common mid-altitude cloud is the **altocumulus**, which appears as white or gray rounded masses, often in rows or patterns. The **altostratus** is a gray or white sheet that covers the whole sky and is thick enough that you can't see the sun clearly through it. Mid-altitude clouds often bring gentle rain or drizzle, and they usually signal that the weather is about to change. When you see these clouds, it's a good time to start paying attention to weather forecasts.

#### Low-Altitude Clouds

**Low-altitude clouds** form below 6,500 feet and are thick enough to block out the sun completely. The **cumulus** cloud is the puffy, white, "fair-weather" cloud with a flat base—like floating cotton balls. These indicate good weather, though they can grow into thunderstorms if conditions are right. The **stratus** cloud is a gray, featureless sheet that covers the sky and often brings light rain or drizzle. The **cumulonimbus** is the giant thunderstorm cloud that towers vertically; it's the most dangerous cloud type and brings heavy rain, lightning, hail, and strong winds.

#### Reading Weather Clues from Clouds

Different cloud types tell you what weather is coming. **Thin, wispy cirrus clouds** usually mean fair weather today but a change tomorrow. **Layered, gray altostratus or stratus clouds** suggest steady rain or drizzle within hours. **Towering cumulonimbus clouds** demand immediate attention—seek shelter because severe weather is imminent. **Puffy cumulus clouds** with flat bases mean pleasant weather right now. Learning to read clouds is like reading nature's weather forecast without needing a weather app. The shape, altitude, and thickness all work together to tell the story.
