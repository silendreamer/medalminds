#### Must-Know Earthquake Facts
1. Earthquakes result from sudden release of stress along faults (elastic rebound)
2. Focus = underground rupture point; epicenter = surface point directly above focus
3. Shallow-focus earthquakes cause most damage (energy doesn't attenuate before surface)
4. Three fault types: normal (tension/divergent), reverse/thrust (compression/convergent), strike-slip (shear/transform)
5. San Andreas Fault = right-lateral strike-slip fault
6. Magnitude scale is logarithmic: each unit = 10× wave amplitude, ~32× energy
7. Largest recorded earthquake: 1960 Valdivia, Chile, M9.5
8. Moment magnitude scale (Mw) is more accurate than Richter for large quakes
9. Tsunamis caused by seafloor displacement during submarine earthquakes
10. Liquefaction: saturated sandy soil behaves like liquid during shaking
11. Aftershocks follow major earthquakes; foreshocks precede (only known in retrospect)
12. P-waves (compression) arrive before S-waves (shear) — basis for early warning systems

#### Fault Type Quick Reference
| Fault type | Motion | Boundary type | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | Hanging wall drops | Divergent | East African Rift |
| Reverse/Thrust | Hanging wall rises | Convergent | Rocky Mountains |
| Strike-slip | Horizontal sliding | Transform | San Andreas Fault |

2. If a magnitude 6 earthquake releases X amount of energy, approximately how much energy does a magnitude 8 release?
3. An earthquake has its focus 300 km underground. How does this affect the damage at the surface compared to a shallow earthquake of the same magnitude?
