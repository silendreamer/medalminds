#### The Puzzle Pieces of Pangaea
Look at a world map and you'll notice that the coastlines of South America and Africa look like they could fit together — like puzzle pieces. German meteorologist **Alfred Wegener** noticed this in 1912 and proposed the theory of **continental drift**: the idea that all continents were once joined as a single supercontinent called **Pangaea** ("all land" in Greek) about 300 million years ago. Over hundreds of millions of years, Pangaea broke apart, and the pieces drifted to their current positions.

#### Evidence #1: Fit of the Continents
The match between South America and Africa is not just rough — it's precise. When geologists fit the continents together at the continental shelves (not just the coastlines), the fit is remarkably exact. Computer modeling shows the fit has less than 1° of angular misalignment across the entire Atlantic Ocean. This alone is suggestive, but Wegener knew he needed more evidence.

#### Evidence #2: Matching Fossils on Separate Continents
Fossils of the same organisms found on continents now separated by thousands of kilometers of ocean provide powerful evidence. **Mesosaurus** — a small freshwater reptile that could not cross saltwater oceans — has been found in both South America and Africa. **Glossopteris** — a plant — has been found in Antarctica, India, South Africa, South America, and Australia. These organisms couldn't have crossed oceans; the continents must have been connected.

#### Evidence #3: Matching Rock Formations
Rock formations on opposite sides of the Atlantic Ocean match in type, age, and sequence. The Appalachian Mountains in eastern North America line up perfectly with the Caledonian Mountains in Scotland and Scandinavia. Mountain chains in West Africa match those in Brazil. These mountain belts formed from the same ancient collision event — when the continents drifted apart, the mountain ranges were split between them.

#### Evidence #4: Ancient Climate Evidence (Paleoclimatology)
Evidence of ancient climates supports continental drift. **Glacial striations** (scratches carved by glaciers) are found in South Africa, India, Australia, and South America — regions now near the tropics. These areas must have been near the South Pole when the glaciers carved them. Conversely, coal deposits (formed from tropical swamp plants) are found in Antarctica — which must have once had a tropical climate. This only makes sense if the continents moved.

#### Review Questions
1. What is Pangaea, and approximately when did it exist?
2. Why does finding Mesosaurus fossils in both South America and Africa support continental drift?
3. Why are coal deposits in Antarctica considered evidence for continental drift?
