#### High-Yield Facts
- **10% rule**: only ~10% of energy passes from one trophic level to the next
- **Producers** = autotrophs; make their own food via photosynthesis (or chemosynthesis)
- **Keystone species** = species with disproportionately large ecosystem impact
- Sea otters → keystone species; eat urchins → prevent urchin overgrazing of kelp
- Wolves reintroduced to **Yellowstone in 1995** → trophic cascade changed river morphology
- Phytoplankton produce ~**50% of Earth's oxygen**
- **Trophic cascade** = ripple effect through food web when one level is disrupted
- Decomposers = bacteria and fungi (NOT detritivores, which are animals that eat dead matter)

#### Bowl-Style Toss-Up Stems
- "This ecological rule states that approximately 10% of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next…" → **10% rule** (or rule of trophic efficiency)
- "This type of species has a disproportionately large effect on an ecosystem relative to its abundance, like sea otters in kelp forests…" → **keystone species**
- "These microscopic, photosynthetic organisms form the base of most marine food webs…" → **phytoplankton**
- "This cascade of ecological effects, triggered by changes at one trophic level, is exemplified by wolf reintroduction to Yellowstone…" → **trophic cascade**

#### Common Traps
- Decomposers = bacteria and fungi (NOT earthworms, which are detritivores)
- "Producer" = autotroph (can include chemosynthetic bacteria, not just photosynthetic plants)
- The 10% rule means 90% is LOST — don't confuse "10% passes" with "10% is lost"

#### Review Questions
1. What percentage of energy is typically transferred between trophic levels?
2. What two types of organisms are decomposers?
3. What happened ecologically when wolves were reintroduced to Yellowstone?
