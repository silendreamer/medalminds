#### What Is a Watershed?
A *watershed* (also called a drainage basin) is all the land that drains into a particular water body (river, lake, ocean). Every property is in a watershed — rain that falls on your lawn, street, or farm eventually drains to a creek, river, and ultimately the ocean. Actions on land — applying fertilizers, paving surfaces, changing vegetation — affect downstream water quality.

#### Nonpoint Source Pollution
*Point source pollution* comes from a specific, identifiable source (a pipe, smokestack). *Nonpoint source pollution* (NPS) comes from dispersed sources across a landscape — agricultural fertilizer and pesticide runoff, urban stormwater, pet waste, road salt. NPS is the leading cause of water quality degradation in U.S. waters (EPA). Because it comes from everywhere, it's harder to regulate than point sources.

#### Solutions: What Actually Works
*Riparian buffers* (strips of native vegetation along stream banks) filter runoff before it enters streams. Constructed wetlands can remove >80% of nitrogen from agricultural runoff. Permeable pavement allows stormwater infiltration. Citizen science programs (macroinvertebrate monitoring, water quality testing) help track ecosystem health. At the individual level: reducing fertilizer use, picking up pet waste, washing cars on grass (not pavement), and planting native species reduce NPS pollution.
