#### Biomes Are Defined by Climate
A *biome* is a large-scale community of organisms adapted to a particular climate. The two key climate factors that determine biomes are *temperature* and *precipitation* (rainfall). Temperature is largely determined by latitude (distance from the equator) and altitude (elevation). Tropical regions near the equator are warm year-round; polar regions are cold year-round. Precipitation determines whether an area is forest, grassland, or desert.

#### The Major Terrestrial Biomes
Tropical rainforest: near equator, high rainfall (>200 cm/year), warm, highest biodiversity of any biome (~50% of all species in ~6% of Earth's land). Temperate deciduous forest: moderate rainfall, four distinct seasons, trees lose leaves in fall (oak, maple, beech). Taiga (boreal forest): long cold winters, cone-bearing trees (conifers — spruce, fir, pine), largest terrestrial biome by area. Tundra: extreme cold, permafrost, very low precipitation, no trees, short growing season. Tropical savanna: seasonal rainfall, dominated by grasses with scattered trees (acacia), large herbivore populations (Africa). Temperate grassland: moderate climate, few trees, deep fertile soils, high winds (North American prairie). Desert: <25 cm precipitation/year, extreme temperature swings, adaptations for water conservation. Chaparral (Mediterranean shrubland): hot dry summers, mild wet winters, fire-adapted shrubs.

#### Aquatic Biomes
Aquatic biomes cover ~75% of Earth's surface. Freshwater biomes include rivers, lakes, wetlands, and ponds. Marine biomes include oceans (open ocean, coral reefs, intertidal zones, deep sea). Estuaries — where freshwater rivers meet saltwater ocean — are among the most productive ecosystems on Earth (nursery grounds for many marine species). Wetlands (swamps, marshes, bogs) have some of the highest biodiversity and provide flood control, water filtration, and carbon storage.

#### Why Biome Patterns Are Predictable
If you travel from the equator to the poles, you pass through the same sequence of biomes you'd pass through climbing a tall mountain from base to summit. Both involve decreasing temperature: rainforest → grassland/forest → taiga-like zone → tundra/snowpack. This predictable pattern reflects the central role of climate in determining biome type.
