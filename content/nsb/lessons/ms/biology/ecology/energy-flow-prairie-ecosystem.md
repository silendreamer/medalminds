#### The Prairie Food Web
A North American tallgrass prairie supports: producers (grasses, wildflowers, legumes) → primary consumers (grasshoppers, prairie dogs, bison) → secondary consumers (badgers, coyotes, ferruginous hawks) → tertiary consumers (great horned owls, golden eagles, historically: wolves). Decomposers (bacteria, fungi, dung beetles) recycle nutrients and are essential to soil health.

#### Calculation Practice
If prairie grasses have 500,000 kcal in a 1-hectare area: (a) How much energy is available to prairie dogs (primary consumers)? (b) How much to badgers (secondary)? (c) How much to golden eagles (tertiary)? Answers: (a) 50,000 kcal, (b) 5,000 kcal, (c) 500 kcal. This is why a single hectare can support thousands of grasshoppers, hundreds of prairie dogs, dozens of badgers, but only a fraction of a golden eagle's territory.

#### What Happens When Bison Are Removed?
Historically, bison grazed prairie grasses, keeping them short and stimulating root growth. Their wallowing created muddy depressions that became ponds. Their dung fertilized soil. When bison were eliminated from most of the Great Plains in the 1800s, grass species composition changed, ponds dried up, and many species dependent on bison-disturbed habitat declined. Bison were a keystone species of the prairie ecosystem.
