#### The Bald Eagle
The bald eagle was reduced to just 417 breeding pairs in the lower 48 states by 1963, primarily due to DDT's eggshell-thinning effects, hunting, and habitat loss. After DDT was banned in 1972 and the Endangered Species Act (1973) provided legal protection, bald eagle populations recovered to over 9,000 breeding pairs by 2006, when the species was removed from the Endangered Species List — one of the greatest conservation success stories.

#### The American Bison
American bison were reduced from ~60 million to fewer than 1,000 by 1890 due to commercial hunting. Conservation efforts began in the early 1900s; today there are ~500,000 bison (mostly on ranches, with ~30,000 in public herds). The Prairie Wind Project and American Prairie Reserve are working to restore large wild bison herds across the Northern Great Plains, with associated grassland and wildlife restoration.

#### California Condor
The California condor (*Gymnogyps californianus*) went entirely extinct in the wild in 1987 (only 27 individuals remained, all captured for captive breeding). Through intensive captive breeding and reintroduction, the wild population has grown to over 300 individuals as of 2023. Threats (lead poisoning from spent ammunition, power line collisions) are still managed. The condor is an example of how extreme intervention can prevent extinction.

#### The Endangered Species Act
The U.S. Endangered Species Act (1973) prohibits "take" of listed species (killing, harming, harassing), requires federal agencies to avoid jeopardizing listed species, and protects critical habitat. Of species listed, ~98% have avoided extinction — though many have not yet recovered to healthy population sizes. Debates continue about balancing species protection with economic development.
