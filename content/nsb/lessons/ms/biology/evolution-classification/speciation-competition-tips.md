#### High-Yield Facts
- **Allopatric speciation** = geographic barrier separates populations
- **Sympatric speciation** = speciation without geographic separation; often via polyploidy in plants
- **Reproductive isolation** = populations cannot interbreed → species diverge
- **Adaptive radiation** = one ancestor → many species filling different niches (Galápagos finches, Hawaiian honeycreepers, African cichlids)
- **Founder effect** = new population has reduced genetic diversity from small founding group
- **Five mass extinctions**: worst was **Permian-Triassic** (~252 mya, ~96% marine species lost)
- **K-Pg extinction** (~66 mya) = asteroid impact, killed non-avian dinosaurs
- **Polyploidy** = extra chromosome sets; common in plants; can cause instant speciation
- A mule is sterile → horse and donkey are **different species** (biological species concept)

#### Bowl-Style Toss-Up Stems
- "This type of speciation occurs when a geographic barrier separates a population…" → **allopatric speciation**
- "This process, in which one ancestral species rapidly diversifies into many species filling different ecological roles, is seen in Galápagos finches…" → **adaptive radiation**
- "This mass extinction, sometimes called 'The Great Dying,' eliminated approximately 96% of marine species about 252 million years ago…" → **Permian-Triassic extinction**
- "This condition, common in plants, results from having more than two complete sets of chromosomes and can produce a new species instantly…" → **polyploidy**

#### Common Traps
- Mules are sterile → horse + donkey = two species (don't be tricked by "they can mate")
- Adaptive radiation requires geographic isolation (usually) + ecological opportunity
- The K-Pg extinction (asteroid, 66 mya) killed non-avian dinosaurs — birds survived

#### Review Questions
1. What is the difference between allopatric and sympatric speciation?
2. What mass extinction killed the non-avian dinosaurs, and what is thought to have caused it?
3. Why is a mule evidence that horses and donkeys are separate species?
