#### What a Punnett Square Does
A Punnett square is a diagram that shows all possible combinations of alleles that offspring of a cross could inherit. It was developed by the English geneticist Reginald Punnett in 1905. The Punnett square doesn't tell you what WILL happen — it tells you the probability of each outcome.

#### Setting Up a Monohybrid Square
For a single gene: (1) Write one parent's alleles across the top (one per column). (2) Write the other parent's alleles down the side (one per row). (3) Fill in each box by combining the column letter with the row letter. (4) Count the combinations. Example: Tt × Tt gives: TT, Tt, Tt, tt — ratio 1:2:1 (genotype) and 3:1 dominant:recessive (phenotype).

#### Reading Genotype and Phenotype Ratios
Genotype ratio: count each unique genotype. In Tt × Tt: 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt. Phenotype ratio: group by what's visible. TT and Tt both look tall (dominant); tt looks short (recessive). So 3 tall : 1 short. This 3:1 phenotype ratio is the classic Mendelian result.

#### Probability Interpretation
The Punnett square gives probabilities, not guarantees. A 1/4 (25%) probability of tt means that, on average, 25% of offspring from this cross will be homozygous recessive. But in a small litter of 4, you might get 0 or 4 — just as flipping a fair coin 4 times doesn't guarantee exactly 2 heads.

#### Key Terminology for Questions
Monohybrid cross = one gene. Dihybrid cross = two genes simultaneously. Testcross = crossing unknown genotype with homozygous recessive (tt) to determine it. If ANY offspring are recessive (tt), the unknown parent must have carried a recessive allele.

#### Review Questions

2. What does a 3:1 phenotype ratio tell you about the parent genotypes in a monohybrid cross?
