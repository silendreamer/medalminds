#### Mutation Types at a Glance

| Type | What changes | Effect on protein |
|---|---|---|
| Silent/synonymous | Single base, same amino acid | None |
| Missense | Single base, different amino acid | Altered function (may be minor or major) |
| Nonsense | Single base, creates stop codon | Truncated, usually nonfunctional protein |
| Frameshift (insertion) | Adds bases (not multiple of 3) | Garbled sequence from point onward |
| Frameshift (deletion) | Removes bases (not multiple of 3) | Garbled sequence from point onward |
| Chromosomal deletion | Loses chromosome segment | Loss of many genes |
| Chromosomal duplication | Copies segment | Extra copies of genes |
| Chromosomal inversion | Segment reversed | Gene regulation disrupted |

#### Causes: Quick Reference
- UV radiation → thymine dimers → skin cancer
- Ionizing radiation (X-rays, gamma rays) → breaks DNA strands
- Chemical mutagens (benzene, cigarette chemicals) → base modifications
- Replication errors → not caught by proofreading (rate: ~1 per billion bp)
- Viral insertion → provirus disrupts gene function

#### Effects on Organisms
- Somatic mutation → potentially cancer; not inherited
- Germline mutation → inherited by offspring
- Most mutations → neutral (in non-coding DNA or silent)
- Rare mutations → disease (sickle cell, CF, Huntington's)
- Rarest but most important → beneficial mutation (lactase persistence, sickle cell trait)

#### Review Questions
1. Why are frameshift mutations typically more damaging than point substitution mutations?
2. A mutation occurs in a skin cell due to UV exposure. Can this mutation be passed to the person's children? Why or why not?
3. What makes a mutation "beneficial," and give one example from human evolution?
