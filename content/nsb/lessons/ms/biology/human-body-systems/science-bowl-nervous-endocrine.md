#### Top 15 High-Yield Facts

1. Neuron parts: dendrites → cell body → axon → terminals
2. Action potential = electrical signal; Na⁺ in (depolarization), K⁺ out (repolarization)
3. Myelin sheath speeds nerve conduction
4. Synapse: neurotransmitter crosses from axon terminal to dendrite
5. CNS = brain + spinal cord; PNS = all other nerves
6. Cerebrum = thought, movement, senses; Cerebellum = coordination
7. Medulla oblongata = breathing, heart rate
8. Hippocampus = memory; Amygdala = emotion
9. Hypothalamus = master link between nervous and endocrine
10. Pituitary = "master gland" — controls other glands
11. Insulin (pancreas) = lowers blood glucose; Glucagon = raises
12. Epinephrine (adrenal medulla) = fight-or-flight
13. ADH (posterior pituitary) = water reabsorption in kidneys
14. Thyroxine (thyroid) = metabolic rate
15. Negative feedback = rising levels suppress the producing gland

#### Practice Toss-Up Stems
1. "This brain region coordinates balance and fine motor control." → **Cerebellum**
2. "Called the 'master gland,' this gland secretes growth hormone and TSH, among others." → **Pituitary gland**
3. "Released by the adrenal medulla during stress, this hormone prepares the body for fight-or-flight." → **Epinephrine (adrenaline)**
4. "In the nervous system, the electrical charge reversal that travels down an axon is called this." → **Action potential**
5. "This hormone, secreted by the pancreas in response to high blood glucose, causes cells to take up glucose." → **Insulin**

#### Review Questions
1. What is the function of the myelin sheath around axons?
2. Name the neurotransmitter responsible for muscle contraction at the neuromuscular junction.
3. What happens to heart rate, breathing rate, and blood glucose during the fight-or-flight response?
