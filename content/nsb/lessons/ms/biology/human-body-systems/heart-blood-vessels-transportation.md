#### The Heart's Structure
The human heart is a muscular pump divided into four chambers: two atria (upper, receiving chambers) and two ventricles (lower, pumping chambers). The right side handles deoxygenated blood; the left side handles oxygenated blood. A thick muscular wall called the septum separates the left and right sides. Valves between chambers and at the exits of the ventricles prevent backflow — they open and close with each heartbeat, creating the "lub-dub" sound.

#### Two Loops of Circulation
The circulatory system has two circuits: (1) Pulmonary circulation: right ventricle → pulmonary arteries → lungs (where blood picks up O₂ and drops CO₂) → pulmonary veins → left atrium. (2) Systemic circulation: left ventricle → aorta → body tissues (where O₂ is delivered and CO₂ picked up) → vena cava → right atrium. The left ventricle has thicker walls because it pumps to the entire body.

#### Blood Vessel Types
Arteries carry blood away from the heart (usually oxygenated, except pulmonary arteries). They have thick, elastic walls to handle high pressure. Veins carry blood toward the heart (usually deoxygenated, except pulmonary veins). They have thinner walls and one-way valves to prevent backflow. Capillaries are tiny, one-cell-thick vessels where gas and nutrient exchange actually occurs between blood and tissues.

#### Blood Pressure
Blood pressure is the force blood exerts on artery walls. It's measured as systolic (pressure when heart contracts) over diastolic (pressure when heart relaxes), in mmHg — normal is about 120/80. High blood pressure (hypertension) stresses blood vessel walls and increases heart attack and stroke risk.

#### Science Bowl Clue
Arteries vs. veins is a classic bowl confusion point: arteries carry blood AWAY from heart (mnemonic: Artery = Away); veins carry blood toward heart. The exception — pulmonary vessels go the "opposite" direction for oxygenation. Pulmonary ARTERY carries deoxygenated blood; pulmonary VEIN carries oxygenated blood.
