#### Top 15 High-Yield Facts

1. Bone matrix = calcium phosphate (mineral) + collagen (organic protein)
2. Red bone marrow → produces blood cells (flat bones: skull, sternum, pelvis)
3. Osteoblasts build; Osteoclasts break down
4. Epiphyseal (growth) plate = where bone length increases during childhood
5. Sarcomere = functional unit of muscle contraction
6. Actin (thin) + Myosin (thick) = sliding filament proteins
7. Ca²⁺ triggers contraction; ATP powers myosin cross-bridge cycle
8. Acetylcholine = neurotransmitter at neuromuscular junction
9. Three muscle types: skeletal (voluntary), cardiac (involuntary, heart), smooth (involuntary, organs)
10. Tendons: muscle → bone; Ligaments: bone → bone
11. Synovial fluid lubricates freely movable joints
12. Cartilage is avascular → heals slowly
13. Sprain = ligament tear; Strain = muscle/tendon tear
14. Slow-twitch (Type I): endurance, aerobic; Fast-twitch (Type II): power, anaerobic
15. Compact bone outer; spongy bone inner; medullary cavity in center

#### Practice Toss-Up Stems
1. "This is the basic contractile unit of skeletal muscle." → **Sarcomere**
2. "These two connective tissue types hold bones together at joints vs. attach muscles to bones." → **Ligaments and tendons**
3. "Released at the neuromuscular junction, this neurotransmitter triggers skeletal muscle contraction." → **Acetylcholine**
4. "Red bone marrow, found in spongy bone, produces what three blood components?" → **Red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets**
5. "In the sliding filament theory, which ion release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum initiates contraction?" → **Calcium ions (Ca²⁺)**

#### Review Questions
1. Why is compact bone stronger than spongy bone, and where is each found?
2. What molecule provides the immediate energy for the myosin power stroke?
3. Why can't cartilage heal as quickly as skin or bone?
