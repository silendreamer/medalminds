#### Fractures
A fracture is a break or crack in bone. Types: simple (bone breaks but doesn't pierce skin), compound/open (bone breaks through skin — infection risk), stress fracture (small crack from repeated impact — overuse), comminuted (shattered into multiple pieces). Bone heals through: hematoma formation → callus (soft then hard cartilage) → ossification (mineral deposition) → remodeling (reshaping to final form). Process takes weeks to months.

#### Sprains and Strains
A sprain is a ligament tear (occurs at joints — ankle, knee). A strain is a muscle or tendon tear. Both involve micro-tears of collagen fibers. Grade I: mild stretch/micro-tear. Grade II: partial tear. Grade III: complete rupture (may require surgery). The RICE protocol: Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation — reduces swelling and pain in the first 48–72 hours.

#### Overuse Injuries
Overuse injuries result from repetitive stress without adequate recovery. Tendinitis = inflammation of tendons (tennis elbow, patellar tendinitis). Shin splints = pain in the tibia from overloading. Stress fractures in long-distance runners. These respond to rest, cross-training, and gradual return to activity.

#### ACL and Meniscus Injuries
The anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) is a major stabilizing ligament of the knee. ACL tears are common in cutting sports (soccer, basketball). The meniscus is cartilage padding inside the knee joint. Both injuries often require surgery and months of rehabilitation. The knee is the most commonly injured joint in sports.
