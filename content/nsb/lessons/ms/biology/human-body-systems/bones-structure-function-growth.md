#### What Bones Are Made Of
Bone is a living tissue, not dead rock. Bone matrix is ~70% mineral (mainly calcium phosphate crystals — hydroxyapatite) and ~30% organic material (mainly collagen fibers). The mineral gives hardness and compression strength; the collagen gives flexibility and resistance to fracture. This combination makes bone stronger than concrete per unit weight. If you removed all the mineral, the bone would be rubbery (bend without breaking). Remove the collagen, and it would be brittle (shatter easily).

#### Bone Structure
The outer layer, compact bone, is dense and strong — composed of Haversian systems (osteons), concentric rings of mineralized matrix with blood vessels through the center. The inner layer, spongy (cancellous) bone, has a lattice structure that reduces weight while maintaining strength. The center of long bones contains the medullary cavity, filled with yellow bone marrow (fat storage). Red bone marrow (in spongy bone of flat bones — skull, sternum, vertebrae, pelvis) produces blood cells.

#### Functions of the Skeleton
The skeleton: (1) Supports the body against gravity. (2) Protects organs (skull → brain, ribcage → heart/lungs). (3) Enables movement via joints and muscle attachment. (4) Produces blood cells (red and white blood cells and platelets in red marrow). (5) Stores minerals (calcium and phosphorus) that can be released into blood.

#### Bone Growth
Long bones grow at epiphyseal (growth) plates — cartilage-rich zones near the ends of bones. Hormones (growth hormone, thyroid hormone, sex hormones) stimulate chondrocytes to divide; new cartilage is then replaced by bone (ossification). Growth plates close when sex hormones surge at puberty — after closure, height cannot increase. Bone density continues to increase until the mid-30s.
