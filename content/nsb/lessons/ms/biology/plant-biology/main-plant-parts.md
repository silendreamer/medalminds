#### The Root System
Roots anchor plants and absorb water and nutrients from soil. Most plants have either a taproot (one main root, like carrots) or fibrous roots (many thin roots spreading out, like grasses). Root hair cells increase surface area for absorption. Water moves up through the xylem, a vascular tissue that transports water from roots to leaves.

#### The Stem and Vascular Tissue
Stems support leaves and flowers above ground and contain vascular bundles — xylem carries water up, and phloem carries sugars down from leaves. In woody plants, stems thicken over time with annual rings. Herbaceous plants (like grass) have soft, green stems, while woody plants (like trees) have thick, hard wood.

#### Leaves and Photosynthesis
Leaves are the plant's food factories. The waxy upper surface (cuticle) reduces water loss. Tiny pores called stomata (singular: stoma) on the leaf's underside allow gas exchange. Inside leaves, photosynthesis happens in cells packed with chloroplasts.

#### Chloroplasts and Photosynthesis Overview
Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, the green pigment that captures light energy. The photosynthesis equation is: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light energy → C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose) + 6O₂. This process converts light energy into chemical energy stored in glucose.
