#### Phototropism: Growth Toward Light
Phototropism is directional growth toward light. Shoots exhibit positive phototropism (grow toward light). Roots exhibit negative phototropism (grow away from light). This makes sense: shoots need light for photosynthesis; roots need darkness to avoid drying.

#### Gravitropism: Growth With or Against Gravity
Gravitropism is directional growth in response to gravity. Roots exhibit positive gravitropism (grow downward). Shoots exhibit negative gravitropism (grow upward). Gravity sensing occurs in special cells (statocytes) containing starch-filled amyloplasts that settle downward.

#### Hydrotropism: Growth Toward Water
Roots grow toward moisture (positive hydrotropism). Sensing likely occurs in root caps where osmoreceptors detect water gradients. Hydrotropism helps roots find soil moisture, critical for water uptake and survival in dry environments.

#### Thigmotropism: Response to Touch
Climbing vines wrap around supports in response to contact (positive thigmotropism). Some plants close leaflets when touched (the Venus flytrap snaps shut). Touch-sensitive plants may detect mechanical stress or chemical signals released by damage.

#### Hyponasty and Epinasty: Unequal Growth
Hyponasty is faster growth on the lower surface of a leaf, causing it to curl upward. Epinasty is faster growth on the upper surface, causing downward curvature. These differential growth rates are controlled by plant hormones and respond to temperature, humidity, and light.
