#### Coevolution of Flowers and Pollinators
Flowers and their pollinators have coevolved — each shaped the other. Bright red flowers attract hummingbirds. Sweet-smelling night-blooming flowers attract moths. Orchids have shapes that physically fit specific bees. This specificity ensures efficient pollination but also means some plants depend entirely on one pollinator species.

#### Why Flowers Evolved
Early plants reproduced by spores; flowers evolved to attract animal pollinators. Animals are more efficient than wind or water at moving pollen over long distances. A single insect can pollinate many flowers quickly. Flowers are thus an adaptation for more reliable pollination.

#### Seed Dispersal Coevolution
Fleshy fruits attracted animals, which ate them and dispersed seeds. Over time, fruits became tastier and seeds more hardy. Animals evolved to seek out fruits. Migratory birds carry seeds long distances, allowing plants to colonize new areas.

#### Genetic Diversity and Evolution
Sexual reproduction provides genetic variation, the raw material for evolution. Genetic diversity increases survival when conditions change. Populations with high genetic diversity are more resistant to pests, disease, and climate change. This is why crop scientists preserve wild relatives of domesticated plants — they carry valuable genetic variation.

#### Asexual Reproduction in Agriculture
Many valuable crops are asexually reproduced (potatoes, grapes, apples) because their genotypes are stable and productive. But this creates vulnerability. Plant breeders must breed new varieties with disease resistance and adapt to changing climates. Combining genes from wild relatives through sexual reproduction is essential.
