#### Predicting Ionic Formulas
When ionic compounds form, the total charge must equal zero. Use group numbers to predict charges, then balance:
- Sodium (Na⁺) + Chlorine (Cl⁻): 1+ and 1− → **NaCl**
- Magnesium (Mg²⁺) + Oxygen (O²⁻): 2+ and 2− → **MgO**
- Aluminum (Al³⁺) + Oxygen (O²⁻): need 2 Al³⁺ and 3 O²⁻ to balance (total charge = 0) → **Al₂O₃**
- Calcium (Ca²⁺) + Chlorine (Cl⁻): need 1 Ca²⁺ and 2 Cl⁻ → **CaCl₂**

#### Lewis Dot Structures
**Lewis dot structures** (electron dot diagrams) show valence electrons as dots around the element symbol. Steps:
1. Determine valence electrons for each atom (group number = valence electrons for main-group elements)
2. Place dots around the symbol (up to 2 per side, max 8 total)
3. When two atoms bond covalently, show shared electrons between them

For NaCl: Na (1 dot) gives its electron to Cl (7 dots → gains 1 → 8 dots → brackets with −1 charge).
For H₂O: O has 6 valence electrons; each H shares 1 pair with O → O has 2 bonding pairs and 2 lone pairs.

#### Review Questions
1. Predict the formula for the ionic compound formed between aluminum (Al³⁺) and sulfur (S²⁻).
2. Draw the Lewis dot structure for a chlorine atom (7 valence electrons). How many more electrons does it need to complete its octet?
3. In calcium chloride (CaCl₂), what are the charges on the calcium and chloride ions?
