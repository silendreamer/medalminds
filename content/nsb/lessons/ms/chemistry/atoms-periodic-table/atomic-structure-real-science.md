#### Ions: Atoms with Unequal Charges
A neutral atom has equal numbers of protons and electrons. When an atom gains or loses electrons, it becomes an **ion**:
- **Cation** (positive ion): atom LOSES electrons → more protons than electrons → net positive charge. Example: Na⁺ (sodium loses 1 electron).
- **Anion** (negative ion): atom GAINS electrons → more electrons than protons → net negative charge. Example: Cl⁻ (chlorine gains 1 electron).

Ions are how salts form: Na⁺ and Cl⁻ attract each other to form NaCl (table salt). Many biological processes depend on ions: nerve signals use Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, and Cl⁻ flowing across cell membranes.

#### Applications of Atomic Structure
- **Medical imaging (PET scans)**: use radioactive isotopes (positron-emitting) to image metabolic activity in the brain and body
- **Cancer radiation therapy**: radioactive isotopes target tumors
- **Nuclear power**: uranium and plutonium isotopes split in fission to release energy
- **Smoke detectors**: contain tiny amounts of Americium-241 (radioactive), which ionizes air to detect smoke

#### The Charge on Common Ions
Science Bowl asks about specific charges:
- Group 1 metals: +1 (Na⁺, K⁺, Li⁺)
- Group 2 metals: +2 (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺)
- Aluminum: +3 (Al³⁺)
- Oxygen: −2 (O²⁻)
- Chlorine/Fluorine: −1 (Cl⁻, F⁻)
