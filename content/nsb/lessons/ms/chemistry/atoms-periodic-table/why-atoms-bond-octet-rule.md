#### Valence Electrons
**Valence electrons** are the electrons in the outermost energy level (outer shell) of an atom. They are responsible for all chemical bonding. The number of valence electrons is determined by group number:
- Group 1 → 1 valence electron
- Group 2 → 2 valence electrons
- Groups 13–18 → 3–8 valence electrons (group number − 10 for groups 13–18, or just use the ones digit for groups 1–2)
- Noble gases (Group 18) → 8 valence electrons (except He, which has 2)

#### The Octet Rule
Most atoms are most stable with **8 valence electrons** in their outer shell (the "octet"). This is called the **octet rule**. Noble gases already have 8 (or 2 for He), which is why they're unreactive. Other atoms bond to gain, lose, or share electrons and achieve a noble gas configuration. Exception: hydrogen needs only 2 (like helium).

#### Why Atoms Bond
Atoms bond because the resulting compound is more stable (lower energy) than the separate atoms. This stability comes from having a filled outer shell. Bonding is essentially atoms "cooperating" to achieve noble-gas electron configurations.

#### Valence Electrons and Reactivity
- 1 valence electron (alkali metals): very reactive — easily lose 1 electron to achieve noble gas config
- 7 valence electrons (halogens): very reactive — easily gain 1 electron to achieve noble gas config
- 8 valence electrons (noble gases): unreactive — already have full outer shell
