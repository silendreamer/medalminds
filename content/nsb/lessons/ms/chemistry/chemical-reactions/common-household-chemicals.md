#### Chemicals Everywhere at Home

Your home is filled with chemicals you use every day. **Chemicals** are the substances that make up everything around us—from water to salt to soap. Many household items are either **acids** (sour substances) or **bases** (bitter or slippery substances). Learning to identify them helps you understand how things work and stay safe. For example, vinegar and lemon juice are acidic, while baking soda and soap are bases.

#### Common Household Acids

**Acids** taste sour and can react with metals or bases. You'll find acids in your kitchen: **vinegar** (acetic acid), **lemon juice** (citric acid), and **cola drinks** (phosphoric acid). Bathroom cleaners like toilet bowl cleaner also contain acids. These acids are mild and safe in small amounts, but you should never drink or touch cleaning products. The strength of an acid matters—stomach acid is strong enough to digest food, but it's protected inside your stomach by special cells.

#### Common Household Bases

**Bases** feel slippery and taste bitter. Examples include **baking soda** (sodium bicarbonate), **window cleaner**, and **soap**. Many cleaning products are bases because they work well on greasy stains. Another name for a base is **alkaline**. When you mix an acid and a base together, they neutralize each other and create water and a salt. This is why baking soda can stop the sting of an acid spill.

#### Knowing Which Is Which

You can tell acids and bases apart using **pH indicators**, including **pH paper** that changes color. Acids turn pH paper red or orange, while bases turn it blue or green. The middle (neutral) is yellow or green. Many products have labels that warn you if they're acidic or basic. For example, oven cleaner (a base) and drain cleaner (often acidic) both need careful handling and good ventilation.

#### Safety First

Always wear **protective gloves** and **goggles** when handling household chemicals—even "safe" ones like vinegar. Never mix cleaning products (especially acids and bases together) because the reaction can create dangerous gases or heat. Read labels carefully, keep products in their original containers, and wash your hands after touching anything chemical. Tell an adult if you spill something chemical or if something gets in your eyes.
