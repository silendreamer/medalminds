#### pH Quick Reference
pH 0–2: very strong acid (HCl, H₂SO₄, stomach acid)
pH 3–6: weak acid (vinegar, citrus, coffee)
pH 7: neutral (pure water)
pH 8–10: weak base (baking soda, sea water)
pH 11–14: strong base (ammonia, bleach, NaOH)

#### Common Acid-Base Pairs
| Acid | Base | Salt | Water |
|---|---|---|---|
| HCl | NaOH | NaCl | H₂O |
| H₂SO₄ | 2KOH | K₂SO₄ | 2H₂O |
| HNO₃ | NaOH | NaNO₃ | H₂O |

#### Indicator Summary
- Litmus: red = acid; blue = base
- Phenolphthalein: colorless = acid; pink = base
- Universal indicator: yellow-green = neutral; red-orange = acid; blue-purple = base

#### Review Questions
1. A solution turns phenolphthalein pink. Is it an acid or a base?
2. What is the salt produced when HNO₃ reacts with NaOH?
3. Arrange in order of increasing pH: stomach acid (pH 2), baking soda solution (pH 9), pure water (pH 7), bleach (pH 13).
