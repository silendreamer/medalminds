#### Acids in Food
Many foods are acidic:
- Citrus fruits (oranges, lemons): contain citric acid (pH ~2–3)
- Vinegar: acetic acid (~5% solution, pH ~2.4)
- Soda: carbonic acid + phosphoric acid (pH ~2.5–3.5)
- Milk: slightly acidic (pH ~6.5)
- Yogurt/cheese: lactic acid from bacterial fermentation (pH ~4)

Fermentation makes food sour — bacteria produce acids (lactic acid in yogurt, acetic acid in vinegar).

#### Acids and Metals
Active metals (Zn, Fe, Mg, Al) react with acids to produce hydrogen gas and a salt:
Zn + H₂SO₄ → ZnSO₄ + H₂↑
Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂↑

You can test for hydrogen gas: burning splint test — a popping sound ("squeaky pop") confirms H₂.

#### Environmental Acids and Bases
- **Acid rain**: SO₂ and NO₂ from burning fossil fuels dissolve in water to form sulfuric acid (H₂SO₄) and nitric acid (HNO₃). pH of acid rain < 5.6 (normal rain pH ~5.6 from CO₂).
- **Ocean acidification**: ocean absorbs CO₂ → forms carbonic acid (H₂CO₃) → lowers ocean pH, threatening coral reefs (calcium carbonate dissolves in acid).
- **Baking**: baking soda (NaHCO₃) is a base; baking powder contains baking soda + an acid that reacts when wet to produce CO₂ bubbles → bread rises.
