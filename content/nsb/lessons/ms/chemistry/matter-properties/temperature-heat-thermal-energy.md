#### Temperature Is Not the Same as Heat
This is one of the most common Science Bowl traps. **Temperature** is a measure of the average kinetic energy of particles in a substance. **Heat** is the transfer of thermal energy from a warmer object to a cooler one. **Thermal energy** is the total kinetic energy of all particles in an object. A bathtub of warm water has more thermal energy than a small cup of boiling water — even though the cup is hotter — because the bathtub has far more particles.

#### Temperature Scales
Three temperature scales matter for Science Bowl:
- **Celsius (°C)**: Water freezes at 0°C, boils at 100°C. Used in science worldwide.
- **Fahrenheit (°F)**: Water freezes at 32°F, boils at 212°F. Common in the U.S.
- **Kelvin (K)**: Absolute scale. 0 K is absolute zero, the coldest possible temperature (−273.15°C). Kelvin = Celsius + 273. There are no negative Kelvin temperatures.

Conversion: K = °C + 273 (for Science Bowl, use 273 as the offset).

#### Heat Transfer Methods
Heat moves in three ways:
- **Conduction**: direct contact (metal spoon in hot soup gets hot)
- **Convection**: through fluid movement (hot air rising, ocean currents)
- **Radiation**: through electromagnetic waves, no medium needed (sunlight warming Earth)

#### Science Bowl Quick Facts
- Absolute zero = 0 K = −273°C — the temperature at which particle motion theoretically stops
- Body temperature ≈ 37°C = 98.6°F = 310 K
- Room temperature ≈ 20–25°C
