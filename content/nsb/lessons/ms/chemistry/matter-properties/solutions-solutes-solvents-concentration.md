#### Solute and Solvent
A **solution** is a homogeneous mixture of two or more substances. The **solvent** is the substance present in the largest amount — it does the dissolving. The **solute** is the substance that dissolves into the solvent. In saltwater: water = solvent, salt = solute. In a sugar/water solution: water = solvent, sugar = solute.

Rule of thumb: **"Like dissolves like."** Polar solvents (like water) dissolve polar solutes (like salt, sugar). Non-polar solvents (like gasoline) dissolve non-polar solutes (like grease). This is why oil doesn't dissolve in water.

#### Concentration
**Concentration** describes how much solute is dissolved in a given amount of solution. Key terms:
- **Concentrated**: a lot of solute (strong coffee)
- **Dilute**: a little solute (weak coffee)
- **Saturated**: the maximum amount of solute is dissolved at a given temperature
- **Unsaturated**: more solute could still dissolve
- **Supersaturated**: more solute than normally dissolves (unstable — will crystallize if disturbed)

#### Factors Affecting Solubility
- **Temperature**: for most solid solutes in water, higher temperature → greater solubility. For gases in liquids, higher temperature → LESS solubility (warm soda goes flat faster).
- **Pressure**: affects gas solubility significantly. Higher pressure → more gas dissolves (carbonation in sealed bottles).
- **Stirring/Agitation**: speeds up dissolving (doesn't change solubility, just rate).
