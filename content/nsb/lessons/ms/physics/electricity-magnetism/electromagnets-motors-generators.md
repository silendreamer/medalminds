#### What Are Electromagnets?

An **electromagnet** is a magnet created by electricity. When you wrap a wire coil around a metal core and run electric current through it, the moving electricity creates a magnetic field. Unlike permanent magnets (like the ones on your fridge), electromagnets can be turned on and off simply by flipping a switch. The more coils you wrap, or the more current you push through, the stronger the magnet becomes. This is why electromagnets are used in everything from doorbells to junkyard cranes.

#### How Electric Motors Work

An **electric motor** converts electricity into motion by combining electromagnets with permanent magnets. Inside a motor, an electromagnet spins between the poles of a permanent magnet. When current flows through the electromagnet, it becomes magnetized and gets pushed and pulled by the permanent magnet's field, causing it to rotate. As the coil spins, a device called a **commutator** flips the direction of the current, keeping the electromagnet spinning continuously. This is how everything from fans to electric drills gets its power.

#### Understanding Generators

A **generator** is the opposite of a motor—it converts motion into electricity. Instead of electricity making something spin, spinning something creates electricity. When you move a wire coil through a magnetic field, the motion causes electrons to move through the wire, generating an electric current. This is how power plants work: they spin large coils in massive magnetic fields to produce the electricity that powers your home. Generators at a power plant might be spun by falling water, steam, or wind.

#### How They're Connected

All three devices—electromagnets, motors, and generators—rely on the same core principle: **electricity and magnetism can transform into each other**. A motor uses electricity to create motion, while a generator uses motion to create electricity. Electromagnets show that electricity can create magnetism. Understanding these three concepts helps explain how we harness energy in the modern world.
