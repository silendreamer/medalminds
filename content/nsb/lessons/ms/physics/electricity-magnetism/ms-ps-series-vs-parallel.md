#### Side-by-Side Comparison

| Property | Series | Parallel |
|---|---|---|
| Current | Same throughout | Divides among branches |
| Voltage | Divides among components | Same across all branches |
| Resistance | R_total = R₁+R₂+... (increases) | 1/R_T = 1/R₁+1/R₂+... (decreases) |
| One component fails | Whole circuit breaks | Rest of circuit unaffected |
| Use case | Simple switches, old lights | Household wiring, most electronics |

#### Reading Circuit Diagrams
Standard symbols: battery (long/short alternating lines), resistor (zigzag), bulb (circle with X), switch (gap in line), ammeter (A in circle, series), voltmeter (V in circle, parallel). Series elements are on the same branch; parallel elements are on separate branches between the same two nodes. Practice identifying from diagram whether components are in series or parallel.

#### Mixed Circuits
Real circuits often combine both: a main series element (like a switch) with parallel branches (like multiple bulbs). Use systematic analysis: find equivalent resistance of parallel sections first, then add series resistances.

#### Review Questions
1. Compare: in which type of circuit does each component receive the full battery voltage?
2. Two identical bulbs in series vs. two in parallel with the same battery — which setup makes the bulbs brighter? Explain.
3. Your kitchen has 5 appliances running simultaneously without affecting each other. Are they wired in series or parallel? How do you know?
