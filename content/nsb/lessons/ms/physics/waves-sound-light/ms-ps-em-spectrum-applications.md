#### X-Rays in Medicine
X-rays penetrate soft tissue but are absorbed by dense tissue (bone, metal). The shadow image on X-ray film shows bones as white (absorbed X-rays) and soft tissue as gray (some absorption) or black (X-rays pass through). Computed Tomography (CT) scans take multiple X-ray images from different angles and combine them for 3D imaging. X-ray radiation can damage DNA — medical doses are minimized, and protective lead aprons are used.

#### Infrared: Heat Vision and Remote Controls
All objects emit infrared radiation based on temperature. Thermal cameras detect IR to "see" heat — used by firefighters to find people in smoke, by military for night vision, and by scientists to track heat loss in buildings. TV remote controls use near-infrared LEDs — press a button, the remote pulses IR at a specific frequency, and the TV sensor reads the code. No sound, no radio interference — simple and cheap.

#### UV Light: Benefits and Dangers
Ultraviolet light (UV) has enough energy to damage DNA — the cause of sunburns and a risk factor for skin cancer. Ozone in the stratosphere absorbs most UV-B and UV-C from the Sun. UV-A (longest UV wavelength) gets through more. Benefits: UV light kills bacteria and viruses (used to sterilize hospitals and water systems); UV causes fluorescent materials to glow (black lights in entertainment and forensics); UV helps skin produce vitamin D.
