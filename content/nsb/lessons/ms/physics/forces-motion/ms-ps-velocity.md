#### Speed vs. Velocity
Speed tells you how fast — velocity tells you how fast and in which direction. A car moving north at 30 m/s and a car moving south at 30 m/s have the same speed but opposite velocities. This distinction matters in physics: velocity is a vector (has both magnitude and direction), while speed is a scalar (magnitude only). When a problem asks for velocity, your answer must include a direction: "20 m/s east" or "15 m/s downward."

#### Vectors and Direction
A vector has two parts: magnitude (the number) and direction (north/south/up/down, or an angle). Velocity, force, acceleration, and displacement are all vectors. Speed, distance, mass, and temperature are scalars. In Science Bowl, questions often test whether you recognize that velocity must include direction. If someone runs in a circle at constant speed, their velocity is constantly changing — because direction changes.

#### Displacement vs. Distance
Distance is the total path length traveled (scalar). Displacement is the straight-line distance from start to finish, with direction (vector). If you run 400 m around a track and return to the start, your distance is 400 m but your displacement is 0 m. Average velocity = displacement ÷ time. If displacement is zero (round trip), average velocity is zero — even if average speed is not.

#### Relative Velocity
Velocity depends on your reference frame. A passenger walking at 2 m/s toward the front of a train moving at 20 m/s is moving at 22 m/s relative to the ground. A boat crossing a river at 4 m/s perpendicular to a 3 m/s current has a resultant speed of √(4² + 3²) = 5 m/s relative to the riverbank. Bowl questions about boats, planes, and trains often use this concept.

#### Review Questions
1. A runner completes a full lap around a 400 m track in 80 s. What is her average speed? What is her average velocity?
2. What makes velocity a vector but speed a scalar?
3. A car drives 60 km east, then 80 km north. What is the magnitude of its displacement?
