#### What Metric Prefixes Do

A **metric prefix** is added to a base unit to make it bigger or smaller by a power of ten. For example, a **kilometer** is 1,000 meters and a **millimeter** is 1/1,000 of a meter. Because the metric system is built on **powers of ten**, you can rescale any unit just by moving the decimal point — no complicated arithmetic needed.

#### The High-Yield Prefix Ladder

Memorize these prefixes, their symbols, and their power of ten:

| Prefix | Symbol | Value |
|---|---|---|
| giga- | G | 10⁹ |
| mega- | M | 10⁶ |
| kilo- | k | 10³ |
| (base) | — | 10⁰ |
| centi- | c | 10⁻² |
| milli- | m | 10⁻³ |
| micro- | μ | 10⁻⁶ |
| nano- | n | 10⁻⁹ |
| pico- | p | 10⁻¹² |

Notice the big jumps: after **kilo-** the next step up is **mega-** (skipping to 10⁶), and on the small side **milli-** drops straight to **micro-** (10⁻⁶). The Greek letter **μ (mu)** stands for micro-.

#### Climbing the Ladder

To convert, count how many powers of ten you move:
- **kilo- to base:** 1 km = 10³ m = 1,000 m.
- **base to milli-:** 1 m = 10³ mm = 1,000 mm.
- **micro- to milli-:** these are 10⁻⁶ and 10⁻³, three steps apart, so 1,000 μL = 1 mL.

#### Buzzer Tip: Just Count the Exponents

For a question like "How many micrometers are in a centimeter?", line up the exponents: centi- is 10⁻² and micro- is 10⁻⁶. The difference is 10⁻²⁻(⁻⁶) = 10⁴, so **1 cm = 10,000 μm**. Subtracting exponents is faster than writing out zeros.

2. How many millimeters are in one kilometer?
3. Which is larger, a **megawatt** or a **gigawatt**, and by what factor?
