#### Setting Up Proportions
Problem: "If 3 apples cost $2, how much do 12 apples cost?" Set up: 3/2 = 12/x. Cross-multiply: 3x = 24, x = 8. 12 apples cost $8.

#### Scaling and Similarity
If a map has a scale of 1 inch = 10 miles, and two cities are 3 inches apart on the map, the actual distance is 30 miles. Scaling works proportionally: if you scale up a recipe by 2, all ingredients double. If you scale a shape by factor k, area scales by k², volume by k³.

#### Percentage Problems
"30% of what number is 15?" Let x be the number. 0.3x = 15, so x = 50. "What percent of 60 is 15?" (15/60) × 100% = 25%. "Increase 80 by 20%." New value = 80 × 1.2 = 96.

#### Compound Percentages
Problem: "A $100 item is marked up 50% then discounted 50%. What's the final price?" After markup: $100 × 1.5 = $150. After discount: $150 × 0.5 = $75. Final: $75. Note: 1.5 × 0.5 = 0.75 ≠ 1, so the net effect is not zero (a common mistake).

#### Direct vs. Inverse Proportion
Direct: y = kx (y proportional to x). Example: distance = speed × time. Inverse: y = k/x (y inversely proportional to x). Example: time = distance/speed. Recognizing which applies helps set up correct equations.

#### Review Questions
1. If 4 oranges cost $3, how much do 10 oranges cost?
2. A scale is 1 cm = 50 km. If two cities are 4 cm apart on the map, what's the real distance?
3. What is 25% of 80?
