## Subtopic: Fractions, Decimals & Percents

### What Is a Fraction? Building Numerator-Denominator Intuition
**Type:** Core Understanding
**Slug:** what-is-a-fraction-building-intuition
**Estimated time:** 12 min
**Key concepts:** numerator/denominator meaning · equivalent fractions · fraction on a number line
**Summary:** Understand fractions as division and ratios before touching any arithmetic.

#### The Fraction as Division
A fraction a/b literally means "a divided by b." The denominator tells you how many equal parts the whole is split into; the numerator counts how many of those parts you have. So 3/4 means a whole split into 4 equal parts, and you have 3 of them. This simple model — whole divided into equal parts — handles every fraction operation that comes later. Students who skip this and jump straight to rules end up guessing when problems look unfamiliar.

#### Equivalent Fractions
Two fractions are equivalent when they represent the same portion of a whole. Multiplying or dividing both numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number gives an equivalent fraction: 3/4 = 6/8 = 9/12. This isn't a trick — it's the same amount, just cut into more or fewer pieces. Finding the simplest form means dividing by the greatest common factor (GCF). Example: 12/18 → GCF is 6 → 12/18 = 2/3.

#### Placing Fractions on a Number Line
To place 3/5 on a number line between 0 and 1, divide that segment into 5 equal parts and count 3 from the left. This visualizes size immediately: 3/5 is less than 1/2? No — 1/2 = 2.5/5, and 3/5 > 2.5/5. A number line beats "butterfly" tricks for building real understanding. Benchmarks to memorize: 1/4 = 0.25, 1/3 ≈ 0.333, 1/2 = 0.5, 2/3 ≈ 0.667, 3/4 = 0.75.

#### Comparing Fractions Quickly
Cross-multiply to compare: for 3/7 vs. 4/9, compute 3×9=27 vs. 4×7=28. Since 27 < 28, we get 3/7 < 4/9. Or convert to a common denominator. Or use decimal approximations. For Science Bowl speed, cross-multiplication is fastest for two fractions. For three or more, convert to decimals.

#### The Fraction-Division Connection
Every fraction problem can be rewritten as a division: 3/4 ÷ 1 = 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75. This means division always produces a fraction. Why does 7 ÷ 2 = 3.5? Because 7/2 = 3 + 1/2 = 3.5. Understanding this connection makes mixed numbers, improper fractions, and long division all click together.

#### Review Questions
1. Simplify 36/48 to lowest terms.
2. Which is greater, 5/8 or 7/11? Show your method.
3. Convert 7/4 to a mixed number, then to a decimal.

### Adding, Subtracting, Multiplying, and Dividing Fractions
**Type:** Core Understanding
**Slug:** fraction-operations-complete
**Estimated time:** 14 min
**Key concepts:** common denominator · multiply straight across · keep-change-flip
**Summary:** Master all four fraction operations with the logic behind each rule, not just the rule.

#### Adding and Subtracting: Find a Common Denominator
You can only add fractions with the same denominator because you're counting the same-sized pieces. 1/4 + 2/4 = 3/4. Different denominators? Find the least common denominator (LCD): for 1/3 + 1/4, LCD = 12. Convert: 4/12 + 3/12 = 7/12. The LCD is the least common multiple (LCM) of the denominators. For 6 and 8: multiples of 8 are 8, 16, 24… multiples of 6 are 6, 12, 18, 24… LCM = 24.

#### Multiplying Fractions: Multiply Straight Across
(a/b) × (c/d) = ac/bd. Simple. 2/3 × 3/5 = 6/15 = 2/5. Before multiplying, look for cross-cancellation: in (4/9) × (3/8), cancel the 4 and 8 (÷4) and cancel the 3 and 9 (÷3) to get (1/3) × (1/2) = 1/6 immediately, without simplifying a big fraction. This saves time on every Science Bowl math question involving fraction products.

#### Dividing Fractions: Keep-Change-Flip
Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal. (a/b) ÷ (c/d) = (a/b) × (d/c). Why? Because dividing by c/d asks "how many c/d's fit in a/b?" Flipping and multiplying gives that count. Example: (3/4) ÷ (3/8) = (3/4) × (8/3) = 24/12 = 2. Check: does 3/8 fit into 3/4 exactly 2 times? Yes, 2 × 3/8 = 6/8 = 3/4. ✓

#### Mixed Numbers in Operations
Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first: 2 3/5 = 13/5. Then operate normally. 2 3/5 + 1 2/5 = 13/5 + 7/5 = 20/5 = 4. To multiply: 1 1/2 × 2 2/3 = 3/2 × 8/3 = 24/6 = 4. Never try to multiply mixed numbers digit by digit — always convert first.

#### Speed Check: Common Fraction Products to Know Cold
- 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4
- 2/3 × 3/4 = 1/2
- 3/4 × 4/3 = 1
- 1/3 ÷ 1/6 = 2
- 3/5 ÷ 3/10 = 2

#### Review Questions
1. Compute 5/6 + 3/8. Express in simplest form.
2. Compute (2 1/4) × (1 1/3).
3. Compute (7/8) ÷ (7/16).

### Converting Between Fractions, Decimals, and Percents
**Type:** Core Understanding
**Slug:** converting-fractions-decimals-percents
**Estimated time:** 11 min
**Key concepts:** percent as per-hundred · decimal shift · benchmark conversions
**Summary:** Convert fluently in all directions using the logic of place value and hundredths.

#### Percent Means "Per Hundred"
37% means 37 per 100, or 37/100, or 0.37. That's it. The % symbol is shorthand for ÷ 100. So to convert any percent to a decimal, divide by 100 (move decimal two places left): 45% = 0.45, 6% = 0.06, 125% = 1.25. To go decimal → percent, multiply by 100 (move decimal two places right): 0.8 = 80%, 0.035 = 3.5%.

#### Fraction → Decimal → Percent
Divide numerator by denominator to get the decimal, then multiply by 100 for percent. 3/8: 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375 = 37.5%. For repeating decimals: 1/3 = 0.333… = 33.3̄%. 1/6 = 0.1666… = 16.6̄%. Science Bowl often uses terminating decimal fractions — know the halves, quarters, eighths, fifths, and thirds cold.

#### Benchmark Table to Memorize
| Fraction | Decimal | Percent |
|---|---|---|
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 1/3 | 0.333… | 33.3̄% |
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 2/3 | 0.666… | 66.6̄% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |
| 3/8 | 0.375 | 37.5% |
| 5/8 | 0.625 | 62.5% |

#### Percent of a Number
"What is 35% of 80?" → 0.35 × 80 = 28. Speed strategy: break the percent apart. 30% of 80 = 24; 5% of 80 = 4; total 28. Or use fraction shortcuts: 25% of 80 = 1/4 × 80 = 20; 10% = 8; 35% = 20 + 8 + 5% (4) = 28.

#### Percent Change
Percent change = (new − old) / old × 100%. A price rises from $40 to $50: change = 10, original = 40, percent change = 25% increase. A drop from 200 to 150: change = −50, original = 200, percent change = −25% (25% decrease). Don't confuse percent change with percent of the new value.

#### Review Questions
1. Convert 5/8 to a decimal and a percent.
2. What is 40% of 65?
3. A score rises from 60 to 75. What is the percent increase?

### Fractions and Percents in Worked Problems
**Type:** Application
**Slug:** fractions-percents-worked-problems
**Estimated time:** 13 min
**Key concepts:** setting up the equation · fractional parts of quantities · percent problems
**Summary:** Apply fraction and percent skills to Science Bowl–style word problems with full solutions.

#### Problem Type 1: Fraction of a Quantity
"A recipe calls for 2/3 cup of sugar. If you make 1.5 batches, how much sugar do you need?" Multiply: 2/3 × 3/2 = 6/6 = 1 cup. Key step: identify what the fraction is acting on (1.5 batches = 3/2 batches), then multiply. These problems always follow the pattern: [fraction] × [whole quantity] = [part].

#### Problem Type 2: Finding the Whole Given a Part
"After spending 3/5 of her money, Maria has $24 left. How much did she start with?" If 3/5 is spent, 2/5 remains. So 2/5 × W = 24 → W = 24 × 5/2 = $60. This "inversion" step (dividing by the fraction) trips up many students. Practice: always ask "what fraction of the whole is the given amount?"

#### Problem Type 3: Percent Problems
"A jacket is $80, on sale for 15% off. What is the sale price?" Discount = 15% × 80 = 0.15 × 80 = $12. Sale price = 80 − 12 = $68. Speed method: 85% of $80 = 0.85 × 80 = $68 directly (multiply by the kept fraction).

#### Problem Type 4: Multi-Step Fraction Problems
"Of 120 students, 1/3 play soccer. Of those, 3/4 also play basketball. How many play both?" Step 1: 1/3 × 120 = 40 soccer players. Step 2: 3/4 × 40 = 30 play both. Multiply the fractions: 1/3 × 3/4 × 120 = 1/4 × 120 = 30. (Notice: 1/3 × 3/4 = 1/4 — multiplying fractions that share a factor simplifies quickly.)

#### Science Bowl Clue Phrases
"What fraction of…" → set up part/whole. "What percent of…" → part/whole × 100. "By what percent did X change?" → (new−old)/old × 100. "X is Y% of what number?" → X = Y% × ? → ? = X ÷ (Y/100).

#### Review Questions
1. A bag has 48 marbles. 5/8 are blue. How many are NOT blue?
2. A price of $250 is increased by 12%. What is the new price?
3. After giving away 2/7 of her stickers, Priya has 35 left. How many did she start with?

### Percent Tricks and Estimation
**Type:** Application
**Slug:** percent-tricks-estimation
**Estimated time:** 10 min
**Key concepts:** 10% anchor · switching percent and number · mental percent computation
**Summary:** Compute percents in seconds using anchoring, splitting, and switching techniques.

#### The 10% Anchor Method
10% of any number = move decimal one place left. 10% of 340 = 34. From there: 5% = half of 10% = 17. 20% = double 10% = 68. 15% = 10% + 5% = 34 + 17 = 51. 1% = 10% ÷ 10 = 3.4. This anchor technique handles 99% of Science Bowl percent problems without a calculator.

#### The Switch Trick: X% of Y = Y% of X
8% of 25 = 25% of 8 = 2. Why? Both equal (8 × 25)/100 = 2. This trick is most useful when one of the numbers is a "nice" percent: 37% of 50 = 50% of 37 = 18.5. Recognize when switching makes the computation trivial.

#### Percent Benchmarks as Fractions
- 10% = 1/10
- 20% = 1/5
- 25% = 1/4
- 33.3̄% ≈ 1/3
- 50% = 1/2
- 66.6̄% ≈ 2/3
- 75% = 3/4

When the percent is a benchmark fraction, multiply by the fraction instead: 66.7% of 24 ≈ 2/3 × 24 = 16.

#### Estimation to Check Answers
Always estimate before committing: 38% of 83 ≈ 40% of 80 = 32. Exact: 0.38 × 83 = 31.54 ≈ 32. Estimation catches large errors. In Science Bowl, if your exact answer is wildly different from your estimate, recompute.

#### Common Traps
"A price increased 20%, then decreased 20%. Is the final price the same as original?" No — if original = $100, after +20% = $120, after −20% of $120 = $96. The base changes. This is one of the most common Science Bowl traps in percent problems.

#### Review Questions
1. Find 35% of 60 using the 10% anchor method.
2. Use the switch trick: what is 4% of 75?
3. A shirt is $80, then increased 25%, then decreased 20%. What is the final price?

### Fraction and Percent Synthesis Challenge
**Type:** Mixed/Review
**Slug:** fraction-percent-synthesis-challenge
**Estimated time:** 12 min
**Key concepts:** multi-step setup · identifying the whole · checking via estimation
**Summary:** Connect fraction and percent skills in multi-step problems typical of Science Bowl math rounds.

#### Problem Set A: Fraction Chains
Work through each step, showing intermediate results:
1. In a school of 600 students, 2/5 are in 6th grade, 1/3 of 6th graders are in band, and 1/4 of band members play trumpet. How many 6th graders play trumpet in band?
   Solution: 600 × 2/5 = 240 → 240 × 1/3 = 80 → 80 × 1/4 = **20 trumpet players**

2. A tank is 3/4 full with 90 liters. 1/3 of the water evaporates. How many liters remain?
   Solution: Full tank = 90 ÷ (3/4) = 120 L. After evaporation: 90 × 2/3 = **60 L remain**.

#### Problem Set B: Percent Chains
1. A store buys items for $50 and marks them up 40%. Then it offers a 10% discount. What is the selling price?
   Solution: $50 × 1.40 = $70 → $70 × 0.90 = **$63**

2. A population of 800 grows 25% in year 1, then decreases 20% in year 2. Final population?
   Solution: 800 × 1.25 = 1000 → 1000 × 0.80 = **800** (back to start! This is a classic trap.)

#### Identifying the Whole
The most common error: using the wrong denominator. "15 is 30% of what number?" Students often write 15 × 0.30 = 4.5. Wrong — that's 30% of 15, not 15 is 30% of something. Correct: 15 = 0.30 × W → W = 15/0.30 = **50**.

#### Strategy: Work Backwards
When given the end and asked for the start, reverse operations. A number after a 25% increase is 75. What was the original? Don't subtract 25%! Instead: 75 = original × 1.25 → original = 75/1.25 = **60**.

#### Competition Tip
Science Bowl math is timed. Estimate first, then solve exactly, then verify your exact answer is near your estimate. If not, you made an arithmetic error — recheck before buzzing.

#### Review Questions
1. A class has 24 students. 1/3 are absent. Of those present, 3/4 pass a quiz. How many students passed?
2. After two successive 10% increases, what single percent increase is equivalent?
3. 45 is 90% of what number?

## Subtopic: Ratios, Rates & Proportions

### Understanding Ratios: Comparing Quantities
**Type:** Core Understanding
**Slug:** understanding-ratios-comparing-quantities
**Estimated time:** 11 min
**Key concepts:** part-to-part vs. part-to-whole · simplifying ratios · ratio language
**Summary:** Build a precise understanding of what ratios measure before solving any problems.

#### What a Ratio Tells You
A ratio compares two quantities of the same or different types. "3 red to 5 blue" is a part-to-part ratio: red:blue = 3:5. But the ratio of red to total = 3:(3+5) = 3:8 — a part-to-whole ratio. These are completely different numbers, and confusing them is the #1 error in ratio problems. Always ask: "Is this comparing part to part, or part to whole?"

#### Writing Ratios Three Ways
A ratio of 4 to 7 can be written as 4:7, 4/7, or "4 to 7." In Science Bowl, all three notations appear. The fraction form is most useful for computation. Ratios can always be simplified by dividing both terms by their GCF: 12:18 = 2:3 (GCF = 6). Unlike fractions, ratios don't have to be less than 1.

#### Scaling Ratios
A recipe uses flour and sugar in ratio 5:2. To make a version using 15 cups of flour, scale both: multiply both terms by 3 → 15:6. So you need 6 cups of sugar. The key insight: ratios scale by the same factor on both sides. Always find the scale factor first: 15 ÷ 5 = 3. Then apply: 2 × 3 = 6.

#### Ratios with Three or More Quantities
If the ratio of A:B:C = 2:3:5, and the total is 100, then: A = 2/10 × 100 = 20; B = 3/10 × 100 = 30; C = 5/10 × 100 = 50. The sum of ratio parts (2+3+5 = 10) becomes the denominator. This technique handles any multi-quantity ratio problem.

#### Units in Ratios
Ratios comparing the same units simplify to pure numbers (no units remain): 6 feet : 9 feet = 2:3. Ratios comparing different units keep their units and become rates: 60 miles per 2 hours = 60 miles : 2 hours = 30 miles/hour. This distinction defines where ratio ends and rate begins.

#### Review Questions
1. A bag has 4 green, 6 red, and 2 blue marbles. Write the ratio of green to total in lowest terms.
2. The ratio of boys to girls in a class is 3:4, and there are 28 girls. How many boys are there?
3. In a ratio A:B:C = 1:4:5, if B = 24, what is C?

### Rates and Unit Rates
**Type:** Core Understanding
**Slug:** rates-and-unit-rates
**Estimated time:** 12 min
**Key concepts:** unit rate as rate per one · miles per hour · best-buy comparisons
**Summary:** Convert any rate to a unit rate to make comparisons instant and comparisons easy.

#### What Makes a Unit Rate
A unit rate expresses a ratio where the second quantity equals 1. "60 miles in 2 hours" → unit rate = 30 miles per hour (per 1 hour). Unit rates make comparison trivial: 45 miles in 1.5 hours vs. 60 miles in 2 hours? Convert: 45/1.5 = 30 mph vs. 60/2 = 30 mph — identical. Divide the first quantity by the second quantity to find any unit rate.

#### Common Unit Rates to Know
- Speed: miles (or km) per hour, meters per second
- Cost: dollars per item, cost per ounce
- Fuel: miles per gallon
- Productivity: items per hour, pages per minute
- Population density: people per square mile

For Science Bowl, speed (distance/time) and unit cost are most common.

#### Best-Buy Comparisons
12 oz for $1.80 vs. 18 oz for $2.52. Unit cost: $1.80/12 = $0.15/oz vs. $2.52/18 = $0.14/oz. The 18-oz size is cheaper per ounce. Method: always divide price by quantity to get cost per unit. Never compare total prices without normalizing to the same unit.

#### Distance-Rate-Time: D = RT
Distance = Rate × Time. If rate = 55 mph and time = 3 hours, distance = 165 miles. Rearrange: T = D/R (time = distance ÷ rate). R = D/T (rate = distance ÷ time). A car travels 210 km in 3.5 hours: rate = 210/3.5 = 60 km/h. Know all three forms cold.

#### Converting Between Rates
60 miles/hour to feet/second: 60 × 5280 feet/hour ÷ 3600 seconds/hour = 88 feet/second. General method: multiply by conversion fractions that cancel the old units and introduce new ones. Keep track of units in the numerator and denominator separately.

#### Review Questions
1. A car travels 187.5 miles in 2.5 hours. What is its average speed?
2. Store A sells 16 oz of juice for $2.40; Store B sells 20 oz for $2.80. Which is the better deal per ounce?
3. A runner completes a 5-km race in 25 minutes. What is her speed in km per minute? In km per hour?

### Proportions and Cross-Multiplication
**Type:** Core Understanding
**Slug:** proportions-and-cross-multiplication
**Estimated time:** 12 min
**Key concepts:** proportion as two equal ratios · cross-product property · setting up the equation
**Summary:** Solve any proportion problem by identifying the two equal ratios and cross-multiplying.

#### What Is a Proportion?
A proportion states that two ratios are equal: a/b = c/d. This means "a is to b as c is to d." The cross-product property says: if a/b = c/d, then a × d = b × c. Why? Multiply both sides by b × d. This gives a universal method for solving any proportion for an unknown.

#### Solving Proportions
"If 3 pencils cost $0.90, how much do 7 pencils cost?" Set up: 3/0.90 = 7/x. Cross multiply: 3x = 7 × 0.90 = 6.30. x = 6.30/3 = $2.10. Alternatively, find unit rate first: $0.90/3 = $0.30 each → 7 × $0.30 = $2.10. Both methods work; unit rate is faster when the setup is simple.

#### Map Scale Problems
A map uses scale 1 inch = 25 miles. Two cities are 3.5 inches apart on the map. Real distance? 1/25 = 3.5/x → x = 3.5 × 25 = 87.5 miles. Or: each inch = 25 miles, so 3.5 inches = 3.5 × 25 = 87.5 miles. Map scale is just a unit rate in disguise.

#### Similar Figures and Proportions
If two triangles are similar with sides in ratio 2:3, and the smaller triangle has a side of 8, the corresponding larger side = 8 × (3/2) = 12. All corresponding sides must be in the same ratio. Area ratios: if sides are in ratio 2:3, areas are in ratio 4:9 (square the side ratio). Volume ratios: cube the side ratio.

#### Recognizing Proportional Relationships
In a table of x and y values, if y/x is constant, the relationship is proportional (direct variation). For x = 2, 4, 6 and y = 5, 10, 15: ratios 5/2, 10/4, 15/6 all equal 2.5. Proportional. If the ratio varies, the relationship is NOT proportional — don't set up a proportion.

#### Review Questions
1. Solve for x: 4/15 = x/60.
2. A model car is built at scale 1:20. If the real car is 3.6 m long, how long is the model in cm?
3. If 5 workers finish a job in 8 days, how many days would 10 workers take (assuming same rate per worker)?

### Ratio and Rate Word Problems
**Type:** Application
**Slug:** ratio-rate-word-problems-applied
**Estimated time:** 13 min
**Key concepts:** choosing the right ratio · setting up proportions · checking reasonableness
**Summary:** Work through five categories of real-world ratio and rate problems with complete solutions.

#### Category 1: Mixing Problems
"A paint mixture uses red and white in ratio 2:5. You have 8 liters of red. How much white do you need, and what is the total?" Scale factor: 8 ÷ 2 = 4. White: 5 × 4 = 20 liters. Total: 8 + 20 = 28 liters. Always find the scale factor from the known quantity first.

#### Category 2: Speed Problems
"A train travels 240 km in 3 hours. At the same speed, how long to travel 400 km?" Speed = 240/3 = 80 km/h. Time = 400/80 = 5 hours. Or proportion: 240/3 = 400/t → 240t = 1200 → t = 5. Both work; direct rate calculation is slightly faster.

#### Category 3: Population and Density
"A city has a population of 450,000 people in 150 square miles. What is the population density?" Density = 450,000/150 = 3,000 people/sq mi. Now: if the density stays the same and area expands to 200 sq mi, new population = 3,000 × 200 = 600,000. Density × area = population.

#### Category 4: Splitting in a Ratio
"Split $700 between A and B in ratio 3:4." Total parts = 7. A gets 3/7 × 700 = $300. B gets 4/7 × 700 = $400. Check: 300 + 400 = 700. ✓ This "sum of parts as denominator" technique works for any ratio split.

#### Category 5: Currency and Exchange
"$1 = 0.85 euros. How many euros is $45?" Unit rate: 0.85 euros/$1. So $45 × 0.85 = €38.25. To convert back: €38.25 ÷ 0.85 = $45. Currency exchange is always a unit rate × quantity calculation.

#### Review Questions
1. Concrete mix uses cement, sand, and gravel in ratio 1:2:3. You need 180 kg total. How much of each ingredient?
2. A car goes 350 miles on 14 gallons. At the same rate, how many gallons are needed for 500 miles?
3. Divide 90 into two parts in ratio 7:3.

### Proportional Reasoning Challenge
**Type:** Mixed/Review
**Slug:** proportional-reasoning-challenge
**Estimated time:** 11 min
**Key concepts:** identifying proportional vs. nonproportional · indirect variation · multi-step setups
**Summary:** Deepen ratio and proportion skills through tricky variations including inverse proportion.

#### Direct vs. Inverse Proportion
Direct: as x increases, y increases proportionally. Example: miles ∝ hours at constant speed.
Inverse: as x increases, y decreases. Example: speed ∝ 1/time for fixed distance. Formula: xy = k (constant). "6 workers complete a job in 10 days. How long for 15 workers?" 6 × 10 = 60 = k. 15 × t = 60 → t = 4 days. Recognize inverse proportion when the two quantities multiply to a constant.

#### Table Analysis: Proportional or Not?
| x | y |
|---|---|
| 2 | 6 |
| 5 | 15 |
| 8 | 23 |
Check y/x: 3, 3, 2.875 — not constant. NOT proportional (the last pair breaks the pattern). In Science Bowl, always check ALL pairs before deciding. One outlier means it's nonproportional.

#### Multi-Ratio Problems
"The ratio of cats to dogs to birds is 2:3:1. There are 72 animals total. If 1/4 of the dogs are puppies, how many puppies are there?" Step 1: Dogs = 3/6 × 72 = 36. Step 2: Puppies = 1/4 × 36 = 9. Breaking into steps is key — never try to set up one complex equation.

#### Compound Rates
"A car averages 30 mph for 2 hours and 60 mph for 3 hours. What is the overall average speed?" TRAP: average speed ≠ average of speeds (not (30+60)/2 = 45). Correct: total distance = 30×2 + 60×3 = 60 + 180 = 240 miles. Total time = 5 hours. Average speed = 240/5 = **48 mph**. Always use total distance ÷ total time.

#### Science Bowl Clue Phrases
"At this rate…" → proportional setup. "Inversely proportional…" → xy = k. "Combined average speed…" → total distance ÷ total time, never average the speeds. "In ratio m:n, the total is T…" → each part = m/(m+n) × T or n/(m+n) × T.

3. The ratio of A to B is 5:8. If A = 35, what is A + B?

### Exponents: Power, Base, and Meaning
**Type:** Core Understanding
**Slug:** exponents-power-base-meaning
**Estimated time:** 12 min
**Key concepts:** base × itself n times · zero exponent · negative exponents
**Summary:** Understand what an exponent actually means before learning the rules, so the rules become obvious.

#### The Definition of a Power
bⁿ means "multiply b by itself n times." So 2⁵ = 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 32. The base is the number being multiplied; the exponent (power) counts how many times. This definition makes most exponent rules self-evident. 2³ × 2⁴ = (2×2×2) × (2×2×2×2) = 2⁷ — you're just counting the total multiplications.

#### The Exponent Rules (Derived, Not Memorized)
- Product: bᵐ × bⁿ = bᵐ⁺ⁿ (count total factors)
- Quotient: bᵐ ÷ bⁿ = bᵐ⁻ⁿ (cancel n factors from numerator)
- Power of power: (bᵐ)ⁿ = bᵐⁿ (multiply n groups of m)
- Power of product: (ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ (exponent distributes)
- Power of quotient: (a/b)ⁿ = aⁿ/bⁿ

Each rule follows directly from the definition. Don't memorize — derive.

#### The Zero Exponent
b⁰ = 1 for any nonzero b. Why? Because b³ ÷ b³ = 1 (any number divided by itself), and using the quotient rule: b³ ÷ b³ = b³⁻³ = b⁰. So b⁰ must equal 1. Note: 0⁰ is undefined (indeterminate form).

#### Negative Exponents
b⁻ⁿ = 1/bⁿ. Reason: b¹ ÷ b⁴ = b⁻³, but also = 1/b³. So b⁻³ = 1/b³. Examples: 2⁻³ = 1/8; 5⁻² = 1/25; 10⁻³ = 0.001. A negative exponent does NOT mean the result is negative. 2⁻³ = 1/8, which is positive.

#### Perfect Squares and Perfect Cubes to Know
Squares: 1,4,9,16,25,36,49,64,81,100,121,144,169,196,225
Cubes: 1,8,27,64,125,216,343,512,729,1000
Science Bowl asks for these constantly — memorize them.

#### Review Questions
1. Simplify: 3⁴ × 3² ÷ 3³.
2. Evaluate: (2³)² × 2⁰.
3. Write 4⁻³ as a fraction.

### Square Roots and Cube Roots
**Type:** Core Understanding
**Slug:** square-roots-and-cube-roots
**Estimated time:** 11 min
**Key concepts:** inverse of squaring/cubing · perfect vs. irrational roots · simplifying radicals
**Summary:** Calculate and simplify roots by understanding them as inverse operations, not magic tricks.

#### Roots as Inverses
√n answers the question "what number, squared, gives n?" √25 = 5 because 5² = 25. ∛n answers "what number, cubed, gives n?" ∛8 = 2 because 2³ = 8. Roots are the inverse of powers, just as subtraction inverts addition and division inverts multiplication.

#### Perfect vs. Irrational Square Roots
√9 = 3 (perfect — exact integer). √10 is irrational — its decimal goes on forever without repeating (≈3.162). In Science Bowl, questions usually involve perfect squares. Between 9 and 10, √9 = 3 and √10 ≈ 3.16. To estimate: √87 is between √81=9 and √100=10, closer to 9. 9.3² = 86.49, 9.4² = 88.36 → √87 ≈ 9.33.

#### Simplifying Radicals
To simplify √72: find the largest perfect square factor. 72 = 36 × 2. √72 = √36 × √2 = 6√2. Technique: factor the radicand and pull out pairs. √180 = √(36 × 5) = 6√5. Never leave a perfect square under a radical sign in a simplified answer.

#### Cube Root Tricks
∛125 = 5 (5³ = 125). ∛1000 = 10 (10³ = 1000). ∛−8 = −2 (negative numbers have real cube roots, unlike square roots). ∛(a³b³) = ab. Science Bowl uses cube roots for volume problems: a cube of volume 64 cm³ has side length ∛64 = 4 cm.

#### Pythagorean Triples (Common Roots in Context)
The Pythagorean theorem a² + b² = c² produces exact square roots in right triangles. Common triples: 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25. Multiples: 6-8-10, 9-12-15. Recognizing these triples lets you avoid computing the root.

#### Review Questions
1. Simplify √200.
2. Between which two consecutive integers does √150 fall?
3. A square has area 169 m². What is its perimeter?

### Scientific Notation
**Type:** Core Understanding
**Slug:** scientific-notation-complete
**Estimated time:** 12 min
**Key concepts:** a × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ a < 10 · converting to/from standard form · operations in sci notation
**Summary:** Master scientific notation as the language of very large and very small numbers in science.

#### Why Scientific Notation Exists
The speed of light is 300,000,000 m/s. The mass of an electron is 0.00000000000000000000000000000091 kg. Writing these in standard form is error-prone. Scientific notation: 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s and 9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg. The coefficient a is always ≥ 1 and < 10 (exactly one digit before the decimal point). The power of 10 handles the magnitude.

#### Converting Standard → Scientific
Move the decimal so one nonzero digit is to its left. Count moves:
- Right moves → negative exponent: 0.00043 = 4.3 × 10⁻⁴ (moved 4 right)
- Left moves → positive exponent: 67,000 = 6.7 × 10⁴ (moved 4 left)
Memory trick: large numbers have positive powers (10⁴ = ten thousand), small numbers have negative powers.

#### Converting Scientific → Standard
Move decimal in the direction indicated by sign of exponent:
- Positive exponent → move right (gets bigger): 3.2 × 10⁵ = 320,000
- Negative exponent → move left (gets smaller): 5.1 × 10⁻³ = 0.0051

#### Multiplying and Dividing in Scientific Notation
Multiply coefficients, add exponents: (3 × 10⁴) × (2 × 10³) = 6 × 10⁷.
Divide coefficients, subtract exponents: (8 × 10⁶) ÷ (4 × 10²) = 2 × 10⁴.
After computing, check: is the coefficient still between 1 and 10? If (5 × 10⁴) × (3 × 10⁵) = 15 × 10⁹ — not proper form. Rewrite: 1.5 × 10¹⁰.

#### Adding/Subtracting: Align Exponents First
(4.2 × 10⁵) + (3.0 × 10⁴) — convert to same power: (4.2 × 10⁵) + (0.30 × 10⁵) = 4.50 × 10⁵. Always express in proper scientific notation at the end.

#### Review Questions
1. Write 0.000725 in scientific notation.
2. Compute (6.0 × 10⁵) × (4.0 × 10⁻³) and express in scientific notation.
3. The diameter of a hydrogen atom is about 1.06 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. Write this in standard form.

## Subtopic: Order of Operations

### PEMDAS: What It Is and Why It Exists
**Type:** Core Understanding
**Slug:** pemdas-what-it-is-and-why-it-exists
**Estimated time:** 11 min
**Key concepts:** PEMDAS · evaluation order · why conventions matter
**Summary:** Understand order of operations as a mathematical convention that prevents ambiguity, not an arbitrary rule.

#### Why We Need a Convention
Without a shared order of operations, 3 + 4 × 2 could equal 14 (left to right: 7 × 2) or 11 (multiplication first: 3 + 8). Mathematics requires exactly one answer, so mathematicians agreed on a hierarchy. PEMDAS isn't a law of nature — it's a convention, like driving on the right. But everyone follows it, so it works perfectly.

#### The PEMDAS Hierarchy
1. **P**arentheses (and brackets, braces — innermost first)
2. **E**xponents (and roots)
3. **M**ultiplication and **D**ivision (left to right, equal priority)
4. **A**ddition and **S**ubtraction (left to right, equal priority)

Evaluate: 3 + 4 × 2 = 3 + 8 = **11**. Parentheses change it: (3 + 4) × 2 = 7 × 2 = **14**.

#### The Left-to-Right Rule for Equal Priority
M and D have equal priority — do them left to right. 12 ÷ 4 × 3: left to right → 3 × 3 = **9**. NOT 12 ÷ 12 = 1. A and S have equal priority — do them left to right. 10 − 3 + 2: left to right → 7 + 2 = **9**. NOT 10 − 5 = 5.

#### Nested Parentheses
Work inside out. 2 × [(3 + 4) × (5 − 2)]: innermost → 7 and 3. Then → 2 × [7 × 3] = 2 × 21 = **42**. Always resolve the innermost grouping symbol first.

#### Implicit Multiplication
In algebra, a number directly next to parentheses means multiplication: 3(4 + 2) = 3 × 6 = 18. The parentheses are evaluated first, then the multiplication by 3. This is one of the most frequent sources of errors in algebraic expressions.

#### Review Questions
1. Evaluate: 5 + 3 × 4 − 2².
2. Evaluate: 18 ÷ (2 + 1) × 3 − 4.
3. Evaluate: 2³ + 4(6 − 2) ÷ 8.

### Common Order-of-Operations Errors and Traps
**Type:** Core Understanding
**Slug:** order-of-operations-errors-and-traps
**Estimated time:** 10 min
**Key concepts:** equal-priority trap · exponent applies only to base · fraction bar as grouping
**Summary:** Identify and fix the five most common order-of-operations errors that cost points in competition.

#### Trap 1: Multiplication Before Division Is Wrong
12 ÷ 4 × 3 ≠ 12 ÷ 12 = 1. Multiplication and division are EQUAL priority — go left to right. 12 ÷ 4 = 3, then 3 × 3 = 9. If you always "do multiplication first," you'll get this wrong. The correct rule: process M and D in the order you encounter them from left to right.

#### Trap 2: Exponent Applies Only to Its Direct Base
−3² ≠ (−3)². −3² means −(3²) = −9 (the exponent applies only to 3, not to the negative sign). (−3)² = 9 (the exponent applies to negative 3). The negative sign is really multiplication by −1, and multiplication has lower priority than exponents.

#### Trap 3: Forgetting the Fraction Bar Groups the Numerator
(3 + 5)/4 ≠ 3 + 5/4. The fraction bar is a grouping symbol, like parentheses around the numerator. (3+5)/4 = 8/4 = 2. But 3 + 5/4 = 3 + 1.25 = 4.25. When typing expressions, write (3+5)/4, not 3+5/4.

#### Trap 4: Distributing Through Wrong Operation
3 + (4 + 5)² ≠ (3 + 4 + 5)². Parentheses must be evaluated completely before any external operation applies. 3 + (9)² = 3 + 81 = 84. Not (12)² = 144.

#### Trap 5: Subtraction Distributes Its Sign
10 − (3 + 4) = 10 − 3 − 4 = 3. NOT 10 − 3 + 4 = 11. Subtracting a quantity in parentheses flips the sign of everything inside. This is equivalent to adding the negative: 10 + (−3 − 4) = 10 − 7 = 3.

#### Review Questions
1. What is −5²? What is (−5)²? Explain the difference.
2. Evaluate: 24 ÷ 8 × 2 − 1.
3. Evaluate: 20 − (3 + 2 × 4).

### Order of Operations with Algebra and Science Bowl Problems
**Type:** Application
**Slug:** order-of-operations-algebra-science-bowl
**Estimated time:** 12 min
**Key concepts:** evaluating expressions with variables · multi-line evaluation · reading expressions precisely
**Summary:** Apply PEMDAS to algebraic expressions and Science Bowl–style numerical problems with variables.

#### Evaluating Algebraic Expressions
"Evaluate 3x² − 2x + 5 when x = −2." Substitute carefully: 3(−2)² − 2(−2) + 5 = 3(4) − (−4) + 5 = 12 + 4 + 5 = **21**. Critical: evaluate (−2)² = 4 before multiplying by 3. If you write 3 × −2² = 3 × −4 = −12 first, you've violated PEMDAS.

#### Formula Evaluation
Science Bowl physics and chemistry questions often ask you to evaluate a formula: "Using F = ma, find the force when m = 3.5 kg and a = 4 m/s²." F = 3.5 × 4 = **14 N**. For compound formulas: "Evaluate d = ½at² for a = 10 m/s² and t = 3 s." d = ½ × 10 × 3² = ½ × 10 × 9 = **45 m**. Exponent (3²) before multiplication by ½ and 10.

#### Multi-Step Science Expressions
"Evaluate: 4(2 + 3)² ÷ 5 − 3." Step by step: (2+3)=5; 5²=25; 4×25=100; 100÷5=20; 20−3=**17**. Science Bowl multi-step math questions often pack five operations into one expression — careful evaluation is the only path to the right answer.

#### Expression vs. Equation
An expression (3x + 5) is evaluated for specific x values. An equation (3x + 5 = 14) is solved to find x. Order of operations applies to evaluating expressions; inverse operations apply to solving equations. Don't confuse the two procedures.

#### Science Bowl Reading Patterns
Bowl questions often say "For 10 points — what is [expression], to the nearest integer?" Read the expression carefully before computing. Clue phrases: "the quantity" = grouping (parentheses implied), "the square of the sum" = (sum)², "the sum of the squares" = a² + b².

#### Top 8 High-Yield Facts
1. M and D are EQUAL priority — left to right, not "multiply before divide"
2. −a² = −(a²), always; only (−a)² includes the negative in the square
3. Fraction bars group: (a+b)/(c+d) means you must add before dividing
4. Subtracting a sum: a − (b+c) = a − b − c
5. "The square of the sum of x and y" → (x+y)², not x² + y²
6. 8 ÷ 2(2+2): ambiguous without parentheses — bowl questions always use unambiguous notation
7. Absolute value bars | | are grouping symbols: evaluate inside first
8. (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²

#### Practice Toss-Up Stems
1. "For 10 points — evaluate: 5 + 3² × 2 − 10." → 5 + 9×2 − 10 = 5 + 18 − 10 = **13**
2. "For 10 points — evaluate 3x² − x + 4 when x = −3." → 3(9)−(−3)+4 = 27+3+4 = **34**
3. "For 10 points — evaluate: [4 + 2(3 + 1)]²." → [4 + 2(4)]² = [4+8]² = 12² = **144**
4. "For 10 points — what is the value of −2⁴?" → −(16) = **−16**
5. "For 10 points — if P = 2L + 2W, find P when L = 12 and W = 7." → 24 + 14 = **38**
6. "For 10 points — what is 20 − 4 × 3 + 2²?" → 20 − 12 + 4 = **12**

#### Review Questions
1. Without calculating: will 3 + 4 × 5 or (3 + 4) × 5 be larger? By how much?
2. Why can't you "add subtraction and addition before multiplication" in 3 × 5 − 2 + 1?
3. Evaluate |2 − 7| + 3² and explain each step.
