#### Variables as Placeholders
A variable is a symbol (usually a letter) that represents a number we don't know yet — or a number that changes. In "x + 5," x could be any number. In "the number of students in a room," x changes depending on the day. Variables let us write general rules instead of one-off calculations. The power of algebra is that one equation can describe infinitely many specific situations.

#### Expressions: Instructions Without a Claim
An expression is a mathematical phrase — it has numbers, variables, and operations, but NO equals sign. "3x + 7" is an expression. You can evaluate it (substitute a value for x and compute), but you can't solve it (there's nothing to solve for). 3x + 7 when x = 4: substitute → 3(4) + 7 = 12 + 7 = 19. Expressions describe quantities; they don't make claims about equality.

#### Equations: Claims of Equality
An equation is a mathematical statement that two expressions are equal: 3x + 7 = 19. This claims that for some specific value of x, the left side equals the right side. Your job: find that value. Solving an equation means finding the value(s) of the variable that make the equation true. Equations have solutions; expressions have values (when evaluated).

#### Terms, Coefficients, and Constants
In 5x² − 3x + 8: the terms are 5x², −3x, and 8. The coefficient of x² is 5; the coefficient of x is −3. The constant is 8 (no variable attached). Like terms share the same variable raised to the same power: 3x and 7x are like terms; 3x and 3x² are NOT.

#### Combining Like Terms
Simplify 4x + 3y − 2x + 5y: group like terms → (4x − 2x) + (3y + 5y) = 2x + 8y. Only like terms can be combined. Think of x-terms as "apples" and y-terms as "bananas" — you can count apples together, but not apples with bananas.

#### Review Questions
1. Evaluate 4a − 2b + 3 when a = 5 and b = 3.
2. Simplify: 6x − 3 + 2x + 7 − x.
3. What is the coefficient of y in the expression 8x − 5y + 12?
