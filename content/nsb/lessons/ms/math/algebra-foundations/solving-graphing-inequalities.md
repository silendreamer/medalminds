#### Inequalities vs. Equations
An equation has one or a few specific solutions. An inequality (like x > 3) has a range of solutions — infinitely many values. The solution set is represented on a number line or in interval notation. The rules for solving are the same as equations, with one critical exception.

#### The Flip Rule
If you multiply or divide both sides by a NEGATIVE number, flip the inequality sign. Why? Multiplying by −1 reverses the number line order: if 3 > 1, then −3 < −1. This is the ONE rule that is different from equation solving. Forgetting to flip is the most common error in inequality problems.

Example: −2x > 8 → divide by −2, flip: x < −4.

#### Graphing on a Number Line
- Strict inequality (< or >): open circle at the boundary (endpoint NOT included)
- Non-strict (≤ or ≥): closed/filled circle (endpoint IS included)
- x > 3: open circle at 3, arrow pointing right
- x ≤ −1: closed circle at −1, arrow pointing left

#### Compound Inequalities
"And" (conjunction): must satisfy BOTH conditions. −2 < x ≤ 5 means x is between −2 and 5, not including −2 but including 5. On a number line: open circle at −2, closed circle at 5, line segment between them.

"Or" (disjunction): satisfies at least one condition. x < 1 OR x > 4. On a number line: two separate rays.

#### Word Problems with Inequalities
"A student must score at least 80 to pass." Score ≥ 80. "At most 12 students can sit in a row." Students ≤ 12. "Between 5 and 10 items." 5 ≤ items ≤ 10 (or 5 < items < 10 depending on whether bounds are included).

#### Review Questions
1. Solve and graph: 3x − 5 > 10.
2. Solve: −4x + 8 ≤ 20.
3. Write an inequality for: "A number is at least 3 but no more than 12."
