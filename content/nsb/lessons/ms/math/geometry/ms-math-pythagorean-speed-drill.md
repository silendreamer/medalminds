#### The Recognition Pattern
Most Science Bowl Pythagorean problems use either (a) common triples scaled up, or (b) simple perfect squares. When given two sides, your mental checklist: (1) Is it a 3-4-5 family? (2) Is it a 5-12-13 family? (3) Is it an 8-15-17 family? (4) Otherwise, compute a²+b²=c² directly. For c given: find a²=c²−b². Practice until identifying triples feels automatic.

#### Speed Drill Problems
Solve each in under 10 seconds: (1) Legs 6 and 8 → hyp = **10** [3-4-5 ×2]. (2) Leg 5 and hyp 13 → other leg = **12** [5-12-13]. (3) Legs 20 and 21 → hyp = **29** [20-21-29 triple]. (4) Legs 9 and 40 → hyp = **41** [9-40-41 triple]. (5) Leg 15 and hyp 17 → other leg = **8** [8-15-17]. (6) Legs 30 and 40 → hyp = **50** [3-4-5 ×10].

#### When It's Not a Triple: Direct Calculation
Legs 4 and 6: c² = 16+36 = 52. c = √52 = 2√13 ≈ 7.21. If the answer isn't a whole number, leave it as a simplified radical unless the problem asks for a decimal. Competition problems are usually designed to produce whole numbers — if you're getting an ugly answer, re-read the problem.

#### Toss-Up Clue Words
Listen for: "right triangle," "legs," "hypotenuse," "right angle," "perpendicular," "ladder against a wall," "diagonal of a rectangle." All of these signal the Pythagorean theorem. The diagonal of a rectangle with length l and width w is √(l²+w²) — the diagonal splits the rectangle into two right triangles.

#### Review Questions
1. For 10 points: a rectangle is 5 units long and 12 units wide. What is the length of its diagonal?
2. For 10 points: a right triangle has a hypotenuse of 25 and one leg of 20. Find the other leg.
3. For 10 points: legs of a right triangle are both equal to 6. What is the hypotenuse? Express as a simplified radical.
