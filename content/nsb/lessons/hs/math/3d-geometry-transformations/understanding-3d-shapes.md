### Introduction to 3D Shapes

Three-dimensional (3D) shapes are objects that have length, width, and height. Unlike two-dimensional shapes, which only have length and width, 3D shapes occupy space and can be measured in terms of volume and surface area. Understanding these properties is crucial for solving problems related to geometry, physics, and engineering, making it essential for students preparing for competitions like the Science Bowl.

### Volume of Prisms

The volume of a prism can be calculated using the formula \( V = B \times h \), where \( B \) is the area of the base and \( h \) is the height of the prism. Different types of prisms, such as rectangular prisms and triangular prisms, have distinct base shapes, which affect the calculation of \( B \). For example, the base area of a rectangular prism is found by multiplying its length and width, while for a triangular prism, it involves calculating the area of the triangle using the formula \( \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} \).

### Surface Area of Cylinders

The surface area of a cylinder can be determined using the formula \( SA = 2\pi r(h + r) \), where \( r \) is the radius of the base and \( h \) is the height of the cylinder. This formula accounts for both the lateral surface area and the areas of the two circular bases. Understanding how to derive this formula involves recognizing that the lateral surface area can be visualized as a rectangle when the cylinder is "unwrapped," with the height as one dimension and the circumference of the base \( (2\pi r) \) as the other.

### Properties of Polyhedra

Polyhedra are 3D shapes with flat polygonal faces, straight edges, and vertices. The most common types of polyhedra include cubes, tetrahedrons, and octahedrons. A key property of polyhedra is Euler's formula, which states that for any convex polyhedron, the relationship between the number of vertices \( V \), edges \( E \), and faces \( F \) is given by \( V - E + F = 2 \). This formula helps in understanding the structure and classification of polyhedra.

### Types of Common 3D Shapes

Common 3D shapes include prisms, cylinders, cones, spheres, and pyramids. Each shape has unique properties and formulas for calculating volume and surface area. For instance, the volume of a cone is given by \( V = \frac{1}{3} \pi r^2 h \), while the surface area includes the base area and the lateral area. Understanding these shapes and their properties is essential for solving complex geometric problems.

### Transformations in 3D Geometry

Transformations in 3D geometry include translations, rotations, and reflections, which can change the position or orientation of a shape without altering its dimensions. For example, a translation moves a shape along a vector, while a rotation involves turning the shape around an axis. Understanding how these transformations affect the coordinates of points in 3D space is crucial for visualizing and solving problems involving 3D shapes.

### Review Questions

1. What is the formula for calculating the volume of a rectangular prism?
2. How do you calculate the surface area of a cylinder?
3. According to Euler's formula, what is the relationship between the number of vertices, edges, and faces of a convex polyhedron?
