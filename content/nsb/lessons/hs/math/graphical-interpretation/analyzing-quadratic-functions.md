### Introduction to Quadratic Functions

Quadratic functions are polynomial functions of degree two, typically expressed in the standard form \( f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c \), where \( a \), \( b \), and \( c \) are constants and \( a \neq 0 \). The graph of a quadratic function is a parabola, which can open either upwards (when \( a > 0 \)) or downwards (when \( a < 0 \)). Understanding the graphical representation of these functions is crucial for analyzing their properties, such as the vertex, axis of symmetry, and roots.

### Vertex of a Parabola

The vertex of a parabola is the highest or lowest point on the graph, depending on the direction it opens. For a quadratic function in standard form, the vertex can be found using the formula \( x = -\frac{b}{2a} \). Once the x-coordinate of the vertex is determined, substituting this value back into the function yields the corresponding y-coordinate, thus providing the vertex as the point \( (h, k) \), where \( h = -\frac{b}{2a} \) and \( k = f(h) \).

### Axis of Symmetry

The axis of symmetry is a vertical line that divides the parabola into two mirror-image halves. This line passes through the vertex and can be expressed with the equation \( x = h \), where \( h \) is the x-coordinate of the vertex. The axis of symmetry is a critical feature for graphing the parabola, as it helps to determine the shape and position of the graph in relation to the x-axis.

### Roots of the Quadratic Function

The roots, or x-intercepts, of a quadratic function are the points where the graph intersects the x-axis. These roots can be found using various methods, including factoring, completing the square, or applying the quadratic formula \( x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \). The discriminant, \( b^2 - 4ac \), plays a vital role in determining the nature of the roots: if it is positive, there are two distinct real roots; if it is zero, there is one real root (a repeated root); and if it is negative, there are no real roots.

### Graphical Interpretation of Quadratic Functions

When graphing a quadratic function, it is essential to plot the vertex, the axis of symmetry, and the roots accurately. The shape of the parabola can be sketched by identifying these key features and plotting additional points if necessary. Understanding the relationship between the coefficients \( a \), \( b \), and \( c \) and the resulting graph is crucial for predicting the behavior of the function, such as its width and direction.

### Applications of Quadratic Functions

Quadratic functions have numerous applications in various fields, including physics, engineering, and economics. They can model projectile motion, optimize area and volume, and analyze profit and loss scenarios. Recognizing the significance of quadratic functions in real-world situations enhances students' ability to apply mathematical concepts to practical problems.

### Review Questions

2. How does the discriminant \( b^2 - 4ac \) determine the number of real roots for a quadratic function?
3. What is the equation of the axis of symmetry for a parabola with vertex at \( (h, k) \)?
