### Introduction to Arithmetic Sequences

An arithmetic sequence is a list of numbers in which the difference between consecutive terms is constant. This difference is known as the common difference, denoted as \(d\). For example, in the sequence 2, 5, 8, 11, the common difference is 3 because each term increases by 3 from the previous term. Understanding arithmetic sequences is fundamental in mathematics as they appear in various real-world contexts, including finance and physics.

### Identifying Terms in an Arithmetic Sequence

To identify terms in an arithmetic sequence, one can start with the first term, denoted as \(a_1\), and repeatedly add the common difference \(d\). The \(n\)-th term of an arithmetic sequence can be expressed using the formula:  
\[ a_n = a_1 + (n - 1) \cdot d \]  
Here, \(a_n\) represents the \(n\)-th term, \(a_1\) is the first term, and \(n\) is the position of the term in the sequence. For example, if \(a_1 = 2\) and \(d = 3\), the 5th term can be calculated as \(a_5 = 2 + (5 - 1) \cdot 3 = 14\).

### Calculating the Common Difference

The common difference \(d\) can be calculated by subtracting any term in the sequence from the term that follows it. For instance, if the sequence is 4, 10, 16, 22, the common difference can be found by calculating \(10 - 4 = 6\) or \(16 - 10 = 6\). This consistent difference is a hallmark of arithmetic sequences and is crucial for determining other terms in the sequence. Recognizing the common difference allows students to quickly generate additional terms or verify the nature of a sequence.

### Sum of Terms in an Arithmetic Sequence

The sum of the first \(n\) terms of an arithmetic sequence can be calculated using the formula:  
\[ S_n = \frac{n}{2} \cdot (a_1 + a_n) \]  
where \(S_n\) is the sum of the first \(n\) terms, \(a_1\) is the first term, and \(a_n\) is the \(n\)-th term. Alternatively, this can also be expressed as:  
\[ S_n = \frac{n}{2} \cdot (2a_1 + (n - 1) \cdot d) \]  
This formula is derived from the fact that the sum of an arithmetic sequence can be viewed as the average of the first and last terms, multiplied by the number of terms.

### Applications of Arithmetic Sequences

Arithmetic sequences have numerous applications across different fields. In finance, they can model scenarios such as regular savings deposits where a fixed amount is added over time. In physics, they can describe uniform motion, where an object moves at a constant speed, resulting in a linear increase in distance over time. Understanding these applications helps students appreciate the relevance of arithmetic sequences in solving real-world problems.

### Practice Problems and Examples

To reinforce understanding, students should engage with practice problems that require them to identify terms, calculate the common difference, and find sums of arithmetic sequences. For example, given the sequence 7, 10, 13, students can be asked to find the 10th term and the sum of the first 10 terms. Such exercises not only solidify the concepts but also prepare students for similar questions they may encounter in competitions like Science Bowl.

### Review Questions

1. What is the common difference in the arithmetic sequence 12, 15, 18, 21?
2. Using the formula for the \(n\)-th term, what is the 6th term of the sequence where \(a_1 = 5\) and \(d = 4\)?
3. Calculate the sum of the first 8 terms of the arithmetic sequence that starts with 3 and has a common difference of 2.
