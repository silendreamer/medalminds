### Definition of Conic Sections

Conic sections are the curves obtained by intersecting a plane with a double-napped cone. Depending on the angle of the intersection, the resulting shape can be a circle, ellipse, parabola, or hyperbola. Each of these shapes has unique properties and equations that define them, making conic sections a fundamental topic in geometry and algebra.

### Types of Conic Sections

There are four primary types of conic sections: circles, ellipses, parabolas, and hyperbolas. A circle is formed when the intersecting plane is perpendicular to the axis of the cone, resulting in a set of points equidistant from a center point. An ellipse occurs when the plane cuts through both nappes of the cone at an angle, creating a closed curve that resembles a stretched circle.

### Standard Equations of Conics

Each type of conic section has a standard equation that describes its shape and position in the Cartesian coordinate system. The standard equation of a circle is \((x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2\), where \((h, k)\) is the center and \(r\) is the radius. For an ellipse, the standard form is \(\frac{(x - h)^2}{a^2} + \frac{(y - k)^2}{b^2} = 1\), where \(a\) and \(b\) are the semi-major and semi-minor axes, respectively.

### Parabolas and Their Equations

A parabola is defined as the set of all points equidistant from a fixed point called the focus and a fixed line known as the directrix. The standard equation of a parabola that opens upwards is given by \(y = a(x - h)^2 + k\), where \((h, k)\) is the vertex of the parabola. Parabolas can also open downwards, left, or right, and their orientation affects the form of their equations.

### Hyperbolas and Their Properties

Hyperbolas consist of two separate curves called branches, which open away from each other. The standard equation of a hyperbola centered at the origin is given by \(\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1\) for a hyperbola that opens left and right, while the equation \(\frac{y^2}{b^2} - \frac{x^2}{a^2} = 1\) describes one that opens up and down. Hyperbolas have unique properties, such as asymptotes, which are lines that the branches approach but never touch.

### Applications of Conic Sections

Conic sections are not just theoretical constructs; they have practical applications in various fields. For instance, the orbits of planets and satellites can be modeled using ellipses, while parabolic reflectors are used in satellite dishes and car headlights to focus light. Understanding the properties of conic sections is essential in fields such as physics, engineering, and computer graphics.

### Review Questions

1. What is the standard equation of a circle with center at \((h, k)\) and radius \(r\)?
2. Describe the shape and standard equation of a hyperbola that opens left and right.
3. What type of conic section is formed when a plane intersects a cone parallel to its axis?
