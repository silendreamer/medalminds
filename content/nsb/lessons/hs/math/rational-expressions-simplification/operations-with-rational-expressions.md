### Introduction to Rational Expressions

Rational expressions are fractions where the numerator and denominator are both polynomials. They can be expressed in the form \( \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} \), where \( P(x) \) and \( Q(x) \) are polynomials. Understanding how to manipulate these expressions is crucial for solving complex algebraic equations and is a foundational skill in higher-level mathematics.

### Simplifying Rational Expressions

To simplify a rational expression, one must factor both the numerator and the denominator completely. After factoring, any common factors can be canceled out, provided they are not equal to zero. For example, in the expression \( \frac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 - 3x} \), we can factor it to \( \frac{(x - 3)(x + 3)}{x(x - 3)} \) and simplify to \( \frac{x + 3}{x} \) for \( x \neq 3 \).

### Adding Rational Expressions

When adding rational expressions, it is essential to have a common denominator. If the denominators are different, find the least common denominator (LCD) and rewrite each expression accordingly. For instance, to add \( \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{x^2} \), the LCD is \( x^2 \), leading to \( \frac{x}{x^2} + \frac{1}{x^2} = \frac{x + 1}{x^2} \).

### Subtracting Rational Expressions

Subtracting rational expressions follows the same principle as addition, requiring a common denominator. Once the expressions are rewritten with the LCD, subtract the numerators while keeping the common denominator. For example, to subtract \( \frac{3}{x^2} - \frac{1}{x} \), we convert \( \frac{1}{x} \) to \( \frac{x}{x^2} \) to get \( \frac{3 - x}{x^2} \).

### Multiplying Rational Expressions

To multiply rational expressions, multiply the numerators together and the denominators together. Before multiplying, it is beneficial to factor all expressions to identify and cancel any common factors. For example, in \( \frac{x^2 - 1}{x + 1} \cdot \frac{x + 1}{x^2 + 2x} \), factoring gives \( \frac{(x - 1)(x + 1)}{x + 1} \cdot \frac{x + 1}{x(x + 2)} \), allowing cancellation of \( x + 1 \) to simplify to \( \frac{x - 1}{x(x + 2)} \).

### Dividing Rational Expressions

Dividing rational expressions involves multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor. To divide \( \frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d} \), rewrite it as \( \frac{a}{b} \cdot \frac{d}{c} \). Similar to multiplication, factor all expressions first to simplify before performing the operation. For instance, dividing \( \frac{x^2 - 4}{x + 2} \div \frac{x^2 - 1}{x} \) becomes \( \frac{(x - 2)(x + 2)}{x + 2} \cdot \frac{x}{(x - 1)(x + 1)} \), allowing for simplification by canceling \( x + 2 \).

### Review Questions

2. When adding \( \frac{2}{x} + \frac{3}{x^2} \), what is the least common denominator?
3. How do you convert the division of rational expressions into a multiplication problem?
