### Introduction to Divisibility Rules

Divisibility rules are shortcuts that help determine whether one integer can be divided by another without leaving a remainder. Understanding these rules is crucial for simplifying fractions, solving problems in number theory, and preparing for competitions like the Science Bowl. This lesson will cover the divisibility rules for the integers 2, 3, 5, and 10, as well as the concept of common divisors.

### Divisibility by 2

A number is divisible by 2 if its last digit is even, which includes the digits 0, 2, 4, 6, or 8. For example, the numbers 14, 28, and 56 are all divisible by 2 because they end in even digits. This rule is particularly useful when quickly assessing the evenness of a number, which is foundational in many mathematical operations and proofs.

### Divisibility by 3

To determine if a number is divisible by 3, you can sum its digits and check if that sum is divisible by 3. For instance, for the number 123, the sum of the digits is 1 + 2 + 3 = 6, which is divisible by 3, thus making 123 divisible by 3. This rule can simplify calculations, especially when dealing with larger numbers, and is often used in various mathematical contexts.

### Divisibility by 5

A number is divisible by 5 if its last digit is either 0 or 5. For example, the numbers 25, 50, and 105 are all divisible by 5 because they end in 5 or 0. This rule is particularly useful in financial calculations and when working with multiples of 5, which frequently appear in real-world applications.

### Divisibility by 10

A number is divisible by 10 if it ends in 0. This is one of the simplest divisibility rules and is frequently used in everyday calculations, such as when rounding numbers or working with monetary values. For example, 40, 150, and 320 are all divisible by 10, making it easy to group items or calculate totals in base-10 systems.

### Common Divisors

Common divisors are integers that can divide two or more numbers without leaving a remainder. For example, the common divisors of 12 and 18 include 1, 2, 3, and 6. Understanding common divisors is essential for simplifying fractions and finding the greatest common divisor (GCD), which is useful in reducing fractions to their simplest form.

### Practical Applications of Divisibility Rules

Divisibility rules are not only theoretical but also have practical applications in various fields such as computer science, cryptography, and data analysis. For example, algorithms often utilize these rules to optimize calculations and improve efficiency. Additionally, they are foundational in understanding more complex mathematical concepts such as prime factorization and least common multiples (LCM).

### Review Questions

1. What is the rule for determining if a number is divisible by 3?
2. If a number ends in 4, is it divisible by 2? Explain.
3. List all common divisors of the numbers 24 and 36.
