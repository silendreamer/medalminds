### Introduction to Light Behavior

Light is a form of electromagnetic radiation that is visible to the human eye. It travels in waves and exhibits both particle-like and wave-like properties, a concept known as wave-particle duality. Understanding how light behaves is crucial in various fields, including physics, engineering, and even medicine. This lesson will cover the fundamental behaviors of light: reflection, refraction, diffraction, and dispersion.

### Reflection of Light

Reflection occurs when light waves bounce off a surface. The angle of incidence, which is the angle between the incoming light ray and the normal (a perpendicular line to the surface), is equal to the angle of reflection, which is the angle between the reflected ray and the normal. This principle is described by the law of reflection and is fundamental in the design of mirrors and optical devices. Reflection can be categorized into two types: specular reflection, which occurs on smooth surfaces, and diffuse reflection, which occurs on rough surfaces.

### Refraction of Light

Refraction is the bending of light as it passes from one medium to another with a different density. This phenomenon occurs because light travels at different speeds in different materials; it slows down when entering a denser medium and speeds up when exiting to a less dense medium. The degree of bending can be described by Snell's Law, which relates the angles of incidence and refraction to the indices of refraction of the two media involved. Refraction is responsible for various optical effects, such as the bending of a straw in a glass of water and the formation of rainbows.

### Diffraction of Light

Diffraction refers to the bending and spreading of light waves when they encounter an obstacle or pass through a narrow aperture. This behavior is most pronounced when the size of the obstacle or aperture is comparable to the wavelength of the light. Diffraction patterns can be observed in experiments using slits or gratings and are crucial for understanding wave behavior. This phenomenon is also the reason why sound can be heard around corners and why light can create patterns of interference.

### Dispersion of Light

Dispersion occurs when light separates into its constituent colors as it passes through a medium, such as a prism. This happens because different wavelengths of light travel at different speeds in a medium, leading to varying degrees of refraction. The classic example of dispersion is the formation of a rainbow, where sunlight is refracted and dispersed by water droplets in the atmosphere. Understanding dispersion is essential in optics, as it explains the behavior of lenses and the creation of spectra in spectroscopy.

### Applications of Light Behavior

The principles of light behavior have numerous applications in technology and science. For instance, reflection is utilized in mirrors and reflective surfaces in telescopes, while refraction is essential in the design of lenses for glasses and cameras. Diffraction is used in applications such as X-ray crystallography and the design of optical instruments. Dispersion is critical in fiber optics and spectroscopy, allowing for the analysis of materials based on their light absorption and emission characteristics.

### Review Questions

2. Describe how Snell's Law relates to the phenomenon of refraction.
