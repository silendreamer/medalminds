#### Magic Numbers
Nuclei with certain numbers of protons or neutrons (2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82, or 126) are unusually stable — they have higher binding energy, more stable isotopes, and lower neutron-capture cross sections. These are magic numbers. Helium-4 (Z=2, N=2), oxygen-16 (Z=8, N=8), calcium-40 (Z=20, N=20), and lead-208 (Z=82, N=126) are "doubly magic" — all have especially high stability.

#### Nuclear Shell Model
Analogous to the electron shell model in chemistry, nucleons fill quantized energy levels (shells) in the nucleus. A completely filled shell creates extra stability — exactly as a noble gas configuration creates chemically inert atoms. Magic numbers correspond to closed nuclear shells. Maria Goeppert Mayer and J. Hans D. Jensen developed the nuclear shell model in 1949, earning the Nobel Prize in Physics.

#### Evidence for Magic Numbers
Tin (Z=50) has 10 stable isotopes — more than any other element. Lead (Z=82) is the heaviest element with a stable isotope (Pb-208). Calcium-48 has an unusually high N/Z ratio for a stable light nucleus (N=28, another magic number). These patterns emerge naturally from the shell model.
