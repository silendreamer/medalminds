#### Alpha Radiation
Alpha particles (⁴₂He, 2 protons + 2 neutrons) are the largest and most ionizing radiation type. They travel only 2–10 cm in air and are stopped by a sheet of paper or the outer layer of skin (~70 μm of dead cells). Despite low penetration, alpha emitters are extremely dangerous if inhaled or ingested: polonium-210 (used to assassinate Alexander Litvinenko in 2006) delivers massive doses to lung tissue. Alpha particles produce ~20 ion pairs per μm of tissue — far denser ionization than beta or gamma.

#### Beta Radiation
Beta particles (fast electrons or positrons) penetrate several meters in air and ~1 cm of tissue. Stopped by ~3 mm of aluminum or thick plastic. Beta emitters on skin can cause radiation burns; if ingested or inhaled, they irradiate internal organs. Strontium-90 (a major fission product) concentrates in bone marrow, causing leukemia. Yttrium-90 (a beta emitter) is used in targeted cancer therapy.

#### Gamma Radiation and X-Rays
Gamma rays are high-energy photons (>100 keV). They penetrate deeply — several centimeters of lead or ~1 meter of concrete reduces intensity by a factor of 10. Gamma attenuation follows I = I₀e^(−μx), where μ is the linear attenuation coefficient (material-dependent) and x is shield thickness. The half-value layer (HVL) is the thickness that halves intensity: HVL = ln2/μ. For 1 MeV gammas: HVL in lead ≈ 1 cm, in water ≈ 10 cm, in concrete ≈ 6 cm.

#### Neutron Radiation
Neutrons are uncharged and highly penetrating. They don't ionize directly but scatter off nuclei, producing recoiling protons in hydrogen-rich materials. Best shielding: hydrogen-rich materials (water, polyethylene, paraffin) that thermalize neutrons via elastic scattering, combined with a neutron absorber (boron, cadmium). Neutrons can activate non-radioactive materials, making reactor vessels and components radioactive over time.

#### Review Questions
1. Rank alpha, beta, and gamma radiation by (a) penetrating power and (b) ionizing power. Explain the inverse relationship.
2. Why is a thin lead sheet effective against gamma radiation but not neutrons?
3. Why is ingesting an alpha emitter far more dangerous than having the same source outside the body?
