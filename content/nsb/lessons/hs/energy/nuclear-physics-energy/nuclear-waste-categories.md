#### Low-Level Waste (LLW)
LLW includes contaminated tools, protective clothing, resins, and medical waste with relatively low radioactivity. Activity decays to safe levels within 100–300 years. LLW is compacted, packaged in drums, and buried in near-surface repositories. The US generates ~3 million cubic feet of LLW annually from reactors, hospitals, and research. LLW makes up ~90% of the volume but only ~1% of total radioactivity of all nuclear waste.

#### Intermediate-Level Waste (ILW)
ILW includes reactor components, filters, and sludges with higher radioactivity than LLW but lower than HLW. Requires shielding during handling. Typically stabilized in concrete or bitumen and buried in medium-depth repositories (tens of meters).

#### High-Level Waste (HLW)
HLW consists primarily of spent nuclear fuel and the separated liquid waste from reprocessing. It contains concentrated fission products (Cs-137, Sr-90 — short-lived but intensely radioactive) and actinides (plutonium, americium, neptunium — long-lived). HLW makes up ~3% of volume but ~95% of total radioactivity. It requires shielding, cooling, and isolation for thousands of years. Currently stored in water pools and dry casks at reactor sites.

#### Transuranic Waste (TRU)
TRU waste contains elements heavier than uranium (actinides: Pu, Am, Np, Cm) produced in reactors by neutron capture. TRU has long half-lives requiring deep geological disposal. The Waste Isolation Pilot Plant (WIPP) near Carlsbad, New Mexico, is the only operating deep geological repository in the US for TRU waste, located in a stable salt formation 655 meters underground.
