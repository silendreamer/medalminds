#### Q-Value of a Nuclear Reaction
The Q-value is the energy released by a nuclear reaction: Q = (Δm_reactants − Δm_products) × 931.5 MeV/u. If Q > 0, the reaction is exothermic (releases energy). If Q < 0, the reaction is endothermic (requires energy input). Q-values from binding energy tables let engineers calculate fuel efficiency without running the reaction.

#### Fission Q-Value: Uranium-235
A typical U-235 fission: ²³⁵U + n → ¹⁴¹Ba + ⁹²Kr + 3n. Approximate Q ≈ 200 MeV per fission event. Compare: burning one carbon atom releases ~4 eV. Nuclear fission releases ~50 million times more energy per atom than coal combustion — the fundamental reason nuclear fuel is so energy-dense. One kilogram of U-235 produces ~80 TJ of thermal energy.

#### Fusion Q-Value: Deuterium-Tritium
The most promising fusion reaction: ²H + ³H → ⁴He + n. Q ≈ 17.6 MeV. Deuterium is abundant (1 in 6,400 water molecules); tritium must be bred from lithium. The energy density of D-T fusion fuel is even greater than fission. To reach Q > 1 (more energy out than in), plasma must reach ~100 million °C — 6× hotter than the sun's core, where gravitational confinement replaces magnetic confinement.

#### Review Questions
1. A nuclear reaction releases 180 MeV. Convert this to joules (1 eV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J).
2. Why does fusion require such extreme temperatures despite releasing more energy per nucleon than fission?
3. Calculate the mass defect in atomic mass units for a reaction releasing 17.6 MeV.
