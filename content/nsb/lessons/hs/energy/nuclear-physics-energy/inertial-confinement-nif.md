#### Inertial Confinement Fusion (ICF)
In ICF, a tiny pellet (~2 mm diameter) of frozen D-T fuel is bombarded symmetrically by powerful laser pulses. The outer layer ablates (vaporizes) explosively, driving the core inward via Newton's third law — a tiny implosion that compresses and heats the fuel to fusion conditions in nanoseconds. If symmetry is perfect, the compressed core reaches 100× the density of lead and ~100 million K, enabling fusion before the pellet flies apart (confinement by inertia).

#### The National Ignition Facility (NIF)
NIF at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory uses 192 laser beams delivering 2.05 MJ of UV laser energy — the world's largest laser. The beams enter a gold cylinder (hohlraum) that converts them to X-rays, providing more symmetric compression. In December 2022, NIF produced 3.15 MJ of fusion energy from 2.05 MJ of laser input — Q_fusion > 1, achieving scientific ignition for the first time. Note: overall efficiency (wall-plug to laser to fusion) is still far below 1 for power generation.

#### Path to ICF Power Plants
Commercial ICF power would require firing ~10–15 pellets per second (current rate: ~1 per day at NIF), much more efficient lasers (NIF lasers are only ~1% efficient), and affordable pellet fabrication. Companies like Xcimer Energy, Marvel Fusion, and Focused Energy are pursuing commercial ICF. The timeline remains decades away, but ignition at NIF was a crucial scientific proof.
