#### High-Yield Facts
- Alpha: stopped by paper/skin; highest ionizing; dangerous if ingested
- Beta: stopped by Al/plastic; burns skin; Sr-90 concentrates in bone
- Gamma: stopped by lead/concrete; follows I = I₀e^(−μx)
- Neutrons: moderated by H-rich material; absorbed by boron/cadmium
- 1 gray = 1 J/kg absorbed dose
- 1 sievert = dose equivalent; H = D × Q
- Quality factors: gamma/beta Q=1; neutrons Q=5–20; alpha Q=20
- Half-value layer: thickness that halves gamma intensity
- ALARA: As Low As Reasonably Achievable
- US annual dose limit: 50 mSv for radiation workers; 1 mSv for public

#### Bowl Toss-Up Stems
- "For 10 points, this type of radiation is stopped by a sheet of paper but causes the most ionization per unit path…" → **Alpha radiation**
- "For 10 points, this principle of radiation protection stands for 'As Low As Reasonably Achievable'…" → **ALARA**
- "For 10 points, this is the thickness of shielding material that reduces gamma intensity by half…" → **Half-value layer (HVL)**

#### Review Questions
1. Why must neutron shields contain both a moderating material and a neutron-absorbing material?
2. Calculate: a source has 800 mSv/hr intensity. After 3 HVLs, what is the intensity?
3. What material would you use to shield a mixed gamma-neutron source, and in what order?
