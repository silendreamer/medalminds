#### The Tokamak Design
A tokamak (from Russian: toroidal chamber with magnetic coils) confines plasma in a donut-shaped (toroidal) magnetic field. Two magnetic field components: (1) toroidal field from external coils; (2) poloidal field from plasma current induced by a central solenoid. The combined helical field keeps plasma from drifting out. Plasma temperatures exceed 100 million K — hotter than the sun's core — contained entirely by magnetic fields, never touching the walls.

#### ITER: The International Experiment
ITER (International Thermonuclear Experimental Reactor) is under construction in Cadarache, France — the world's largest fusion experiment. Cost: ~€20 billion. Partners: EU, US, Russia, China, Japan, South Korea, India. ITER aims to produce Q = 10 (10× more fusion power than input heating power) and sustain a 500 MW plasma for 400 seconds. First plasma is expected in the late 2020s. ITER will not generate electricity — it's a proof-of-concept experiment for DEMO, the planned demonstration power plant.

#### The Lawson Criterion
For fusion to be self-sustaining (ignition), the plasma must satisfy the Lawson criterion: nτT must exceed a minimum value (~10²¹ m⁻³ · s · keV for D-T). n is plasma density, τ is energy confinement time, T is temperature. Triple product: difficult to maximize all three simultaneously. Record: JET tokamak (UK) achieved ~1.8 × 10²¹ m⁻³ · s · keV in 2022, producing 59 MJ of fusion energy — a world record.

#### JET's 2022 Record
In February 2022, the Joint European Torus (JET) in Culham, UK, produced 59 MJ of fusion energy in 5 seconds — the world record. Previous record: 21.7 MJ (JET, 1997). NIF (National Ignition Facility, Lawrence Livermore) achieved ignition (Q > 1) in December 2022 using laser-driven inertial confinement. These milestones demonstrate fusion's scientific feasibility.
