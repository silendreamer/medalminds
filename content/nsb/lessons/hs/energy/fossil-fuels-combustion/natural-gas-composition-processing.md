#### Composition and Formation
Natural gas is primarily methane (CH₄), typically 70–90% by volume in conventional reservoirs. It also contains ethane (C₂H₆, 0–20%), propane (C₃H₈, 0–5%), butane (C₄H₁₀, 0–2%), and inert gases (N₂, He, CO₂, 1–5%). Associated gas (produced with oil) has high ethane and propane; dry gas (produced from gas reservoirs) is nearly pure methane. Natural gas forms through two pathways: thermogenic (deep, hot burial of organic matter; biogenic (bacterial decomposition of organic matter at shallow depths). Biogenic gas dominates shallow gas fields and coal seams; thermogenic dominates conventional reservoirs deeper than ~800 m. Global conventional natural gas reserves are ~190 trillion cubic meters.

#### Raw Gas Processing
Raw natural gas from wells contains water vapor, hydrogen sulfide (H₂S), CO₂, and liquid hydrocarbons (condensates) that must be removed before transport. Water is removed by contact with glycol in absorption towers or by cooling below the dew point. H₂S is removed via amine absorption (monoethanolamine, MEA) or physical absorption: H₂S dissolves in solution, which is regenerated by heating. CO₂ is removed similarly or by molecular sieve adsorption. Condensates (C₅+ hydrocarbons) are recovered as natural gas liquids (NGLs)—ethane, propane, and butane—separated by low-temperature separation or adsorption. Treated gas (>99% methane, <0.1 ppm water) is pipeline-quality and suitable for long-distance transport.

#### Liquefaction and LNG
Natural gas liquefaction compresses and cools gas to −162°C at 1 atm, condensing it to a liquid 1/600th the volume of gas. This enables intercontinental transport via LNG tankers. The process: (1) pretreatment removes CO₂, water, mercury; (2) liquefaction via cascaded refrigeration (propane → ethylene → methane in series) or optimized single-cycle systems; (3) storage in insulated tanks at −162°C; (4) regasification at terminal (warming with seawater or LNG vaporizer). LNG technology enabled trade between non-pipeline-connected regions—Australia, Qatar, and the U.S. export LNG globally. Global LNG capacity is ~400 million tonnes per year; transport costs ~$2–4/MMBtu, making LNG economical for overseas delivery if gas is cheaper than ~$6/MMBtu at origin.

#### Safety and Odorization
Methane is odorless, odorless, and tasteless, making gas leaks difficult to detect. For safety, mercaptan (CH₃SH, methyl mercaptan) or tetrahydrothiophene (THT) is added to gas at concentrations of ~0.5–1% (by volume odorant per million volumes of gas, ppm). These sulfur compounds have a "rotten egg" or "skunk" smell detectable at <1 ppm. Odorants ensure gas leaks are quickly identified by smell, enabling rapid repair and preventing dangerous accumulation in homes. Natural gas stored underground (salt caverns, depleted oil/gas fields) is not odorant-treated, as it cannot leak into populated areas during storage.

#### Energy Density and Combustion Properties
Methane has a specific energy density of ~55 MJ/kg (higher than liquid fuels due to high H/C ratio: CH₄ vs C₈H₁₈). Natural gas combustion in turbines reaches ~45% efficiency; in combined-cycle plants with steam recovery, efficiency approaches 60%. The lower carbon-to-hydrogen ratio of methane produces less CO₂ per unit energy than coal or oil. Combustion: CH₄ + 2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2 H₂O; with stoichiometric O₂, complete combustion occurs at low temperatures (~600°C) compared to coal (~1,600°C). This makes natural gas ideal for electricity generation and heating, and explains its rising share of global energy (~23% of primary energy as of 2023).
