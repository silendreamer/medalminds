#### Fluid Catalytic Cracking (FCC)
FCC thermally cracks heavy hydrocarbons (gas oil, C₁₆–C₃₀) into lighter fractions over zeolite catalysts at high temperature (500–550°C) and moderate pressure (1–2 atm). Mechanism:

1. **Carbenium ion formation:** Heavy hydrocarbons are protonated on zeolite acid sites (Brønsted acid sites):
   RH + H⁺(zeolite) → R⁺ + H₂O

2. **β-scission:** Carbenium ions fragment:
   R⁺ → R₁⁺ + R₂ (smaller carbenium and alkene)

3. **Cracking selectivity:** Shorter chains evaporate more readily, shifting equilibrium toward gasoline (C₅–C₁₀).

**Product distribution:**
- Gasoline (C₅–C₁₂): ~45–55%
- Light gas oil (C₁₃–C₁₅, recycle): ~20–30%
- Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG, C₃–C₄): ~10–15%
- Coke (carbon residue): ~4–6%
- Dry gas (C₁–C₂): ~5–8%

FCC coke deposits on catalyst, deactivating it. Coke is burned in a regenerator to restore catalyst activity; heat from coke combustion is used for cracking endotherm (~200 kJ/kg feed), creating an energy-coupled system with no net heat input required (except startup). Modern fluidized-bed FCC reactors operate continuously, with catalyst circulating between riser (cracking reactor) and regenerator.

#### Catalytic Reforming
Light naphtha (C₆–C₁₀, typically 40–60 octane from crude) is upgraded to high-octane reformate (80–95 octane) over platinum-rhenium catalysts at 450–530°C, 10–30 atm. Reactions:

1. **Dehydrocyclization:** Alkanes → aromatics (raise octane):
   C₆H₁₄ (hexane) → C₆H₆ (benzene) + 4 H₂

2. **Isomerization:** Straight-chain → branched (raise octane):
   n-heptane → iso-heptane (raise octane ~20 points)

3. **Dehydrogenation:** Cycloalkanes → aromatics:
   Cyclohexane → benzene + 3 H₂

Hydrogen is produced as byproduct (~50–100 kg H₂/bbl feedstock) and used for hydrotreating or sold. Reformate contains 30–40% aromatic hydrocarbons (benzene, toluene, xylene) and is highly octane-rich (~95 octane), but volatility and environmental concerns (aromatic content) limit blending to ~30% of gasoline pool. **Benzene is toxic and is limited to <1% in gasoline** in developed nations.

#### Hydrotreating for Desulfurization
Hydrotreating uses hydrogen and catalysts (CoMo, NiMo on alumina support) to remove sulfur, nitrogen, and metals from petroleum fractions. Reactions at 300–400°C, 3–17 MPa:

**Desulfurization:**
R-S-R + H₂ → R-H + R-H + H₂S

**Denitrification:**
R-N + H₂ → R-H + NH₃

Hydrogen consumption is ~50–150 m³ H₂/ton feedstock (varies with sulfur/nitrogen content). H₂S product is recovered via the Claus process. Modern deep desulfurization removes >99% of sulfur, producing gas oil with <10 ppm S (meeting strict environmental regulations).

#### Polymerization and Alkylation
Light gaseous olefins (propene, butene) from cracking are polymerized or alkylated to form higher-octane gasoline. 

**Polymerization:** Propene and butene dimerize/trimerize over acid catalysts to heavier olefins (C₆–C₁₀), forming **alkylate gasoline** with octane ~85–95. Selectivity to gasoline-range products (C₅–C₁₀) is ~90%.

**Alkylation:** Isobutane reacts with olefins over acid catalysts to form alkylate (branched alkanes, octane ~90–95). Reaction: (CH₃)₃C-H + CH₂=CHR → (CH₃)₃C-CH₂-CHR (iso-alkylate). Alkylation is economical only when olefin/isobutane feed costs and product value justify capital and operation. Output is ~1.5 bbl alkylate per barrel olefin feedstock.

#### Worked Example: FCC Yield Analysis
A refinery processes 100,000 b/d of crude oil. Gas oil cut (C₁₆–C₃₀, 28% of crude) is sent to FCC. Using the product distribution from FCC (45% gasoline, 15% LPG, 5% coke, 8% dry gas, 27% recycle), calculate:

(a) Gasoline yield (b/d)
(b) LPG production (tons/day)
(c) Value of coke by-product (assuming coke worth $100/ton, density ~1.5 kg/L)

**Solution:**
(a) **Gas oil feed to FCC:**
100,000 b/d × 28% = 28,000 b/d (42 U.S. gal/barrel, so 28,000 × 42 = 1.176 × 10⁶ gal/day)

**Gasoline production:**
28,000 b/d × 45% = 12,600 b/d gasoline from FCC (value-add ~$25/bbl, contributing $315,000/day revenue increment)

(b) **LPG production:**
28,000 b/d × 15% = 4,200 b/d LPG
LPG density ~0.55 kg/L, so 4,200 × 42 × 3.785 L × 0.55 = 3.4 × 10⁵ kg/day = 340 tons/day LPG

(c) **Coke by-product:**
28,000 b/d × 5% = 1,400 b/d coke
Coke density ~1.5 kg/L, so 1,400 × 42 × 3.785 L × 1.5 = 3.6 × 10⁵ kg = 360 tons/day coke
Value = 360 tons/day × $100/ton = **$36,000/day or $13.1M/year**

#### Review Questions
1. Explain the mechanism of fluid catalytic cracking (β-scission) and why it produces a mixture of gasoline, LPG, and coke.
2. Compare the octane improvements from catalytic reforming (alkane → aromatic) vs. alkylation (isobutane + olefin → branched alkane). Which is preferred and why?
3. Calculate the H₂S production when hydrotreating 1,000 bbl/day of gas oil containing 1.5% sulfur (assume complete removal, oil density 0.85 kg/L, M_S = 32 g/mol).
