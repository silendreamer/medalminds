#### Fuel Detergents and Deposit Control
Fuel contains trace metals (copper, iron), sulfur compounds, and water that promote gum formation (oxidative polymerization of unsaturated hydrocarbons) when fuel is stored. Deposits accumulate in fuel injectors, intake valves, and combustion chambers, reducing efficiency and increasing emissions. Detergent additives (typically polyetheramine or polyol ester at 100–500 ppm) adsorb onto deposit surfaces, preventing aggregation and keeping deposits suspended or dissolved.

**Example:** Gasoline containing 0.5 ppm copper promotes gum formation; the same fuel with 100 ppm detergent and metal deactivator (N,N'-disalicylidene 1,2-propanediamine) suppresses gum formation. EPA Tier III gasoline contains mandatory detergents (~11 ppm) to meet fuel system deposit limits.

Detergents improve:
- Fuel economy: ~0.5–1% improvement by maintaining injector cleanliness
- Emissions: ~5–10% reduction in NOₓ and PM from improved combustion
- Durability: Extended intervals between fuel system maintenance

#### Antioxidants and Metal Deactivators
Unsaturated hydrocarbons (alkenes, alkadienes) in fuel are susceptible to oxidation, forming peroxides, aldehydes, and polymers that increase gum and sediment. Storage at elevated temperature (60°C, equivalent to ~30 years at room temperature in accelerated aging tests) rapidly oxidizes fuel. Antioxidants (hindered phenols like 2,6-di-tert-butyl-4-methylphenol, BHT) donate hydrogen to radical intermediates, terminating oxidation chains:

RO₂• + AH → RO₂H + A• (antioxidant sacrifices one electron, but A• is stable)

Typical concentration is 100–200 ppm. Synergism with metal deactivators: metals (Cu, Fe) catalyze oxidation through Fenton-like chemistry:

Fe²⁺ + H₂O₂ → Fe³⁺ + OH• + OH⁻

Metal deactivators (e.g., N,N'-disalicylidene 1,2-propanediamine) chelate iron and copper, preventing catalytic oxidation even at high storage temperatures.

#### Corrosion Inhibitors and Lubricity Improvers
Fuel systems (tanks, fuel pumps, injectors) are often steel or aluminum, vulnerable to corrosion if water is present. Corrosion inhibitors (fatty amine derivatives, typically 5–20 ppm) form a protective film on metal surfaces, reducing corrosion rate. In diesel, water in fuel can cause:

1. Microbiological growth (bacteria, fungi) in fuel tank, producing organic acids and biomass
2. Electrochemical corrosion of fuel system components
3. Fuel-water emulsion, interfering with fuel injection

Biocides (e.g., 2-phenoxyethanol, tributyltin compounds, now phased out; newer: isothiazolones) prevent microbial growth at 50–200 ppm, used mostly in diesel and heating oil.

**Lubricity:** Modern low-sulfur diesel and biodiesel blends have reduced lubricity due to removal of sulfur compounds that provided boundary lubrication for fuel pumps. Lubricity improvers (synthetic esters, fatty acid methyl esters, ~500 ppm) restore film strength, preventing pump wear.

#### Thermal Stability and Additives
Modern jet fuel (and some automotive diesel) must withstand high temperatures in aerospace applications. At 80–120°C, fuel begins degrading; at >150°C, rapid thermal degradation produces sludge, particulates, and gases that block fuel nozzles. Thermal stability improvers (e.g., antioxidants, corrosion inhibitors at higher concentrations) stabilize fuel, but ultimate limits are chemistry-based. Synthetic hydrocarbons (isoparaffins, cycloalkanes) and aromatic hydrocarbons are more thermally stable than alkenes or naphthenes.

**Static dissipator additives (SDA):** Jet fuel is refined to very high purity, reducing electrical conductivity to ~50 pS/m (picosiemens per meter), which can lead to dangerous static electricity accumulation during refueling. SDA (1–5 ppm, based on imidazole chemistry) increases conductivity to >450 pS/m, enabling safe grounding and charge dissipation.

#### Worked Example: Fuel Additive Blending
Design a gasoline blend with the following requirements:
- Base fuel: straight-run gasoline, 85 octane, contains 0.2 ppm copper
- Target: 87 octane, <0.05 ppm copper, deposit control

**Solution:**
1. Add ethanol (10% v/v) to raise octane: 85 + (10% × 15) ≈ 86.5 octane (assume ethanol = 100 octane equivalent)
2. Add isoparaffins (3% v/v, 95 octane equivalent): 86.5 + (3% × 95) ≈ 87 octane ✓
3. Add metal deactivator (6 ppm by weight): chelates copper to <0.05 ppm ✓
4. Add detergent (100 ppm): ensures deposit control ✓
5. Add antioxidant (150 ppm): ensures oxidation stability ✓

**Final blend composition (by volume):**
- Straight-run gasoline: 87%
- Ethanol (10% v/v): 10%
- Isoparaffins: 3%
- Additives package: 0.4–0.5% (detergent 100 ppm, antioxidant 150 ppm, metal deactivator 6 ppm, biocide 2 ppm)
