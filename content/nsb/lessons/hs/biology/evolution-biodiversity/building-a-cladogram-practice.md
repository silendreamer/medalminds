#### Character Matrix Construction
To build a cladogram: (1) list taxa and characters; (2) code characters as present (1) or absent (0); (3) determine outgroup; (4) identify synapomorphies (characters present in ingroup taxa but absent in outgroup); (5) group taxa by shared derived characters; (6) apply parsimony to choose among alternative arrangements.

**Example character matrix:**

| Taxon | Vertebral column | Jaws | Four limbs | Amnion | Hair |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lamprey (outgroup) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Shark | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| Frog | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| Lizard | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| Mouse | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

Reading: Vertebral column groups Shark + Frog + Lizard + Mouse (excluding lamprey outgroup). Jaws further groups them. Four limbs groups Frog + Lizard + Mouse. Amnion groups Lizard + Mouse. Hair is unique to Mouse.

#### Interpreting the Resulting Tree
The tree shows: (Lamprey(Shark(Frog(Lizard, Mouse)))). Frog and Lizard are not sister taxa — Lizard is more closely related to Mouse (both have amnion) than to Frog. This matches accepted vertebrate phylogeny.

#### Review Questions
1. In the character matrix above, what is the synapomorphy that groups lizards and mice to the exclusion of frogs?
2. Why is the lamprey used as the outgroup, and what would happen if an ancestral character (shared with outgroup) were mistakenly treated as a synapomorphy?
3. If you added a snake to the matrix, where would it fall relative to lizards and mice? What characters would you use to place it?
