#### Why Evolution Doesn't Produce "Perfect" Organisms
Natural selection can only act on existing variation; it cannot produce traits from scratch. Evolutionary history constrains what is possible — the vertebrate eye has a "blind spot" because the retina evolved inside-out from a nerve, forcing the optic nerve to pass through the retinal layer. An engineer designing a visual system would not make this choice. Genetic constraints: pleiotropic genes affect multiple traits simultaneously, so selection on one trait can be limited by negative effects on another (antagonistic pleiotropy). The sickle-cell allele causes disease in homozygotes but protects against malaria in heterozygotes — a genuine trade-off maintained by balancing selection.

#### Sexual Selection Trade-offs
Sexual selection drives traits that increase mating success but can reduce survival. Male peacock tails attract females but cost energy, impair flight, and attract predators. Male Irish elk evolved antlers so large they may have contributed to extinction. The "handicap principle" (Zahavi): honest signals of fitness must be costly to produce — only genuinely healthy individuals can afford an extravagant tail or loud call.

#### Review Questions
1. Why does the vertebrate retina have a blind spot, and what does this reveal about how evolution works?
2. In what environment would the sickle-cell allele be maintained at high frequency by natural selection? In what environment would it be selected against strongly?
3. Define antagonistic pleiotropy and give one example.
