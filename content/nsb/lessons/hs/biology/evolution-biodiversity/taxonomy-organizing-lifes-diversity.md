#### Linnaean Hierarchy
Carolus Linnaeus (18th century) established the hierarchical classification system still in use: **Domain → Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**. Mnemonic: "Dear King Philip Came Over For Good Soup." Each level (taxon, plural taxa) is more inclusive going up the hierarchy — a phylum contains many classes. Humans: Domain Eukarya, Kingdom Animalia, Phylum Chordata, Class Mammalia, Order Primates, Family Hominidae, Genus *Homo*, Species *sapiens*. Full binomial: *Homo sapiens*.

#### Binomial Nomenclature
Each species has a unique two-part scientific name: genus (capitalized) + species epithet (lowercase), written in italics or underlined. Examples: *Escherichia coli* (E. coli), *Panthera leo* (lion), *Rosa canina* (dog rose), *Tyrannosaurus rex* (T. rex). The same species may have dozens of common names in different languages but only one scientific name — this prevents confusion across scientific literature. When writing, the genus may be abbreviated after first use: *E. coli*, *P. leo*.

#### Three Domains
Carl Woese (1977) used rRNA sequences to identify three domains: **Bacteria** (prokaryotes, no nucleus, peptidoglycan cell walls), **Archaea** (prokaryotes, no nucleus, distinct membrane lipids, extremophiles and gut methanogens), **Eukarya** (membrane-bound nucleus, includes protists, fungi, plants, and animals). Archaea are more closely related to Eukarya than to Bacteria. This overturned the two-empire (prokaryote/eukaryote) view.
