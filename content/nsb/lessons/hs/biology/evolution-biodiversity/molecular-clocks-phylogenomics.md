#### The Molecular Clock Hypothesis
Zuckerkandl and Pauling (1960s) proposed that neutral mutations accumulate at a roughly constant rate over time in a given gene. If mutation rate is known (calibrated against the fossil record or known geological events), the number of sequence differences between two species can be used to estimate when they diverged. The human-chimpanzee divergence is estimated at ~6–7 million years ago using molecular clocks calibrated against fossil hominins. **Strict molecular clocks** assume uniform rates across lineages; **relaxed clocks** allow rates to vary. Rates vary by gene (rapidly evolving: mitochondrial DNA; slowly evolving: rRNA genes).

#### Applications and Limitations
Molecular clocks have been used to date: human migration out of Africa (~60,000 years ago), divergence of placental mammal orders after the K-Pg extinction, the origin of HIV from SIV in chimpanzees (~1920s, before the AIDS epidemic was recognized). Limitations: rates vary among lineages (generation time effects — organisms with shorter generation times accumulate mutations faster); rates vary among genes; calibration depends on fossil accuracy.

#### Phylogenomics
Using whole-genome sequences to reconstruct phylogenies. Advantages: massive amounts of data resolve even difficult relationships (the "tree of life" debates). The genomic era resolved long-standing debates: whales are most closely related to hippos (not other even-toed ungulates); birds are not paraphyletic (all descend from a single common ancestor). Horizontal gene transfer in bacteria complicates phylogenomics — the "tree of life" for bacteria is actually more like a "web of life."

#### Review Questions
1. How is a molecular clock calibrated, and why does calibration require fossil or geological data?
2. Why do mitochondrial genes evolve faster than ribosomal RNA genes, and how does this affect their use in phylogenetic analysis?
3. What genomic finding placed whales most closely with hippos, and what does this illustrate about morphology vs. molecular data?
