#### Hardy-Weinberg as the Null Hypothesis
Under Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, allele and genotype frequencies remain constant generation to generation if: (1) no mutation, (2) no gene flow, (3) random mating, (4) no genetic drift (infinitely large population), (5) no natural selection. When any condition is violated, evolution is occurring. The equations p + q = 1 and p² + 2pq + q² = 1 predict genotype frequencies from allele frequencies. Deviations from H-W predictions tell biologists which evolutionary force is acting.

#### Which Force Dominates When?
- Large population + strong selection → **natural selection** dominates
- Small population → **genetic drift** dominates, can override selection
- Connected populations + migrants → **gene flow** homogenizes allele frequencies
- All populations → **mutation** provides raw material but alone changes allele frequencies too slowly to matter much per generation

#### Review Questions
1. A population has allele frequencies p = 0.6 and q = 0.4. What are the expected genotype frequencies under Hardy-Weinberg?
2. You observe that heterozygotes are more common than H-W predicts. What evolutionary force could explain this?
3. Rank the four evolutionary mechanisms by the speed at which they can change allele frequencies, from fastest to slowest.
