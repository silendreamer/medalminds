#### Artificial Selection as Proof of Concept
Darwin used artificial selection — selective breeding by humans — as the opening argument of *On the Origin of Species* because his readers were familiar with it. All domestic dog breeds (from Chihuahuas to Great Danes) descend from wolves within ~15,000 years of selective breeding. Corn (maize) was bred from teosinte grass over thousands of years; wild teosinte is barely recognizable as corn's ancestor. Selectively bred crops include disease-resistant wheat, high-yield rice (Green Revolution), and seedless watermelons. If humans can produce such dramatic change in hundreds of generations, natural selection acting over millions of years can produce far greater change.

#### Evolution in Real Time: Antibiotic Resistance
Bacteria evolve antibiotic resistance rapidly because they reproduce quickly (E. coli divides every ~20 minutes) and mutation generates variation constantly. When a population is exposed to antibiotics, sensitive bacteria die and rare resistant mutants survive and reproduce. MRSA (methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus) kills tens of thousands annually. The "evolution in a pettle dish" experiment (Kishony lab, 2016) filmed bacteria evolving resistance to 1,000× the lethal dose in 11 days on a gradient plate. This demonstrates that antibiotic resistance is not acquired by individual organisms — it is a population-level evolutionary process.

#### Review Questions
1. How does the range of domestic dog breeds support Darwin's argument for natural selection?
2. Explain why the emergence of antibiotic-resistant bacteria is an example of natural selection, not Lamarckian inheritance.
3. A doctor prescribes a full 10-day course of antibiotics. Why is it important to finish the entire course even if symptoms disappear after 5 days?
