#### Fungal Cell Structure

Fungi are eukaryotic **heterotrophs** that digest food externally (**absorptive nutrition**) by secreting enzymes into the substrate and absorbing the products. The fundamental unit of fungal growth is the **hypha** (pl. hyphae) — a microscopic filament with a rigid cell wall made of **chitin** (a nitrogen-containing polysaccharide, distinct from plant cellulose). A mass of branching hyphae forms the **mycelium**, which maximizes surface area for absorption. In most fungi, hyphae are divided by cross-walls called **septa** (septate hyphae); in some phyla (Zygomycota), hyphae lack septa and are **coenocytic** (multinucleate).

#### Major Fungal Phyla

- **Zygomycota** (zygote fungi) — coenocytic hyphae; reproduce sexually via **zygospores** formed when two compatible hyphae fuse. Example: *Rhizopus stolonifer* (black bread mold).
- **Ascomycota** (sac fungi) — largest phylum; produce sexual spores (**ascospores**) inside a sac-like **ascus**. Includes yeasts (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*), morels, truffles, and *Penicillium*. Asexual spores called **conidia** are common.
- **Basidiomycota** (club fungi) — produce **basidiospores** on club-shaped **basidia**. Includes mushrooms, puffballs, rusts, and smuts — many of which are plant pathogens (wheat rust) or edible species.
- **Chytridiomycota** (chytrids) — most primitive; only fungi with **flagellated spores** (zoospores); mostly aquatic; *Batrachochytrium dendrobatidis* causes chytridiomycosis, decimating amphibian populations.
- **Glomeromycota** — obligate symbionts forming **arbuscular mycorrhizae** with plant roots; asexual reproduction only.

#### Fungi as Decomposers

Fungi are primary **saprotrophs** in terrestrial ecosystems, secreting cellulases, ligninases, and proteases to break down **dead organic matter** — especially lignin and cellulose in wood, which few other organisms can degrade. This decomposition recycles carbon and minerals, making nutrients available to plants. **White-rot fungi** (basidiomycetes) are among the few organisms that can fully degrade **lignin**, the most recalcitrant plant polymer.

#### Fungi as Symbionts

Fungi form two key mutualistic associations:
- **Mycorrhizae** — fungal hyphae colonize plant roots, vastly expanding the root's absorptive surface for water and phosphorus; the plant provides photosynthate. Over 90% of land plants have mycorrhizal partners.
- **Lichens** — a mutualism between a fungus (usually an ascomycete) and a photosynthetic partner (green alga or cyanobacterium); the fungus provides structure and water retention while the alga/cyanobacterium supplies carbon via photosynthesis. Lichens are pioneer species on bare rock.
