#### What Are Protists?

**Protists** are a paraphyletic assemblage of eukaryotes that are not plants, animals, or fungi. They are almost exclusively unicellular (though some form colonies or are multicellular), possess a **membrane-bound nucleus**, and exhibit extreme diversity in form, nutrition, and locomotion. Modern systematics places them across several supergroups (Excavata, SAR, Archaeplastida, Amoebozoa, Opisthokonta), but competition courses often organize them by nutrition mode and movement type.

#### Protozoa: Heterotrophic Protists

**Protozoa** are animal-like protists that obtain nutrition by ingesting other organisms or organic material (**phagocytosis**). They are classified largely by locomotion:
- **Amoeba** (Amoebozoa) — move via **pseudopodia** (cytoplasmic extensions); *Entamoeba histolytica* causes amoebic dysentery.
- **Flagellates** (e.g., *Giardia*, *Trypanosoma*) — use one or more **flagella**; many are parasitic.
- **Ciliates** (e.g., *Paramecium*) — covered with **cilia** for movement and feeding; possess a macronucleus and micronucleus.
- **Apicomplexans** (e.g., *Plasmodium*) — nonmotile, obligate intracellular parasites with an **apical complex** for host-cell invasion; *Plasmodium* causes malaria.

#### Algae: Photosynthetic Protists

**Algae** are plant-like protists that perform **photosynthesis** using chlorophyll and accessory pigments. Key groups include:
- **Green algae** (Chlorophyta) — closest relatives of land plants; store **starch**, have chlorophylls a and b.
- **Diatoms** (Bacillariophyta) — silica **frustules** (glass-like cell walls); major component of marine phytoplankton and oxygen production.
- **Dinoflagellates** — two flagella; some cause **red tides** (harmful algal blooms) via toxin production.
- **Brown algae** (Phaeophyta) — contain **fucoxanthin**; include giant kelp forming marine forests.
- **Red algae** (Rhodophyta) — deepest-dwelling algae due to **phycoerythrin** absorbing blue/green light.

#### Classification by Nutrition and Movement

Protists are often grouped by their **trophic strategy**: **photoautotrophs** (algae), **heterotrophs** (protozoa), or **mixotrophs** (e.g., *Euglena*, which photosynthesize in light but ingest food in the dark). Movement structures — **pseudopodia**, **flagella**, **cilia**, or none — serve as classical classification markers and reflect distinct cytoskeletal adaptations.

#### Ecological Roles of Protists

Protists are ecologically indispensable. Photosynthetic protists (especially diatoms and dinoflagellates) generate roughly **half of Earth's atmospheric oxygen** and form the base of aquatic food webs. **Decomposer-like** water molds (Oomycetes, e.g., *Phytophthora*) break down organic matter, though some are devastating plant pathogens (Irish Potato Famine). Protozoa regulate bacterial populations and cycle nutrients in soil and aquatic ecosystems.

2. Why can *Euglena* be considered a mixotroph, and why does this challenge a strict autotroph/heterotroph classification of protists?
