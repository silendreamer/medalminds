#### The Fluid Mosaic Model (1972)
Singer and Nicolson proposed the fluid mosaic model: the plasma membrane is a fluid phospholipid bilayer in which proteins float and move laterally ("mosaic" of proteins in a "fluid" lipid sea). The bilayer is ~7–8 nm thick. Phospholipids diffuse laterally (10⁷ times/second) but rarely flip-flop between leaflets (transverse diffusion, <once per month without enzymes). FRAP (fluorescence recovery after photobleaching) demonstrates membrane fluidity: bleach a patch of fluorescent lipids → fluorescence recovers as unbleached lipids diffuse in.

#### Membrane Proteins
Integral (transmembrane) proteins span the bilayer — they contain hydrophobic α-helical segments that interact with the lipid interior. Examples: ion channels, transporters, receptor tyrosine kinases, G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs). Peripheral proteins are associated with membrane surfaces via electrostatic or hydrogen bond interactions, easily removed by high salt or pH changes. Lipid-anchored proteins are covalently attached to lipid molecules embedded in the bilayer. The protein-to-lipid ratio varies by membrane: the mitochondrial inner membrane is ~76% protein (very active) while myelin sheaths are ~18% protein (insulating, metabolically quiet).

#### Membrane Fluidity Factors
Fluidity depends on: (1) Temperature — higher temperature = more fluid; (2) Fatty acid saturation — unsaturated fatty acids (kinked tails, don't pack tightly) increase fluidity; (3) Cholesterol — at high temperatures, cholesterol restricts phospholipid movement (reduces fluidity); at low temperatures, it prevents tight packing (prevents solidification). This dual role makes cholesterol a "fluidity buffer." Poikilotherms (cold-blooded animals) adjust their membrane fatty acid composition seasonally — increasing unsaturated fats in winter to maintain membrane fluidity in the cold.

#### Lipid Rafts and Membrane Asymmetry
Lipid rafts are cholesterol and sphingolipid-enriched microdomains that are more ordered (less fluid) than surrounding membrane. They serve as platforms for signaling complexes, organizing signaling molecules spatially. Membrane asymmetry: the two leaflets have different lipid compositions. Phosphatidylserine (negative charge) is normally restricted to the inner leaflet — when it flips to the outer leaflet, it signals apoptosis and triggers platelet aggregation. Glycolipids and glycoproteins face only the extracellular space, contributing to the glycocalyx — a carbohydrate-rich layer involved in cell recognition and protection.
