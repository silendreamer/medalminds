#### Cell Theory
Cell theory has three tenets: (1) all living organisms are composed of cells; (2) the cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life; (3) all cells arise from pre-existing cells (Virchow's contribution, disproving spontaneous generation for cells). Extensions: all cells share similar chemical composition; all metabolic reactions occur within cells; genetic information is passed from cell to cell during division.

#### Prokaryotes
Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus — DNA is in the nucleoid region, an area of the cytoplasm not bounded by membrane. They also lack membrane-bound organelles. Features: plasma membrane, cell wall (peptidoglycan in bacteria, pseudopeptidoglycan or other materials in archaea), ribosomes (70S = 30S + 50S subunits), flagella (different structure from eukaryotic), pili (for attachment and conjugation), plasmids (small circular extra-chromosomal DNA). Average size: 1–10 µm. Two domains: Bacteria and Archaea (archaea have distinct membrane lipids with ether linkages instead of ester linkages, and some thrive in extreme environments — extremophiles).

#### Eukaryotes
Eukaryotic cells contain a membrane-bound nucleus and extensive membrane systems. Average size: 10–100 µm. Key organelles: nucleus (houses DNA, site of transcription); endoplasmic reticulum — rough (studded with ribosomes, synthesizes membrane and secreted proteins) and smooth (lipid synthesis, drug detoxification, Ca²⁺ storage); Golgi apparatus (sorts, modifies, and packages proteins); mitochondria (ATP synthesis via oxidative phosphorylation); lysosomes (hydrolytic enzyme-filled vesicles for intracellular digestion); vacuoles (large storage in plants — central vacuole maintains turgor pressure); chloroplasts (photosynthesis in plant cells); cytoskeleton (microtubules, actin filaments, intermediate filaments).

#### Endosymbiotic Theory
Lynn Margulis proposed that mitochondria and chloroplasts originated as free-living prokaryotes engulfed by a host cell in a symbiotic relationship. Evidence: (1) mitochondria and chloroplasts are bounded by two membranes (inner = prokaryotic membrane; outer = phagocytic vesicle membrane); (2) they contain their own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes (like prokaryotes, not 80S like eukaryotes); (3) they reproduce by binary fission; (4) their genome sequences are most similar to specific bacterial groups (mitochondria to α-proteobacteria; chloroplasts to cyanobacteria); (5) they can be inhibited by antibiotics that target prokaryotic ribosomes (like chloramphenicol).

#### Review Questions

2. Compare the ribosomes of prokaryotes and eukaryotes in terms of size (Svedberg units) and location.
3. Why do plant cells contain both mitochondria and chloroplasts, while animal cells contain only mitochondria?
