#### Must-Know Facts
1. Fluid mosaic model: Singer and Nicolson (1972) — membrane is a fluid phospholipid bilayer with mobile proteins.
2. Amphipathic phospholipids spontaneously form bilayers — hydrophilic heads out, hydrophobic tails in.
3. Simple diffusion: nonpolar/small molecules; no energy, no protein.
4. Facilitated diffusion: channels (fast, ion-gated) and carriers (slower, specific); no ATP.
5. Active transport: against gradient, requires ATP; Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase: 3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in per ATP.
6. Osmosis: water moves from low solute to high solute (from high Ψ to low Ψ).
7. Tonicity: isotonic = no change; hypotonic → cell swells; hypertonic → cell shrinks.
8. Crenation = animal cell shrinkage in hypertonic solution; plasmolysis = plant cell membrane pulls from wall.
9. Turgor pressure = pressure potential in plants; drives cell wall support.
10. Aquaporins = water channels; AQP2 in kidney collecting duct regulated by ADH.
11. SGLT1/2 = Na⁺-glucose cotransporters (secondary active transport).
12. Endocytosis types: phagocytosis (large particles), pinocytosis (fluid), receptor-mediated (specific).
13. Clathrin = coat protein for receptor-mediated endocytosis.
14. Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase is electrogenic — net 1 positive charge exported per cycle.
15. FRAP = Fluorescence Recovery After Photobleaching — demonstrates membrane fluidity.

#### Review Questions
1. Classify each: (a) O₂ moving from blood to tissue cells, (b) glucose uptake in the intestine, (c) Na⁺ expelled from a nerve cell during action potential repolarization.
2. Why does the Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase generate a membrane potential? What is the stoichiometry?
3. A cell is placed in a 10% NaCl solution. Its cytoplasm contains 2% NaCl equivalent solutes. Predict what will happen to cell volume and explain.
