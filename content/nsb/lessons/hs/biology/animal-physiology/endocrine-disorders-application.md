#### Case Studies
**Hyperthyroidism (Graves' disease)**: autoimmune antibodies mimic TSH → continuous thyroid stimulation → excess T3/T4 → high metabolic rate, weight loss, heat intolerance, rapid heart rate, exophthalmos. TSH is suppressed by negative feedback. Treatment: radioactive iodine, thyroid surgery, anti-thyroid drugs.

**Cushing's syndrome**: excess cortisol (from pituitary adenoma producing excess ACTH, or adrenal tumor, or exogenous corticosteroids) → central obesity ("moon face," "buffalo hump"), hyperglycemia, immunosuppression, osteoporosis, hypertension, easy bruising. ACTH levels differ by cause (high in pituitary tumor, low in adrenal tumor).

**Acromegaly**: excess growth hormone in adults (after growth plates fuse) from pituitary adenoma → enlarged hands, feet, jaw, internal organs; arthritis; cardiomegaly. In children before growth plate fusion, excess GH causes gigantism.

**Diabetes insipidus**: insufficient ADH (central DI: hypothalamic/pituitary damage) or kidney unresponsive to ADH (nephrogenic DI) → excretion of large amounts of dilute urine → dehydration → extreme thirst. Distinguished from diabetes mellitus: no glucose in urine.

#### Review Questions
1. A patient has elevated blood glucose, weight gain around the abdomen, thinning skin, and frequent infections. Lab results show extremely high cortisol and low ACTH. What is the most likely diagnosis and where is the tumor?
2. Why do patients on long-term corticosteroid therapy (e.g., for asthma) have suppressed adrenal function when they stop taking the drug suddenly?
3. Compare and contrast diabetes insipidus and diabetes mellitus in terms of symptoms, underlying mechanisms, and what is found in the urine.
