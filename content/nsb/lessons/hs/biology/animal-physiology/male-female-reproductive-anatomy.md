#### Male Reproductive System
**Testes**: in scrotum (2–3°C cooler than body temperature — required for spermatogenesis); divided into seminiferous tubules (site of spermatogenesis) and interstitial cells (Leydig cells → testosterone). **Spermatogenesis**: spermatogonia (2n) → primary spermatocytes → meiosis I → secondary spermatocytes → meiosis II → spermatids → spermatozoa. Continuous from puberty throughout life; ~300 million sperm/day. Sperm: head (nucleus + acrosome with hydrolytic enzymes), midpiece (mitochondria for ATP), tail (flagellum). Accessory glands: seminal vesicles (fructose, prostaglandins), prostate gland (alkaline secretion neutralizes acidic vagina), bulbourethral glands.

#### Female Reproductive System
**Ovaries**: contain ~700,000 primary oocytes (arrested in prophase I of meiosis) at birth; ~400 will ovulate over a lifetime. **Oogenesis**: asymmetric — one large ovum + 2–3 small polar bodies (discard excess cytoplasm). Primary oocyte completes meiosis I at ovulation → secondary oocyte (arrested in metaphase II) → meiosis II completed only if fertilized. **Ovarian follicle**: granulosa cells surround oocyte, secrete estrogen; corpus luteum (post-ovulation) secretes progesterone.

#### Comparison of Gametogenesis
| Feature | Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
|---|---|---|
| Output per meiosis | 4 functional sperm | 1 ovum + 2–3 polar bodies |
| Timing | Continuous from puberty | Arrested in prophase I until ovulation |
| Location | Seminiferous tubules | Ovarian follicle |
| Size of gamete | Small, motile | Large, non-motile |

#### Review Questions
1. Why are testes located in the scrotum outside the body cavity, and what medical complication can arise if a testicle fails to descend?
2. At what stage of meiosis is the secondary oocyte arrested, and what event triggers completion of meiosis II?
3. Why does oogenesis produce only one functional egg cell from each primary oocyte, and what is the fate of the other cells?
