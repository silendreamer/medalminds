#### What Science Actually Does
Science is a systematic process for explaining natural phenomena through evidence. It begins with observation — noticing something in the natural world that demands explanation. A good scientific question is one that can be answered through empirical investigation. From a question, a scientist formulates a hypothesis: a specific, testable, falsifiable prediction about what will happen and why. The hypothesis must be falsifiable — there must be some possible result that could prove it wrong. "All living things are made of cells" is scientific; "nature is beautiful" is not.

#### Designing a Controlled Experiment
A controlled experiment tests one variable at a time. The independent variable is what the experimenter changes. The dependent variable is what is measured in response. All other conditions — the controlled variables — are held constant between the experimental group and the control group. The control group receives no treatment (or a baseline treatment like a placebo) and serves as the comparison standard. Without a control, it is impossible to attribute observed changes to the independent variable.

#### Sample Size and Replication
A single trial is never enough. Random variation in biological systems means any one result could be an outlier. Increasing sample size reduces the effect of individual variation and makes results more reliable. Replication — repeating the experiment independently — tests whether results are reproducible, a cornerstone of scientific validity. In biology, this is especially important because organisms vary: a drug that works in ten mice may fail in ten others for genetic reasons.

#### From Data to Conclusion
After collecting quantitative data, scientists apply statistical analysis to determine whether differences between groups are real or due to chance. A p-value below 0.05 conventionally means results are statistically significant — less than 5% probability the result occurred by chance. If data support the hypothesis, the experiment is published and other labs attempt replication. If data contradict the hypothesis, the hypothesis is revised or discarded. Science is self-correcting: conclusions are always provisional, subject to revision with new evidence.

#### The Difference Between a Theory and a Hypothesis
In everyday speech, "theory" means a guess. In science, a theory is a well-substantiated explanation supported by extensive evidence, predictive power, and broad consensus — like cell theory, germ theory, or evolution by natural selection. A hypothesis is a single testable prediction; a theory integrates many confirmed hypotheses into a unifying framework. Understanding this distinction is critical for interpreting scientific literature and avoiding a common misconception in Science Bowl questions.
