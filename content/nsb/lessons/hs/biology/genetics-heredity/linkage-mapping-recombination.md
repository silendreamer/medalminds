#### Recombination Frequency as Distance
Two genes on the same chromosome are linked — they tend to be inherited together. But crossing over can separate them. The recombination frequency (RF) is the percentage of offspring that show a recombinant phenotype (a new combination of alleles not seen in either parent). RF is proportional to the distance between genes: RF = (number of recombinants / total offspring) × 100%.

#### Mapping Units (centiMorgans)
1 map unit (m.u.) = 1 centiMorgan (cM) = 1% recombination frequency. If genes A and B show 5% RF, they are 5 m.u. apart. This unit is based on the assumption that crossing over occurs randomly and that one crossover event between two loci is observable. For genes far apart on the same chromosome, interference (one crossover inhibits nearby crossovers) can reduce observed RF.

#### Three-Point Cross Example
To map three genes (A, B, C) on a chromosome, researchers perform a three-point cross: cross a triple heterozygote (AaBbCc) with a homozygous recessive (aabbcc). Examine offspring phenotypes. Parental types appear most frequently (no crossing over between any pair). Single crossovers between A–B are next most frequent, then single crossovers between B–C. Double crossovers (between both pairs) are rarest. Use the data to calculate RF between each pair and determine gene order.

#### Worked Example: Fruit Fly Mapping
In *Drosophila*, researchers cross white-eyed, vestigial-winged (wvg) females (heterozygous) with white, wild-type males (wvg+). They observe offspring and count phenotype frequencies: If wild-type eyes/vestigial wings appears 40 times and white eyes/wild-type wings 38 times (recombinant classes), with 100 total offspring, RF = (40+38)/100 = 78%. The genes are 78 m.u. apart — far enough that crossing over occurs almost half the time, so they assort nearly independently.

#### Review Questions

2. In a three-point cross, which classes of offspring appear most frequently, and why?
3. Why is interference important when mapping genes far apart on the same chromosome?
