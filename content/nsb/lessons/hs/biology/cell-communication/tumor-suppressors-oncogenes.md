#### Proto-Oncogenes and Oncogenes
**Proto-oncogenes** are normal genes encoding proteins that promote growth (growth factors, receptors, signal transducers, transcription factors). A gain-of-function mutation converting a proto-oncogene into an **oncogene** causes constitutive activity — the cell grows without a signal. Examples:
- **RAS**: point mutation → locked in GTP-bound state
- **HER2**: amplification → too many growth factor receptors
- **BCR-ABL**: chromosomal translocation → constitutively active tyrosine kinase (cause of CML; treated by imatinib/Gleevec)
- **MYC**: amplification → constitutive transcription of growth genes

Only **one mutation** in one allele is needed (dominant).

#### Tumor Suppressors
**Tumor suppressors** encode proteins that inhibit proliferation or promote apoptosis. Loss-of-function mutations remove the brake. Classic examples:
- **Rb**: regulates G1/S checkpoint (mutated in retinoblastoma, osteosarcoma)
- **p53**: "guardian of the genome" — responds to DNA damage by halting the cell cycle or triggering apoptosis (mutated in >50% of all cancers)
- **PTEN**: degrades PIP₃, limiting AKT signaling
- **BRCA1/2**: DNA repair; mutations dramatically increase breast/ovarian cancer risk

**Two-hit hypothesis (Knudson):** Both alleles of a tumor suppressor must be inactivated for loss of function. Inherited cancer syndromes (familial retinoblastoma) carry one germline mutation — only one somatic "hit" is needed to lose the second copy.

#### p53: The Guardian of the Genome
p53 is a transcription factor activated by DNA damage (via ATM/ATR kinases), oncogene activation, and hypoxia. Active p53 induces:
1. **p21** → CDK inhibitor → halts cell cycle at G1/S
2. **GADD45** → promotes DNA repair
3. **BAX/PUMA** → promotes apoptosis if damage is irreparable

MDM2 normally ubiquitinates p53 for degradation, keeping levels low. DNA damage activates ATM, which phosphorylates p53 to block MDM2 binding, stabilizing p53.
