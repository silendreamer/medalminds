#### PI3K Activation by RTKs
Phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K) is recruited to activated RTKs (like the insulin receptor or EGF receptor) via SH2-domain interactions with phosphotyrosine residues. Once activated, PI3K phosphorylates PIP₂ in the membrane at the 3-position, producing **PIP₃**. PIP₃ serves as a docking site, recruiting proteins with PH (pleckstrin homology) domains to the membrane.

#### AKT: The Central Survival Kinase
AKT (also called PKB) is recruited to the membrane by PIP₃ and activated by phosphorylation. Activated AKT phosphorylates dozens of downstream targets:
- **BAD** → inhibits apoptosis (cell survival)
- **GSK3** → stimulates glycogen synthesis
- **FOXO transcription factors** → inhibits them (preventing pro-death gene expression)
- **mTORC1** → promotes cell growth and protein synthesis

The net effect: AKT is a pro-survival, pro-growth kinase that integrates growth factor and nutrient signals.

#### mTOR: Sensing Nutrients and Growth
mTOR (mechanistic target of rapamycin) is a serine/threonine kinase that integrates signals about nutrient availability, energy status, and growth factors. When activated by AKT (and by amino acids via the Ragulator complex), mTOR promotes ribosome biogenesis, protein synthesis, and cell growth. **Rapamycin** (an antibiotic from Easter Island bacteria) inhibits mTOR and is used as an immunosuppressant in organ transplant patients — it prevents T-cell proliferation.

#### PTEN: The Tumor Suppressor That Reverses PI3K
**PTEN** is a phosphatase that degrades PIP₃ back to PIP₂, opposing PI3K. PTEN is one of the most commonly mutated tumor suppressors in cancer — when PTEN is lost, PIP₃ accumulates, AKT is constitutively active, and cells survive and proliferate without growth signals. PTEN mutations occur in glioblastoma, prostate cancer, and endometrial cancer.
