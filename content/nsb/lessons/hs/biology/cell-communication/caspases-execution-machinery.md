#### Caspase Structure and Activation
Caspases (cysteine-aspartate proteases) cleave after aspartate residues and are produced as inactive **zymogens** (procaspases). Initiator caspases (caspase-8, -9, -10) are activated by dimerization in large signaling complexes (DISC for extrinsic, apoptosome for intrinsic). Once active, initiator caspases cleave and activate executioner caspases (caspase-3, -6, -7) by removing their prodomain.

#### Key Substrates of Executioner Caspases
The power of caspases is their broad substrate range:
- **ICAD** → releases DNase (CAD) → DNA laddering at nucleosomal intervals (a hallmark of apoptosis)
- **Lamins** → nuclear envelope disintegration and chromatin condensation
- **PARP** → DNA repair inhibited (cells stop trying to survive)
- **Cytoskeletal proteins** (gelsolin, fodrin) → cell rounding and blebbing
- **Caspase-activated scramblase** → flips phosphatidylserine to outer leaflet → "eat me" signal for macrophages

#### IAP Proteins: Caspase Inhibitors
**Inhibitor of Apoptosis Proteins (IAPs)** (including XIAP, survivin) directly bind and inhibit caspase-3, -7, and -9. Cancer cells frequently overexpress IAPs to escape apoptosis. Smac/DIABLO is a mitochondrial protein released alongside cytochrome c that neutralizes IAPs, committing the cell to death. IAP antagonist drugs (Smac mimetics) are being developed to restore apoptosis sensitivity in cancer.

#### Phosphatidylserine as an "Eat Me" Signal
One of the cleverest features of apoptosis is the "eat me" signal. Healthy cells maintain phosphatidylserine (PS) exclusively in the inner leaflet of the plasma membrane. Caspase-activated scramblase flips PS to the outer leaflet, where it is recognized by macrophage receptors (TIM4, BAI1, stabilin-2). This triggers phagocytosis of the apoptotic body without releasing inflammatory contents — a "clean" death that doesn't trigger inflammation (unlike necrosis).
