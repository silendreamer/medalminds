#### RTK Structure and Activation
Receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs) are single-pass transmembrane proteins with an extracellular ligand-binding domain and an intracellular kinase domain. Unlike GPCRs, RTKs are monomers in the resting state. When a growth factor (like EGF or insulin) binds, it causes **dimerization** — two RTK monomers come together. Dimerization allows the kinase domains to **transphosphorylate** each other on tyrosine residues, activating the receptor.

#### Downstream Signaling: The RAS-MAP Kinase Pathway
Phosphorylated tyrosines on activated RTKs serve as docking sites for adaptor proteins like GRB2, which recruits SOS. SOS activates **RAS** (a small GTP-binding protein) by promoting GTP binding. Active RAS triggers a **MAP kinase cascade**: RAS → RAF → MEK → ERK. ERK (a MAP kinase) phosphorylates transcription factors in the nucleus, turning on genes for cell growth and proliferation.

#### RTKs vs. GPCRs: A Comparison
While GPCRs use cAMP as a second messenger and act transiently, RTKs use phosphotyrosine as a docking signal and activate longer-lasting transcriptional changes. GPCRs are ideal for rapid physiological responses (heart rate, smooth muscle tone); RTKs drive sustained decisions about growth and differentiation.

#### Oncogenic RAS Mutations
RAS is mutated in ~30% of all human cancers. A point mutation that destroys RAS's GTPase activity locks RAS in the GTP-bound (active) state permanently — the cell proliferates without needing a growth signal. This is why RAS is one of the most studied oncogenes. Understanding the RAS pathway led to the development of MEK and RAF inhibitors used in melanoma treatment.

#### Insulin Receptor: An RTK in Metabolism
The insulin receptor is an RTK that triggers glucose uptake. When insulin binds, the receptor autophosphorylates and activates PI3K → AKT signaling, which drives GLUT4 glucose transporters to the cell surface. In type 2 diabetes, downstream signaling is impaired despite normal insulin binding — this "insulin resistance" at the signaling level is why cells can't import glucose properly.
