#### Phospholipase C and PIP₂ Cleavage
When certain GPCRs (coupled to Gq proteins) are activated, Gα activates **phospholipase C-β (PLCβ)**. PLCβ cleaves the membrane phospholipid **PIP₂ (phosphatidylinositol 4,5-bisphosphate)** into two second messengers: **IP₃ (inositol trisphosphate)** and **DAG (diacylglycerol)**. This single cleavage event launches two parallel signaling branches simultaneously.

#### IP₃ Branch: Calcium Release
IP₃ is water-soluble and diffuses to the **endoplasmic reticulum (ER)**, where it binds IP₃ receptors — calcium channels in the ER membrane. This opens the channels and releases Ca²⁺ from ER stores into the cytoplasm, raising cytoplasmic [Ca²⁺] 10–100 fold. Calcium ions then bind **calmodulin**, a calcium-sensing protein that activates calmodulin-dependent kinases (CaM kinases), triggering responses from muscle contraction to neurotransmitter release.

#### DAG Branch: Protein Kinase C
DAG remains in the membrane and, together with calcium, activates **protein kinase C (PKC)**. PKC phosphorylates serine/threonine residues on diverse target proteins, influencing gene expression, cytoskeletal rearrangement, and secretion. Phorbol esters (tumor-promoting compounds from plants) mimic DAG, constitutively activating PKC — this is how they promote cancer.

#### Calcium as a Versatile Second Messenger
Calcium is one of the most versatile intracellular messengers. Beyond PKC and CaM kinases, Ca²⁺ directly regulates: muscle troponin (triggering contraction), synaptotagmin (triggering vesicle fusion for neurotransmitter release), calcineurin (regulating T-cell activation, target of the immunosuppressant cyclosporine), and fertilization responses in eggs. Cells invest enormous energy in pumps (SERCA, plasma membrane Ca²⁺-ATPase) to maintain low cytoplasmic [Ca²⁺] so signals remain precise.
