#### Activation Energy and the Energy Diagram

**Activation energy (Ea)** is the minimum energy that colliding molecules must possess for a reaction to occur. On a potential energy diagram, Ea is the height of the energy barrier between reactants and the **transition state** (activated complex) — the highest-energy, unstable arrangement of atoms at the peak of the barrier. The overall ΔH of the reaction equals the difference between the energy of reactants and products, which is independent of Ea.

#### The Arrhenius Equation

The temperature dependence of the rate constant k is captured by the **Arrhenius equation**: k = A·e^(−Ea/RT), where A is the **pre-exponential (frequency) factor** (related to collision frequency and steric orientation), R is the gas constant (8.314 J/mol·K), and T is the absolute temperature in Kelvin. Taking the natural log gives the linear form: ln k = ln A − Ea/RT. Plotting ln k vs. 1/T yields a straight line with slope = −Ea/R, allowing Ea to be calculated from experimental k values at two temperatures: ln(k₂/k₁) = −(Ea/R)(1/T₂ − 1/T₁).

#### How Catalysts Lower Activation Energy

A **catalyst** increases the reaction rate by providing an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. Crucially, catalysts are **not consumed** in the overall reaction — they participate in intermediate steps and are regenerated. Because Ea is lower, a much larger fraction of collisions (given by the Boltzmann factor e^(−Ea/RT)) have sufficient energy to react, dramatically increasing k. Catalysts do not change ΔH or the equilibrium position; they only accelerate the approach to equilibrium.

#### Homogeneous vs. Heterogeneous Catalysis

- **Homogeneous catalysts** are in the same phase as the reactants (e.g., H⁺ in aqueous acid-catalyzed ester hydrolysis). They often form transient intermediates with substrates.
- **Heterogeneous catalysts** are in a different phase (e.g., solid platinum in the catalytic converter of a car, or iron in the Haber–Bosch process). Reactants **adsorb** onto the catalyst surface, weakening bonds and orienting molecules favorably.
- **Enzymes** are biological catalysts (proteins) that use an **active site** to achieve extraordinary specificity and enormous rate enhancements (factors of 10⁶–10¹²).
