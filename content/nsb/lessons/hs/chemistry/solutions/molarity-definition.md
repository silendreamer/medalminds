#### Molarity Formula
Molarity (M) = moles of solute / liters of solution. Example: If 0.5 mol of NaCl is dissolved to make 1.0 L of total solution, molarity = 0.5 M. A 1.0 M solution contains 1 mole of solute dissolved in enough solvent to make 1 liter total volume.

#### Preparing a Molarity Solution
To prepare a 0.50 M solution of CaCl₂: Calculate mass needed: 0.50 mol × 110.98 g/mol = 55.5 g. Dissolve 55.5 g of CaCl₂ in a beaker with some water. Transfer to a 1 L volumetric flask and rinse the beaker. Add water to bring the level to the 1 L mark on the flask. Stopper and mix thoroughly.

#### Interpreting Molarity
A 2 M solution is twice as concentrated as 1 M. It contains twice as many moles of solute per liter. The label "0.1 M HCl" means 0.1 mol of HCl per 1 L of solution—or equivalently, 0.05 mol per 500 mL.

#### Advantage Over Other Units
Molarity is used in chemistry because it directly relates to mole ratios in balanced equations. If a reaction requires a 2:1 mole ratio of reactants, and both are in solution, you can use molarity to find the volume ratio: V₁M₁ = n₁ moles and V₂M₂ = n₂ moles. Setting n₁:n₂ = 2:1 solves for volume ratio.

#### Review Questions
1. What is the molarity of a solution containing 2.0 mol NaCl in 500 mL of solution?
2. How many moles of H₂SO₄ are in 250 mL of 0.40 M H₂SO₄?
3. To prepare 100 mL of 1.5 M KCl, how many grams of KCl do you need?
