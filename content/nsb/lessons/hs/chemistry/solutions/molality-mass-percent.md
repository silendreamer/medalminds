#### Molality Definition
Molality (m) = moles of solute / kilograms of solvent. Example: 0.5 mol of NaCl dissolved in 1.0 kg of water gives 0.5 m NaCl. Unlike molarity (which depends on solution volume), molality depends only on mass—it doesn't change with temperature.

#### Mass Percent Definition
Mass percent = (mass of solute / mass of solution) × 100%. For 10 g of NaCl dissolved in 90 g of water (100 g total solution), mass percent = (10 / 100) × 100% = 10%. A 10% NaCl solution contains 10 g NaCl per 100 g solution.

#### Why Molality Matters
Molality is used for colligative properties (properties that depend on particle count, not identity)—freezing point depression, boiling point elevation, osmotic pressure. These properties are proportional to molality because they depend on the number of solute particles relative to solvent mass, not solvent volume.

#### Worked Example: Converting Between Units
A 0.50 M NaCl solution has a density of 1.02 g/mL. What is its molality? In 1 L (1000 mL) of solution: moles of NaCl = 0.50 mol. Mass of solution = 1000 mL × 1.02 g/mL = 1020 g. Mass of NaCl = 0.50 mol × 58.5 g/mol = 29.25 g. Mass of water = 1020 - 29.25 = 990.75 g = 0.99075 kg. Molality = 0.50 mol / 0.99075 kg = 0.505 m.

#### Review Questions
1. What is the molality of a solution containing 3.0 mol of ethanol (C₂H₅OH) dissolved in 5.0 kg of water?
2. Calculate the mass percent of a 12 g salt in 88 g water.
3. A 0.25 M solution has a density of 1.01 g/mL. Is it more or less concentrated in molality than molarity? (Hint: compare the two values.)
