#### Bowl-Style Toss-Up #1
"A 0.50 M solution of NaCl is diluted by adding water to double the volume. What is the new molarity?" (Answer: 0.25 M.)

#### Bowl-Style Toss-Up #2
"In a titration, 20 mL of unknown HCl requires 25 mL of 0.40 M NaOH. What is the molarity of the HCl?" (Answer: 0.50 M.)

#### Common Traps
- Forgetting that M₁V₁ = M₂V₂ applies to initial/final conditions, not mixing two solutions
- Confusing molarity with molality
- Making errors in stoichiometry during titration (forgetting 2:1 ratio for diprotic acids)
- Incorrect molar mass lookups

#### Speed Strategies
- Dilution always reduces molarity (if volume increases, M decreases)
- In titration, moles of titrant at equivalence point = moles of analyte (from stoichiometry)
- Cross-multiply for dilution: M₁V₁ = M₂V₂

#### Review Questions
1. For 10 points: How many mL of 6.0 M H₂SO₄ are needed to prepare 500 mL of 0.50 M H₂SO₄?
2. For 15 points: A 30 mL sample of unknown acid requires 24 mL of 0.15 M KOH. If the acid is diprotic, what is its molarity?
3. Bonus (20 points): Serial dilution from 2.0 M to 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ M. Minimum number of 1:100 dilution steps needed?
