#### Toss-Up Patterns
**Pattern 1 — PV = nRT directly:**
"For 10 points — what is the volume in liters occupied by 1.00 mole of ideal gas at standard temperature and pressure?"
→ **22.4 liters**

**Pattern 2 — Identify the law:**
"For 10 points — this law states that the total pressure of a mixture of gases equals the sum of the partial pressures of each gas."
→ **Dalton's Law** (of Partial Pressures)

**Pattern 3 — Name the law for inverse P-V:**
"For 10 points — what law states that, at constant temperature, the pressure of a gas is inversely proportional to its volume?"
→ **Boyle's Law**

**Pattern 4 — Ideal gas constant:**
"For 10 points — in the ideal gas equation PV = nRT, what is the numerical value and units of R in liter-atmospheres per mole-Kelvin?"
→ **0.08206 L·atm/mol·K**

#### Speed Strategies
- Always check: is T in Kelvin? Is R consistent with pressure units?
- For ratios of two states, use combined law (cleaner algebra than plugging into PV=nRT twice)
- Mole fraction × total pressure = partial pressure (one step, no KT calculation needed)
- STP = 0°C, 1 atm, 22.4 L/mol — memorize cold

#### Review Questions
1. [Bowl-style] For 10 points — what value, in liters, does 2.00 moles of ideal gas occupy at 27°C and 2.00 atm?
2. [Bowl-style] For 10 points — what law relates the rate of effusion of a gas to the square root of its molar mass?
3. [Bowl-style] For 10 points — at constant temperature, what mathematical relationship exists between pressure and volume of a gas?
