#### The Five Postulates
1. **Gases consist of tiny particles** (atoms or molecules) with negligible volume compared to the total volume occupied.
2. **No intermolecular forces** — gas particles neither attract nor repel each other between collisions.
3. **Particles are in constant, random motion** in straight lines until they collide.
4. **Collisions are perfectly elastic** — kinetic energy is conserved; no energy lost to friction.
5. **Average kinetic energy is proportional to absolute temperature**: KE_avg = (3/2)kT, where k is Boltzmann's constant and T is in Kelvin.

#### What These Postulates Explain
- **Pressure:** Results from particles colliding with container walls. More collisions or harder collisions = higher pressure.
- **Temperature:** A macroscopic measure of average molecular kinetic energy. Raise temperature → particles move faster → more forceful collisions.
- **Volume:** Gases expand to fill any container because particles move randomly and freely.

#### Ideal vs. Real Gases
KMT describes an **ideal gas** — a theoretical model. Real gases deviate at high pressure (volume of particles matters) and low temperature (intermolecular attractions matter). At room temperature and atmospheric pressure, most gases behave nearly ideally.

#### Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution
Not all gas particles move at the same speed. The **Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution** shows a bell-shaped curve of speeds. The peak is the most probable speed; the average speed is slightly higher; the root-mean-square (rms) speed is highest of the three. Increasing temperature shifts the entire curve to higher speeds and broadens it.

#### Review Questions
1. State the postulate of KMT that explains why gas pressure increases when temperature increases.
2. Why do real gases deviate from ideal behavior at high pressure?
3. Two gases at the same temperature have the same average kinetic energy. If gas A has a higher molar mass than gas B, which moves faster on average?
