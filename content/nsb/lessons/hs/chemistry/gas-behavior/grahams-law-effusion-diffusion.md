#### Effusion vs. Diffusion

**Effusion** is the process by which gas molecules escape through a tiny pinhole into a vacuum, while **diffusion** is the spreading of gas molecules throughout a space by random motion. Both processes depend on the average speed of gas molecules, which is determined by the **root-mean-square (rms) speed**: u_rms = sqrt(3RT/M), where R is the gas constant, T is temperature in Kelvin, and M is molar mass.

#### Graham's Law

**Graham's law of effusion** states that the rate of effusion of a gas is inversely proportional to the square root of its molar mass. For two gases compared at the same temperature and pressure:

rate1 / rate2 = sqrt(M2 / M1)

where rate1 and rate2 are the effusion rates and M1, M2 are the molar masses of gas 1 and gas 2 respectively. A lighter gas effuses faster because its molecules move at higher average speeds.

#### Applying Graham's Law

To compare gases, identify the molar masses from the periodic table or formula, then take the square root of the ratio with the heavier mass in the numerator. For example, H2 (M = 2 g/mol) effuses sqrt(32/2) = sqrt(16) = 4 times faster than O2 (M = 32 g/mol). The **time** required for a fixed amount to effuse is inversely related to rate: t1/t2 = sqrt(M1/M2).

#### Molar Mass Determination

Graham's law can be used to **determine the molar mass of an unknown gas** experimentally. If gas A has a known molar mass M_A and takes time t_A to effuse, and gas X takes time t_X, then: M_X = M_A × (t_X / t_A)^2. This technique is useful for identifying unknown gases in competition problems.

#### Review Questions
1. Nitrogen gas (N2, M = 28 g/mol) and neon (Ne, M = 20 g/mol) are placed in identical containers with pinholes. Which gas effuses faster, and by what factor?
2. A sample of H2 effuses through a membrane in 10 seconds. How long would it take the same amount of CO2 (M = 44 g/mol) to effuse under identical conditions?
3. Explain why Graham's law predicts that lighter gases diffuse more rapidly at a given temperature, connecting the result to kinetic molecular theory.
