#### The Equation
**PV = nRT**
- P = pressure (atm)
- V = volume (L)
- n = moles of gas
- R = 0.08206 L·atm/mol·K (the ideal gas constant)
- T = temperature (Kelvin, always)

When using kPa: R = 8.314 L·kPa/mol·K. When using mmHg: R = 62.36 L·mmHg/mol·K.

#### Derivation from Combined Gas Law
The Combined Gas Law (P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂) applies at constant moles. Adding that PV/T ∝ n gives PV/T = nR → PV = nRT.

#### Standard Calculations
**Example 1 — Find pressure:** 2.00 mol N₂ at 25°C in 10.0 L.
P = nRT/V = (2.00)(0.08206)(298)/(10.0) = **4.89 atm**

**Example 2 — Find moles:** 3.5 L of gas at 1.2 atm and 300 K.
n = PV/(RT) = (1.2)(3.5)/[(0.08206)(300)] = 4.2/24.62 = **0.171 mol**

**Example 3 — Find temperature:** 0.500 mol gas in 12.0 L at 2.00 atm.
T = PV/(nR) = (2.00)(12.0)/[(0.500)(0.08206)] = 24/0.0410 = **585 K**

#### Molar Volume at STP
At STP (0°C, 1 atm): V = nRT/P = (1)(0.08206)(273)/1 = **22.4 L/mol** — the molar volume.

#### Molar Mass from PV = nRT
n = PV/(RT); molar mass M = mass/n = m·RT/(PV). This allows determination of molar mass from gas density measurements.
