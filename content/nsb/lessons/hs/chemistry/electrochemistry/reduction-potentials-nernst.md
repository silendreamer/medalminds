#### Standard Reduction Potentials

A **standard reduction potential** (E°) is the voltage measured for a half-reaction under standard conditions (1 M concentrations, 1 atm gas pressures, 25 °C) relative to the **standard hydrogen electrode** (SHE), which is assigned E° = 0.00 V. More positive E° values indicate a stronger tendency to be reduced. The **standard cell potential** is calculated as **E°cell = E°cathode - E°anode**, where the cathode undergoes reduction and the anode undergoes oxidation.

#### Calculating Cell Potential

To find E°cell, look up the reduction potentials for both half-reactions. The half-reaction with the higher (more positive) E° serves as the **cathode** (reduction), and the other is reversed to serve as the **anode** (oxidation). For example, the Cu²⁺/Cu couple (E° = +0.34 V) paired with Zn²⁺/Zn (E° = -0.76 V) gives E°cell = 0.34 - (-0.76) = +1.10 V. A positive E°cell indicates a **spontaneous** galvanic cell; a negative value means the reaction is non-spontaneous as written.

#### The Nernst Equation

Under **non-standard conditions**, cell potential depends on the reaction quotient Q. The **Nernst equation** accounts for this:

E = E° - (RT/nF) ln Q

At 25 °C this simplifies to:

E = E° - (0.0592/n) log Q

where n is the number of moles of electrons transferred and F = 96,485 C/mol (**Faraday's constant**). As Q increases (products accumulate), E decreases. At equilibrium, E = 0 and Q = K, giving the relationship ln K = nFE°/RT = n(0.0592)^-1 · E° (at 25 °C: log K = nE°/0.0592).

#### Relationship Between E°cell, ΔG°, and K

The three key thermodynamic quantities are linked by: **ΔG° = -nFE°cell** and ΔG° = -RT ln K. A positive E°cell corresponds to a negative ΔG° (spontaneous) and K > 1. These relationships allow conversion between electrochemical, thermodynamic, and equilibrium perspectives on the same reaction, a common Science Bowl connection.

#### Review Questions
1. The standard reduction potential for Ag⁺/Ag is +0.80 V and for Ni²⁺/Ni is -0.25 V. What is E°cell when these are combined in a galvanic cell?
2. A galvanic cell has E° = +0.46 V and transfers 2 moles of electrons. Using the Nernst equation, what is the cell potential at 25 °C when Q = 100?
3. If E°cell for a reaction is +0.30 V and n = 2, what is ΔG° for the reaction in kJ/mol? (F = 96,485 C/mol)
