#### Anode and Cathode

A **galvanic cell** (also called a voltaic cell) converts spontaneous chemical energy into electrical energy via a redox reaction. The **anode** is the negative electrode where **oxidation** occurs — the reducing agent loses electrons here. The **cathode** is the positive electrode where **reduction** occurs — the oxidizing agent gains electrons here. The mnemonic **AN OX / RED CAT** (ANOde = OXidation; REDuction = CAThode) is widely used in competition. Electrons flow through the external circuit from the anode to the cathode.

#### Salt Bridge and Ion Flow

The two half-cells are connected by a **salt bridge** (typically a gel containing an inert electrolyte such as KNO₃ or KCl). The salt bridge serves two purposes: it completes the electrical circuit and maintains charge neutrality in each half-cell. As the anode metal dissolves, positive ions accumulate in the anode compartment; anions from the salt bridge migrate toward the anode to balance charge. Conversely, cations migrate from the salt bridge toward the cathode compartment. Without the salt bridge, the cell potential drops to zero and current ceases.

#### Standard Cell Potential

The **standard cell potential** (E°_cell) is calculated as: E°_cell = E°_cathode − E°_anode, where each standard reduction potential (E°_red) is taken from a standard reduction potential table at 25 °C and 1 M concentration. A positive E°_cell indicates a spontaneous reaction. The relationship to Gibbs free energy is: ΔG° = −nFE°_cell, where n is the number of moles of electrons transferred and F is **Faraday's constant** (96,485 C/mol).

#### Cell Notation

Standard **cell notation** (line notation) describes the cell from left (anode) to right (cathode): Anode | Anode solution || Cathode solution | Cathode. A single vertical line (|) denotes a phase boundary; a double vertical line (||) represents the salt bridge. Dissolved species are listed with their concentrations when specified. If no solid electrode participates (e.g., Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺), an inert electrode such as Pt is inserted: Pt | Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺ || ... For example, a zinc-copper cell is written: Zn(s) | Zn²⁺(aq) || Cu²⁺(aq) | Cu(s).

#### Review Questions

1. Write the cell notation for a galvanic cell constructed from a silver half-cell (Ag⁺/Ag) as the cathode and an iron half-cell (Fe²⁺/Fe) as the anode.
2. Given E°_red(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V and E°_red(Zn²⁺/Zn) = −0.76 V, calculate E°_cell for the Zn-Cu galvanic cell and determine ΔG° for n = 2.
3. Explain why a salt bridge is necessary for a galvanic cell to sustain a continuous current.
