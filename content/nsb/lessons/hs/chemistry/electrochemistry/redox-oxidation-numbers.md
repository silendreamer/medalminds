#### Oxidation Number Rules

An **oxidation number** (oxidation state) is the hypothetical charge an atom would carry if all bonds were ionic. Key rules, applied in priority order: (1) free elements have oxidation number 0; (2) monoatomic ions equal their charge; (3) O is typically −2 (except in peroxides, −1, and OF₂, +2); (4) H is +1 with nonmetals and −1 with metals; (5) fluorine is always −1; (6) the sum of oxidation numbers equals the charge of the species (0 for neutral molecules, the ion charge for polyatomic ions).

#### Oxidation and Reduction

**Oxidation** is the loss of electrons, causing an increase in oxidation number. **Reduction** is the gain of electrons, causing a decrease in oxidation number. The mnemonic **OIL RIG** — Oxidation Is Loss, Reduction Is Gain — summarizes this. In every redox reaction, the species that loses electrons is the **reducing agent** (it is oxidized), and the species that gains electrons is the **oxidizing agent** (it is reduced). The total electrons lost must equal the total electrons gained.

#### Half-Reaction Method for Balancing Redox

The **half-reaction method** splits the net ionic equation into separate **oxidation half-reaction** and **reduction half-reaction**, then balances each independently:
1. Assign oxidation numbers and identify atoms that change.
2. Balance atoms other than O and H first.
3. In acidic solution, balance O by adding H₂O; balance H by adding H⁺.
4. Add electrons to balance charge; electrons appear as reactants in the reduction half and as products in the oxidation half.
5. Multiply each half-reaction by an integer so electrons cancel, then add the half-reactions.
6. In **basic solution**, after balancing in acid, add OH⁻ to both sides to neutralize every H⁺, then combine H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O and cancel duplicate water molecules.

#### Identifying Oxidizing and Reducing Agents

Common strong **oxidizing agents** include permanganate (MnO₄⁻), dichromate (Cr₂O₇²⁻), hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂), and halogens. Common strong **reducing agents** include metals such as Zn and Fe, and hydride reagents. Disproportionation occurs when a single species is simultaneously oxidized and reduced — for example, the reaction of H₂O₂ into O₂ and H₂O.

#### Review Questions

1. Assign the oxidation number of chromium in K₂Cr₂O₇.
2. Balance the following reaction in acidic solution using the half-reaction method: MnO₄⁻ + Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + Fe³⁺.
3. Identify the oxidizing agent and the reducing agent in the reaction: Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu. Justify your answer using oxidation numbers.
