#### The Gram-to-Gram Roadmap
Every mass-based stoichiometry problem follows the same chain: **given grams → moles given → moles wanted → grams wanted**. You need three conversion factors: (1) molar mass of given substance (g → mol), (2) mole ratio from balanced equation (mol given → mol wanted), (3) molar mass of wanted substance (mol → g).

#### Worked Example
How many grams of H₂O form when 25.0 g of H₂ reacts completely with O₂? Balanced equation: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Molar masses: H₂ = 2.02 g/mol, H₂O = 18.02 g/mol.

25.0 g H₂ × (1 mol H₂ / 2.02 g H₂) × (2 mol H₂O / 2 mol H₂) × (18.02 g H₂O / 1 mol H₂O)
= 25.0 × (1/2.02) × (2/2) × 18.02
= **223 g H₂O**

#### Checking Your Setup
After writing the chain, check: (1) Are all intermediate units cancelled? Only g H₂O should remain. (2) Does the mole ratio put the wanted substance in the numerator? (3) Does the answer make chemical sense? (H₂ is light, O₂ is heavy; water is heavier than hydrogen — producing more mass than you started with makes sense because O₂ adds mass.)

#### STP Volume Conversions
How many liters of CO₂ at STP are produced from 44.0 g of CO₂ in a reaction?
44.0 g × (1 mol / 44.01 g) × (22.4 L / 1 mol) = **22.4 L**

One mole of any ideal gas at STP (0°C, 1 atm) occupies 22.4 L.

#### Review Questions
1. How many grams of O₂ are needed to completely react with 10.0 g of CH₄? (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O)
2. How many liters of H₂ at STP are produced when 5.00 g of Zn reacts with excess HCl? (Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂; Zn = 65.38 g/mol)
3. Explain why the mole ratio must come from the BALANCED equation, not the unbalanced one.
