#### Safety Quick-Check
1. Chemical in eye → eyewash station, flush for **15 minutes**
2. Add **acid to water** (never water to acid)
3. Volatile chemicals → use in **fume hood**
4. SDS = **Safety Data Sheet** (formerly MSDS)
5. GHS signal word for more severe hazards → **DANGER** (vs. WARNING)
6. CO₂ fire extinguisher: appropriate for **chemical/electrical fires** (not water — spreads solvent fires)

#### Graph Interpretation Scenarios
- Curve becomes horizontal → reaction rate approached zero / equilibrium reached / reactant depleted
- Straight line through origin in concentration vs. time → zero-order reaction
- Exponential decay in concentration vs. time → first-order reaction
- Y-intercept of a best-fit line in a calibration curve → instrumental blank/background signal

#### Common Lab Report Errors
1. Missing units on data table headers → makes data uninterpretable
2. Graph with no axis labels → reader can't determine what was measured
3. Conclusion says "I proved my hypothesis" → science doesn't "prove"; it supports or fails to support
4. Error analysis says "human error" → not specific enough; name the specific source and its directional effect

#### Review Questions
1. A student reports a density as "3.45" without units. Why is this problematic, and what units should be included?
2. Draw a sketch of what a calibration curve looks like for a Beer's Law spectrophotometry experiment, with proper axis labels.
3. Why do chemists say "the data support the hypothesis" rather than "the data prove the hypothesis"?
