#### The Seven SI Base Units
The SI system has seven base units from which all other units derive. For chemistry, the critical four are: **meter (m)** for length, **kilogram (kg)** for mass, **kelvin (K)** for temperature, and **mole (mol)** for amount of substance. Also important: **second (s)** for time, **ampere (A)** for electric current, and **candela (cd)** for luminous intensity. Note: the base unit of mass is the kilogram (not the gram) — the only SI base unit with a prefix.

#### SI Prefixes You Must Know
| Prefix | Symbol | Factor |
|--------|--------|--------|
| giga | G | 10⁹ |
| mega | M | 10⁶ |
| kilo | k | 10³ |
| deci | d | 10⁻¹ |
| centi | c | 10⁻² |
| milli | m | 10⁻³ |
| micro | μ | 10⁻⁶ |
| nano | n | 10⁻⁹ |
| pico | p | 10⁻¹² |

In chemistry, you'll most often use milli (mg, mL, mm), micro (μg, μL), and nano (nm for light wavelengths).

#### Derived Units in Chemistry
Derived units combine base units. **Volume**: m³ (SI) or L (1 L = 10⁻³ m³ = 1 dm³); 1 mL = 1 cm³. **Density**: kg/m³ or g/cm³ or g/mL. **Pressure**: pascal (Pa = N/m² = kg/m·s²); 1 atm = 101,325 Pa = 760 mmHg = 760 torr. **Energy**: joule (J = kg·m²/s²); 1 calorie = 4.184 J; 1 Cal (food calorie) = 1000 cal = 4184 J.

#### Temperature Conversions
Chemistry uses Kelvin (K), not Celsius, in calculations. K = °C + 273.15. Celsius is used for everyday temperature. Fahrenheit conversion: °F = (9/5)°C + 32. Absolute zero = 0 K = −273.15°C. On the Kelvin scale, there are no negative temperatures — a critical point for gas laws.

#### Science Bowl Trap
Students often confuse mass (kg) and weight (N). Mass is the amount of matter; weight is the gravitational force on that mass (weight = mg, where g = 9.8 m/s²). In chemistry labs, balances measure mass, not weight. Also: temperature in gas law calculations MUST be in kelvin — forgetting this is the #1 gas law calculation error.

#### Review Questions
1. Convert 2.5 mg to kg. Convert 350 nm to m. Convert 98°C to Kelvin.
2. A density is reported as 8.96 g/cm³. Convert this to kg/m³.
3. Why does chemistry use Kelvin instead of Celsius in gas law calculations?
