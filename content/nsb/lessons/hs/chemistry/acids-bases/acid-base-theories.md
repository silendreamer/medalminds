#### Arrhenius Theory
The **Arrhenius definition** is the most restrictive: an **Arrhenius acid** produces H+ (hydronium) ions in aqueous solution, and an **Arrhenius base** produces OH- (hydroxide) ions. For example, HCl dissociates to give H+ and Cl-, while NaOH dissociates to give Na+ and OH-. This model is limited to aqueous systems and cannot explain bases like ammonia (NH3) that lack an OH group.

#### Bronsted-Lowry Theory
The **Bronsted-Lowry definition** broadens the scope: an acid is a **proton donor** (H+ donor) and a base is a **proton acceptor**. This allows non-aqueous reactions to be described. When an acid donates a proton, it becomes its **conjugate base**; when a base accepts a proton, it becomes its **conjugate acid**. For example, in CH3COOH + H2O → CH3COO- + H3O+, the conjugate pairs are CH3COOH/CH3COO- and H3O+/H2O. A **conjugate pair** differs by exactly one proton (H+).

#### Lewis Theory
The **Lewis definition** is the most general: a **Lewis acid** is an electron-pair acceptor and a **Lewis base** is an electron-pair donor. This framework encompasses all Bronsted-Lowry reactions and also covers reactions with no proton transfer, such as BF3 + :NH3 → F3B-NH3, where BF3 (with an empty orbital) acts as the Lewis acid and NH3 (lone pair on N) acts as the Lewis base. Metal ions acting as central atoms in coordination complexes are classic Lewis acids.

#### Comparing the Three Models
Every Arrhenius acid/base is also a Bronsted-Lowry acid/base, and every Bronsted-Lowry reaction involves Lewis acid-base behavior. The hierarchy is: **Arrhenius ⊂ Bronsted-Lowry ⊂ Lewis**. Competition questions frequently test whether you can identify the correct theory that applies, especially for reactions in non-aqueous solvents or involving metal-ligand bonding.

#### Identifying Conjugate Pairs
To find a **conjugate acid-base pair**, locate a species that differs by one H+. In the reaction NH3 + H2O ⇌ NH4+ + OH-, the pairs are NH4+/NH3 (acid/conjugate base) and H2O/OH- (acid/conjugate base). The **amphoteric** species water can act as either acid or base depending on its reaction partner. Strong conjugate bases correspond to weak acids, and vice versa — a principle critical for buffer chemistry.

#### Review Questions
1. In the reaction HF + H2O ⇌ F- + H3O+, identify the two conjugate acid-base pairs and classify each species under the Bronsted-Lowry definition.
2. Explain why the Lewis acid-base theory can describe the reaction AlCl3 + Cl- → AlCl4- but the Arrhenius theory cannot.
3. Which theory — Arrhenius, Bronsted-Lowry, or Lewis — is most appropriate for explaining why ammonia (NH3) behaves as a base in water, and why?
