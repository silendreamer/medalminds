#### Titration Setup and Stoichiometry

A **titration** is a quantitative technique in which a solution of known concentration (the **titrant**) is added from a burette to a solution of unknown concentration (the **analyte**) until the reaction reaches completion. For a strong acid–strong base titration, the governing stoichiometry is moles of acid = moles of base at the **equivalence point**: n_acid = M_acid × V_acid = M_base × V_base. This relationship lets you calculate the unknown molarity once the equivalence volume is identified.

#### The Titration Curve

A **titration curve** plots pH on the y-axis against volume of titrant added on the x-axis. Key features include:

- The **equivalence point** — where moles of titrant exactly equal moles of analyte; appears as the steep inflection in the S-shaped curve.
- The **half-equivalence point** — for weak acid/strong base titrations, pH = pKa of the weak acid at this point (from Henderson-Hasselbalch with [A-] = [HA]).
- The **buffer region** — the relatively flat zone on either side of the half-equivalence point where pH changes slowly.

For a **strong acid–strong base** titration the equivalence point pH = 7.00. For a **weak acid–strong base** titration the equivalence point pH > 7 because the conjugate base hydrolyzes water.

#### Selecting an Appropriate Indicator

An **acid-base indicator** is a weak acid (HIn) whose conjugate base (In-) has a different color; it changes color over a **transition range** of approximately pKa(In) ± 1. To avoid titration error, choose an indicator whose transition range **overlaps the steep portion of the titration curve** near the equivalence point:

- Strong acid / strong base: equivalence pH ≈ 7 → use **phenolphthalein** (range 8.2–10.0) or **bromothymol blue** (6.0–7.6).
- Weak acid / strong base: equivalence pH > 7 → **phenolphthalein** is ideal.
- Strong acid / weak base: equivalence pH < 7 → use **methyl orange** (3.1–4.4) or **methyl red** (4.4–6.2).

#### Review Questions

1. A 25.00 mL sample of HCl is titrated with 0.150 M NaOH; the equivalence point is reached at 32.40 mL. What is the molarity of the HCl solution?
2. Why is the equivalence point pH greater than 7 when a weak acid is titrated with a strong base, and which indicator would you select for this titration?
3. On a titration curve for a weak acid, at what added volume of base does pH equal the pKa of the acid, and why?
