#### Physical Properties
A physical property can be measured or observed without changing the substance's chemical composition. Examples: color, odor, density, melting point, boiling point, hardness, conductivity, malleability, ductility, solubility, viscosity, mass, volume. These properties describe how the substance looks or behaves as itself — not how it reacts. You can measure water's boiling point (100°C) without converting it to something else.

#### Chemical Properties
A chemical property describes how a substance reacts to form different substances. Examples: flammability (does it burn?), reactivity with acids, oxidation tendency (does iron rust?), toxicity, acidity, ability to corrode. To observe a chemical property, you must carry out a chemical change. You can't determine flammability without actually burning — and after burning, you no longer have the same substance.

#### Intensive vs. Extensive Properties
**Intensive** properties don't depend on the amount of substance: density, color, melting point, boiling point, specific heat, conductivity. These are used to identify substances — density = 0.789 g/mL identifies ethanol regardless of how much you have. **Extensive** properties depend on amount: mass, volume, heat content, length. Doubling the amount of water doubles its mass and volume but not its boiling point or density. Ratio of two extensive properties = intensive property (mass/volume = density).

#### Why This Classification Matters
Chemical analysis uses physical properties to identify substances without destroying them (non-destructive testing). Chemical properties require testing reactivity — necessarily consuming or altering the sample. Forensic scientists, geologists, and quality-control chemists exploit physical properties first. Only when physical properties are insufficient do they perform chemical tests.

#### Science Bowl Classification Drill
Classify each as physical (P) or chemical (C): melting point (P), flammability (C), density (P), corrosion (C), color (P), solubility (P), reactivity with water (C), ductility (P), ability to tarnish (C).

#### Review Questions
1. Classify each as physical or chemical property: (a) gold's yellow color, (b) sulfur's odor, (c) iron's tendency to rust, (d) water's boiling point at 100°C, (e) gasoline's flammability.
2. Which is an intensive property: density, mass, volume, or heat energy? Explain why.
3. Why can you determine a substance's melting point without performing a chemical reaction?
