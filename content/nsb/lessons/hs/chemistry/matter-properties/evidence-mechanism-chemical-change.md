#### What Defines a Chemical Change
A chemical change produces new substances with different chemical formulas. At the molecular level: chemical bonds between atoms are broken, and new bonds form, creating products with different arrangements of atoms. Example: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O. In this combustion, C–H and O=O bonds break; C=O and O–H bonds form. The products CO₂ and H₂O have completely different properties than the reactants methane and oxygen.

#### Five Observable Signs of Chemical Change
(1) **Temperature change**: exothermic reactions release heat (combustion, neutralization, rusting); endothermic reactions absorb heat (photosynthesis, dissolving ammonium nitrate). (2) **Color change**: copper → blue in CuSO₄ solution; silver tarnish; litmus paper pH indicator. (3) **Gas production**: CO₂ from acid + carbonate (bubbles); H₂ from acid + metal. (4) **Precipitate formation**: mixing clear Pb(NO₃)₂ and KI solutions produces yellow PbI₂ precipitate. (5) **Light emission**: burning magnesium emits brilliant white light; chemiluminescence in glow sticks.

#### The Conservation of Mass
In any chemical change, mass is conserved (Lavoisier's Law). The total mass of reactants = total mass of products. This is because no atoms are created or destroyed — they are only rearranged. Example: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Reactants: 2(2) + 32 = 36 g. Products: 2(18) = 36 g. ✓ This law is what allows us to balance chemical equations.

#### When "Evidence" Isn't Enough
Some signs that look like chemical change are physical: dissolving sodium hydroxide in water releases heat (feels hot) — but it's a physical change. Dissolving ammonium nitrate absorbs heat (cold packs) — physical. These are physical changes because the solute can be recovered by evaporation. True chemical change produces substances that cannot be recovered by simple physical means.

#### Review Questions
1. Identify the chemical change(s): (a) salt dissolving in water, (b) milk souring, (c) ice melting, (d) iron rusting, (e) photosynthesis.
2. A student mixes two clear solutions and observes a yellow solid forming. What type of change is this? What is the solid called?
3. How does the law of conservation of mass apply to combustion? If 100 g of wood produces 60 g of ash, does mass disappear? Explain.
