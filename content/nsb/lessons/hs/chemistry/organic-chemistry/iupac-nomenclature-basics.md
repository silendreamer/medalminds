#### Selecting the Parent Chain
The **parent chain** is the longest continuous carbon chain in the molecule. Its length determines the base name: meth- (1C), eth- (2C), prop- (3C), but- (4C), pent- (5C), hex- (6C), hept- (7C), oct- (8C), non- (9C), dec- (10C). When two chains of equal length exist, choose the one bearing the greater number of substituents. The parent chain sets the carbon-numbering framework used for all **locants**.

#### Suffixes for Functional Groups
The **principal characteristic group** — the highest-priority functional group — determines the suffix:
- **-ane**: alkane (single bonds only), e.g., butane
- **-ene / -yne**: alkene or alkyne (double/triple bond), e.g., but-2-ene
- **-ol**: alcohol, e.g., propan-1-ol (OH on C-1 of propane)
- **-al**: aldehyde (the CHO carbon is always C-1), e.g., propanal
- **-one**: ketone, e.g., pentan-2-one
- **-oic acid**: carboxylic acid (C-1), e.g., ethanoic acid (acetic acid)
- **-amine**: amine, e.g., ethanamine

When a lower-priority group cannot be expressed as a suffix, it becomes a prefix (e.g., **oxo-**, **hydroxy-**).

#### Numbering and Locants
Number the parent chain from the end that gives the **principal characteristic group the lowest possible locant**. If no functional group determines direction, give substituents the lowest set of locants. A **locant** immediately precedes the part of the name it modifies: 2-methylpropane, but-2-ene, pentan-3-one. For multiple identical substituents, use multiplying prefixes (**di-, tri-, tetra-**) and list each locant: 2,3-dimethylbutane.

#### Naming Substituents
Substituents attached to the parent chain are named as **alkyl groups** (methyl-, ethyl-, propyl-, isopropyl-, etc.) or **halo substituents** (fluoro-, chloro-, bromo-, iodo-). List substituents **alphabetically** (ignoring multiplying prefixes) before the parent name: 4-bromo-2-methylpentane, not 2-methyl-4-bromopentane.

#### Review Questions
1. Draw the condensed structural formula for 3-ethyl-2-methylhexane and identify the parent chain and all substituents with their locants.
2. Give the IUPAC name for the compound CH3CH2CH(OH)CH2CH3, justifying the direction of numbering.
3. A compound has the formula CH3COCH2CH2CH3. Provide its IUPAC name, including the correct suffix and locant for the principal characteristic group.
