#### Reading Mole Ratios from Equations
In 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, the coefficients tell you the mole ratio: 2 mol H₂ : 1 mol O₂ : 2 mol H₂O. This ratio holds regardless of the absolute amounts. So 4 mol H₂ requires 2 mol O₂ and produces 4 mol H₂O (scaled by 2).

#### Worked Example: Nitrogen Fixation
The Haber process makes ammonia: N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃. If 10 mol of H₂ reacts, how much NH₃ forms? Use the mole ratio 3 H₂ : 2 NH₃. (10 mol H₂) × (2 mol NH₃ / 3 mol H₂) = 6.67 mol NH₃.

#### Gram-to-Gram Conversions
If 28 g of N₂ reacts with excess H₂, how much NH₃ forms? Step 1: Moles of N₂ = 28 g / 28 g/mol = 1 mol. Step 2: From equation, 1 mol N₂ → 2 mol NH₃. Step 3: Mass of NH₃ = 2 mol × 17 g/mol = 34 g.

#### Scaling Recipes
The stoichiometric ratio is like a recipe. Want to make twice as much product? Use twice as much of each reactant. The ratio stays constant.

#### Review Questions
1. In 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O, how many moles of CO₂ form from 3 mol of C₂H₆?
2. How many grams of CO₂ are produced when 10 g of C₂H₆ burn?
3. In the reaction Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu, how many grams of Cu form from 5.6 g of Fe?
