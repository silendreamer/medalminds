#### Acid-Base (Neutralization)
An acid reacts with a base to form a salt and water. HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O. The H⁺ from acid and OH⁻ from base combine: H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O. Strong acid + strong base → neutral salt. Weak acid + strong base → basic salt. Strong acid + weak base → acidic salt.

#### Precipitation Reactions
Two aqueous solutions mix, and an insoluble salt forms. AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃. The arrow ↓ indicates a precipitate. Solubility rules predict which salts precipitate: salts of Ag⁺, Pb²⁺, and Ba²⁺ with specific anions are often insoluble; salts of Na⁺, K⁺, and NH₄⁺ are usually soluble.

#### Solubility Rules Summary
- Nitrates (NO₃⁻), perchlorates, and ammonium salts: always soluble
- Halides (Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻): soluble except with Ag⁺, Pb²⁺, Hg₂²⁺
- Sulfates (SO₄²⁻): soluble except with Ba²⁺, Pb²⁺, Ca²⁺ (some)
- Carbonates, phosphates, sulfides: generally insoluble except with Na⁺, K⁺, NH₄⁺
- Hydroxides: soluble only for alkali metals and Ba(OH)₂

#### Spectator Ions and Net Ionic Equations
In AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl↓ + NaNO₃, the Na⁺ and NO₃⁻ don't participate—they're spectator ions. The net ionic equation shows only the reacting ions: Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s).
