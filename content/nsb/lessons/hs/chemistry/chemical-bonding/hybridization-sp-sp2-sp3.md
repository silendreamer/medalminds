#### Why Hybridization?
Carbon's ground-state configuration is [He]2s²2p². It has two unpaired p electrons — so it should form only 2 bonds. But methane (CH₄) has 4 equivalent C−H bonds. Hybridization resolves this: the 2s and all three 2p orbitals combine to form four equivalent sp³ hybrid orbitals, each forming one σ bond.

#### sp³ Hybridization
1 s + 3 p orbitals → 4 sp³ hybrids; tetrahedral geometry, 109.5°. All 4 bonds are equivalent σ bonds. Examples: CH₄, NH₃, H₂O (lone pairs occupy sp³ orbitals).

#### sp² Hybridization
1 s + 2 p orbitals → 3 sp² hybrids; trigonal planar, 120°; one unhybridized p orbital perpendicular to the plane forms a π bond with a neighboring atom. Examples: C₂H₄ (ethylene), BF₃, SO₃. Double bond = 1 σ (sp²–sp²) + 1 π (p–p).

#### sp Hybridization
1 s + 1 p orbital → 2 sp hybrids; linear, 180°; two unhybridized p orbitals form two π bonds. Examples: C₂H₂ (acetylene), CO₂, BeCl₂. Triple bond = 1 σ + 2 π.

#### Hybridization Shortcut
Count electron domains around central atom: 2 domains → sp; 3 domains → sp²; 4 domains → sp³; 5 domains → sp³d; 6 domains → sp³d².

#### Review Questions
1. What hybridization does the nitrogen atom in NH₃ have? What orbitals form the lone pair?
2. Why does a carbon atom in a C=C double bond have sp² hybridization rather than sp³?
3. Describe the bonding in acetylene (HC≡CH): what types of bonds are present and what hybridization does each C have?
