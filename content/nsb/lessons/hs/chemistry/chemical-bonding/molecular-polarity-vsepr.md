#### From Bond Polarity to Molecular Polarity
Each polar bond is a vector (points from δ+ to δ−). Molecular polarity = vector sum of all bond dipoles. If the vectors cancel completely → nonpolar molecule (even if bonds are polar). If vectors don't cancel → polar molecule with a net dipole moment (μ, measured in Debye, D).

#### Symmetric Nonpolar Molecules with Polar Bonds
CO₂ (linear): two C=O dipoles point in opposite directions → cancel → nonpolar (μ = 0).
BF₃ (trigonal planar): three B−F dipoles at 120° → cancel → nonpolar.
CCl₄ (tetrahedral): four C−Cl dipoles at 109.5° → cancel symmetrically → nonpolar.
SF₆ (octahedral): six S−F dipoles → cancel → nonpolar.

#### Asymmetric Polar Molecules
NH₃ (trigonal pyramidal): three N−H dipoles point down-and-out + lone pair points up → net dipole upward → polar (μ = 1.47 D).
H₂O (bent): two O−H dipoles + two lone pairs → net dipole toward oxygen → polar (μ = 1.85 D, highest of common molecules).
SO₂ (bent): two S−O dipoles don't cancel → polar.

#### Review Questions
1. Explain why CO₂ is nonpolar despite having two polar C=O bonds.
2. Predict whether each molecule is polar or nonpolar: CHCl₃, BCl₃, H₂S, PCl₅.
3. Which would you expect to have the higher boiling point: CCl₄ or CHCl₃? Explain using polarity.
