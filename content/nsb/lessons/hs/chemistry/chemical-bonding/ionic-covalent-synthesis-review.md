#### Quick Classification
For each compound, state bond type and give one predicted property:
- MgBr₂: ionic (ΔEN ≈ 1.8) → high MP
- CO₂: polar covalent (two polar bonds, nonpolar molecule) → gas at RT
- HF: polar covalent (ΔEN=1.9, but individual bonds are polar covalent — compound is molecular) → liquid at RT due to H-bonding
- N₂: nonpolar covalent → gas at RT, very stable (triple bond)

#### Conceptual Connections
Bond type → molecular/ionic structure → properties → macroscopic behavior. This chain explains why we care about bonds: it allows us to predict and engineer material properties.

#### Review Questions
1. Why do ionic compounds have higher melting points than most covalent compounds?
2. Water (H₂O) has polar O−H bonds. Why does water conduct electricity poorly even though it is polar?
3. The compound with the greatest ionic character would have the largest ΔEN. Which compound is most ionic: LiF, NaF, KF, or RbF? Explain your reasoning.
