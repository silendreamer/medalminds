#### Electron Transfer and Ion Formation
Ionic bonds form when the electronegativity difference between two atoms is large (typically ΔEN > 1.7), causing complete electron transfer. The metal loses electrons to form a cation; the nonmetal gains electrons to form an anion. Example: Na (EN 0.93) + Cl (EN 3.16) → Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl. Na reaches the noble gas configuration of Ne; Cl reaches the configuration of Ar.

#### Lattice Energy
Lattice energy is the energy released when gaseous ions combine to form one mole of solid ionic compound. Highly exothermic (released → negative ΔH for lattice formation). Magnitude depends on ion charge and size: higher charge → greater lattice energy; smaller ions → greater lattice energy. NaCl: −787 kJ/mol; MgO: −3,791 kJ/mol (Mg²⁺O²⁻ vs Na⁺Cl⁻ — higher charges and smaller size).

#### Properties of Ionic Compounds
Ionic compounds form crystal lattices with alternating cations and anions. Properties: high melting/boiling points (strong electrostatic attraction in lattice); conduct electricity when dissolved in water or melted (ions free to move); hard but brittle (lattice shatters along slip planes when ions of like charge align).

#### The Ionic Bond Is Not Directional
Unlike covalent bonds, ionic bonds have no preferred direction — the attraction between ions is electrostatic and acts equally in all directions. This allows the formation of extended lattice structures rather than discrete molecules.

#### Review Questions
1. Why does MgO have a much higher lattice energy than NaCl?
2. Explain why NaCl conducts electricity when dissolved in water but not in solid form.
