#### Why Shape Matters: A Few Examples
- Water's bent shape (104.5°) creates a large dipole moment → H-bonding → high boiling point (100°C) → life as we know it
- DNA's double helix depends on specific H-bonding between complementary base pairs — only possible because of N−H and C=O geometry
- Enzyme active sites are shape-specific: substrate must match the geometry of the active site

#### Full Geometry Prediction: Step by Step
Sulfur dioxide (SO₂): (1) Lewis structure → S central, one S=O, one S−O with lone pairs (or resonance). (2) Electron domains on S: 2 bonds + 1 lone pair = 3 electron domains. (3) Electron geometry: trigonal planar. (4) Molecular geometry: bent. (5) Bond angle: ~119° (slightly less than 120° due to lone pair repulsion). (6) Polar? Yes — bent with polar bonds → net dipole.

#### Shape and Solubility
"Like dissolves like": polar solvents (water) dissolve polar solutes; nonpolar solvents (hexane) dissolve nonpolar solutes. Knowing shape → knowing polarity → predicting solubility. CCl₄ (nonpolar tetrahedral) is immiscible with water; CHCl₃ (polar) has limited water solubility; ethanol (polar) is infinitely miscible.

#### Review Questions
1. Predict the electron geometry, molecular geometry, polarity, and bond angle for each: PF₃, ClF₃, SF₄.
2. Why does NH₃ have a higher boiling point (−33°C) than PH₃ (−87°C), given that PH₃ is larger?
3. A molecule has the formula AX₃E (3 bonding domains, 1 lone pair). What is its molecular geometry and approximate bond angle?
