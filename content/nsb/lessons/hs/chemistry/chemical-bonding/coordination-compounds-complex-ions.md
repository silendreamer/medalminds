#### Structure of Coordination Compounds

A **coordination compound** consists of a **central metal atom or ion** (typically a transition metal) surrounded by **ligands** — molecules or anions that donate electron pairs to the metal, forming **coordinate (dative) covalent bonds**. The entire unit enclosed in brackets is the **coordination complex**; species with a net charge are called **complex ions**. For example, in [Cu(NH3)4]^2+, copper(II) is the central metal and four ammonia molecules are the ligands. The overall compound **tetraamminecopper(II) sulfate** is written [Cu(NH3)4]SO4.

#### Ligands and Denticity

**Ligands** are classified by the number of donor atoms they supply. **Monodentate** ligands bind through one atom: NH3 (ammine), H2O (aqua), Cl^- (chlorido), CN^- (cyano), CO (carbonyl). **Bidentate** ligands bind through two atoms: **ethylenediamine (en, H2NCH2CH2NH2)** and **oxalate (ox, C2O4^2-)**. **Polydentate** ligands such as **EDTA** (ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid, a hexadentate ligand) wrap around the metal at up to six points, forming very stable **chelate** rings. Chelation is why EDTA is used as an antidote for heavy-metal poisoning.

#### Coordination Number and Geometry

The **coordination number (CN)** is the total number of donor atoms directly bonded to the central metal. Common coordination numbers and associated geometries:

- **CN = 2**: linear (e.g., [Ag(NH3)2]^+)
- **CN = 4**: tetrahedral (e.g., [CoCl4]^2-) or square planar (e.g., [PtCl4]^2-)
- **CN = 6**: **octahedral** — the most common geometry (e.g., [Fe(CN)6]^4-, ferrocyanide; [Co(NH3)6]^3+)

The geometry affects the **color**, **magnetic properties**, and **reactivity** of the complex.

#### Naming Coordination Compounds

IUPAC naming rules: ligands are listed alphabetically before the metal name; anionic ligands end in **-o** (chloro, cyano, oxalato); neutral ligands use special names (ammine, aqua, carbonyl, nitrosyl). The metal's oxidation state is given in Roman numerals. For example, [Fe(CN)6]^3- is **hexacyanoferrate(III)**; K3[Fe(CN)6] is **potassium hexacyanoferrate(III)** (common name: potassium ferricyanide). [Cr(en)3]^3+ is **tris(ethylenediamine)chromium(III)**.

#### Biological and Industrial Relevance

Coordination chemistry underpins many biological molecules and industrial applications. **Hemoglobin** contains iron(II) coordinated by a porphyrin ring with O2 as an exchangeable ligand. **Chlorophyll** has magnesium(II) at its center. The anti-cancer drug **cisplatin** (cis-[PtCl2(NH3)2]) is a square-planar platinum complex. The **Mond process** for nickel purification uses volatile **nickel tetracarbonyl, Ni(CO)4**, a CN = 4 complex.

#### Review Questions
1. What is the coordination number and geometry of the complex ion [Co(NH3)6]^3+, and how many coordinate bonds does the central metal form?
2. Explain why EDTA is classified as a hexadentate ligand and why chelating ligands generally form more stable complexes than monodentate ligands.
3. Write the IUPAC name for the complex ion [Fe(CN)6]^4- and identify the oxidation state of iron in this species.
