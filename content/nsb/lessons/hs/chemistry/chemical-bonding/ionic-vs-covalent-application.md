#### Electronegativity Cutoffs
| ΔEN | Bond type |
|-----|-----------|
| 0–0.4 | Nonpolar covalent |
| 0.4–1.7 | Polar covalent |
| > 1.7 | Ionic |

These are guidelines, not absolute rules. NaCl has ΔEN = 2.23 → ionic. HCl has ΔEN = 0.96 → polar covalent. N₂ has ΔEN = 0 → nonpolar covalent.

#### Property Comparison
| Property | Ionic | Covalent (molecular) |
|----------|-------|---------------------|
| Melting point | High (NaCl: 801°C) | Low–moderate (H₂O: 0°C) |
| Electrical conductivity (solution) | Yes (ions) | No (molecules, not ions) |
| Electrical conductivity (solid) | No | No |
| Solubility in water | Generally high | Variable |
| Physical state at room temp | Solid | Gas, liquid, or solid |

#### Worked Prediction
Compound: CaO. ΔEN = EN(O) − EN(Ca) = 3.44 − 1.00 = 2.44 → ionic. Predicted properties: high MP, conducts when dissolved, crystalline solid at room temperature. Actual MP of CaO = 2,613°C ✓.

#### Review Questions
1. Predict the bond type (ionic/polar covalent/nonpolar covalent) for: KBr, PH₃, F₂, AlCl₃.
2. Why does NaCl dissolve in water and conduct electricity when dissolved, while sugar (C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁) dissolves but does not conduct?
3. A substance has a high melting point, dissolves in water, and conducts electricity in solution but not in solid form. Is it ionic or covalent? Explain.
