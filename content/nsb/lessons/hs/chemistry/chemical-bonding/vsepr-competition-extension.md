#### Toss-Up Stems
"For 10 points, this molecular geometry, exemplified by water, results from four electron domains with two lone pairs and two bonding pairs, giving a bond angle of approximately 104.5 degrees." → **bent**

"For 10 points, the electron geometry of a molecule with five electron domains around the central atom, like PCl₅, is named for this geometric solid." → **trigonal bipyramidal**

"For 10 points, a molecule with this geometry has all bond dipoles canceling due to perfect symmetry — exemplified by carbon tetrachloride and boron trifluoride." → **nonpolar (or the specific geometry: tetrahedral / trigonal planar)**

#### Must-Know Bond Angles
- Linear: 180°
- Trigonal planar: 120°
- Tetrahedral: 109.5°
- Trigonal pyramidal: ~107°
- Bent (from tetrahedral): ~104.5°
- Trigonal bipyramidal: 120° (equatorial), 90° (axial-equatorial)
- Octahedral: 90°

#### Review Questions
1. What is the bond angle and molecular geometry of NH₃?
2. Why is the H−N−H bond angle in NH₃ (107°) larger than the H−O−H angle in H₂O (104.5°)?
3. Identify the molecular geometry: molecule has 4 bond pairs and 2 lone pairs around central atom.
