#### Pressure and Its Definition

**Pressure** is defined as force per unit area: **P = F/A**, where F is the force applied perpendicular to a surface and A is the area over which it acts. The SI unit of pressure is the **pascal (Pa)**, equal to 1 N/m². Other common units include atmospheres (1 atm ≈ 101,325 Pa) and millimeters of mercury (mmHg). Because pressure depends on area, a small force concentrated on a tiny area can produce enormous pressure.

#### Hydrostatic Pressure

In a fluid at rest, pressure increases with depth due to the weight of fluid above. The **hydrostatic pressure** relation is **P = rho·g·h**, where rho (ρ) is the fluid density, g is gravitational acceleration (9.8 m/s²), and h is the depth below the free surface. Total pressure at depth h below an open surface is P_total = P_atm + rho·g·h. Importantly, hydrostatic pressure depends only on depth, not on the horizontal position or the shape of the container — a result sometimes called the **hydrostatic paradox**.

#### Pascal's Principle

**Pascal's principle** states that a pressure change applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished to every point in the fluid and to the walls of its container. Mathematically, if an external pressure ΔP is applied, every point in the fluid experiences the same ΔP increase. This principle underlies **hydraulic systems**: applying a small force F1 over a small piston area A1 creates a pressure P = F1/A1 that is transmitted to a larger piston of area A2, producing an output force F2 = P·A2 >> F1. Hydraulic lifts, car brakes, and hydraulic presses all operate on this principle.

#### Pressure in Connected Containers

In interconnected fluid systems at equilibrium, fluid surfaces at the same height are at the same pressure, regardless of tube shape or cross-section. This is why a **U-tube manometer** measures pressure differences: the height difference Δh between the two fluid columns satisfies ΔP = rho·g·Δh. Absolute pressure gauges account for atmospheric pressure; **gauge pressure** reports only the excess above atmospheric: P_gauge = P_abs - P_atm.

#### Review Questions
1. A hydraulic press has an input piston of area 0.01 m² and an output piston of area 0.5 m². If a force of 200 N is applied to the input piston, what is the output force?
2. What is the gauge pressure at the bottom of a 10-meter-deep freshwater pool? (Use rho = 1000 kg/m³ and g = 9.8 m/s².)
3. A pressure of 3 × 10⁴ Pa is applied to the surface of an enclosed liquid. By how much does the pressure increase at every interior point of the liquid?
