#### Pauli Exclusion Principle
No two electrons in an atom can have identical quantum numbers. Each orbital (defined by n, l, m_l) can hold at most two electrons (differing in spin m_s = ±½). This principle determines how electrons fill orbitals, explaining the structure of the periodic table. Helium (two electrons) fills the 1s orbital; lithium (three electrons) starts the second shell; and so on.

#### Electron Configuration & Aufbau Principle
Electrons fill orbitals in order of increasing energy according to the aufbau principle: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 3d, 4s, ... The periodic table's structure reflects this filling: the s-block (Groups 1–2) fills s orbitals; the p-block (Groups 13–18) fills p orbitals; the d-block (transition metals) fills d orbitals. Valence electrons (outermost shell) determine chemical properties, explaining why elements in the same group (same valence configuration) have similar chemistry.

#### Shielding & Effective Nuclear Charge
Inner electrons shield outer electrons from the full nuclear charge. An outer electron experiences an effective nuclear charge Z_eff < Z. The degree of shielding depends on how close the inner electrons are and their number. Electrons in the same shell shield each other less effectively than inner electrons. Across a period, Z_eff increases as nuclear charge increases while shielding remains roughly constant—causing atomic radius to decrease.

#### Periodic Trends
Atomic radius decreases left to right across a period (increasing Z_eff) and increases down a group (more electron shells). Ionization energy (energy to remove an electron) increases left to right and increases up a group. Electronegativity (tendency to attract electrons in a bond) increases left to right and increases up a group. These trends follow from Z_eff and electron configuration.

#### Review Questions
1. What is the Pauli exclusion principle, and how does it explain the periodic table?
2. Why is atomic radius larger for atoms farther down a group?
3. Explain shielding and effective nuclear charge.
