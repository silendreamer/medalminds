#### Photon Properties
A photon (particle of light) has: energy E = hf = ℏω (ℏ = h/(2π)), momentum p = E/c = hf/c = h/λ, and travels at speed c. The de Broglie wavelength is λ = h/p. Unlike massive particles, photons are massless; their momentum is purely relativistic. A photon can be thought of as a packet of electromagnetic waves or as a quantum of light.

#### Double-Slit Experiment with Light
In the double-slit experiment, light passes through two slits and interferes at a screen, creating an interference pattern (many bright and dark fringes). This demonstrates wave behavior. However, if detectors are placed at the slits to determine which slit each photon passes through, the interference pattern disappears and a simple two-slit diffraction pattern appears. The act of measurement forces the photon to choose a slit, destroying interference—demonstrating particle-like behavior.

#### Complementarity
Niels Bohr's principle of complementarity states that particle and wave aspects of quantum objects are mutually exclusive—you cannot simultaneously measure both. If you measure which slit a photon passes through (particle aspect), you destroy the interference pattern (wave aspect). If you allow interference (wave aspect), you cannot determine which slit the photon used (particle aspect). This is a fundamental feature of quantum mechanics, not a limitation of measurement technology.

#### Practical Consequences
The wave-particle duality limits optical resolution. Diffraction effects (wave behavior) prevent focusing light into arbitrarily small spots. The best resolution achievable is roughly the wavelength: δx ~ λ/2. This is why X-ray and electron microscopes are needed for atomic-scale imaging (shorter wavelength = better resolution).
