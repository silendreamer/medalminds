#### Classic Photoelectric Problem
"Light of wavelength 300 nm shines on a metal with work function 3.6 eV. What is the maximum kinetic energy of ejected electrons?" Solution: Photon energy E = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3 × 10⁸)/(300 × 10⁻⁹) ≈ 6.63 × 10⁻¹⁹ J ≈ 4.14 eV. KE_max = 4.14 − 3.6 = 0.54 eV.

#### Compton Scattering Calculation
"A 1 MeV photon scatters 180° (backscatters) off an electron. What is the electron's kinetic energy?" Solution: The Compton shift at 180° is Δλ = 2h/(m_e c) ≈ 4.85 × 10⁻¹² m. For the initial photon, λ = hc/E = (1240 eV·nm)/(1 × 10⁶ eV) ≈ 1.24 × 10⁻³ nm. After scattering, λ' = λ + Δλ ≈ 1.24 × 10⁻³ nm (shift is tiny, but significant). The scattered photon's energy is E' = hc/λ', and the electron receives KE = E − E'.

#### Photon Momentum in Radiation Pressure
"A 1000 W laser (1 μm wavelength) shines on a perfectly absorbing surface. What is the radiation pressure?" Solution: Photon momentum p = h/λ. Number of photons per second: N = (Power)/(Energy per photon) = P/(hf) = Pλ/(hc). Force on surface: F = (dp/dt) = N × p = [Pλ/(hc)] × (h/λ) = P/c. Pressure P_rad = F/Area = I/c, where I is intensity. For I = Power/Area = 1000/A, pressure depends on beam size.

#### Threshold Problems
"For what wavelength does a given metal's photoelectric threshold occur?" Solution: At threshold, f = f₀, so λ₀ = c/f₀ = c/(W/h) = hc/W. If W = 2 eV, then λ₀ = (1240 eV·nm)/(2 eV) = 620 nm (red light).
