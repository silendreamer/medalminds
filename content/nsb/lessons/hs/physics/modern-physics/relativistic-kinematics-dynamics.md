#### Relativistic Force & Acceleration
Force is still F = dp/dt, but now p = γmv. As v approaches c, dp/dv increases, so the acceleration a = dv/dt for a given force decreases. A constant force no longer produces constant acceleration; instead, acceleration asymptotically approaches zero as v → c. This is consistent with the impossibility of reaching light speed: any finite force produces continuously smaller accelerations at high speeds.

#### Energy-Momentum Relation
A powerful relation connects energy and momentum: E² = (pc)² + (mc²)². At rest (p = 0), E = mc². For a highly relativistic particle (v ≈ c), pc ≈ E, so E ≈ pc. This relation is the same for all observers, even if they measure different E and p values (in their respective frames).

#### Doppler Effect for Light
The relativistic Doppler effect for light is f' = f√[(1 + β)/(1 − β)], where β = v/c and v is the relative velocity (positive for approach, negative for recession). This differs from the classical sound wave Doppler formula and accounts for time dilation. It predicts: (1) approach (v > 0) increases frequency (blueshift), (2) recession (v < 0) decreases frequency (redshift), and (3) the shift depends only on relative velocity, not on the medium.

#### Lorentz Transformations
The Lorentz transformations relate spatial and temporal coordinates between inertial frames. A spacetime event (x, t) in one frame relates to (x', t') in another by: x' = γ(x − vt), t' = γ(t − vx/c²). These transformations preserve the speed of light invariant: if light travels c in one frame, it travels c in all frames. They reduce to Galilean transformations (x' = x − vt, t' = t) in the limit v << c.
