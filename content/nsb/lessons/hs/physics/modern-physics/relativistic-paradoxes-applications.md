#### The Twin Paradox
One twin stays on Earth; the other travels in a spaceship at high speed and returns. The traveling twin experiences less proper time (time dilation) and ages less than the Earth-bound twin. This seems paradoxical because relativity treats all inertial frames equally—why doesn't the Earth-bound twin age less from the traveling twin's perspective? The resolution: the traveling twin's frame is not inertial (it accelerates to turn around), breaking the symmetry. When accounting for acceleration, the traveling twin indeed ages less. Time dilation is real and has been measured for high-speed particles and orbiting clocks (atomic clocks on airplanes age slightly less).

#### Length Contraction Paradox
A contracted moving rod seems to fit into a shorter stationary garage that is normally shorter than the rod. The paradox asks: does the rod fit or not? Resolution: "fitting" requires simultaneity. In the rod's frame, the rod is full length and the garage is contracted—the rod does not fit. In the garage's frame, the rod is contracted and does fit. Both viewpoints are correct in their respective frames; simultaneity of the rod's ends entering and exiting the garage is frame-dependent.

#### GPS & Relativity
GPS satellites move at high speed and orbit at high altitude (weaker gravity). Special relativity (satellite speed → time dilation, running slow) and general relativity (weak gravity → time dilation, running fast) affect satellite clock rates by about 38 microseconds per day. Without relativistic corrections, GPS position errors would accumulate at ~10 meters per day—rendering the system useless. Modern GPS relies crucially on relativity.

#### High-Energy Particle Colliders
In particle accelerators, particles reach speeds with γ > 1000. Time dilation dramatically extends particle lifetimes. A muon with 2 μs proper lifetime, traveling at γ = 1000, lives 2000 microseconds (2 ms) in the lab frame—long enough to traverse kilometers of detector. Similarly, length contraction affects how detectors observe fast particles.
