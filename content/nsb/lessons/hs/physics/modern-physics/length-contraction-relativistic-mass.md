#### Length Contraction
An object's length measured in its rest frame (proper length L₀) appears contracted in a frame where the object moves. The measured length is L = L₀/γ = L₀ √(1 − v²/c²). Contraction occurs only along the direction of motion; perpendicular dimensions are unaffected. At v = 0.95c, √(1 − v²/c²) ≈ 0.31, so lengths contract to about 31% of rest length.

#### Invariant Proper Length
While length contracts in different frames, all observers agree on the proper length L₀ (length in the object's rest frame). Similarly, all observers agree on proper time intervals (time in an object's rest frame). These invariant quantities are the true physical properties of an object.

#### Relativistic Momentum & Mass
A moving object's momentum p = γmv increases beyond the classical p = mv as v approaches c. The relativistic mass is m_rel = γm, where m is the rest mass. This increase in inertia means it takes ever-greater force to accelerate an object as it approaches c. At v = c, γ → ∞, so infinite force would be required—no massive particle can reach light speed.

#### Conservation of Energy & Momentum
In special relativity, energy and momentum are both conserved. The total energy of a particle is E = γmc². At rest (v = 0), E = mc² (rest energy). The kinetic energy is KE = (γ − 1)mc² = E − mc². As v → c, KE → ∞, confirming that no massive particle can reach light speed with finite energy.

#### Review Questions
1. A spaceship 100 m long travels at 0.9c. What length does a stationary observer measure?
2. What is the rest energy of an electron (m_e ≈ 9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg)?
3. Why does the relativistic mass increase with velocity?
