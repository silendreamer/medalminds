#### Postulates of Special Relativity
(1) The laws of physics are identical in all inertial (non-accelerating) frames of reference. (2) The speed of light in vacuum is the same in all inertial frames (c ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s), independent of the motion of the light source or observer. These seemingly innocent postulates lead to profound consequences: time and space are not absolute; simultaneity is relative; and mass and energy are interchangeable.

#### Relativity of Simultaneity
Two events simultaneous in one frame are not simultaneous in another frame moving relative to the first. This challenges our intuition that "now" has universal meaning. The relativity of simultaneity arises because observers in different frames must synchronize clocks differently, accounting for light travel time.

#### Time Dilation
A moving clock runs slower than a stationary clock. If a moving observer measures a time interval Δt (proper time, in their frame), a stationary observer measures Δt' = γΔt, where γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²) is the Lorentz factor. As v approaches c, γ increases, and the time dilation becomes extreme. At v = 0.9c, γ ≈ 2.3, so moving clocks run about 2.3 times slower. At v = c (light), γ → ∞, and time effectively stops.

#### Worked Example: Muon Decay
Muons (subatomic particles) decay with a proper lifetime of about 2 microseconds. Cosmic-ray muons travel at v ≈ 0.99c (γ ≈ 7). In the lab frame, they live about 7 × 2 = 14 microseconds. This extended lifetime allows muons produced high in the atmosphere to reach Earth's surface before decaying—direct evidence of time dilation.
