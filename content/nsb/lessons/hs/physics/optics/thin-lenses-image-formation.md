#### The Thin-Lens Equation
The **thin-lens equation** relates object distance d_o, image distance d_i, and focal length f:

1/f = 1/d_o + 1/d_i

All three quantities are measured from the lens center. The **focal length** f is positive for a **converging (convex) lens** and negative for a **diverging (concave) lens**. d_o is positive for a real object (light arriving from the object side). d_i is positive when the image forms on the opposite side of the lens from the object (**real image**) and negative when it forms on the same side (**virtual image**).

#### Magnification
**Linear magnification** m is defined as:

m = h_i / h_o = −d_i / d_o

where h_i is the image height and h_o is the object height. Key sign rules:
- m > 0 → image is **upright** (same orientation as object); occurs for virtual images.
- m < 0 → image is **inverted**; occurs for real images through a converging lens.
- |m| > 1 → image is **magnified**; |m| < 1 → image is **diminished**.

#### Real vs Virtual Images
A **real image** is formed where refracted rays actually converge; it can be projected onto a screen. A **virtual image** is formed where diverging rays appear to originate when extended backward; it cannot be projected. For a **converging lens**: if d_o > f, a real, inverted image forms; if d_o < f, a virtual, upright, magnified image forms (magnifying glass). A **diverging lens** always produces a virtual, upright, diminished image regardless of object position.

#### Three-Ray Diagram Method
To locate an image graphically, draw any two of the three **principal rays**:
1. A ray parallel to the principal axis → refracts through the far focal point (converging) or appears to come from the near focal point (diverging).
2. A ray through the optical center → passes straight through undeviated.
3. A ray through the near focal point → exits parallel to the principal axis (converging).

The image is at the intersection of the outgoing rays (real) or their backward extensions (virtual). Ray diagrams should always be cross-checked with the thin-lens equation.

#### Review Questions
1. An object is placed 30 cm from a converging lens with f = 10 cm. Find the image distance, state whether the image is real or virtual, and calculate the magnification.
2. A diverging lens has f = −15 cm. An object is 45 cm from the lens. Where does the image form, and is it upright or inverted?
3. A converging lens is used as a magnifying glass, producing a virtual image 25 cm from the lens on the same side as the object. If f = 5 cm, how far from the lens is the object?
