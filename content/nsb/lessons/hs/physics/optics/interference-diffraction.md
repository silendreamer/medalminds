#### Wave Superposition and Coherence
**Interference** occurs when two or more coherent light waves overlap. **Coherent** sources maintain a constant phase relationship. When crests meet crests the waves undergo **constructive interference**; when a crest meets a trough they undergo **destructive interference**. A stable interference pattern requires sources of the same frequency and a fixed phase difference.

#### Double-Slit Interference
In Young's double-slit experiment, two slits separated by distance **d** act as coherent point sources. The **path difference** between waves arriving at a point on a screen determines the fringe type. For a point at angle θ from the central axis:

- **Bright fringe (constructive):** d · sin θ = m λ, where m = 0, ±1, ±2, …
- **Dark fringe (destructive):** d · sin θ = (m + ½) λ

Here **λ** is the wavelength and **m** is the **order** of the fringe. The central bright fringe (m = 0) has zero path difference. Fringe spacing on a screen at distance L is y = m λ L / d.

#### Diffraction Gratings
A **diffraction grating** contains thousands of equally spaced slits with spacing d. It produces sharply defined bright maxima (called **principal maxima**) wherever the same condition d · sin θ = m λ is satisfied. Because the condition sharpens with more slits, gratings resolve wavelengths far better than a double slit. Longer wavelengths diffract at larger angles, so white light fans into a spectrum at each order.

#### Single-Slit Diffraction
Even a single slit of width **a** produces a diffraction pattern. **Dark fringes** (minima) appear where a · sin θ = m λ (m = ±1, ±2, …). The central bright maximum is twice as wide as the secondary maxima. Narrowing the slit widens the central maximum — an expression of the **wave-particle duality** principle that confinement spreads a wave's angular spread.

#### Review Questions
1. In a double-slit experiment with slit separation d = 0.50 mm and λ = 500 nm, at what angle does the second-order bright fringe (m = 2) appear?
2. A diffraction grating produces a first-order maximum for red light (λ = 700 nm) at 30°. What is the slit spacing d?
3. How does the central diffraction maximum of a single slit change if the slit width is halved?
