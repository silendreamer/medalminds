#### Head-to-Tail Method
To add vectors graphically, place the tail of the second vector at the head of the first. The resultant goes from the original tail to the final head. For vectors at right angles, the resultant magnitude comes from the Pythagorean theorem: R = √(A² + B²).

#### The Component Method
Any vector **A** can be broken into: Aₓ = A cos(θ) and Aᵧ = A sin(θ), where θ is measured from the positive x-axis. To add vectors **A** and **B**: Rₓ = Aₓ + Bₓ, Rᵧ = Aᵧ + Bᵧ. Then R = √(Rₓ² + Rᵧ²) and θ_R = arctan(Rᵧ/Rₓ).

#### Example: Adding Two Forces
Force **F₁** = 40 N at 0° (east). Force **F₂** = 30 N at 90° (north). F₁ₓ = 40, F₁ᵧ = 0. F₂ₓ = 0, F₂ᵧ = 30. Rₓ = 40, Rᵧ = 30. R = √(40² + 30²) = √(1600+900) = √2500 = 50 N. θ = arctan(30/40) = 36.9° north of east.

#### Vector Subtraction
**A** − **B** = **A** + (−**B**). Negating a vector reverses its direction. This is critical for finding change in velocity: Δ**v** = **v**f − **v**i.
