#### What Makes a Quantity a Vector?
A scalar is a single number with a unit: mass (5 kg), temperature (300 K), speed (10 m/s), energy (50 J). A vector has both magnitude and direction: displacement (10 m north), velocity (10 m/s north), force (50 N at 30°). You can't fully specify displacement without saying which way you moved.

#### Representing Vectors
Vectors are drawn as arrows. The arrow's length represents magnitude; the arrowhead shows direction. In equations, vectors are written in bold (**F**) or with an arrow (→F). The magnitude of a vector **A** is written |**A**| or simply A.

#### Key Physics Vectors
Vectors: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum, electric field, magnetic field. Scalars: distance, speed, mass, energy, time, temperature, work (technically a scalar — force dotted with displacement). Note: speed is scalar, velocity is vector. Distance is scalar, displacement is vector.

#### Why Direction Matters
If you walk 3 m east and then 3 m west, your displacement is 0 m — you're back where you started. But your distance traveled is 6 m. Speed (scalar) uses distance; velocity (vector) uses displacement. This distinction is fundamental to kinematics.

#### Review Questions
1. Is acceleration a scalar or vector? Is kinetic energy a scalar or vector?
2. You walk 4 m north and then 3 m east. What is your displacement magnitude? (Hint: Pythagorean theorem.)
3. A car drives 60 km/h north, then 60 km/h south. What is the average velocity? What is the average speed?
