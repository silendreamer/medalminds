#### Unit Vectors: î, ĵ, k̂
Unit vectors have magnitude 1 and point along coordinate axes: î (x-direction), ĵ (y-direction), k̂ (z-direction). Any vector can be written: **A** = Aₓî + Aᵧĵ + Azk̂. Example: a force of 30 N east and 40 N north is written **F** = 30î + 40ĵ N.

#### The Dot Product (Scalar Product)
**A** · **B** = AB cos(θ), where θ is the angle between them. The result is a scalar. Component form: **A** · **B** = AₓBₓ + AᵧBᵧ + AzBz. Special cases: perpendicular vectors → dot product = 0 (cos 90° = 0). Parallel vectors → dot product = AB (cos 0° = 1).

#### Work as a Dot Product
Work W = **F** · **d** = Fd cos(θ). If you push a box with 50 N at 30° to the horizontal, and it moves 10 m horizontally, W = 50 × 10 × cos(30°) = 500 × 0.866 = 433 J. Only the component of force along the displacement does work.

#### When the Dot Product is Zero
If force is perpendicular to motion (θ = 90°), no work is done. Gravity does no work on a satellite in circular orbit (force toward center, motion perpendicular). A normal force does no work on a sliding object (normal force ⊥ surface, motion along surface).

#### Review Questions
1. Calculate **A** · **B** if **A** = 3î + 4ĵ and **B** = 2î − 3ĵ.
2. A force **F** = 10î + 0ĵ N pushes a block that moves **d** = 5î + 3ĵ m. Calculate the work done.
3. At what angle between force and displacement is work maximized? Minimized?
