#### Deceleration from Friction
When a car brakes with locked wheels (skidding), kinetic friction decelerates the car: f = μkN = μkmg → a = −μkg. Using kinematics (v² = v₀² + 2aΔx) with final velocity 0: 0 = v₀² − 2μkgΔx → **Δx = v₀²/(2μkg)**. Doubling speed quadruples stopping distance — critical safety implication.

#### Worked Problem
A 1200 kg car traveling at 25 m/s brakes with μk = 0.7. Find stopping distance.
- a = −μkg = −0.7(9.8) = −6.86 m/s²
- Δx = v₀²/(2|a|) = 625/(2 × 6.86) = 625/13.72 = 45.5 m

#### Anti-Lock Brakes (ABS)
ABS prevents wheels from fully locking by maintaining controlled slipping. Since μs > μk, a rolling-but-decelerating wheel has more friction force than a fully skidding wheel — so ABS actually gives *shorter* stopping distances and maintains steering control.

#### Review Questions
1. A car moving at 30 m/s stops in 60 m. What is the coefficient of kinetic friction between tires and road?
2. If a car's initial speed doubles, by what factor does stopping distance increase (same friction)?
3. A 10 kg crate slides across a warehouse floor (μk = 0.3) and decelerates. It enters the frictionless section at 6 m/s. How far does it travel on the frictionless section before... (it keeps going, so explain what happens).
