#### The Core Equation
**ΣF = ma** — the net force (vector sum of all forces) equals mass times acceleration. Acceleration is in the *same direction* as the net force. Key implications: (1) double the net force → double the acceleration; (2) double the mass → half the acceleration for the same force. Units: force in Newtons (N), where 1 N = 1 kg·m/s².

#### Applying the Law: Strategy
Standard approach — (1) identify all forces on the object; (2) choose a coordinate system; (3) write ΣFx = max and ΣFy = may separately; (4) solve. Forces must be treated as vectors — they have direction. A 10 N force right and a 6 N force left give ΣF = 4 N right, not 16 N.

#### The Weight Equation
Weight is the gravitational force on a mass: **W = mg**, where g = 9.8 m/s² near Earth's surface. A 5 kg object weighs 5 × 9.8 = 49 N. Weight and mass are different: mass is intrinsic (doesn't change), weight depends on gravitational field. On the Moon (g = 1.6 m/s²), a 5 kg object still has mass 5 kg but weighs only 8 N.

#### Multi-Force Example
A 3 kg box is pushed right with 20 N of applied force. Friction acts left with 8 N. Find acceleration: ΣF = 20 − 8 = 12 N right. a = ΣF/m = 12/3 = 4 m/s² right.

#### Superposition of Forces
When multiple forces act simultaneously, they add as vectors. ΣF is the vector sum. Components: ΣFx = F1x + F2x + …, ΣFy = F1y + F2y + … Treat x and y directions independently. Only after finding ΣFx and ΣFy do you compute the magnitude: |ΣF| = √(ΣFx² + ΣFy²).

#### Review Questions
1. A 2 kg object has forces 15 N east and 9 N west acting on it. Find its acceleration (magnitude and direction).
2. What net force is required to accelerate a 1200 kg car from rest to 20 m/s in 5 seconds?
3. On the Moon (g = 1.6 m/s²), what is the weight of a 60 kg astronaut? What is the astronaut's mass?
