#### What Belongs in an FBD
An FBD shows ONE object (drawn as a dot or simple shape) with arrows representing every external force acting ON that object. Forces the object exerts on OTHER objects are NOT included. Common forces: weight (W = mg, downward from center of mass), normal force (N, perpendicular to contact surface), friction (f, parallel to surface opposing motion), tension (T, along rope toward the rope), applied force (F_app, in direction of push/pull), air drag (opposing velocity).

#### Rules for Drawing FBDs
1. Isolate the object — mentally remove it from its surroundings.
2. Identify every surface/object in contact with your object → each gives at least one force (normal, possibly friction).
3. Include gravity on every object with mass.
4. Include any ropes, springs, or fields.
5. Draw arrows from the object (or center), scaled approximately to magnitude.
6. Label each arrow clearly (W, N, f, T, F_app, etc.).

#### Common Mistakes
- Including forces the object exerts on others (Third Law partner forces).
- Forgetting gravity (every massive object has weight).
- Forgetting normal force when the object is on a surface.
- Drawing velocity or acceleration as if they were forces (they are NOT).
- Including internal forces (for a system, you'd need to isolate each piece separately).

#### FBD for Complex Situations
For a block on an inclined plane: (1) gravity (mg, straight down); (2) normal force (perpendicular to incline surface); (3) friction (parallel to incline, opposing motion). When you tilt your coordinate system to align with the incline, mg must be resolved: mg·sinθ down the incline, mg·cosθ into the surface.

#### Review Questions
1. Draw and label the FBD for a book sitting on a table.
2. Draw the FBD for a box being pulled up a ramp by a rope (include friction), then list the forces by name.
3. A ball swings in a horizontal circle on a string. What forces act on it? What is the direction of the net force?
