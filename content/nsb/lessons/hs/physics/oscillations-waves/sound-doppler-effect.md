#### Sound as a Longitudinal Wave
**Sound waves** are **longitudinal mechanical waves** that propagate through a medium by compressing and rarefying the particles in the direction of travel. Unlike transverse waves, the particle displacement is parallel to wave propagation. The **speed of sound** in air at 20 °C is approximately 343 m/s, and it increases with temperature and is higher in denser elastic media such as water and steel. **Frequency** (pitch) and **amplitude** (loudness) are the two principal perceptual qualities linked to physical wave properties.

#### Frequency, Wavelength, and Wave Speed
The fundamental wave relation v = fλ connects wave speed, frequency, and wavelength for any wave including sound. **Frequency** f is measured in hertz (Hz) and **wavelength** λ in meters. For a fixed medium the wave speed is constant, so increasing frequency shortens wavelength proportionally. The **intensity** of a sound wave falls off as the inverse square of distance (I ∝ 1/r²), and intensity level in **decibels** is β = 10 log(I/I₀), where I₀ = 10⁻¹² W/m² is the threshold of hearing.

#### The Doppler Effect
The **Doppler effect** is the observed change in frequency when a source and observer are in relative motion. The general equation is:

f' = f (v ± v_o) / (v ∓ v_s)

where f is the emitted frequency, v is the speed of sound in the medium, v_o is the observer's speed, and v_s is the source's speed. **Sign convention:** use the upper signs (+ for observer, − for source) when the source and observer approach each other (increasing f'), and lower signs when they recede (decreasing f'). When v_s approaches v, the **sonic boom** condition is reached; at v_s = v the wavefronts pile up into a **Mach cone**.

#### Doppler Calculation Strategy
1. Identify the **source frequency** f and the speeds v_o and v_s relative to the medium.
2. Determine the direction of motion: approaching → numerator increases and/or denominator decreases → f' > f; receding → opposite.
3. Substitute into f' = f(v ± v_o)/(v ∓ v_s) and solve.

Example: an ambulance siren at 800 Hz approaches a stationary observer at 34.3 m/s (v = 343 m/s). f' = 800 × 343/(343 − 34.3) = 800 × 343/308.7 ≈ 889 Hz.

#### Review Questions
1. A train horn emits 500 Hz. The train moves toward a stationary observer at 68.6 m/s (v_sound = 343 m/s). What frequency does the observer hear?
2. How does the Doppler formula change if the observer moves toward a stationary source versus the source moving toward a stationary observer at the same speed?
3. Explain why the observed frequency returns to f after the source passes the observer, and calculate the new f' if the train in question 1 is now receding.
