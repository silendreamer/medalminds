### Introduction to Rocks and the Rock Cycle  
Rocks are fundamental components of the Earth's crust and are classified into three main types: igneous, sedimentary, and metamorphic. Each type is formed through distinct geological processes and contributes to the dynamic nature of the Earth's surface. Understanding these rock types and the rock cycle is essential for grasping broader geological concepts, including plate tectonics, erosion, and sedimentation.

### Igneous Rocks  
Igneous rocks form from the solidification of molten rock material called magma or lava. When magma cools and crystallizes beneath the Earth's surface, it creates intrusive igneous rocks, such as granite. Conversely, when lava erupts onto the surface and cools rapidly, it forms extrusive igneous rocks, like basalt. The mineral composition and cooling rate of the magma or lava determine the texture and characteristics of the resulting igneous rock.

### Sedimentary Rocks  
Sedimentary rocks are formed from the accumulation and compaction of mineral and organic particles over time. These rocks often develop in layers and can include fragments of other rocks, minerals, and fossils. Common examples include sandstone, limestone, and shale, which are formed through processes such as lithification, cementation, and chemical precipitation. Sedimentary rocks provide valuable information about Earth's history, including past environments and life forms.

### Metamorphic Rocks  
Metamorphic rocks arise from the transformation of existing rocks—igneous, sedimentary, or even other metamorphic rocks—under heat, pressure, and chemically active fluids. This process, known as metamorphism, alters the mineral composition and structure without melting the rock. Common examples include schist, gneiss, and marble, which exhibit foliation or banding due to the alignment of minerals. Metamorphic rocks are often found in regions of tectonic activity, such as mountain ranges.

### The Rock Cycle  
The rock cycle illustrates the continuous transformation of rocks through various geological processes. It describes how igneous rocks can weather and erode to form sediments, which may then compact into sedimentary rocks. Additionally, sedimentary and igneous rocks can undergo metamorphism to become metamorphic rocks, which can eventually melt back into magma, restarting the cycle. This cyclical nature highlights the interconnectedness of Earth's geological processes and the dynamic changes that shape our planet.

### Geological Features Related to Rock Types  
Different rock types are associated with specific geological features. For instance, igneous rocks often form volcanic landforms, such as mountains and plateaus, while sedimentary rocks are typically found in riverbeds, deltas, and ocean floors. Metamorphic rocks are frequently located in regions of mountain building, where tectonic forces create high pressure and temperature conditions. Understanding these associations helps geologists interpret the Earth's history and predict geological events.
