#### Orogenic Belts and Their Origins
An **orogeny** is an episode of mountain building caused by horizontal compressive forces. Modern orogenic belts form at: (1) Continent-continent collision zones (Himalayas, Alps, Appalachians — each represents a different collision age); (2) Active continental margins with subduction (Andes — volcanic arc + fold-and-thrust belt). Ancient orogenic belts are preserved as **cratons** (stable continental cores) after erosion reduces the mountains to roots. The Appalachians were once as high as the Himalayas; 400 million years of erosion reduced them to modest heights.

#### Isostasy: Mountains Float on the Mantle
The crust floats on the denser mantle according to the principle of **isostasy** — just as an iceberg floats with most of its mass below water, mountain ranges have deep **crustal roots** extending into the mantle. The Himalayas have a root ~70 km deep (normal crust is 30–35 km). As erosion removes material from the surface, the mountain range isostatically rises (like a boat rising as cargo is removed). This is why even heavily eroded ancient mountain belts can have exposures of deep crustal rocks (granulites, high-pressure metamorphic minerals) that were once 30+ km below the surface.

#### Fold-and-Thrust Belts
In compressional settings, sedimentary strata are shortened and thickened by **thrust faults** (reverse faults with shallow dip) and **folds** (anticlines and synclines). The **Valley and Ridge Province** of the Appalachians exposes eroded fold-and-thrust belt structures: ridges of resistant sandstone and limestone formed the crests of anticlines or thrust sheets; valleys developed in weaker shales. The Canadian Rockies, Swiss Alps, and Zagros Mountains (Iran) all show classic fold-and-thrust geometry.
