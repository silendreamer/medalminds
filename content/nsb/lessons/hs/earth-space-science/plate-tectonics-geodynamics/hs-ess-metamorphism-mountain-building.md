#### Regional vs. Contact Metamorphism
**Regional metamorphism** affects large volumes of rock during mountain building — burial under thick thrust sheets increases temperature and pressure, recrystallizing minerals without melting. **Contact metamorphism** occurs locally around igneous intrusions — heat from magma bakes surrounding rock into a **contact aureole**. Regional metamorphism is associated with orogenic belts; contact metamorphism with any magmatic body regardless of tectonic setting.

#### Metamorphic Grade and Index Minerals
As burial depth and temperature increase through a metamorphic terrane, Barrovian zones (defined by Scottish geologist George Barrow) appear in sequence: chlorite → biotite → garnet → staurolite → kyanite → sillimanite. Each **index mineral** first appears at a specific P-T threshold. The **garnet isograd** marks where garnet first forms; rock above (lower grade) contains biotite but no garnet; rock below (higher grade) contains garnet and higher-grade minerals. Kyanite forms under high pressure (20+ km depth); sillimanite at high temperature; andalusite at low pressure.
