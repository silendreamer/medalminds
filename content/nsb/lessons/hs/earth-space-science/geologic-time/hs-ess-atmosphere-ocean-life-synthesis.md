#### The Gaia Hypothesis and Earth System Science
The **Gaia hypothesis** (James Lovelock, Lynn Margulis) proposes that Earth's biosphere actively regulates planetary conditions (temperature, atmospheric composition) to remain habitable — a form of planetary homeostasis. While the strong version is debated, the concept of **Earth system science** — treating the atmosphere, ocean, solid Earth, and biosphere as interacting components — is now standard. The GOE exemplifies this coupling: biological evolution (cyanobacteria) triggered atmospheric chemistry change, which fed back on evolution (ozone shield enabling land colonization) and climate (Snowball Earth glaciation).

#### Feedback Loops in the Carbon Cycle
The **long-term carbon cycle** operates over millions of years and regulates CO₂ via silicate weathering: CO₂ + CaSiO₃ → CaCO₃ + SiO₂. When CO₂ rises, temperature increases, rainfall increases, weathering increases, CO₂ is drawn down — a negative feedback stabilizing Earth's climate over geologic time. This is why Earth has remained habitable for 4 billion years despite the Sun brightening by ~30% (the "Faint Young Sun" paradox was likely resolved by higher Archean CO₂ and CH₄ keeping Earth warm).
