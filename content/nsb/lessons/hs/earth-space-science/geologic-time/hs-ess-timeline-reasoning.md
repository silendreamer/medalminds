#### Integrating Relative and Absolute Methods
Relative dating tells you the order of events; absolute (radiometric) dating tells you how long ago they occurred in years. In practice, geologists use both together. A geologist might determine from stratigraphy that a volcanic ash layer lies between two fossil-bearing units, then date the ash radiometrically to get an absolute age for the fossils above and below.

#### Practice Timeline Exercise
Place these events in correct chronological order and assign approximate dates: (1) First land plants colonize terrestrial environments; (2) Dinosaurs go extinct; (3) Earth forms; (4) First multicellular animals appear; (5) Humans appear; (6) Pangaea begins to break apart. Answers: Earth forms ~4.6 Ga → first multicellular animals ~600 Ma → first land plants ~470 Ma → Pangaea breaks up ~200 Ma → dinosaurs extinct ~66 Ma → humans ~300 ka.

#### Review Questions
1. Why is relative dating still useful even when radiometric dating is available?
2. Arrange the following periods from oldest to youngest: Permian, Cambrian, Cretaceous, Triassic, Ordovician.
3. A volcanic ash layer dates to 250 Ma. The layer above it contains Triassic reptile fossils and the layer below contains the last Permian brachiopods. What event does this ash layer likely record?
