#### What Defines a New Epoch?
Formal time units require a **Global Boundary Stratotype Section and Point (GSSP)** — a physical rock or sediment section representing the boundary, approved by the International Commission on Stratigraphy (ICS). A candidate GSSP for the Anthropocene must have a globally synchronous signal. Proposed markers include: plutonium fallout from nuclear weapons testing beginning ~1950 CE; the "Great Acceleration" of industrial activity post-WWII; or the Columbian Exchange (1492 CE) recorded in pollen and species invasions.

#### The 2024 Decision
In 2024, the ICS voted against formally ratifying the Anthropocene as an official epoch, citing debate over the boundary age and whether human impact meets the geological criteria for a new time unit. However, the concept remains scientifically and culturally influential, driving research on human stratigraphic markers in sediment cores, ice cores, and coral records.

1. What is a GSSP, and why is it required to define a new geologic time unit?
2. What stratigraphic signals are proposed as markers for the Anthropocene?
3. Why did the ICS decline to formally ratify the Anthropocene epoch in 2024?
