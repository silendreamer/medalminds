#### From Molecular Cloud to Protostar
Giant molecular clouds (GMCs) contain cold (10–30 K) gas and dust, primarily H₂ and CO, with masses up to 10⁶ M☉. Collapse begins when a region becomes gravitationally unstable — the Jeans criterion: a cloud fragment collapses when gravitational energy exceeds thermal energy. Triggers include supernova shockwaves, galaxy spiral arm compression, and cloud collisions. As the fragment collapses, it heats (gravitational PE → thermal KE). When the core becomes opaque to its own radiation, it traps heat and forms a protostar — a hot, luminous, hydrostatically-supported object still accreting material from a surrounding disk.

#### T Tauri Phase and Pre-Main Sequence Tracks
A T Tauri star is a pre-main-sequence star (like a proto-Sun) visible optically after it blows away surrounding gas with stellar winds. T Tauri stars show strong emission lines, variability, and sometimes jets. On the H-R diagram, they descend along a Hayashi track (nearly vertical, luminous and cool) as they contract. A solar-mass T Tauri star takes ~50 million years to reach the main sequence; higher-mass protostars contract faster. Nuclear ignition occurs when core temperature exceeds ~10 million K, and hydrogen fusion via the proton-proton chain begins.

2. Why does a contracting protostar get hotter over time even before nuclear fusion begins?
3. What observable phase between protostar and main-sequence star is characterized by strong emission lines and stellar winds?
