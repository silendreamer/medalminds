#### Black Hole-Galaxy Co-evolution
The M-σ relation: a tight correlation between supermassive black hole mass and the velocity dispersion (σ) of the host galaxy's bulge — despite the black hole being 1,000 times smaller than the bulge. This implies the BH and galaxy bulge co-evolved. AGN feedback: energy and momentum from the AGN can heat or expel the host galaxy's gas, quenching star formation. This "self-regulation" may explain why massive elliptical galaxies are "red and dead." The peak of quasar activity was at z ≈ 2–3 (~10–11 billion years ago); most massive black holes have since run out of fuel.

#### Review Questions
1. What is the M-sigma relation, and why does it suggest black holes and galaxies co-evolved?
2. Why were quasars much more common in the early universe than they are today?
3. How might AGN feedback quench star formation in a host galaxy?
