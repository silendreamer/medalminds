#### The Distance Ladder in Practice
No single method spans all cosmic distances. Astronomers use a bootstrap approach: parallax for nearby stars (up to ~3 kpc with Gaia), Cepheid variables for nearby galaxies (up to ~30 Mpc), and Type Ia supernovae (standard explosions with known intrinsic brightness) for the most distant galaxies. Each rung is calibrated against the one below it, so systematic errors can compound. The "Hubble tension" — the ~9% discrepancy between early-universe (Planck CMB) and late-universe (Cepheid+SN Ia) measurements of H₀ — is one of the biggest open problems in cosmology.

#### Cepheid Variables: The Key Rung
Cepheid variables pulsate with periods of 1–100 days. Henrietta Leavitt (1912) discovered that their period correlates with absolute luminosity: longer period = intrinsically brighter. Measure the period → know the luminosity → compare to apparent brightness → get the distance. Hubble used Cepheids in M31 (Andromeda) to prove it lies far outside the Milky Way, establishing the extragalactic nature of the universe. JWST has recently provided sharper Cepheid measurements to try to resolve the Hubble tension.
