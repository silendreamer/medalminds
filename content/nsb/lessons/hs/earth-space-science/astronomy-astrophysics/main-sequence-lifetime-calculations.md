#### Lifetime Estimation
A star's main-sequence lifetime ≈ fuel available / consumption rate ∝ M/L. Since L ∝ M⁴, lifetime ∝ M/M⁴ = M⁻³. A 10 M☉ star is 10,000 times more luminous but has only 10 times more hydrogen — it lives ~1,000 times shorter than the Sun. Sun: ~10 billion years total MS lifetime (currently ~4.6 billion years old). 0.3 M☉ red dwarf: ~100 billion years (longer than the current age of the universe). 50 M☉ star: ~1–3 million years. Massive O stars end their lives before their home galaxy finishes one rotation.

#### Cluster Age from Turnoff Point
In a star cluster, all stars formed at roughly the same time. The most massive (most luminous, hottest) leave the main sequence first. The "main sequence turnoff" — where stars start veering off toward giants — reveals the cluster's age. The Pleiades (young cluster, ~100 million years) still has bright B stars. Globular clusters (ancient, ~12 billion years) have turnoffs at low-luminosity F stars near the Sun's position.

3. A globular cluster has a main-sequence turnoff at stars with mass ~0.8 M☉. Roughly how old is the cluster?
