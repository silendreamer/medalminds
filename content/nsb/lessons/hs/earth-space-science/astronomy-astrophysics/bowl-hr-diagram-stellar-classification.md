#### Key Facts for Competition
- OBAFGKM: O hottest (~30,000 K+), M coolest (~3,500 K); mnemonic "Oh Be A Fine Girl/Guy, Kiss Me"
- Sun = G2V (G-type, luminosity class V = main sequence dwarf)
- Sirius = A1V (hottest commonly-named bright star visible to naked eye in northern sky)
- Betelgeuse = M2Iab (cool red supergiant; upper right of H-R diagram)
- Rigel = B8Ia (hot blue supergiant; upper left)
- Chandrasekhar limit = 1.4 M☉ (max mass of white dwarf before collapsing to neutron star)
- Main sequence: H → He fusion; time on MS ∝ M⁻³ approximately
- Wien's law: λ_max·T = 2.898 × 10⁶ nm·K
- Stefan-Boltzmann: L ∝ R²T⁴
- H-R diagram: luminosity on Y axis, temperature on X axis (hot LEFT, cool RIGHT — reversed)

#### Toss-Up Recognition Patterns
- "…hotter on the left, cooler on the right…" or "…temperature increases to the left…" → H-R diagram axis convention
- "…Oh Be A Fine Girl Kiss Me…" or "…spectral class O through M…" → stellar spectral classification sequence
- "…maximum mass before a white dwarf collapses…" → Chandrasekhar limit (1.4 solar masses)
- "…discovered that period correlates with luminosity in variable stars…" → Henrietta Leavitt / Cepheid period-luminosity relation
- "…expands to a red giant, sheds layers as a planetary nebula, leaves behind…" → white dwarf formation (for solar-mass stars)

#### Review Questions
1. For 10 points — what law states that the wavelength at which a star emits most intensely is inversely proportional to its surface temperature?
2. What is the maximum mass of a white dwarf, above which it will collapse to a neutron star?
3. Place these stars in order from hottest to coolest surface temperature: Betelgeuse, Sirius, Rigel, Proxima Centauri, the Sun.
