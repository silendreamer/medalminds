#### Copernicus to Newton: The Scientific Revolution in Astronomy
Ptolemy's geocentric model (~140 CE) used epicycles to match planetary observations. Copernicus (1543) proposed heliocentrism, simplifying the model but still using circles. Brahe's precise observations (1570s–1590s) without a telescope were the most accurate data of the era. Kepler used Brahe's data to derive elliptical orbits (1609–1619). Galileo (1610) used the telescope to observe Jupiter's moons, Venus's phases, and sunspots — all incompatible with strict geocentrism. Newton (1687) unified terrestrial and celestial mechanics: the same gravity that drops an apple keeps the Moon in orbit.

#### Review Questions
1. What specific telescopic observations did Galileo make that supported heliocentrism?
2. Why was Tycho Brahe's precise naked-eye observational data critical to Kepler's discoveries?
3. How did Newton's law of universal gravitation explain and extend Kepler's empirical laws?
