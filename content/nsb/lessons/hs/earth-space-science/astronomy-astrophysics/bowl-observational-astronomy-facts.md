#### Key Facts for Competition
- 1 parsec = 3.26 light-years = 206,265 AU; closest star Proxima Centauri at 4.24 ly = 1.30 pc
- Apparent magnitude scale: lower = brighter; Sirius (brightest star) m = –1.46; Full Moon m ≈ –12.7; Sun m ≈ –26.7
- Stellar parallax: p = 1/d(pc); only useful to ~a few thousand parsecs even with Gaia
- North Star = Polaris (Alpha Ursae Minoris); ~0.7° from North Celestial Pole
- Vernal equinox = RA origin, ~March 20; Sun crosses celestial equator moving northward
- Saros cycle = 18 years 11 days; used to predict eclipses
- Earth's axial tilt = 23.5° (more precisely 23.44°)
- Perihelion in early January; aphelion in early July (counterintuitive — Southern Hemisphere summer = Earth closest to Sun)

#### Toss-Up Recognition Patterns
- "…sweeps equal areas in equal times…" → Kepler's second law
- "…period squared proportional to semi-major axis cubed…" → Kepler's third law
- "…apparent shift of a nearby star against background stars…" → stellar parallax
- "…observer in the umbra sees a total…" → solar eclipse (total)
- "…redder because Earth's atmosphere refracts…" → lunar eclipse explanation

#### Review Questions
1. Sirius has apparent magnitude –1.46 and Betelgeuse has apparent magnitude 0.42. Which is brighter as seen from Earth?
2. A star at 2 parsecs has what parallax angle in arcseconds?
3. For 10 points — what law states that an imaginary line connecting a planet to the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal times?
