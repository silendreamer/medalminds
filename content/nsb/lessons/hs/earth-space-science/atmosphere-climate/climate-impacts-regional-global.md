#### Water Resources
Warming increases evaporation globally, intensifying drought and flood extremes. Snow-dominated water supplies (Alps, Himalayas, Andes, Sierra Nevada) are declining as spring snowpack decreases, causing summer water stress. California's drought (2011–2017, with recurrences 2021–2023) was exacerbated by reduced snowpack. The Colorado River (supply for ~40 million people) is over-allocated and declining due to warming-driven demand increases and snowpack loss. Groundwater depletion (Ogallala Aquifer, Indian aquifers) is accelerated by irrigation demands from drought. Conversely, increased precipitation in some regions (northern high latitudes, southeastern US) increases flood risk. Glacier loss threatens water supplies for ~2 billion people dependent on glacier-fed rivers (Himalayas, Andes).

#### Agriculture and Food Security
Warming affects crop yields nonlinearly: some regions and crops benefit from longer growing seasons and CO2 fertilization (trees, perennials), but most crops have optimum temperatures. Maize, wheat, rice yields decline above ~30°C growth temperatures. Heat stress during flowering/grain fill (critical periods) reduces yields substantially. Tropics and subtropics face yield declines; some temperate crops shift poleward. Extreme events (floods, droughts, hail) damage crops—the 2012 US Corn Belt drought reduced yields by ~20%. Climate change shifts pest and disease ranges northward; range expansion of pests (armyworms, coffee rust) threatens production. Soil moisture changes from altered precipitation patterns—southwestern North America faces semi-aridification. Overall, global crop yields are projected to decline 2–6% per °C of warming without adaptation. Food security is threatened, especially for nations dependent on imports.

#### Ecosystem Disruption
Ecosystems are under stress from warming, species range shifts, and phenological mismatches. Coral bleaching kills reef-building corals; if warming exceeds 1.5–2°C, tropical coral reefs are projected to decline by 70–90%. Mountain ecosystems face "upslope migration" of species until they run out of mountain—alpine species face extinction. Forest composition shifts as trees reach thermal limits (e.g., white pine drought die-off in Western North America). Permafrost thaw releases methane and CO2 (positive feedback), destabilizes infrastructure. Lake ice duration shortens—fish phenology may mismatch with zooplankton. Marine ecosystems see shifting fish distributions, changing food web structure. Some species adapt/shift; others face extinction.

#### Extreme Events
Climate change intensifies heat waves (hotter, longer, more frequent), droughts (more severe, longer duration), and precipitation extremes (heavier rainfall, more intense cyclones). Heat waves are now the leading cause of weather-related deaths; the 2021 Pacific Northwest heat dome (50°C+) was "virtually impossible" without climate change. Wildfires are accelerated by hotter, drier conditions—California's 2020 fire season was its worst on record. Precipitation intensity increases: for every 1°C of warming, the atmosphere can hold ~7% more moisture (Clausius-Clapeyron), so heavy downpours intensify. Hurricane intensification may accelerate over warmer oceans; the proportion of Cat 4–5 hurricanes increases. Flooding from extreme precipitation and storm surge increases in coastal areas.

#### Socioeconomic Impacts
Economic costs of climate impacts are rising: agricultural losses, damages from extreme weather, health costs from heat/air quality, infrastructure damage from sea level rise, migration and conflict driven by resource scarcity. The 2011 Russian heatwave and drought killed tens of thousands and destroyed harvests, triggering grain price spikes that contributed to Arab Spring instability. Tropical regions and developing nations, least responsible for emissions, are most vulnerable. Small island states face existential threats from sea level rise. Sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia face water stress and agricultural decline. Economic modeling suggests unmitigated warming of 3–4°C could reduce global GDP by 5–20% by 2100. Adaptation requires massive infrastructure investment; mitigation requires emissions reductions.

#### Review Questions
1. Explain how warming reduces snowpack-dependent water supplies and affects summer water availability.
2. Why do tropical crops face greater yield risk from warming than temperate crops?
3. How does the Clausius-Clapeyron relationship connect to precipitation intensity increases?
