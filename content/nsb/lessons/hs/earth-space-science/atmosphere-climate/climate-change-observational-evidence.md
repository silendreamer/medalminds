#### Global Temperature Trends
Global average surface temperature has risen ~1.1–1.3°C since pre-industrial times (1850–1900 baseline). The rate of warming is accelerating: 1900–1975 (~0.01°C/decade), 1975–2000 (~0.17°C/decade), 2000–2024 (~0.20°C/decade). The warmest years on record are 2023 (1.35°C above 1850–1900), 2016 (1.27°C), and 2024 (1.26°C). This warming is observed in multiple independent datasets (NASA GISS, NOAA, UK Met Office, Japan Met), all showing consistent trends. Satellite data since 1979 confirms the warming. Warming is not uniform: land warms faster than ocean (~1.5× faster), polar regions warm faster than tropics (Arctic amplification), and nighttime lows increase more than daytime highs (reduced diurnal temperature range).

#### Sea Level Rise
Global mean sea level has risen ~8–9 inches (~21–24 cm) since 1880, with the rate accelerating from ~1.4 mm/year (1900–1990) to ~4 mm/year (2010–2024). Causes: (1) thermal expansion of ocean water (~40% of rise), (2) melting of mountain glaciers (~25%), (3) Greenland ice sheet loss (~25%), (4) Antarctic ice sheet loss (~10%). These fractions vary interannually. Future sea level rise is projected at 0.3–2.5 m by 2100 depending on emissions scenario and ice sheet dynamics. Coastal cities like Miami, Venice, and Bangkok face existential threats from sea level rise. Even "low" scenarios of 0.3 m require billions in adaptation infrastructure.

#### Ice Loss
Arctic sea ice extent has declined ~13% per decade since 1979, with September minimum (least ice) declining faster (~13% per decade). The Arctic is now ice-free (<1 million km²) every September; historically this occurred rarely. Greenland ice sheet mass loss has accelerated from ~25 Gt/year (1990s) to ~280 Gt/year (2010s). This loss occurs through surface melting (enhanced by lower albedo as snow becomes dirty) and ice sheet calving. The Antarctic ice sheet is losing mass at ~150 Gt/year, mainly from West Antarctic ice sheet destabilization. Global glacier mass loss is ~400 Gt/year (equivalent to ~1 mm/year of sea level rise). These ice losses are well-documented by satellite measurement (GRACE, altimetry) and are accelerating.

#### Ocean Warming and Acidification
The ocean has warmed ~0.13°C per decade since 1901, with most warming since 1975. Ocean warming is occurring from surface to depth—the 0–2,000 m layer has warmed ~0.1°C since 1960. This heat uptake is massive: the ocean has absorbed ~90% of the excess heat from greenhouse gas warming, delaying atmospheric warming. Ocean acidification: CO2 dissolves in seawater forming carbonic acid (H2CO3), lowering pH. Ocean pH has decreased by 0.11 units since pre-industrial times—a 30% increase in acidity. Calcifying organisms (corals, mollusks, pteropods, foraminifera) are affected: their shells dissolve in low-pH water. Coral bleaching events have increased dramatically—2016 saw mass bleaching of the Great Barrier Reef due to warm water.

#### Biological Indicators
Species are responding to warming: ranges shifting poleward (~1 km/year latitudinally, ~100 m upslope in mountains), breeding times shifting earlier (~2–5 days per decade), migration patterns changing. Phenological shifts (timing of life cycle events) are well-documented across taxa. For example, spring leaf-out in European forests has advanced by ~1 week since 1950. Bird migration is timed to spring food availability; if plants leaf out earlier but birds arrive at their historical times, there's a mismatch ("phenological mismatch") risking food shortages. Extinctions have already occurred: the Bramble Cay melomys (Australia) went extinct in 2016, likely due to sea level rise and habitat loss.

#### Review Questions
1. What is the rate of global temperature increase in the last two decades and how does it compare to the 20th century average?
2. Explain why global sea level is rising even if some regions (like Eastern Canada) experience local sea level fall.
3. How has ocean acidification occurred and why is pH change concerning for marine ecosystems?
