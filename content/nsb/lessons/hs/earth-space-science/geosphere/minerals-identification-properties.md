#### What Is a Mineral?
A mineral is a naturally occurring, inorganic, crystalline solid with a definite chemical composition. There are ~5,500 known minerals; about 30 are common. Rocks are aggregates of minerals (or occasionally one mineral: quartzite = almost pure quartz).

#### Physical Properties for Identification
- **Hardness** (Mohs scale 1–10): Talc(1), Gypsum(2), Calcite(3), Fluorite(4), Apatite(5), Feldspar(6), Quartz(7), Topaz(8), Corundum(9), Diamond(10). Memory: "Tall Girls Can Flirt And Every Quite Tall Cute Dog." Fingernail ≈ 2.5; copper penny ≈ 3.5; steel file ≈ 6.5.
- **Cleavage**: tendency to break along flat planes (e.g., mica cleaves in one direction; halite in three directions at 90°; calcite in three directions not at 90°)
- **Fracture**: irregular break (e.g., quartz has conchoidal/shell-like fracture)
- **Luster**: metallic vs. nonmetallic (vitreous/glassy, resinous, pearly, silky)
- **Color**: often unreliable due to impurities; **streak** (color of powder) more reliable
- **Specific gravity**: density relative to water (gold = 19.3; galena = 7.6; quartz = 2.65)

#### Key Minerals to Know
- **Quartz** (SiO₂): hardness 7, conchoidal fracture, vitreous luster; most common mineral in continental crust
- **Feldspar group** (hardness 6): most abundant mineral family (~60% of crust); K-feldspar and plagioclase feldspar
- **Mica** (biotite, muscovite): perfect single-plane cleavage, flaky
- **Calcite** (CaCO₃): hardness 3, reacts with HCl (fizzes), forms limestone and marble
- **Olivine** ((Mg,Fe)₂SiO₄): common in mantle, mafic rocks; forms green gemstone peridot
- **Pyrite** (FeS₂): "fool's gold," metallic cubic crystals, streak = black

#### Silicate Minerals
Most common mineral group: silicates, based on the SiO₄ tetrahedron. Olivine → pyroxene → amphibole → mica → feldspar → quartz = Bowen's Reaction Series (order of crystallization from cooling magma, from high to low temperature).
