#### Key Facts
- C-14 half-life: 5,730 years; useful range ~50,000 years; organic material only
- U-238 → Pb-206 half-life: 4.47 billion years; zircon dating; age of Earth ~4.54 Ga
- K-40 → Ar-40 half-life: 1.25 billion years; volcanic rocks; used to date the KT boundary (66 Ma)
- Rb-87 → Sr-87 half-life: 47 billion years; very old rocks
- Jack Hills zircons: oldest known terrestrial material, ~4.4 Ga (Western Australia)
- δ¹⁸O in foraminifera: higher (more positive) during glacials (¹⁶O locked in ice); proxy for past ice volume and temperature
- C-14 produced by: cosmic ray bombardment of N-14 in upper atmosphere
- Isochron method: eliminates uncertainty about initial daughter isotope abundance

#### Toss-Up Patterns
- "…5,730 years…organic material…" → radiocarbon (C-14) dating
- "…4.47 billion years…uranium-238 to lead-206…zircon…" → U-Pb dating
- "…foraminifera shells…oxygen-18 to oxygen-16 ratio…past ice volume…" → δ¹⁸O paleoclimate proxy
- "…half-life…parent-daughter ratio…absolute age…" → radiometric dating

#### Review Questions
1. For 10 points — what radiometric dating system is used to date organic materials up to ~50,000 years old, using an isotope produced by cosmic rays in the atmosphere?
2. A rock contains equal amounts of U-238 and Pb-206 with no initial Pb-206. How old is the rock? (U-238 half-life = 4.47 Ga)
3. Why does the δ¹⁸O of marine carbonates increase during glacial periods?
