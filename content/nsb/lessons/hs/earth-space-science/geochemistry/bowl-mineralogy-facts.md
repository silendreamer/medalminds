#### Key Facts
- Mohs hardness scale: talc(1), gypsum(2), calcite(3), fluorite(4), apatite(5), feldspar(6), quartz(7), topaz(8), corundum(9), diamond(10)
- Quartz (SiO₂): hardness 7, conchoidal fracture, vitreous luster, no cleavage
- Calcite (CaCO₃): hardness 3, rhombohedral cleavage, fizzes with HCl
- Halite (NaCl): 3 perfect cleavages at 90°, salty taste, cubic crystals
- Feldspar: hardness 6, 2 cleavages at ~90°; most abundant mineral group in crust
- Olivine: (Mg,Fe)₂SiO₄; common in mantle and basalt; green color; no cleavage
- Pyrite (FeS₂): "fool's gold"; cubic, brassy yellow; black streak (differs from gold's yellow streak)
- Magnetite (Fe₃O₄): strongly magnetic; black, metallic luster
- Diamond: hardest natural substance (10); pure carbon; isometric; perfect octahedral cleavage
