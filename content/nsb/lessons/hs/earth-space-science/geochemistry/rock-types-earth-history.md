#### Stratigraphy and Sedimentary Records
Principle of original horizontality: sedimentary strata were deposited horizontally. Principle of superposition: in an undisturbed sequence, older layers are below younger ones. Principle of cross-cutting relationships: a feature that cuts across other features is younger. Unconformities — gaps in the sedimentary record — represent periods of erosion or non-deposition: angular unconformity (tilted layers below horizontal layers), disconformity (both horizontal, but missing time), nonconformity (sedimentary over igneous/metamorphic). Index fossils (from short-lived but geographically widespread species) are used for correlation of strata across distances.

#### Metamorphic Rocks as Tectonic Records
The grade of metamorphism (low → high) records burial depth and geothermal gradient. Blueschist facies (high pressure, low temperature): formed in cold subducting slabs. Eclogite facies (very high pressure): subducted oceanic crust at depth. Granulite facies (high T, moderate P): lower continental crust. The sequence of mineral assemblages (index minerals: chlorite → biotite → garnet → staurolite → kyanite → sillimanite in pelitic rocks) records the P-T path a rock followed — key for reconstructing ancient mountain belts.
