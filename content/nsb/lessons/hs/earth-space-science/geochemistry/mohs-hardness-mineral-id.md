#### The Mohs Hardness Scale
The **Mohs hardness scale**, devised by Friedrich Mohs in 1812, ranks minerals from 1 (softest) to 10 (hardest) based on scratch resistance. The ten reference minerals in order are: **1 Talc, 2 Gypsum, 3 Calcite, 4 Fluorite, 5 Apatite, 6 Orthoclase feldspar, 7 Quartz, 8 Topaz, 9 Corundum, 10 Diamond**. A mineral scratches any mineral with a lower number and is scratched by any with a higher number. Common field references: a fingernail (~2.5), copper coin (~3.5), steel knife (~5.5), and glass plate (~5.5).

#### Luster and Color
**Luster** describes how a mineral's surface reflects light and is one of the first properties noted in identification. **Metallic luster** looks like polished metal (e.g., pyrite, galena); **non-metallic luster** includes subtypes such as vitreous (glassy — quartz), resinous (sulfur), pearly (muscovite), silky (selenite gypsum), and adamantine (diamond). **Color** is often the least reliable property because trace impurities can drastically change a mineral's apparent color (e.g., quartz appears in many colors).

#### Streak and Cleavage
**Streak** is the color of a mineral's powder, tested by rubbing the specimen across an unglazed porcelain streak plate (hardness ~6.5). Streak is more consistent than surface color: hematite always yields a red-brown streak regardless of specimen color. **Cleavage** is the tendency to break along flat planes of weak atomic bonding. It is described by the number of planes and their angles — for example, halite has **three cleavages at 90°** (cubic), calcite has **three cleavages not at 90°** (rhombohedral), and mica has **one perfect cleavage** (basal). Minerals that break irregularly show **fracture** rather than cleavage.

#### Other Diagnostic Properties
Several additional properties narrow identification decisively. **Specific gravity** (density relative to water) is high for metallic ore minerals: galena (~7.6), magnetite (~5.2). **Magnetism** is diagnostic for **magnetite**. **Effervescence** (fizzing) with dilute HCl identifies **calcite** (and distinguishes it from dolomite, which fizzes only when powdered). **Double refraction** (splitting a single image into two) is unique to **Iceland spar calcite**. **Fluorescence** under UV light identifies certain fluorite and scheelite specimens.

#### Review Questions
1. A mineral scratches glass (hardness 5.5) but is scratched by a steel knife (hardness 5.5). Using the Mohs scale, what is the approximate hardness range of this mineral, and name one reference mineral in that range?
2. Why is streak considered a more reliable identification property than color, and what streak color does hematite produce?
3. Describe how cleavage differs from fracture, and give the cleavage description for halite.
