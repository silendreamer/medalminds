### Introduction to Wave Properties

Waves are disturbances that transfer energy through space or a medium without the permanent displacement of particles. They can be classified into mechanical waves, which require a medium (like water or air), and electromagnetic waves, which can travel through a vacuum. Understanding wave properties such as frequency, amplitude, and speed is crucial for analyzing various natural phenomena, including ocean waves and sound waves.

### Wave Frequency

Frequency refers to the number of complete wave cycles that pass a given point in one second, measured in hertz (Hz). Higher frequency waves have shorter wavelengths and carry more energy, while lower frequency waves have longer wavelengths and carry less energy. In coastal processes, the frequency of waves can influence how they interact with the shoreline, affecting erosion and sediment deposition.

### Wave Amplitude

Amplitude is the maximum displacement of points on a wave from its rest position, which directly relates to the energy carried by the wave. In ocean waves, a higher amplitude indicates larger waves, which can result in more significant coastal impact during storms or high tides. Understanding amplitude is essential for predicting wave behavior, especially in terms of wave breaking and the potential for flooding in coastal areas.

### Wave Speed

Wave speed is defined as the distance a wave travels per unit of time and is influenced by the medium through which it propagates. In water, wave speed can be calculated using the formula \( v = f \lambda \), where \( v \) is wave speed, \( f \) is frequency, and \( \lambda \) is wavelength. Coastal processes are affected by wave speed, as faster waves can lead to more intense wave action against shorelines, contributing to erosion and changes in coastal topography.

### Wave Interference

Wave interference occurs when two or more waves overlap and combine to form a new wave pattern. There are two types of interference: constructive interference, where waves align to increase amplitude, and destructive interference, where waves cancel each other out. This phenomenon is particularly important in coastal environments, where waves from different sources can interact, leading to complex patterns of wave behavior that can affect navigation and coastal management.

### Coastal Processes Influenced by Waves

Waves play a critical role in shaping coastlines through processes such as erosion, sediment transport, and deposition. The energy from waves can erode rocks and cliffs, while the movement of water can transport sand and other materials along the shore. Understanding wave dynamics is essential for predicting changes in coastal landscapes and for implementing effective conservation and management strategies.
